👂 LO1 · External ear (slides 4–5)

Three components of the external ear listed on slide 4…Auricle (pinna), external acoustic meatus (ear canal), cartilages
Two named features of the auricle (pinna) (slide 4)?Marginal cutaneous sac (5) and tragus (2)
Number of the marginal cutaneous sac on slide 4's figure, and where it sits?(5) — a skin pocket on the caudolateral margin of the auricle near its base
Number of the tragus on slide 4's figure?(2) — at the rostral edge of the opening of the ear canal
Other name for the external acoustic meatus (slide 4)?Ear canal (labeled 'external ear canal' on slide 3)
Three cartilages of the external ear (slide 4)…Auricular cartilage, annular cartilage, scutiform cartilage
Which ear cartilage is interposed in the auricular muscles (slide 4)?Scutiform cartilage — the rostral and caudal auricular muscles attach here → move the ear
Which cartilage forms the shape of the auricle/pinna?Auricular cartilage — forms the BULK of the pinna
Annular cartilage — what is it (slide 4)?Ring-like cartilage at the BASE of the ear canal, below the auricular cartilage
What is the small separate cartilage labeled 9 on slide 5?No legend on slide 5 — likely the scutiform cartilage (outside knowledge — confirm)
What is shown at the right of slide 5's figure next to the dog's auricular cartilage?A human ear for comparison (numbers 5–8 match labels on the dog cartilage; no legend on the slide)
Job of the external ear (pinna)?Acoustics: concentrates sound waves into the ear canal (external acoustic meatus)

🥁 LO2 · Middle ear — walls & windows (slides 7–8)

Middle ear parts listed in bold on slide 7…Tympanic membrane (eardrum), tympanic cavity; medial wall = petrous temporal bone
Medial wall of the tympanic cavity is formed by… (slide 7)Petrous temporal bone (petrous part of the temporal bone)
Vestibular window — other name, position, connects to, covered by (slide 7)?OVAL window; DORSAL; connects to the vestibule; covered by the stapes
Cochlear window — other name, position, connects to, closed by (slide 7)?ROUND window; VENTRAL; connects to the cavity of the cochlea; closed by the secondary tympanic membrane
Promontory (slide 7)?Where the medial wall bulges over the cochlea, ventral to BOTH windows
Tympanic membrane — other name (slide 7)?Eardrum
Which bony structure houses the ventral part of the tympanic cavity (slides 8, 12)?Tympanic bulla
Limbus of the tympanic membrane (slide 8)?The thickened rim where the eardrum attaches to the bone
Which part of the malleus is visible through/attached to the tympanic membrane on slide 8?Manubrium of the malleus
Which nerve crosses the medial surface of the tympanic membrane on slides 8 and 11?Chorda tympani
Structures labeled on the cochlea cut-away of slide 8…Scala tympani, osseous spiral lamina, secondary spiral lamina, scala vestibuli, lamina of modiolus, modiolus, cupula, cut edge of promontory
Modiolus (slide 8)?The bony central core of the cochlea that the spiral wraps around
Cupula (slide 8)?The apex (dome) of the cochlea
Nerves in the internal acoustic meatus on slide 8…Facial nerve, vestibular nerve, cochlear nerve
Bony landmark labeled at the bottom left of slide 8?Retroarticular process (of the temporal bone)
Three parts of the temporal bone?Squamous, tympanic, petrous (petrous = medial wall of the tympanic cavity + houses the inner ear)

🔨 LO2 · Auditory ossicles & muscles (slides 10–11)

Sound path he walked through in lecture…Pinna concentrates sound → ear canal → tympanic membrane vibrates → malleus → incus → stapes → vestibular (oval) window → inner ear
Order of the auditory ossicles from tympanic membrane to vestibular window (slide 10)…Malleus (hammer) → incus (anvil) → stapes (stirrup)
Malleus — common name, and its 3 bullet points (slide 10)?Hammer; manubrium attaches to the tympanic membrane; head articulates with the incus; tensor tympani muscle
Incus — common name and role (slide 10)?Anvil; connects the malleus to the stapes
Stapes — common name and its 3 bullet points (slide 10)?Stirrup; articulates with the incus; attaches to the vestibular window; stapedius muscle
Which part of the malleus attaches to the tympanic membrane (slide 10)?Manubrium
Which muscle acts on the malleus? Which on the stapes? (slide 10)Malleus → tensor tympani muscle; stapes → stapedius muscle
Innervation of the tensor tympani and stapedius?Tensor tympani → mandibular nerve (trigeminal); stapedius → facial nerve (outside knowledge — confirm; slide 11 labels 'nerve to tensor tympani' and the facial nerve beside the stapedius)
Middle ear structures labeled on slide 11 (left side)…Lateral wall of vestibule, facial nerve, base of stapes, long crus of incus, tensor tympani muscle, major petrosal nerve, nerve to tensor tympani muscle, rostral ligament of malleus, chorda tympani, tensor veli palatini muscle, auditory tube
Structures labeled on slide 11 (right side)…Anterior semicircular canal, floccular fossa, lateral semicircular canal, stapedius muscle, facial nerve, tympanohyoid cartilage, jugulohyoideus muscle
Which part of the incus is labeled on slide 11?Long crus of the incus
Which ligament of the malleus is labeled on slide 11?Rostral ligament of the malleus
What does the tympanohyoid cartilage connect (slide 11)?Hyoid apparatus to the skull near the middle ear (outside knowledge — confirm)
What do numbers 1–8 on slide 10's figure mark?No legend on the slide — figure shows the tympanic membrane with the manubrium (4) running into it; red muscle (6) = likely tensor tympani; 5 = likely chorda tympani (outside knowledge — confirm)

🌀 LO3 · Inner ear — labyrinths (slides 12–13)

Which bony part houses which soft-tissue part?VESTIBULE → utricle + saccule · SEMICIRCULAR CANALS → semicircular ducts · COCHLEA → cochlear duct
Is the 'cochlea' soft tissue or bone?BONE (part of the bony labyrinth). The soft tissue inside it is the cochlear DUCT
Job of the perilymph?Suspends the membranous labyrinth in the bony excavations — acts like a shock absorber
Where are the bony labyrinth excavations?In the PETROUS part of the temporal bone, carved to the exact shape of the soft structures (like the cranium housing the brain)
Two labyrinths of the inner ear and their fluids (slide 12)…Membranous labyrinth = endolymph; bony labyrinth = perilymph
Parts of the membranous labyrinth (slide 12)…Utricle and semicircular ducts; saccule and cochlear duct
Parts of the bony labyrinth (slide 12)…Vestibule, semicircular canals, cochlea
Which membranous part pairs with the semicircular ducts? With the cochlear duct? (slide 12)Utricle ↔ semicircular ducts; saccule ↔ cochlear duct
Fluid in the membranous labyrinth (slide 12)?Endolymph
Fluid in the bony labyrinth (slide 12)?Perilymph
Membranous labyrinth structures labeled on slide 12…Semicircular ducts, utricle, saccule, endolymphatic duct, endolymphatic sac, cochlear duct
Endolymphatic duct leads to… (slide 12)Endolymphatic sac (beside the dura mater)
Spaces of the cochlea on slide 12, around the cochlear duct…Scala vestibuli and scala tympani
Other labels on slide 12 (right side)…Semicircular canals, petrous part of temporal bone, perilymphatic space of vestibule, base of stapes in vestibular window, stapes, incus, malleus, external acoustic meatus, cochlear window, tympanic membrane, auditory tube, tympanic cavity, tympanic bulla
Cochlear canaliculus (slides 12–13)?Small channel from the cochlea's perilymph space through the petrous bone (labeled on both slides)
Three semicircular canals named on slide 13…Anterior, lateral, posterior semicircular canals
Openings/channels labeled on the petrous bone on slide 13…Internal acoustic meatus, cochlear canaliculus, vestibular aqueduct
Which nerve exits through the internal acoustic meatus on slide 13?Cochlear nerve (vestibular and facial nerves also on slide 8)
Bone that houses the inner ear (slides 12–13)?Petrous part of the temporal bone
Other bony landmark labeled on slide 13?Zygomatic process of the temporal bone
Function of the vestibular apparatus vs the cochlea?Vestibular apparatus (utricle, saccule, semicircular ducts) = balance; cochlea = hearing (outside knowledge — confirm)

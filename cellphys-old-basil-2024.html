  2. B.Adenine – Thymine
  3. C.Guanine – Cytosine
  4. D.Guanine – Thymine
⬇️ Answer?

💡 Why + source
Guanine–cytosine pairs share three hydrogen bonds (adenine–thymine two), so they have the highest binding energy. A–C and G–T aren't normal Watson–Crick pairs.

  2. B.False
⬇️ Answer?
B. False
💡 Why + source
False. The glycerol backbone of triglycerides is a gluconeogenic substrate (via dihydroxyacetone phosphate). Only the even-chain fatty acids can't make net glucose.

Basil metabolism · 2024 Exam 3 · Q46in Exam 3 scopeWhat mechanism is activated during early fasting to maintain glucose homeostasis when exogenous glucose uptake starts to drop?
  1. A.Glycogenolysis
  2. B.Glycolysis
  3. C.Gluconeogenesis
  4. D.Ketogenesis
  5. E.Lipolysis
⬇️ Answer?
A. Glycogenolysis
💡 Why + source
Early fasting (meal absorbed, no gut glucose): falling insulin and moderate glucagon trigger liver glycogenolysis as the main blood glucose source.

Basil metabolism · 2024 Exam 3 · Q47in Exam 3 scopeWhat process is strongly upregulated during starvation?
  1. A.Glycogenolysis
  2. B.Glycolysis
  3. C.Gluconeogenesis
  4. D.Ketogenesis
  5. E.Lipolysis
⬇️ Answer?
D. Ketogenesislikely
💡 Why + source
In starvation gluconeogenic substrates run out, gluconeogenesis falls off and ketone bodies rise dramatically — ketosis is the hallmark of starvation.

Basil metabolism · 2024 Exam 3 · Q48in Exam 3 scopeWhat metabolic change marks the transition from early to late fasting?
  1. A.Depletion of exogenous glucose
  2. B.Depletion of glycogen
  3. C.Depletion of gluconeogenic substrates
  4. D.Depletion of triglycerides
⬇️ Answer?
B. Depletion of glycogen
💡 Why + source
Early to late fasting = running out of glycogen. Postprandial to early = gut glucose gone; late fasting to starvation = gluconeogenic substrates gone.

Basil metabolism · 2024 Exam 3 · Q49in Exam 3 scopeWhich hormone tends to increase energy availability in response to stressful stimuli?
  1. A.Insulin
  2. B.Epinephrine
  3. C.Glucagon
  4. D.Aldosterone
⬇️ Answer?
B. Epinephrine
💡 Why + source
Epinephrine (sympathetic, fight-or-flight) mobilizes glucose and fatty acids in response to stress. Glucagon responds to low blood glucose, not stress.

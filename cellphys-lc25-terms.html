⚠️ Precision traps

  • Cholesterol derivative vs non-cholesterol ligand: estradiol, testosterone, aldosterone, cortisol, progesterone = cholesterol (S3). T4, T3, reverse T3, retinoic acid (and vitamin D3 pictured) = NOT (S4). Same receptor family, different origin.
  • Mifepristone blocks TWO receptors: progesterone receptor AND glucocorticoid receptor (S5). Do not pair it with the mineralocorticoid receptor (that is spironolactone).
  • Cytosolic vs nuclear: glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid receptors = cytosolic; thyroid, vitamin D3, retinoid receptors = nuclear, sitting inactive on DNA (S13).
  • Cortisol to cortisone = 11-beta hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase (dehydrogen-ase); testosterone to DHT = 5-alpha reductase (S14). Do not swap enzyme and substrate.
  • Renin vs ACE drugs: aliskiren blocks renin (angiotensinogen to angiotensin I); captopril blocks ACE (angiotensin I to II); losartan blocks AT1R; spironolactone blocks the MR (S18).
  • T4 vs T3: T4 = prohormone, mostly what the thyroid releases, and the slide's feedback signal on TSH; T3 = active hormone made by de-iodinases in kidney and liver (S20, S22).
  • Iodine isotopes, unit trap: 131I = beta, ~1 mm, half-life 8 days (therapy); 123I = gamma, half-life 13 hours (imaging); 127I = stable (S24).
  • Aldosterone retains Na+ and water, EXCRETES K+ (S16, S19). Direction flip is the classic trap.
  • Carriers reduce renal excretion and create a reservoir (S11); termination needs liver conjugation THEN renal excretion (S15). Bound ligand is NOT the active form; free ligand enters cells.
  • Adenoma vs adenocarcinoma: feline hyperthyroidism is usually a benign adenoma; only 2% are adenocarcinoma (S24).

Reverse T3S4Reverse triiodothyronine; listed with the non-cholesterol steroid-receptor ligands.

Vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol)S4, S5Pictured on the non-cholesterol-derivative slide; agonist of the vitamin D3 receptor (nuclear).
AgonistS5Ligand that binds and activates the receptor.
AntagonistS5Ligand that binds the receptor and blocks activation.
Androgen receptorS5Agonists testosterone, dihydrotestosterone (DHT), nandrolone; antagonists flutamide, enzalutamide.

SpironolactoneS5, S18Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (blocks aldosterone).

Vitamin D binding protein (VDP)S6Carrier for vitamin D.

TerminationS15Requires hormone excretion: conjugation in the liver, then renal excretion.
AldosteroneS16-S19Mineralocorticoid from the adrenal cortex; released when blood pressure drops; sodium and water retention, potassium excretion.
Renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS)S16, S18Blood pressure drop leads to renin, angiotensin II, then aldosterone.
ReninS18Kidney enzyme (slide: macula densa) converting angiotensinogen to angiotensin I; blocked by aliskiren.

Angiotensin IIS18Stimulates aldosterone release, vasoconstriction, and vasopressin (antidiuretic hormone, ADH) secretion.
Angiotensin II type 1 receptor (AT1R)S18Receptor for angiotensin II on the adrenal cortex; blocked by losartan; valsartan listed as "biased".
AmilorideS18Drug listed next to Na+ (sodium) retention on the RAAS slide; mechanism not stated on the slide (see flag).
Aldosterone target genesS19Apical sodium channel, apical potassium channel, basolateral sodium/potassium ATPase (Na+/K+ ATPase).
Thyrotropin releasing hormone (TRH)S22Hypothalamic hormone released by cold and stress; reaches anterior pituitary via portal circulation.
Thyroid stimulating hormone (TSH, thyrotropin)S22Released by pituitary thyrotrophs; stimulates the thyroid to release mostly T4.
De-iodinasesS22Enzymes converting T4 to T3; mainly in kidney and liver.
Thyroid hormone effectsS20, S22General increase in metabolism: oxygen consumption, ventilation, thermogenesis, fat and protein mobilization.
Feline hyperthyroidismS23Most common endocrine disorder of older cats: weight loss with good appetite, tachycardia, hyperexcitability.
Polybrominated diphenyl ethers (PBDEs)S23Flame-retardant chemicals suspected as a cause of feline hyperthyroidism.
Thyroid adenomaS24Most common cause of feline hyperthyroidism; only 2% are adenocarcinoma.
Methimazole (Tapazole)S24Drug that inhibits T3/T4 production; listed first of three feline treatments (slide spells it Tapazol).
Iodine-131 (131I)S24Radio-iodine therapy: beta particles destroy tissue within ~1 mm; half-life 8 days.
Iodine-127 (127I)S24Stable (non-radioactive) iodine isotope.
Iodine-123 (123I) scintigraphyS24Gamma emitter, half-life 13 hours; imaging uptake in thyroid and salivary glands.
Negative feedbackS22, S25Pathway activity is limited by feedback loops (e.g., T4 inhibits TSH release).

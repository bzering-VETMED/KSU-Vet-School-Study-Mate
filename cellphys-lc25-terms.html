🧬 LC25 · Steroid Hormone Receptors (Fri 10/23)

Steroid hormone receptorS1Intracellular (cytosolic or nuclear) receptor family; ligand binding turns it into a transcription factor.
CholesterolS2, transcriptC27 starting molecule (S2 figure: polar OH head, rigid planar steroid ring, nonpolar tail); not every steroid-receptor ligand comes from it.
Cholesterol derivatives (steroid hormones)S3Estradiol, testosterone, aldosterone, cortisol, progesterone.
ProgesteroneS3, transcriptC21; first major stopping point, made "on the way to anywhere"; precursor for testosterone and the corticoids (transcript).
Carbon countsS3, transcriptCholesterol C27; progesterone and corticoids C21; testosterone C19; estradiol C18. Carbons are cleaved along the pathway (transcript).
CorticosteronetranscriptNOT on slides: primary glucocorticoid in birds and rodents; intermediate toward aldosterone; differs from cortisol by one hydroxyl (transcript).
CortisolS3, S5, transcriptAdrenal-cortex glucocorticoid; binds BOTH glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid receptors ("key for multiple locks"); about 100x aldosterone concentration (transcript).
Dihydrotestosterone (DHT)S5, S14, transcript"Supercharged" androgen: binds the androgen receptor more readily, at lower concentration, with a bigger effect than testosterone (transcript).
AromatasetranscriptEnzyme converting testosterone to estradiol; testosterone must be present to make estradiol (transcript; not on slides).
LetrozoletranscriptAromatase inhibitor: blocks estradiol formation in endocrine-sensitive cancer (transcript).
Non-cholesterol agonistsS4Thyroxine (T4), triiodothyronine (T3), reverse T3, retinoic acid (vitamin D3 also pictured) act at this receptor family without being cholesterol-derived.
Thyroxine (T4)S4, S20, S22, transcriptProhormone with four iodines; predominant form released by the thyroid; the slide's feedback signal to the pituitary.
Triiodothyronine (T3)S4, S20, S22Biologically active thyroid hormone; formed by removing one iodine from T4.
Reverse T3S4, transcriptReverse triiodothyronine; inactive form (transcript); listed with the non-cholesterol ligands.
Retinoic acidS4, S5Non-cholesterol agonist of the retinoic acid receptor (nuclear receptor).
Vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol)S4, S5, transcriptAnimal form (D3). Transcript: a cholesterol derivative but not a steroid. Agonist of the nuclear vitamin D3 receptor.
Ergocalciferol (vitamin D2)S4, transcriptPlant form; the S4 structure actually depicts ergocalciferol. D2 vs D3 potency is species dependent (maybe ~1000-fold dosing) - specify which (transcript).
1,25-dihydroxycholecalciferoltranscriptActive vitamin D3: 25-hydroxyl added in the liver, 1-hydroxyl added in the kidney (transcript).
AgonistS5Ligand that binds and activates the receptor.
AntagonistS5Ligand that binds the receptor and blocks activation.
Physiological agonists (bolded on S5)S5, transcriptWhat Schultz wants known: testosterone, DHT, estradiol, progesterone, cortisol, aldosterone, vitamin D3, retinoic acid, T3. The drugs are context.
Androgen receptorS5Agonists testosterone, dihydrotestosterone (DHT), nandrolone; antagonists flutamide, enzalutamide.
FinasterideS14, transcript5-alpha reductase (ENZYME) inhibitor: prostatic hyperplasia, prostate cancer, post-castration; hair growth. Not a receptor blocker (transcript).
Flutamide / enzalutamideS5, transcriptAndrogen RECEPTOR antagonists; enzalutamide used more in veterinary medicine (transcript). Trap: finasteride hits the enzyme instead.
NandroloneS5, transcriptAnabolic androgen receptor agonist; abuse example: greyhounds dosed with anabolic steroids (transcript).
Estrogen receptorS5Agonist estradiol (clomiphene in parentheses); antagonists fulvestrant, tamoxifen.
Progesterone receptorS5Agonist progesterone; antagonist mifepristone (RU-486).
Glucocorticoid receptor (GR)S5, S13Agonists cortisol, dexamethasone, prednisolone; antagonist mifepristone. Cytosolic receptor.
Mineralocorticoid receptor (MR)S5, S13, S16Agonist aldosterone; antagonist spironolactone. Cytosolic receptor; drives sodium and water retention.
Vitamin D3 receptorS5, S13Agonists vitamin D, maxacalcitol; antagonist ZK159222 (non-clinical). Nuclear receptor.
Retinoic acid receptorS5, S13, transcriptAgonists retinoic acid, tazarotene; antagonist YCT-529. Nuclear. "Promiscuous": dimerizes with other receptor classes (know that, not the partners).
Thyroid hormone receptorS5, S13, S20Agonist T3 (triiodothyronine); no antagonist listed. Nuclear receptor.
Mifepristone (RU-486)S5, transcriptAntagonist at BOTH progesterone and glucocorticoid receptors; developed by Roussel, became an abortifacient; shows how hard receptor selectivity is (transcript).
SpironolactoneS5, S18, transcriptMineralocorticoid receptor antagonist; also blocks androgen receptors (transcript: used for acne in women, not men).
Carrier proteinsS6, S11, transcriptBind hydrophobic ligands: protect, add solubility, create a reservoir, greatly extend half-life, smooth swings, reduce renal excretion.
Vitamin D binding protein (VDP)S6Carrier for vitamin D.
Retinol binding protein (RBP4)S6Carrier protein for retinol.
Thyroxine binding globulin (TBG)S6, S20Carrier for thyroid hormones (with transthyretin).
Transthyretin (TTR)S6, S20Second carrier for thyroid hormones (with thyroxine binding globulin).
Sex hormone binding globulin (SHBG)S6Carrier for steroid androgens and estrogens.
TranscortinS6, S16, transcriptCarries ~75% of cortisol (transcript); lower affinity for progesterone and aldosterone (S16: under 25%; transcript: under 20% of aldosterone).
AlbuminS16, transcriptLow-affinity, high-capacity carrier of hydrophobic molecules: ~50% of aldosterone, ~80% of progesterone (transcript). Listed first for aldosterone (S16).
Three receptor domainsS7, S25Ligand (hormone)-binding domain, DNA binding domain, transcription-activating domain; listed as "critical" in the summary.
DNA binding domainS7, S8, S11, transcriptBinds the specific DNA sequence; Schultz: gives SPECIFICITY to the DNA interaction. Old-exam answer for "which portion is exposed."
Transcription-activating domainS7, S8Binds co-activator proteins (S8 figure) to start transcription of target genes.
Inhibitory proteinS8, S10Bound to the ligand-binding domain of the inactive receptor (S8); released when ligand binds (step 4).
DimerizationS10, S11, transcriptLigand-receptor complex dimerizes, THEN enters the nucleus via the nuclear pore (S10; confirmed in transcript); the dimer binds DNA.
Common signaling mechanismS8-S11Ligand binds, inhibitory protein released, dimerize, nuclear pore, DNA, co-activators, transcription, RNA out (S9 steps 1-8). Emphasized in class.
Hormone response element (HRE)S8, S19DNA site bound by the activated receptor (S19 figure); S8 calls it the receptor-binding element.
Primary response genesS12Genes activated directly by steroid hormone receptor complexes (S12 figure).
Primary response proteinsS12A few early proteins that shut off primary response genes AND turn on secondary response genes.
Secondary response genes / proteinsS12Later wave: genes switched on by primary response proteins, producing many secondary response proteins.
jun, fos, mycS12, transcriptExamples of primary response proteins (jun, fos on S12; myc added verbally); drive further rounds of RNA synthesis.
Free vs bound ligandS9-S11, transcriptMass-action equilibrium: most bound, some free; only FREE ligand crosses membranes into cells.
Vascular reservoirS11, S15Carrier-bound ligand stays in circulation for up to hours, buffering free concentration.
Hydrophobic / lipid-soluble ligandS11Needs carriers in blood; free form crosses lipid membranes by diffusion.
Co-activatorsS13, S25, transcriptSlide lists GRIP1, SRC1-3, TRAP, SMRT. Transcript: co-activators AND co-repressors recruited; give cell-type specificity. Names NOT expected.
GRIP1S13Glucocorticoid receptor co-activator 1 (slide expansion).
SRC1, 2, 3S13Sex hormone receptor complex 1, 2, 3 (slide expansion).
TRAPS13Thyroid hormone receptor associated proteins.
SMRTS13Signaling mediator for retinoic acid and thyroid hormone receptor (slide expansion).
Specificity of signalingS14, transcriptHormone is a broadcast signal; a cell responds only with the receptor, needed enzymes (11-beta HSD2, 5-alpha reductase), and co-activators/co-repressors.
11-beta hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase (11b-HSD)S14, transcriptCortisol to cortisone. HSD2: one direction, inactivates cortisol so nephron cells respond to aldosterone. HSD1: both directions (transcript).
LicoricetranscriptBlocks 11-beta HSD, so cortisol occupies the mineralocorticoid receptor: looks like aldosterone excess (transcript).
5-alpha reductaseS14, transcriptConverts testosterone to dihydrotestosterone (DHT); blocked by finasteride (transcript).
Time courseS15Slow: minutes to hours or days; needs transcription and translation.
TerminationS15Requires hormone excretion: conjugation in the liver, then renal excretion.
AldosteroneS16-S19Mineralocorticoid from the adrenal cortex; released when blood pressure drops; sodium and water retention, potassium excretion.
Renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS)S16-S18, transcriptRenin, then angiotensin, then aldosterone ("goes in that order"). Schultz: "a pathway you better know." Every step has a drug.
AngiotensinogenS17, S18, transcriptPrecursor secreted by the LIVER; renin cleaves it to angiotensin I.
Juxtaglomerular apparatusS17, transcriptSenses decreased renal perfusion and releases renin; increased perfusion INHIBITS renin release (negative feedback, S17).
ReninS17, S18, transcriptEnzyme released by the kidney juxtaglomerular apparatus (S17; S18 says macula densa) when renal perfusion falls; angiotensinogen to angiotensin I.
Angiotensin converting enzyme (ACE)S17, S18, transcriptAngiotensin I to angiotensin II; on pulmonary and renal endothelium (S17), mostly in the lungs (transcript); blocked by captopril.
Angiotensin IIS17, S18Five effects (S17): sympathetic activity, tubular Na+Cl- reabsorption/K+ excretion, aldosterone secretion, arteriolar vasoconstriction, posterior pituitary ADH secretion.
Angiotensin II type 1 receptor (AT1R)S18, transcriptAngiotensin II receptor (adrenal cortex, vessels, pituitary); cell-surface G protein-coupled receptor via phospholipase C/DAG, plus arrestin (transcript).
AliskirenS18, transcriptRenin inhibitor (blocks angiotensinogen to angiotensin I); spelled A-L-I-S-K-I-R-E-N in class.
Captopril / enalaprilS18, transcriptACE inhibitors (block angiotensin I to II); captopril on S18; enalapril likely named verbally ("Anapapril").
Losartan / valsartan / telmisartanS18, transcriptAT1R blockers; losartan and valsartan ("biased") on S18; telmisartan likely named verbally ("Telmotran").
AmilorideS18, S19Listed beside Na+ retention (S18); Bethany's notes: blocks the Na+ channel. OUTSIDE detail: the epithelial sodium channel (ENaC), shown on S19.
Aldosterone target genesS19Apical Na+ channel (ENaC), apical K+ channel (ROMK), basolateral Na+/K+ ATPase (S19 text; channel names on the figure).
Serum- and glucocorticoid-regulated kinase 1 (SGK1)S19, transcriptEarly aldosterone/MR product (~20 minutes, transcript) that raises apical ENaC and ROMK. Expansion is OUTSIDE.
Epithelial sodium channel (ENaC)S19, transcriptApical Na+ channel; more in membrane = Na+ reabsorption; detectable ~2 hours (transcript). Expansion OUTSIDE.
Renal outer medullary potassium channel (ROMK)S19, transcriptApical K+ channel for K+ secretion; detectable ~2 hours (transcript). Expansion OUTSIDE.
Nedd4-2S19Removes ENaC from the membrane; SGK1 phosphorylates it, 14-3-3 then holds it, so it cannot remove ENaC.
14-3-3S19Binds phosphorylated Nedd4-2 and holds it away from ENaC.
WNK4S19Removes ENaC and ROMK from the membrane; SGK1 inhibits it.
NHERF2S19Partners with SGK1 to send ROMK to the membrane.
Dot1 / AF9S19Repress ENaC transcription; SGK1 relieves the repression.
Blood volume "managed"S18, transcriptTrap: RAAS conserves volume (prevents loss, S18) rather than necessarily raising it; thirst adds water (transcript).
Thyrotropin releasing hormone (TRH)S22Hypothalamic hormone released by cold and stress; reaches anterior pituitary via portal circulation.
Thyroid stimulating hormone (TSH, thyrotropin)S22Released by pituitary thyrotrophs; stimulates the thyroid to release mostly T4.
De-iodinasesS22Enzymes converting T4 to T3; mainly in kidney and liver.
Thyroid hormone effectsS20, S22General increase in metabolism: oxygen consumption, ventilation, thermogenesis, fat and protein mobilization.
Feline hyperthyroidismS23Most common endocrine disorder of older cats: weight loss with good appetite, tachycardia, hyperexcitability.
Polybrominated diphenyl ethers (PBDEs)S23Flame-retardant chemicals suspected as a cause of feline hyperthyroidism.
Thyroid adenomaS24Most common cause of feline hyperthyroidism; only 2% are adenocarcinoma.
Methimazole (Tapazole)S24Drug that inhibits T3/T4 production; listed first of three feline treatments (slide spells it Tapazol).
Iodine-131 (131I)S24Radio-iodine therapy: beta particles destroy tissue within ~1 mm; half-life 8 days.
Iodine-127 (127I)S24Stable (non-radioactive) iodine isotope.
Iodine-123 (123I) scintigraphyS24Gamma emitter, half-life 13 hours; imaging uptake in thyroid and salivary glands.
Negative feedbackS22, S25Pathway activity is limited by feedback loops (e.g., T4 inhibits TSH release).

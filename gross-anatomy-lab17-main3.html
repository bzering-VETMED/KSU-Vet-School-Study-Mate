Pulmonary ligament
🧩 What is it? Pleural fold: caudal lobe → mediastinum (on the right, usually also the accessory lobe)
⭐ Key fact? Caudal extension of the pleural reflection at the root of the lung
📝 Slide note (professor) Note:
The pulmonary ligament is a connection between the caudal lobe of the lung and the mediastinum at the level of the esophagus.

This may be difficult to access if you did not cut the ribs near the vertebra to allow for the costal wall to be fully reflect away. The ligament also tightly holds this lung lobe down and may be difficult to pull away to visualize as I am in the image.

You may be still able to see part of the ligament attached to the lungs after they have been removed.

❓ Identify the highlighted structure.
Thymus
📍 Where? Cranial mediastinum, ventral to the great vessels
⭐ Key fact? Large in young dogs, regresses with age
📝 Slide note (professor) Note:
The thymus can be found in the cranial mediastinum. Think back to radiology and where you are expected to visualize it at.

There may be fat covering this region making it hard to visualize.

Also remember, the thymus is a structure that typically atrophies away with age, to the point sometimes where only a trace of it may be found in geriatric animals. Find it in your cadaver and then look around at nearby lab stations to see how much the thymus varies between cadavers.

Internal thoracic artery
📍 Arises from? Subclavian a.
🌿 Branches? Ventral intercostal + perforating brr. → ends as musculophrenic + cranial epigastric aa.
📝 Slide note (professor) Note:
You may have already found internal thoracic artery as you were trying to separate out transversus thoracis.

Internal thoracic artery is a branch of the subclavian arteries, it travels ventrocaudally and is deep to the cranial border of transversus thoracis. If you cut too closely to the sternum when reflecting the ribs, you may have inadvertanely transected this artery. Figure 3-14 in the guide showcases this area well.

Follow this artery caudally to the costal arch

Musculophrenic artery
📍 Arises from? Internal thoracic a. (terminal branch)
🧭 Course? Along the costal arch at the diaphragm attachment
📝 Slide note (professor) Note:
Near the level of the diaphragm, the internal thoracic artery will have a branch coming off, this is the musculophrenic artery.

You may need to pick through fat and fascia to find this branch, also reflecting transversus thoracis to help visualize.

Dissection:
After identifying this artery, you will need to cut the mediastinum near the sternum and reflect the sternum cranially. You will need to cut the cranial epigastric arteries in order to do so.

🔄 LC31 · Signaling and Cell Cycle quiz

30 questions · Dr. Basel style · Exam 4

Exam-style questions on LC31 only, written from the slides (not an official key). Tap an answer to check it; select-all needs ✓ Check. Keep your FIRST answer unless you can name the fact that proves it wrong. 📖 = slide number.

Q1A cell has just finished DNA replication and now holds two sets of chromosomes, but mitosis has not begun. Which phase is it in?

✅ A · G2 is the growing stage with 2 sets of chromosomes; G1 has 1 set, S is replication itself, G0 is quiescence.📖 S5; transcript

Q2Which phases together make up interphase?

✅ D · The slide defines interphase as G1, S, + G2; M is mitosis and G0 is outside the cycle.📖 S6; transcript

Q3During which mitotic stage does the nuclear envelope disassemble while the centrosomes sit on opposite sides of the cell?

✅ B · Prometaphase = nuclear envelope disassembles, centrosomes on opposite sides (S6 image). Telophase is when the envelope reforms.📖 S6; transcript

Q4A contractile ring splits the cell and microtubules form the interphase array. Which stage is this?

✅ C · The slide pairs the contractile ring and the interphase microtubule array with cytokinesis.📖 S6; transcript

Q5What does the M checkpoint check for?

✅ A · M checkpoint = spindle attachment + chromosome distribution. DNA replication is G2/M; growth signals are G1/S.📖 S8; transcript

Q6Which of the following is NOT checked at the G1/S checkpoint?

✅ B · G1/S checks growth signals, cell size, and DNA damage. Spindle attachment is the M checkpoint.📖 S8; transcript

Q7Which of the following is NOT required to activate a cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK)?

✅ D · CDK needs cyclin binding, an added phosphate, and a removed phosphate (phosphatase). Cyclin degradation happens after the step, not to activate it.📖 S9; transcript

Q8Epithelial growth factor (EGF) binds its receptor, which is phosphorylated and recruits SOS. What happens next?

✅ C · SOS switches Ras-GDP to Ras-GTP, which leads to mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) → Jun/Fos/Myc → transcription of G1 cyclin, G1-CDK, and E2F.📖 S11; transcript

Q9Wnt binds Frizzled and LRP, and Disheveled (DVL) is phosphorylated. Which event leads to cyclin D transcription?

✅ C · Dishevelled binds Axin/GSK3 more strongly than APC, so the complex loses kinase activity; beta-catenin is not phosphorylated, survives, enters the nucleus, and transcribes cyclin D.📖 S13; transcript

Q10Which cyclin forms mitosis promoting factor (MPF) with a cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK)?

✅ A · The slide labels MPF as the CDK–cyclin B complex.📖 S14; transcript

Q11What prevents the cell cycle from reversing once a step has been triggered?

✅ B · Activated cyclin–cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK) complexes trigger the next step and degradation of the current cyclin, so cyclins act as a one-way clock.📖 S14; transcript

Q12Growth signals activate G1-CDK–cyclin, which phosphorylates the retinoblastoma protein (pRb). What happens next?

✅ D · Phosphorylated pRb lets go of E2F; E2F travels to the nucleus and transcribes DNA replication genes.📖 S15; transcript

Q13A cell at the G1/S checkpoint has DNA damage. Which sequence stops the cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK)?

✅ C · S15 figure: DNA damage → ATM phosphorylates p53 → p53 transcribes p21 → p21 inhibits G1-CDK–cyclin.📖 S15; transcript

Q14Which protein binds and inactivates E2F at the G1/S checkpoint?

✅ A · pRb binds and inactivates E2F, a transcription factor for DNA replication genes.📖 S15; transcript

Q15A 7-year-old greyhound has a distal radius tumor of malignant mesenchymal cells with osteoid matrix. Its pRb is mutated and cannot bind E2F. What is the effect?

✅ B · Mutated pRb cannot bind E2F, so E2F is always free; the cell enters S phase regardless of other signals. Basel: pRb mutation drives many (not all) canine osteosarcomas.📖 S18; transcript

Q16An objective states that pRb mutations 'turn the G1/S checkpoint permanently on.' What does this mean mechanistically?

✅ D · Basel: if pRb cannot bind E2F, the cell is always moving into S phase. "On" = the go signal (E2F) always on, not arrest.📖 S16, S18; transcript

Q17Which pair are both listed as steroid hormone growth signals?

✅ C · Steroid hormones on the slide: estrogen (female reproductive cell growth) and testosterone (male). Erythropoietin and platelet derived growth factor are growth factors.📖 S10; transcript

Q18Epithelial growth factor (EGF) activates PI3-K, then PDPK1, then Akt (protein kinase B). What does Akt do to the cell?

✅ D · Basel: AKT blocks apoptosis and prevents cell cycle arrest = "removing the brakes" (S12 T-bars).📖 S12; transcript

Q19A hepatocyte has duplicated its DNA during S phase, but growth signals then disappear. Per Basel, what are the cell's options?

✅ A · Basel: once you pass G1/S and duplicate chromosomes, you must divide or die; you can't go back to resting. G1/S is the most important decision point.📖 S8; transcript

Q20A cell population is not dividing, but a specific growth signal makes the cells re-enter the cycle. Where were these cells?

✅ B · G0 cells are quiet but can be reactivated with the right signals; exited (terminally non-dividing) cells normally cannot be made to divide.📖 S5; transcript

Q21A cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK) has its cyclin bound and its inhibitory phosphate removed, but the activating site is not phosphorylated. What is its status?

✅ D · CDK needs cyclin bound + phosphate removed at one site + phosphate added at another; missing any input = inactive. That's why it integrates signals as a trigger switch.📖 S9; transcript

Q22Epithelial growth factor (EGF) binds its receptor tyrosine kinase. Which order of events follows?

✅ B · Receptor dimerizes, cross-phosphorylates, recruits adapters (SOS); SOS swaps GDP for GTP on Ras; Ras → MAPKKK → MAPKK → MAPK.📖 S11; transcript

Q23In the epithelial growth factor pathway, what does son of sevenless (SOS) do to Ras?

✅ A · SOS is a guanine nucleotide exchange factor for the monomeric GTPase Ras: GDP out, GTP in = active. Ras has no beta-gamma (that's heterotrimeric G proteins).📖 S11; transcript

Q24Wnt binds Frizzled and its co-receptor LRP. Which event comes next in the pathway?

✅ C · Wnt + Frizzled + LRP recruit Dishevelled, which binds Axin/GSK3 more strongly than APC does.📖 S13; transcript

Q25Per Dr. Basel, why does Dishevelled binding to Axin/GSK3 stop beta-catenin phosphorylation?

✅ C · Dishevelled outcompetes APC for Axin/GSK3; unbound APC loses kinase function, so beta-catenin is not phosphorylated.📖 S13; transcript

Q26Epithelial growth factor (EGF) activates both arms of its receptor tyrosine kinase. Per Basel, which pairing is correct?

✅ B · AKT blocks apoptosis and cell cycle arrest (brakes off); Ras → MAPK drives G1 cyclin, E2F, G1-CDK (gas on). EGF does both.📖 S11–S12; transcript

Q27Per Dr. Basel, which event is the final decision point for entering S phase?

✅ A · "This is the final decision point: do we phosphorylate retinoblastoma protein or not?" Phosphorylated pRb releases E2F → S-phase genes.📖 S15; transcript

Q28Mitosis promoting factor activity peaks at metaphase, then drops sharply. What causes the sharp drop?

✅ D · One CDK target is its own cyclin; phosphorylated cyclin is degraded → "close the door behind us," preventing reversal.📖 S14; transcript

Q29Per Dr. Basel, which cyclin gets really high right at the G1/S checkpoint?

✅ B · E = G1/S; A = G2/M (Basel); B = M checkpoint (metaphase); D = general/master regulator across the cycle.📖 S14; transcript

Q30A G1 cell with DNA damage activates ATM, which phosphorylates p53. What does p53 do next?

✅ A · Phosphorylated p53 goes to the nucleus, transcribes p21; p21 inhibits the cyclin-dependent kinase so pRb stays unphosphorylated.📖 S15; transcript

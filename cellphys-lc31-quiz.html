🔄 LC31 · Signaling and Cell Cycle quiz

18 questions · Dr. Basel style · Exam 4

Exam-style questions on LC31 only, written from the slides (not an official key). Tap an answer to check it; select-all needs ✓ Check. Keep your FIRST answer unless you can name the fact that proves it wrong. 📖 = slide number.

Q1A cell has just finished DNA replication and now holds two sets of chromosomes, but mitosis has not begun. Which phase is it in?

✅ A · G2 is the growing stage with 2 sets of chromosomes; G1 has 1 set, S is replication itself, G0 is quiescence.📖 S5

Q2Which phases together make up interphase?

✅ D · The slide defines interphase as G1, S, + G2; M is mitosis and G0 is outside the cycle.📖 S6

Q3During which mitotic stage does the nuclear envelope disassemble while the centrosomes sit on opposite sides of the cell?

✅ B · Prometaphase = nuclear envelope disassembles, centrosomes on opposite sides (S6 image). Telophase is when the envelope reforms.📖 S6

Q4A contractile ring splits the cell and microtubules form the interphase array. Which stage is this?

✅ C · The slide pairs the contractile ring and the interphase microtubule array with cytokinesis.📖 S6

Q5What does the M checkpoint check for?

✅ A · M checkpoint = spindle attachment + chromosome distribution. DNA replication is G2/M; growth signals are G1/S.📖 S8

Q6Which of the following is NOT checked at the G1/S checkpoint?

✅ B · G1/S checks growth signals, cell size, and DNA damage. Spindle attachment is the M checkpoint.📖 S8

Q7Which of the following is NOT required to activate a cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK)?

✅ D · CDK needs cyclin binding, an added phosphate, and a removed phosphate (phosphatase). Cyclin degradation happens after the step, not to activate it.📖 S9

Q8Epithelial growth factor (EGF) binds its receptor, which is phosphorylated and recruits SOS. What happens next?

✅ C · SOS switches Ras-GDP to Ras-GTP, which leads to mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) → Jun/Fos/Myc → transcription of G1 cyclin, G1-CDK, and E2F.📖 S11

Q9Wnt binds Frizzled and LRP, and Disheveled (DVL) is phosphorylated. Which event leads to cyclin D transcription?

✅ C · Disheveled shuts down Axin/GSK3/APC, so beta-catenin survives, partners with LEF/TCF, and transcribes cyclin D.📖 S13

Q10Which cyclin forms mitosis promoting factor (MPF) with a cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK)?

✅ A · The slide labels MPF as the CDK–cyclin B complex.📖 S14

Q11What prevents the cell cycle from reversing once a step has been triggered?

✅ B · Activated cyclin–cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK) complexes trigger the next step and degradation of the current cyclin, so cyclins act as a one-way clock.📖 S14

Q12Growth signals activate G1-CDK–cyclin, which phosphorylates the retinoblastoma protein (pRb). What happens next?

✅ D · Phosphorylated pRb lets go of E2F; E2F travels to the nucleus and transcribes DNA replication genes.📖 S15

Q13A cell at the G1/S checkpoint has DNA damage. Which sequence stops the cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK)?

✅ C · S15 figure: DNA damage → ATM phosphorylates p53 → p53 transcribes p21 → p21 inhibits G1-CDK–cyclin.📖 S15

Q14Which protein binds and inactivates E2F at the G1/S checkpoint?

✅ A · pRb binds and inactivates E2F, a transcription factor for DNA replication genes.📖 S15

Q15A 7-year-old greyhound has a distal radius tumor of malignant mesenchymal cells with osteoid matrix. Its pRb is mutated and cannot bind E2F. What is the effect?

✅ B · Mutated Rb cannot bind E2F, so E2F is constitutively active and drives runaway division independent of growth signals.📖 S18

Q16An objective states that pRb mutations 'turn the G1/S checkpoint permanently on.' What does this mean mechanistically?

✅ D · 'On' means the go signal: E2F is constitutively active, the brake is gone, and the checkpoint is bypassed.📖 S16

Q17Which pair are both listed as steroid hormone growth signals?

✅ C · Steroid hormones on the slide: estrogen (female reproductive cell growth) and testosterone (male). Erythropoietin and platelet derived growth factor are growth factors.📖 S10

Q18Epithelial growth factor (EGF) activates PI3-K, then PDPK1, then Akt (protein kinase B). What does Akt do to the cell?

✅ D · S12 shows inhibitory T-bars from Akt to apoptosis and to cell cycle arrest: Akt is a pro-growth survival signal.📖 S12

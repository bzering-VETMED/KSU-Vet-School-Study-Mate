⚠️ Precision traps

  • Longitudinal folds → DIAMETER; transverse folds → LENGTH.
  • Vagina = folds; vestibule = NO folds (smooth).
  • Internal UTERINE orifice (cervix) vs internal URETHRAL orifice vs URETERIC orifice — spelling matters.
  • Abdominal ostium = entrance (infundibulum); uterine ostium = exit (into the horn).
  • Suspensory ligament (ribs → ovary, broken in spay) vs proper ligament of the ovary (ovary → horn, clamp it).
  • Round ligament of UTERUS (gubernaculum remnant) ≠ round ligament of bladder (umbilical arteries) ≠ round ligament of liver (umbilical vein).
  • Mesovarium (ovary), mesosalpinx (tube), mesometrium (uterus) — together = broad ligament.
  • Cervix is part of the UTERUS, lies obliquely. Vaginal fornix is part of the VAGINA.
  • Uterine artery comes from the VAGINAL artery (internal pudendal), not the ovarian artery — the ovarian gives the uterine BRANCH.
  • Female external urethral orifice → vestibule, at the urethral tubercle. Catheter: withdraw, go dorsal.
  • Left ovarian/testicular vein → left renal vein; right → caudal vena cava.

🎗️ Broad ligament (slides 3–7)

Broad ligament?Paired double folds of peritoneum attaching ovary, uterine tube, uterus to the abdominal/pelvic walls
Broad ligament also contains?Vessels and nerves to the genitalia, and fat
3 regions of the broad ligament?Mesovarium, mesosalpinx, mesometrium
★ All three = broad ligament
Mesovarium suspends?Ovary — from the dorsolateral abdominal wall
Mesosalpinx suspends?Uterine tube (salpinx) — extends laterally from the mesovarium
Mesometrium suspends?Uterine horns and body — continuous with the mesovarium
'Meso-' refers to?Mesentery: double fold of peritoneum
Ovarian bursa?Pouch formed by mesovarium + mesosalpinx enclosing the ovary
★ Has an opening you can see in lab
Round ligament of the uterus?Fibrous cord in the free border of the lateral mesometrium, ovary → inguinal canal
★ Remnant of the gubernaculum (male: scrotal ligament)
Round ligament of the uterus runs perpendicular to?Mesometrium
Suspensory ligament of the ovary?Middle/ventral thirds of the last 2 ribs (12th/13th) → ventral ovary
★ Broken in a spay (ovariohysterectomy)
How is the suspensory ligament broken in a spay?Strumming technique — tear it, don't pull (could rupture vessels)
Proper ligament of the ovary?Caudal continuation of the suspensory ligament: ovary → uterine horn
★ Sturdy — safe to clamp in surgery

🥚 Ovary (slide 8)

Ovary functions?Produces oocytes; endocrine — estrogen and progesterone
Ovary location?Caudal to the kidney — stays there (testes descend)
Two extremities of the ovary?Tubal extremity and uterine extremity
Two borders of the ovary?Free border and mesovarian border
Ovary surface before vs after estrus?Smooth before estrus; rough and nodular in multiparous bitches

🌸 Uterine tube (slide 9)

Other names for the uterine tube?Fallopian tube (human), salpinx, oviduct (birds/reptiles)
Uterine tube function?Transports oocytes to the uterus
Fimbriae?Finger-like projections from the infundibulum that capture the ovulated oocyte
Infundibulum?Thin-walled funnel; site of fertilization
★ Infundibulum = fertilization site
Abdominal ostium?Entrance of the uterine tube (opening in the infundibulum)
★ Two openings: abdominal ostium (in) vs uterine ostium (out)
Uterine ostium?Exit: opening of the uterine tube into the uterine horn
External junction of tube and horn?Tubouterine junction
Why can ectopic pregnancy happen (human)?Ovarian bursa opening gives brief access to the peritoneal cavity

🍐 Uterus & cervix (slides 10–13)

Uterus functions?Transports sperm; implantation and nourishment of the young
Uterus shape?Y-shaped: tubes cranially, vagina caudally
3 parts of the uterus?Horns (2), body, cervix
★ Cervix is PART of the uterus
Uterine horns size?Left and right are the same size
Horn connected to the ovary by?Proper ligament of the ovary
Uterine velum?Internal partition where peritoneum unites the two horns before the body
How do the horns open into the body?Separately
Uterine body?Very short simple muscular tube; abdominal and pelvic cavity
Cervix meaning?Neck
Cervix position?Most caudal part of the uterus; lies obliquely across the uterovaginal junction
★ Oblique — key for catheterizing
Cervical canal?Lumen of the cervix, internal → external uterine orifice
Internal uterine orifice?Cervical opening toward the uterine body
★ Uterine vs urethral vs ureteric orifice — spelling matters
External uterine orifice?Cervical opening toward the vagina

🌷 Vagina & vestibule (slides 14–15)

Vagina?Dilatable canal from uterus (cervix) to vestibule; very long in dogs
Vagina peritoneal relation?Mostly retroperitoneal; only cranial part covered by peritoneum
Longitudinal folds of the vagina allow?Increase in DIAMETER
★ Longitudinal folds → diameter
Transverse folds of the vagina allow?Increase in LENGTH
★ Transverse folds → length
Identifying feature of the vagina?Longitudinal folds with transverse folds
Vaginal fornix?Cranial vagina extending cranial to the cervix (reflection onto the cervix)
★ Same concept as the preputial fornix
Is the vaginal fornix part of the cervix?No — it's in the vaginal lumen
Clinical problem with the fornix?A probe follows the fornix (dead end); cervix is oblique, so redirect
Vestibule connects?Vagina to the vulva
Identifying feature of the vestibule?NO mucosal folds — smooth
★ Vagina = folds; vestibule = smooth
Urethral tubercle?Ridge on the cranioventral vestibule wall; contains the external urethral orifice
Female external urethral orifice location?In the vestibule, ventral to (on) the urethral tubercle
★ Male: tip of the glans
Catheter hits the urethral tubercle — what next?Withdraw and direct DORSALLY to reach the vagina/uterus
★ Withdraw → dorsal
Vestibular bulb?Erectile tissue on each lateral vestibule wall (like the bulb of the penis)
Muscles around the vestibule?Constrictor vestibuli muscles (not found in lab)

🌺 External genitalia (slides 16–17)

Female external genitalia?Vulva, clitoris, urethra
Vulva location?Caudal to the vestibule — most caudal
Vulva consists of?Two lips (labia) joined by dorsal and ventral commissures
Dorsal vs ventral commissure shape?Dorsal = rounded; ventral = pointed
Rima pudendi?Narrow cleft between the vulvar lips
★ Pudendum = Latin for vulva
Clitoris is homologue of?Male penis
Clitoris location?Floor of the vestibule near the vulva
Parts of the clitoris?Pair of crura, body, glans
Glans of the clitoris projects into?Fossa clitoridis
★ The glans is what you see in lab
Os clitoridis?Bone in the clitoris — sometimes present (os penis always in male)

🩸 Blood supply & lymph (slides 18–20)

Ovarian artery is a branch of?Abdominal aorta (direct, left and right)
★ Same as the testicular artery; ligated in spays
Branches of the ovarian artery?Uterine branch (cranial horn) and tubal branch (uterine tube)
Second main source?Internal pudendal artery
Internal pudendal branches (female)?Vaginal artery → uterine artery; artery of the clitoris; artery of the vestibular bulb
Uterine artery comes from?Vaginal artery
★ Anastomoses with the uterine branch of the ovarian a.
Left ovarian vein drains into?Left renal vein
★ Same as male
Right ovarian vein drains into?Caudal vena cava
Ovary & uterus lymph?Lumbar aortic and medial iliac lymph nodes
Vagina & vestibule lymph?Hypogastric (and lateral sacral) lymph nodes
Vulva & clitoris lymph?Superficial inguinal lymph nodes
★ External → superficial inguinal
Lymph nodes you must identify in lab?Medial iliac and superficial inguinal
★ Only these two in lab

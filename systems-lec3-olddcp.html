Q1Why does red blood cell transit time decrease from rest to exercise?

✅ D · Transit time = capillary volume ÷ cardiac output; output rises much more than volume

Q2Breathing more deeply (increased tidal volume) is more efficient for gas exchange because:

✅ D · Fixed ~150 mL dead space is a smaller fraction of a big breath

Q3German shepherd with a large airway obstruction has decreased its alveolar ventilation by 50%. Metabolism unchanged. Arterial PCO₂ (mmHg)?

✅ D · ½ ventilation → 2 × 40 = 80

Q4Ventilating a ram at sea level (760 mmHg), you dial in 12% inspired O₂. PaCO₂ falls to 20 (R = 0.8). Alveolar PO₂?

✅ D · (760 − 47) × 0.12 = 85.6 · 85.6 − (20 ÷ 0.8 = 25) ≈ 61

Q5Fick: Thoroughbred at max, cardiac output 400 L/min, arterial O₂ 25, venous O₂ 2 mL/100 mL. VO₂?

✅ B · 400 × (25 − 2) ÷ 100 = 92 L/min. 🟠 The posted key highlights the last option (61), which looks like a highlighting slip; Poole worked this exact problem in the 11/4 review and got 92.

Q6500 kg bear after a myocardial infarction: cardiac output 10 L/min, VO₂ 1,200 mL/min. Arteriovenous O₂ difference (mL/100 mL)?

✅ B · 1,200 mL/min ÷ 10 L/min = 120 mL/L = 12 mL/100 mL

Q7What shifts the O₂ dissociation curve to the RIGHT and weakens hemoglobin–O₂ affinity?

Q18Pony with hypoxemia, worse when walking, roaring on inhalation: suspected hypoventilation from laryngeal hemiplegia. How to confirm/quantify?

✅ A · OFFICIAL KEY = A. Roaring is a dynamic, exercise-induced obstruction, so you gallop her (hypoxemia worsens) and scope her. 🟠 In the 2023/2024 'confirm hypoventilation most simply' questions (resting cat/dog), the answer is check PaCO₂.

Q19Why do Thoroughbred racehorses become hypoxemic and hypercapnic during galloping?

✅ E · All true: hypoventilation (low ventilation for VO₂) + huge cardiac output → short transit → incomplete equilibration

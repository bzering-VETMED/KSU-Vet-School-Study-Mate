⚠️ Precision traps

  • Median nerve: cutaneous branch but NO autonomous zone.
  • Cranial circumflex humeral = axillary branch; caudal circumflex humeral = subscapular branch.
  • Axillary → brachial after cranial circumflex humeral; brachial → median after common interosseous.
  • Superficial paw = COMMON DIGITAL; deep paw = METACARPAL. Prefix dorsal/palmar by surface.
  • Cephalic drains MEDIAL; accessory cephalic drains DORSAL and LATERAL.
  • External thoracic → superficial pectorals; lateral thoracic → deep pectoral.
  • Cranial pectoral nerve → superficial pectorals; caudal pectoral nerve → deep pectoral.
  • No terminal lymph node for thoracic limb; head = medial retropharyngeal; pelvic limb has one.
  • Axillary lymph node = constant; accessory axillary = inconstant, drains INTO axillary.
  • Superficial cervical node drains limb EXCEPT medial arm, forearm, shoulder.
  • Brachial plexus = VENTRAL branches C6–T2; dorsal branches go epaxial.
  • Deep digital flexor is dual: median AND ulnar nerves.
  • Radial nerve autonomous zone stops at lateral half of digit 5 — that's ulnar.

Accessory cephalic vein drains which aspect?Dorsal and lateral aspect

🫧 Lymph nodes

Afferent lymph vessels…Bring unfiltered lymph into the node
Efferent lymph vessels…Carry filtered lymph to another node, a vein, or a duct
Terminal lymph node?Node through which all or most lymph from a region must drain
Sentinel lymph node?First node draining an area; biopsied/removed in neoplasia or infection
Terminal lymph node of the thoracic limb?None — no single terminal lymph node
★ 'Important to remember'; repeated twice
Terminal lymph node of the head?Medial retropharyngeal lymph node
Does the pelvic limb have a single terminal node?Yes (covered later)
All lymph nodes draining the thoracic limb?Superficial cervical, axillary, accessory axillary lymph nodes
★ Blueprint: list all nodes draining the limb
Two constant lymph nodes of the thoracic limb?Superficial cervical and axillary lymph nodes
★ Stated as 'the main thing' in summary
Superficial vs deep lymph system?Superficial: superficial cervical; deep: axillary and accessory axillary
Clinical name for the superficial cervical lymph node?Prescapular lymph node
Superficial cervical lymph node location?Cranial to shoulder, under brachiocephalicus and omotransversarius
Superficial cervical node drains which limb regions?Most of limb EXCEPT medial arm, forearm, shoulder
★ Blueprint: which limb parts drain to which node
Superficial cervical node also drains…Caudal head skin, part of ear, lateral neck
Superficial cervical efferents go to…Left: thoracic duct; right: tracheal trunk; either: external jugular
Is the axillary lymph node always present?Yes — constant
Axillary lymph node location?Caudal to shoulder, ventral to thoracodorsal vessels, at distal teres major
Axillary lymph node drains…Mammae 1–3, shoulder/arm skin, bones except digits, joints, limb muscles
★ Blueprint: which limb parts drain to which node
Joints drained by the axillary lymph node?Humeral (shoulder), cubital, carpal joints
Bones NOT drained by the axillary lymph node?Digits
Axillary lymph node efferents go to…External jugular vein (variants: thoracic duct, tracheal trunks)
Is the accessory axillary lymph node always present?No — inconstant
Accessory axillary lymph node location?2nd–3rd intercostal space, ventral to latissimus, dorsal to deep pectoral
Accessory axillary lymph node drains…Mammae 1–3, lateral/ventral thoracic wall skin, shoulder, arm, elbow
Accessory axillary efferents go to…Axillary lymph node
Mammary glands draining to axillary nodes?First three (1–3)

⚡ Brachial plexus & nerves

Brachial plexus is formed by…Ventral branches of spinal nerves C6–T2
★ Blueprint objective; 'just remember C6 through T2 as a whole'
Do dorsal branches contribute to the brachial plexus?No — ventral branches only
Spinal nerve dorsal branches supply…Epaxial muscles and dorsal one-third of skin
Spinal nerve ventral branches supply…Hypaxial muscles, limbs, ventral two-thirds of skin
Brachial plexus nerves, cranial to caudal?Suprascapular, subscapular, cranial pectoral, musculocutaneous, axillary, thoracodorsal, radial, median, ulnar, lateral thoracic, caudal pectoral

★ Professor: must know function of each nerve for lecture exam

📍 Autonomous zones & testing sites

Cutaneous area?Total skin area innervated by one cutaneous nerve
Overlap zone?Periphery of cutaneous area; two or more nerves
Autonomous zone?Center of cutaneous area; only one nerve
★ Definition underlies blueprint objective
Clinical use of an autonomous zone?Pinch response proves that nerve (and its segments) works
Nerves with an autonomous zone?Musculocutaneous, axillary, radial, ulnar
★ Blueprint: nerves that have an autonomous zone
Nerve with a cutaneous branch but NO autonomous zone?Median nerve
★ Repeated three times; blueprint objective
Axillary nerve autonomous zone?Lateral shoulder/brachium
Musculocutaneous nerve autonomous zone?Medial forearm
Radial nerve autonomous zone?Dorsal forearm and paw, except lateral half of digit 5
Ulnar nerve autonomous zone (paw)?Lateral half of digit 5
Musculocutaneous nerve testing site?2 cm distal to medial epicondyle of humerus
Axillary nerve testing site?2 cm caudal to shoulder (humeral) joint
Radial nerve testing site?Dorsal proximal ends of metacarpals 2 and 3
Ulnar nerve: how many testing sites?Two — caudal cutaneous antebrachial nerve and palmar branch
Caudal cutaneous antebrachial nerve testing site?4 cm distal to the olecranon
Caudal cutaneous antebrachial nerve is a branch of…Ulnar nerve
Palmar branch of ulnar nerve testing site?Lateral surface of digit 5

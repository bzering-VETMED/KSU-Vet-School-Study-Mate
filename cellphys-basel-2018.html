Basel 2018 Exam 4 · Q117Exam 4 lectureshort answerYou are studying the thromboxane A2 receptor (TxA2R) in platelets. During this study you identify an extremely potent agonist called BOP. Upon further study, the potency of this agonist in concentration response studies seems to be significantly greater than its affinity in binding studies. You also identify an irreversible antagonist, I-PTA-PON3, that covalently bonds to TxA2R and inactivates it. Use this information to answer the following questions:
  • a. What effect do you expect BOP to cause in the platelets (i.e. what effect could you measure)? Please list a final effect (what will happen to the platelet) and a second messenger effect (what intracellular signals you could measure) that you could measure.
  • b. What quantity describes potency? What quantity describes affinity? Write a mathematical formula that relates these two quantities for BOP in this situation.
  • c. Why do you think the potency of BOP is significantly higher than the affinity for TxA2R? Propose a mechanism for the enhanced potency.
  • d. How do you expect the BOP concentration response curve to change if you add a low concentration of I-PTA-PON3 to the solution? (Please refer to EC50 and Emax in your answer)
⬇️ Answer?
(a) TxA2 receptor = Gq → platelet aggregation (final effect); ↑ IP3, DAG, Ca2+ (second messengers). (b) Potency = EC50; affinity = KD; for BOP, EC50 < KD (EC50 << KD). (c) Spare receptors / signal amplification: full response with only a fraction of receptors bound. (d) A low dose of the irreversible antagonist uses up the receptor reserve: EC50 shifts RIGHT with Emax unchanged (looks competitive); at higher doses Emax falls.likely
💡 Why + source
Combines LC28 (TxA2 = Gq, aggregation) with LC30 spare receptors.

Basel 2018 Exam 4 · Q118not tested in 2026short answerAPC is a protein that binds both to β-catenin and the Axin/GSK3 complex and allows Axin/GSK3 to phosphorylate β-catenin. Use this information to answer the following questions:
  • a. What growth factor pathway is APC associated with?
  • b. How does signaling through this pathway affect APC?
  • c. What happens to phosphorylated β-catenin? If β-catenin is not phosphorylated, what does it do?
  • d. Mutations in APC that prevent APC from interacting with Axin/GSK3 would cause what effect in a cell?
  • e. Based on this information, is wild-type APC an oncogene, a proto-oncogene, or a tumor-suppressor gene?
  • f. If a mutated APC that could not interact with Axin/GSK3 was encoded by a retrovirus, would this retrovirus be oncogenic? Why or why not?
⬇️ Answer?
(a) Wnt. (b) Wnt → Frizzled/LRP → Dishevelled pulls APC off the Axin/GSK3 complex → no phosphorylation of β-catenin. (c) Phosphorylated β-catenin → proteasome; unphosphorylated β-catenin enters the nucleus and transcribes cyclin D → cell cycling. (d) β-catenin never phosphorylated → always on → unregulated division/neoplasia. (e) Tumor suppressor (cancer from LOSS of activity). (f) Probably not: a loss-of-function APC carried by a virus doesn't remove the cell's own working APC, so it wouldn't act as an oncogene.likely
💡 Why + source
Part (f) is a viral-oncogene question.

LC14 · Protein structure & diagnostics

Q1A protein placed in a nonpolar solvent loses its compact globular shape. Which force that normally drives its tertiary collapse has been disrupted?

✅ B · Tertiary collapse is driven mainly by hydrophobic side chains hiding from water.📖 LC14 S9, S12

Q2Select all that apply. A kinase transfers a phosphate onto a protein. Which amino acid side chains are possible targets?

✅ A, B, C · The alcohol (–OH) amino acids serine, threonine and tyrosine are the phosphorylatable ones.📖 LC14 transcript

Q3An unvaccinated 3-year-old donkey stabled outside became aggressive and can no longer stand. Which test on brain tissue confirms the most likely diagnosis?

✅ C · Rabies is confirmed with a direct fluorescent antibody test (a type of immunohistochemistry): labeled antibody finds viral protein in tissue.📖 LC14 S18–19
LC15 · Protein regulation & enzymes

Q4Which statement best explains why a reaction with a very large negative ΔG can still proceed immeasurably slowly?

✅ A · ΔG tells direction and energy released; Ea tells speed. Enzymes lower Ea and never change ΔG.📖 LC15 S15–17

Q5An enzyme with a Km of 0.5 mM is working at 20 mM substrate. What happens to the reaction rate if substrate doubles?

✅ D · Far above Km the enzyme is saturated, so rate barely changes. Near Km the rate is very sensitive.📖 LC15 S19

Q6A quarter horse has progressive weakness, muscle wasting, colic-like bouts, and very high AST and CK. Which mechanism best explains the disease?

✅ A · EPSM1: a dominant glycogen synthase mutation makes it active without its feed-forward activator (G6P) → glycogen piles up and damages muscle.📖 LC15 S25–26
LC16 · Glucose transport & glycolysis

Q7After a meal, blood glucose rises from 4 to 7.5 mM. Why does the liver sharply increase glucose phosphorylation while most tissues barely change?

✅ C · Glucokinase (Km ~15 mM) rises 58% over that range; hexokinase (Km < 1 mM) rises only 1.7%.📖 LC16 S9

Q8Which transporter moves glucose UP its concentration gradient from the intestinal lumen into the enterocyte?

✅ B · SGLTs are secondary active (Na⁺-driven). GLUTs only move glucose down its gradient.📖 LC16 S6

Q9A sprinting greyhound's muscle converts pyruvate to lactate. What is the main metabolic purpose of this step?

✅ D · Low O₂ → NAD⁺ runs out; lactate dehydrogenase refills NAD⁺ so glycolysis keeps making its 2 ATP.📖 LC16 S18–19
LC17 · TCA cycle & electron transport

Q10A dog pulled from a house fire has severe tissue hypoxia, so its electron transport chain stops. What happens first in its hepatocyte mitochondria?

✅ A · Without O₂, NADH can't be oxidized → NADH accumulates, NAD⁺ is depleted.📖 LC17 S9–10

Q11Which complex accepts electrons from succinate but does NOT pump protons?

✅ B · Complex II = succinate dehydrogenase; it feeds coenzyme Q but pumps no H⁺, so FADH₂ yields less ATP.📖 LC17 S20–21

Q12Restoring blood flow to an ischemic limb causes oxidative injury. Which mechanism best explains it?

✅ D · Ischemia stores succinate; on reperfusion step 6 runs fast → FADH₂ surge → superoxide.📖 LC17 S12–13, S26
LC18 · Glycogen & gluconeogenesis

Q13Why does a hepatocyte store carbohydrate as glycogen rather than as free glucose?

✅ C · Glucose is an osmolyte → water rushes in → cell ruptures. Glycogen is insoluble, so no osmotic pull.📖 LC18 S8

Q14A dog has fasted for 18 hours and glucagon is high. Which combination occurs in its liver?

✅ C · Glucagon → cAMP → PKA phosphorylation: breaks glycogen down, stops storage, and turns gluconeogenesis on.📖 LC18 S15, S26

Q15Fructose-2,6-bisphosphate falls in the liver. Which pathway is favored, and why?

✅ A · F2,6BP activates PFK-1 and inhibits FBPase-1. Less F2,6BP → PFK-1 slows, FBPase-1 speeds up.📖 LC18 S25
LC19 · Amino acid catabolism

Q16A fasting animal burns only leucine for fuel. Why can this not produce any net glucose?

✅ D · Leucine and lysine make only acetyl-CoA/acetoacetate; acetyl-CoA's 2 carbons are fully oxidized to CO₂.📖 LC19 S11

Q17A 2-year-old Irish wolfhound has ataxia, seizures, high ammonia, high bile acids, and a low BCAA:AAA ratio. Why is the ratio low?

✅ B · Patent ductus venosus: gut blood skips the liver → AAA (phenylalanine, tyrosine) stay high → ratio drops.📖 LC19 S9, S20

Q18After a high-protein meal, the urea cycle speeds up mainly because:

✅ A · More amino acid breakdown → more glutamate → N-acetylglutamate → allosteric activation of carbamoyl phosphate synthetase.📖 LC19 S17
LC20 · Lipid metabolism & ketones

Q19Epinephrine binds an adipocyte during a stress response. What happens to hormone-sensitive lipase (HSL) and acetyl-CoA carboxylase (ACC)?

✅ C · Epinephrine → PKA → phosphorylation → burn fat (HSL on), stop making fat (ACC off).📖 LC20 S11

Q20A 7-year-old beagle has polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, urine glucose of 300 mg/dL, and ketones above 3 mM. Why is she making ketones?

✅ B · Diabetes mellitus: plenty of glucose but it can't get in → cells act starved → fat → acetyl-CoA → ketones.📖 LC20 S18

Q21The liver makes ketone bodies but cannot use them itself. Which enzyme does it lack?

✅ D · Without 3-oxoacid CoA transferase the liver can't reactivate acetoacetate, so it exports ketones to heart and brain.📖 LC20 S16
LC21 · Cell signaling

Q22Vasopressin binds V1 receptors on vascular smooth muscle. Which sequence follows?

✅ D · V1 = vessels (IP3 + DAG → contraction). V2 = kidney (cAMP → aquaporins).📖 LC21 "Vasopressin/ADH Signaling"

Q23Which pairing matches the lecture's example concentrations for endocrine vs synaptic signaling?

✅ C · Endocrine reaches most cells at low concentration; synaptic reaches selected cells but needs high local concentration.📖 LC21 "Endocrine vs. Synaptic Signaling"

Q24A drug binds a hormone's receptor, causes no response, and prevents the hormone from acting. The drug is a(n):

✅ A · Agonist = binds and triggers a response. Antagonist = binds and blocks/changes another ligand's action.📖 LC21 "Terminology Review"

Q25Select all that apply. Which signaling molecules are lipid soluble and typically travel bound to carrier proteins in plasma?

✅ B, C · Lipid-soluble signals ride carriers and cross membranes to intracellular receptors; water-soluble ones use surface receptors.📖 LC21 "Solubility of Signaling Molecules"

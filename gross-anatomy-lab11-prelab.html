1Identify this two-headed muscle.

    Tap to reveal answer
    Sartorius
    The sartorius has cranial and caudal straplike parts. The cranial part inserts on the patella; the caudal part on the cranial border of the tibia.

    2Identify the indicated muscle highlighted in red.

      Tap to reveal answer
      Adductor
      The adductor is a large pyramidal medial-thigh muscle, deep to gracilis and partly covered by biceps femoris laterally.

      3During dissection of the caudal hip, a student sees two small adjacent muscles separated by a tendon. Which statement best describes this?

      1. They are two portions of the external obturator separated by its tendon.
      2. They are the gemelli, grooved by the tendon of the internal obturator.
      3. They are portions of the deep gluteal separated by the obturator nerve.
      4. They are divisions of the quadratus femoris separated by the sciatic nerve.
      Tap to reveal answer
      They are the gemelli, grooved by the tendon of the internal obturator.
      The gemelli are two fused muscles that are deeply grooved by the tendon of the internal obturator.

      4Identify the indicated muscle.

        Tap to reveal answer
        Semitendinosus
        The semitendinosus is a caudal-thigh muscle running from the ischiatic tuberosity toward the tibia and also contributes to the common calcaneal tendon via fascia.

        5The piriformis muscle is associated with which gluteal muscle?

        1. Middle gluteal
        2. Deep gluteal
        3. Superficial gluteal
        Tap to reveal answer
        Middle gluteal
        The piriformis is described as the deep caudal portion of the middle gluteal.

        6Before transecting biceps femoris, a student separates its deep surface from adductor. Which nerve is directly at risk?

        1. Cranial gluteal nerve
        2. Femoral nerve
        3. Obturator nerve
        4. Sciatic nerve
        Tap to reveal answer
        Sciatic nerve
        The sciatic nerve lies in the plane between biceps femoris and adductor.

        7In the femoral triangle, where should markers for the femoral vein and saphenous nerve lie relative to the femoral artery?

        1. Vein and nerve both caudal
        2. Vein caudal; nerve cranial
        3. Vein cranial; nerve caudal
        4. Vein and nerve both cranial
        Tap to reveal answer
        Vein caudal; nerve cranial
        Remember VAN from caudal → cranial: Vein, Artery, Nerve.

        8Both sartorius parts flex the hip. How do their actions at the stifle differ?

        1. Cranial part extends; caudal part flexes
        2. Cranial part flexes; caudal part extends
        3. Both extend
        4. Both flex
        Tap to reveal answer
        Cranial part extends; caudal part flexes
        The cranial sartorius part extends the stifle; the caudal sartorius part flexes it.

        9Which later exposure specifically requires transecting the sacrotuberous ligament?

        1. Reflecting the cranial sartorius toward its patellar insertion
        2. Exposing the internal obturator tendon over the lesser ischiatic notch
        3. Following middle gluteal to the apex of the greater trochanter
        4. Tracing tensor fasciae latae into the lateral femoral fascia
        Tap to reveal answer
        Exposing the internal obturator tendon over the lesser ischiatic notch
        The tendon of the internal obturator crosses the lesser ischiatic notch ventral to the sacrotuberous ligament; the ligament is transected to expose it.

        10A fan-shaped muscle covers the obturator foramen on the ventral pubis/ischium and inserts in the trochanteric fossa. Which nerve supplies it?

        1. Obturator nerve
        2. Sciatic nerve
        3. Caudal gluteal nerve
        4. Cranial gluteal nerve
        Tap to reveal answer
        Obturator nerve
        Those landmarks identify the external obturator, which is innervated by the obturator nerve.

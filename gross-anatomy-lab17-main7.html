🧭 Orient first
Say it out loud: view, recumbency, cranial/caudal, dorsal/ventral. Then check the orientation tags on the image.
🧩 Segments?
Ascending aorta → aortic arch → descending aorta
📝 Slide note (professor)
Note:
The aorta has 3 segments we want you to identify in the thoracic cavity. The ascending aorta, aortic arch and descending aorta.
The ascending aorta extends cranially from the left ventricle medial to the pulmonary trunk, this segment typically is very short and is covered by thymus tissue and fat/fascia and other vessels, you will likely need to reflect these to better visualize the area (not visible in image)
The aortic arch begins when the ascending aorta makes a sharp turn dorsally and to the left. You also can identify this area as this is where the main arteries branch off the thoracic aorta. (partially visible in image)
The descending aorta runs caudally and is located ventral to the vertebrae, your intercostal arteries branch off the descending aorta.

❓ Name it (complete + specific)
Brachiocephalic trunk
📍 Arises from?
Aortic arch (1st branch)
🌿 Branches?
LEFT common carotid, RIGHT common carotid, then continues as the right subclavian a.
📝 Slide note (professor)
Note:
The brachiocephalic trunk is the first branch coming off the aortic arch. It will pass obliquely across the ventral surface of the trachea.

It gives rise to the right and left common carotid arteries and the right subclavian artery. Trace this artery to find this branching pattern.

This area will most likely be covered in fat/fascia, you will need to clean that off in order to visualize these structures, watch out for nearby nerves.

Brachiocephalic trunk (its continuation)
📝 Slide note (professor)
Note:
The right subclavian is the last branch of the brachiocephalic trunk. It is traveling to the right forearm where once it exits the thoracic cavity, it turns into the axillary artery. This artery will have nerves wrapping around it on its way to the limb, be very careful not to inadvertently transect these nerves as you are clearing fat and fascia to follow where the artery is headed.

Left subclavian artery
📍 Arises from?
Aortic arch directly (2nd branch)
📝 Slide note (professor)
Note:
The left subclavian artery differs from nearby arteries as it branches off of the aortic arch further caudally. It passes obliquely to the left across the ventral surface of the esophagus as it heads towards the left forearm.

It then gives off multiple branches to other arteries, the right subclavian gives off the same branches but to the right side.

Again, there will be nerves in this area wrapping around the left subclavian artery, be very careful not to transect these nerves.

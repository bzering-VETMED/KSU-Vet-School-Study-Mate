🫧 LO1 · Paranasal sinuses (slides 20–23)

Paranasal sinuses — definition (slide 20)?Air-filled diverticula between the external and internal lamina of skull bones, lined by respiratory mucosa
Four paranasal sinuses listed (slide 20)…1. Maxillary recess (sinus); 2. frontal sinus; 3. palatine sinus; 4. sphenoidal sinus
Labels on the dorsal-view skull figure, Nickel fig. 293 (slide 21)…Lateral frontal sinus (b), medial frontal sinus (c), rostral frontal sinus (d), maxillary recess (a)
Maxillary recess — 4 features (slide 22)…Only in DOGS AND CATS; diverticulum of the NASAL CAVITY; at the level of the MIDDLE nasal concha; does NOT increase in size with age
Fig 04-06 (slides 22–23): 1 vs 2?1 = maxillary recess; 2 = frontal sinus
Frontal sinus — location (slide 23)?Rostral two-thirds of the frontal bone
Three frontal sinus compartments and their size (slide 23)…1. Lateral — LARGEST; 2. medial — small; 3. rostral
Do the frontal sinus compartments communicate (slide 23)?NO — the three compartments do not communicate with each other
How does each frontal sinus compartment drain (slide 23)?Opens separately into the nasal cavity via the space between the 2nd and 3rd ECTOturbinates
Clinical relevance of non-communicating frontal compartments?Each must be addressed separately (e.g., trephination/drainage of sinusitis) (outside knowledge — confirm)

🗣️ LO2 · Larynx overview (slides 24–25)

What happens to the epiglottis during swallowing (slides 25, 28)?Tilts backward to (partially) cover the opening (rostral opening/inlet) of the larynx
The larynx is caudally continuous with… (slide 25)Lumen of the trachea
Fig 03-27 legend, pharynx schematic (slide 25)…1 nasal cavity; 2 oral cavity; 3 soft palate; 4 nasopharynx; 5 root of tongue; 6 larynx (protruding through pharyngeal floor); 7 laryngopharynx (piriform recess); 8 caudal end of palatopharyngeal arch; 9 esophagus; 10 lamina of cricoid cartilage; 11 trachea
In Fig 03-27, broken arrow vs single arrow (slide 25)?Broken arrow = FOOD passage; single arrow = AIR passage
Where does food cross the airway (slide 25)?In the pharynx — food passes around the larynx through the laryngopharynx (piriform recess) to the esophagus

🌬️ LO2 · Laryngeal cavity (slides 33–34)

Vestibule — position and extent (slide 33)?Rostral to the glottis; extends from the LARYNGEAL INLET to the VESTIBULAR FOLD
Glottic cleft / rima glottidis (slide 33)?The middle, narrow portion of the laryngeal cavity
Infraglottic cavity (slide 33)?Caudal to the glottis (continues into the trachea)

Aryepiglottic fold (slide 34)?Fold from the arytenoid to the epiglottis forming the side of the laryngeal inlet

💪 LO2 · Laryngeal muscles (slides 37–43)

Extrinsic laryngeal muscles — definition + 4 names (slide 37)…Connect the larynx with the hyoid bone, pharynx and sternum: 1. thyrohyoideus; 2. sternothyroideus; 3. thyropharyngeus; 4. cricopharyngeus
Intrinsic laryngeal muscles — definition + 2 functions (slide 37)…Pass from one cartilage to another and move them relative to one another; WIDEN and NARROW the glottic cleft; TENSE and RELAX the vocal fold
Cricothyroideus — origin, insertion, action, innervation (slide 39)?O: cricoid arch (lateral surface); I: thyroid cartilage (caudal cornu, lateral surface); A: TENSES the vocal fold; N: CRANIAL laryngeal nerve
Cricoarytenoideus dorsalis — origin, insertion, action, innervation (slide 40)?O: cricoid cartilage (lateral to the medial crest); I: arytenoid cartilage (muscular process); A: MAIN ABDUCTOR of the arytenoid — opens the glottis, widens the glottic cleft; N: caudal laryngeal nerve
Main abductor of the arytenoid cartilage (slide 40)?Cricoarytenoideus dorsalis
Clinical correlation of the main abductor?Laryngeal paralysis — loss of cricoarytenoideus dorsalis function (caudal/recurrent laryngeal nerve) → arytenoid can't abduct → inspiratory stridor (outside knowledge — confirm)
Cricoarytenoideus lateralis — origin, insertion, action, innervation (slide 41)?O: cricoid cartilage (lateral and rostral surface); I: arytenoid (muscular process); A: NARROWS, CLOSES the glottic cleft; N: caudal laryngeal nerve
Thyroarytenoideus — origin, insertion, action, innervation (slide 42)?O: thyroid cartilage (internal midline); I: arytenoid (muscular process and vocal process); A: RELAXES the vocal fold, constricts the glottis; N: caudal laryngeal nerve
Two divisions of the thyroarytenoideus (slide 42)…Ventricularis (rostral) and vocalis (medial)
Ventricularis muscle — division and action (slide 42)?ROSTRAL division; constricts the glottis, DILATES the laryngeal ventricle
Vocalis muscle — division, location, action (slide 42)?MEDIAL division; lies inside the vocal fold with the vocal ligament; draws the arytenoid cartilage ventrally → RELAXES the vocal fold
Arytenoideus transversus — origin, insertion, action, innervation (slide 43)?O and I: arytenoid cartilage (muscular process) — connects the muscular processes of the TWO arytenoids; A: constricts the glottis, narrowing the glottic cleft; N: caudal laryngeal nerve
Which intrinsic muscles WIDEN the glottic cleft vs NARROW it (slides 40–43)?Widen: cricoarytenoideus dorsalis only. Narrow/constrict: cricoarytenoideus lateralis, thyroarytenoideus (incl. ventricularis), arytenoideus transversus
Which intrinsic muscle TENSES the vocal fold vs RELAXES it (slides 39, 42)?Tenses: cricothyroideus. Relaxes: thyroarytenoideus (vocalis)
Which intrinsic muscle has origin AND insertion on the same cartilage type (slide 43)?Arytenoideus transversus (arytenoid → arytenoid)

Two branches of the cranial laryngeal nerve (slide 45)?External branch and internal branch
Internal branch of the cranial laryngeal nerve goes to… (slide 45)Laryngeal mucosa (and toward the epiglottis) — sensory (sensory role is outside knowledge — confirm)
External branch of the cranial laryngeal nerve supplies… Cricothyroideus (matches slide 39; branch-to-muscle link is outside knowledge — confirm)
Ramus anastomoticus (slide 45)?Connecting branch between the caudal laryngeal nerve and the cranial laryngeal nerve (internal branch)
Muscle branches of the caudal laryngeal nerve on slide 45…To arytenoideus transversus & ventricularis, to cricoarytenoideus dorsalis, to cricoarytenoideus lateralis, to vocalis, to thyroarytenoideus (+ to trachea, to pharyngeal mucosa)
Caudal laryngeal nerve is the continuation of…Recurrent laryngeal nerve of the vagus (outside knowledge — confirm)

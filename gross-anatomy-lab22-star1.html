Major splanchnic nerves
The major splanchnic nerve leaves the sympathetic trunk at the level of the 12th or 13th thoracic sympathetic ganglion. It passes dorsal to the crus of the diaphragm, enters the abdominal cavity, and courses to the adrenal and celiacomesenteric ganglia and plexus.

Renal impression of caudate lobe
📝 Slide note (professor)
The caudate lobe is indistinctly separated from the central mass of the liver. It lies transversely but it is mainly to the right of and dorsal to the main bulk of the organ. It has multiple parts that you need to identify. A caudate process, which caps around the right kidney and consists of a renal impression where the right kidney lies. A papillary process which can be seen through the lesser omentum if the liver is pulled forward. It lies in the lesser curvature of the stomach.

Cardiac part stomach
📝 Slide note (professor)
The stomach is divided into parts that blend imperceptibly with one another. The cardiac part is the smallest part and is situated nearest the esophagus. Epiploic foramen
📝 Slide note (professor)
The epiploic foramen is the opening into the omental bursa. It lies dorsally to the right of the median plane at the level of the cranial duodenal flexure, caudomedial to the caudate lobe of the liver. It is bound dorsally by the caudal vena cava, ventrally by the portal vein, caudally by the hepatic artery in the mesoduodenum and cranially by the liver. This foramen can be difficult to find. The probe in the image is entering into the epiploic foramen. This foramen has more clinical relevance in large animals.

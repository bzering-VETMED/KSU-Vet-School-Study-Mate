Q1Which of the following is a cholesterol derivative that acts at an intracellular steroid hormone receptor? (select one correct response)

✅ B · Aldosterone is on the cholesterol-derivative slide; the other four are the non-cholesterol agonists.📖 S3, S4

Q2Which physiological agonist of a 'steroid hormone receptor' is NOT a steroid or cholesterol derivative? (select one correct response)

✅ A · Retinoic acid is listed with T4, T3 and reverse T3 as a non-cholesterol agonist; the rest are cholesterol derivatives.📖 S3, S4

Q3Which molecules are lipid soluble and act through cytosolic or nuclear receptors? (select all that apply)

✅ B, C, D · Testosterone, retinoic acid and vitamin D3 are steroid-receptor ligands (S3-S5, S13). OUTSIDE: ADH and TSH are water-soluble peptides acting at membrane receptors; the slides do not say so.📖 S3-S5, S13

Q4Which circulating ligand associates with both transthyretin and thyroxine binding globulin? (select one correct response)

✅ D · Transthyretin and thyroxine binding globulin carry thyroid hormones.📖 S6, S20

Q5Cortisol in plasma is carried mainly by which carrier protein? (select one correct response)

✅ C · Transcortin carries cortisol, with lower affinity for progesterone and aldosterone.📖 S6

Q6After hormone binding, which receptor region must be exposed for the complex to recognize its specific DNA sequence? (select one correct response)

✅ E · The DNA binding domain binds the specific DNA site; the transcription-activating domain acts after binding.📖 S7, S10

Q7Cortisol has bound a cytosolic glucocorticoid receptor and the inhibitory protein has been released. What happens next? (select one correct response)

✅ A · Step 4 (inhibitory protein released) is followed by step 5: dimerize and access the nucleus via the nuclear pore. DNA binding is step 6. Transcript confirms: dimerize, then enter via the nuclear pore.📖 S10

Q8Which is NOT required for a cell to respond to a steroid hormone? (select one correct response)

✅ E · Specificity requires the receptor, any ancillary enzymes, and the right gene regulatory proteins; no membrane G protein step.📖 S14

Q9Which receptors reside inactive on DNA in the nucleus before ligand binding? (select all that apply)

✅ A, B, D · The slide lists thyroid, vitamin D3 and retinoid receptors as nuclear; glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid receptors as cytosolic.📖 S13

Q10An epithelial cell has high 11-beta hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase activity, converting cortisol to cortisone. Which naturally occurring molecule increases its expression of an apical Na+ channel, an apical K+ channel and basolateral Na+/K+ ATPase? (select one correct response)

✅ C · Aldosterone drives transcription of these three transport proteins (S19). Cortisol is ruled out because 11-beta HSD2 converts it to cortisone, which cannot bind the MR (transcript). Spironolactone is a drug.📖 S14, S19, transcript

Q11In reference to the previous question (high 11-beta hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase epithelial cell; Na+ channel, K+ channel and Na+/K+ ATPase upregulated), which receptor mediates this effect? (select one correct response)

✅ B · Aldosterone acts at the cytosolic mineralocorticoid receptor; 11-beta HSD2 keeps cortisol off it (transcript).📖 S13, S16, S19

Q12A newly discovered molecule, Kansatrol, has a large positive logP (octanol/water partition coefficient), circulates bound to a carrier protein, and activates a cytosolic receptor. Which real molecule does it most resemble? (select one correct response)

✅ D · OUTSIDE: high logP = lipid soluble. Lipid soluble ligands need carriers (S11); cortisol's glucocorticoid receptor is cytosolic, while T3, retinoic acid and vitamin D3 receptors are nuclear (S13).📖 S6, S11, S13
⚠️ Not 100% sure from the slides. Confirm with the transcript.

Q13New hormone alpha is water soluble; new hormone beta is lipid soluble and carrier-bound. Which statement is correct? (select one correct response)

✅ A · Carrier binding creates a vascular reservoir, reduces renal excretion and slows the time course.📖 S11, S15

Q14Which drug-target pairing matches the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone slide? (select one correct response)

✅ B · Aliskiren = renin; captopril = ACE; losartan = AT1R; spironolactone = mineralocorticoid receptor.📖 S18

Q15Per the slides, which extracellular signal inhibits thyroid stimulating hormone (TSH) release from the anterior pituitary? (select one correct response)

✅ C · S22 states T4 exerts negative feedback on TSH release. Textbooks emphasize T3 (see flag); use the slide.📖 S22

Q16An older cat has weight loss despite a good appetite, tachycardia, hyperexcitability and a poor hair coat; plasma thyroxine (T4) is high. What is the most likely cause? (select one correct response)

✅ D · Feline hyperthyroidism is most often a thyroid adenoma; only 2% are adenocarcinoma.📖 S23, S24

Q17Which statements about iodine isotopes in feline thyroid disease are correct? (select all that apply)

✅ A, D · 131I: beta, ~1 mm, 8 days (therapy). 123I: gamma, 13 hours (imaging). 127I is stable.📖 S24

Q18Mifepristone (RU-486) is listed as an antagonist at which pair of receptors? (select one correct response)

✅ E · The ligand table lists mifepristone under both the progesterone and glucocorticoid receptors.📖 S5

Q19A hypertensive dog receives a drug that blocks conversion of angiotensinogen to angiotensin I. Which drug was given? (select one correct response)

✅ E · Aliskiren = renin; captopril = ACE; losartan = AT1R; spironolactone = MR; amiloride = Na+ channel.📖 S18, transcript

Q20In the S19 aldosterone-sensitive cell, how does SGK1 increase apical ENaC? (select one correct response)

✅ A · SGK1 phosphorylates Nedd4-2 (held by 14-3-3), inhibits WNK4, sends ENaC to the membrane, and relieves Dot1/AF9 repression.📖 S19

Q21Which are effects of angiotensin II on the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone diagram? (select all that apply)

✅ A, B, C · Angiotensin II's five arrows: sympathetic activity, tubular Na+Cl- reabsorption/K+ excretion, aldosterone, vasoconstriction, ADH. Renin is upstream and is inhibited by increased perfusion; angiotensinogen comes from the liver.📖 S17

Q22A dog eats a large amount of black licorice, which blocks 11-beta hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase in nephron cells. Which outcome is expected? (select one correct response)

✅ B · 11-beta HSD2 normally inactivates cortisol (~100x aldosterone) so the MR responds only to aldosterone; blocking it lets cortisol activate the MR.📖 S14, transcript

Q23A dog with prostatic hyperplasia needs the androgen receptor itself blocked, not DHT synthesis. Which drug fits? (select one correct response)

✅ C · Finasteride blocks the 5-alpha reductase enzyme; enzalutamide (and flutamide) block the androgen receptor; letrozole blocks aromatase; mifepristone and tamoxifen act at other receptors.📖 S5, transcript

Q24Letrozole is used for an endocrine-sensitive cancer. Which conversion does it block? (select one correct response)

✅ D · Letrozole blocks aromatase (testosterone to estradiol). 5-alpha reductase makes DHT; 11-beta HSD makes cortisone.📖 transcript

Q25Aldosterone reaches a collecting duct cell at time zero. Per lecture, when do newly made SGK1 and ENaC/ROMK become detectable? (select one correct response)

✅ B · SGK1 is an early (primary) product, ~20 minutes; ENaC and ROMK come later, ~2 hours (transcript).📖 S12, S19, transcript

Q26After moderate fluid loss, the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system drives renal salt retention and ADH-mediated water retention. Per Dr. Schultz, what best describes the effect on blood volume? (select one correct response)

✅ A · S18: conserves blood volume. Schultz: not necessarily increased (where would the water come from?), it is managed; drinking adds water. S17's 'effective circulating volume increases' — see flag.📖 S17, S18, transcript

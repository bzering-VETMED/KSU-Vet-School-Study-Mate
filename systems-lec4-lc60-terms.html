Basel · potassium & acid-base handling, anion gap, RTA · Wed 11/11 · LC59+60 S25–51 · 45 terms · say it, then tap the pink box

K⁺ inside vs outside cells~140–145 inside · ~4.5–5 outside · K⁺ Nernst potential ≈ −90 mV (sets resting membrane potential)
Why a small K⁺ rise is dangerous5 → 6 changes the ratio a lot → depolarizes cells → heart, muscle, endocrine problems
★ Fast K⁺ response (eating a banana)INSULIN ↑ Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase → pushes K⁺ into SKELETAL MUSCLE temporarily (S26)
Kidney's K⁺ roleThe slow, long-term fix: excrete the K⁺ that was parked in muscle
★ Proximal tubule K⁺~80% · passive, lumen-positive charge (S29)
★ Thick ascending limb K⁺~10% · passive, lumen-positive charge (S30)
★ Alpha-intercalated cells and K⁺H⁺/K⁺-ATPase: secrete H⁺, REABSORB K⁺ → up to ~8% more → ~98% reabsorbed on a low-K⁺ diet (S31)
★ Principal cells and K⁺Can SECRETE up to ~180% of filtered K⁺ on a high-K⁺ diet (S32)
★ ROMKRenal outer medullary K⁺ channel · principal-cell K⁺ secretion · ↑ by ALDOSTERONE (paired with ENaC Na⁺ uptake) (S33)
★ BK (maxi-K) channel"Big K⁺" · high-capacity · NOT hormone-controlled · opened by FLOW (S33)
★ How flow opens BK (steps)1 Flow bends the primary cilium · 2 TRPV4 (mechanosensitive channel) opens · 3 cell depolarizes, Ca²⁺ rises · 4 BK opens → K⁺ secreted
★ High plasma K⁺ → more flowHigh K⁺ inhibits NCC in the distal tubule → more Na⁺ downstream → more flow → BK opens
★ Main long-term K⁺ regulatorALDOSTERONE (high K⁺ itself stimulates aldosterone release)
Insulin in the kidneyAlso ↑ Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase in principal cells (minor)
★ Low volume + low K⁺Aldosterone ↑ for volume, but LOW flow → BK off + ROMK equilibrates → little K⁺ lost
★ High volume + high K⁺Aldosterone would worsen hypertension → instead high GFR/FLOW opens BK → K⁺ dumped WITHOUT Na⁺ retention ("safety valve")
★ Acid-base and K⁺ linkACIDOSIS → HYPERkalemia · ALKALOSIS → HYPOkalemia · and in reverse: hypokalemia → alkalosis, hyperkalemia → acidosis (S34, S51)
Why acidosis raises K⁺More H⁺ secretion = more K⁺ reabsorbed (H⁺/K⁺-ATPase) · H⁺ inhibits Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase and ROMK (S34)
Reading K⁺ + pH on bloodworkSame direction (hyperK + acidosis, hypoK + alkalosis) → likely linked · use the rest of the picture to find which came first (S51)
★ Why loop/thiazide diuretics waste K⁺ (2 reasons)1 More Na⁺ to principal cells → more ENaC Na⁺ in, ROMK K⁺ out · 2 more FLOW → BK opens (S35)
K⁺-sparing diureticsENaC blockers (amiloride) + mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (spironolactone) (S35)
Normal blood pH7.4 ± 0.05
Lungs vs kidneys for acid-baseLungs = FAST (blow off CO₂ from metabolism) · kidneys = SLOW, handle DIETARY acid + compensate respiratory disorders (S38)
★ Carnivores/omnivores vs herbivoresCarnivores/omnivores: dietary acid load → more ALPHA cells, acidic urine · herbivores: tend alkaline → more BETA cells, neutral-to-basic urine (S39)
Two places the kidney secretes H⁺Proximal tubule (reclaims ~100% filtered HCO₃⁻) + alpha-intercalated cells (make NEW HCO₃⁻) (S40)
★ Why the filtrate must be bufferedLowest urine pH ≈ 4.5 · unbuffered, daily acid would need urine pH ~1 → buffers let H⁺ secretion continue (S41)
★ Titratable buffersNon-volatile (stay in the cup): PHOSPHATE (main), creatinine, uric acid · limited to the FILTERED load (S41–42)
★ Non-titratable bufferAMMONIA (NH₃) · gas · made BY the kidney, so not limited by filtered load (S42)
NH₃ vs NH₄⁺Ammonia = neutral gas, crosses membranes · ammonium = +1 charged, TRAPPED
★ Where ammonia is madeProximal tubule: GLUTAMINE → NH₄⁺ + alpha-ketoglutarate (S43)
Ammonia concentrated in the medullaSecreted in the thin DESCENDING limb, reabsorbed in the ASCENDING limb → loop current builds medullary NH₃ (S44)
★ Ammonium trappingNH₃ diffuses into the collecting duct, grabs secreted H⁺ → NH₄⁺ can't get back out → buffers the urine (S45)
RhCG (Rhesus C glycoprotein)Channel that speeds NH₃ across membranes · regulated by ALDOSTERONE (another K⁺–H⁺ link) (S45)
★ Two ways kidney failure causes acidosis1 ↓ GFR → less filtered phosphate + low flow → can't secrete H⁺ · 2 tubules can't secrete H⁺ even with normal GFR (RTA) (S46)
★ Metabolic vs respiratory acidosisRespiratory: ↑ CO₂ (>40), HCO₃⁻ normal/↑ · metabolic: ↓ HCO₃⁻, CO₂ normal or ↓ (compensation) · kidney problems → METABOLIC (S46)
★ Anion gapAG = Na⁺ − (Cl⁻ + HCO₃⁻) · normal 8–12 mEq (unmeasured proteins, phosphate) (S47)
★ High anion gap meansAn unmeasured anion other than HCO₃⁻ is involved (an added acid) (S47–48)
★ GOLD MARK (high-gap causes)Glycols (oxalate) · Oxoproline (acetaminophen) · L-lactate · D-lactate · Methanol/alcohols · Aspirin (salicylate) · Renal failure · Ketones (DKA) (S48)
★ Kidney acidosis and the gapRTA = LOW/NORMAL gap (straight HCO₃⁻ loss) · early CKD/AKI = minimal acidosis · LATE CKD/AKI = HIGH gap (phosphate, sulfate, organic acids build up) (S49)
Saline and the gap0.9% NaCl → hyperchloremic, normal-gap acidosis
★ RTA types1, 2, and 4 (type 3 turned out to be a mix of the others) (S50)
★ Type 1 RTA (distal)Alpha-intercalated cells can't secrete H⁺ · urine pH >5.5 · STRONGEST acidosis (S50)
★ Type 2 RTA (proximal)Proximal tubule can't reclaim HCO₃⁻ · carbonic anhydrase deficiency or Fanconi · urine pH <5.5 (after adapting) · milder (S50)
★ Type 4 RTA (hypoaldosteronism)Low aldosterone → less RhCG → less ammonium trapping · milder · caused outside the kidney: diabetes, ACE inhibitors, adrenal insufficiency (S50)
Why CKD patients get low-phosphate dietsCan't filter phosphate → hyperphosphatemia + acidosis

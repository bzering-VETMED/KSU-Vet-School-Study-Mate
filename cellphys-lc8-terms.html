🧪 LC08 · Acids, bases & ion trapping (Wed 10/21)

pHNegative log of the H⁺ concentration: pH = −log₁₀[H⁺].
pH example1 mM (10⁻³ M) HCl → pH 3.
Strong acidDissociates FULLY in water (HCl). H₂SO₄ is strong for its first proton.
Weak acidOnly partly dissociates; e.g., HSO₄⁻ (pKa 1.9).
Acid (Brønsted–Lowry)Any molecule that DONATES a proton (H⁺).
Base (Brønsted–Lowry)Any molecule that ACCEPTS a proton (H⁺).
Conjugate pairAcid form is always the protonated one; one of the pair carries a net charge.
KaAcid dissociation constant = [H⁺][A⁻] / [HA].
pKa−log Ka; the pH where [HA] = [A⁻] (50:50).
pH below pKaProtonated form (HA) dominates.
pH above pKaDeprotonated form (A⁻) dominates.
Henderson–HasselbalchpH − pKa = log([A⁻]/[HA]) → 10^(pH − pKa) = [A⁻]/[HA].
Each pH unitA 10-fold change in the ratio (log scale).
Buffer pKa on a titration curveThe inflection point.
Water (Kw)Weak acid and weak base: [H₃O⁺][OH⁻] = 10⁻⁷ × 10⁻⁷ = 10⁻¹⁴.
Normal pHPlasma/extracellular fluid ~7.4; cytosol ~7.2.
Mass actionChange one component of a reaction → the others shift.
Bicarbonate bufferHCO₃⁻ / H₂CO₃, pKa 6.4.
Phosphate bufferHPO₄²⁻ / H₂PO₄⁻, pKa 7.2.
Deoxyhemoglobin bufferpKa 7–7.7, from its histidines.
Ideal bufferpKa near the target pH + high capacity (many H⁺ sites).
Why body buffers work anywayPlentiful, and their components can be adjusted physiologically.
Bicarbonate response to added H⁺H⁺ + HCO₃⁻ → H₂CO₃ → H₂O + CO₂; breathing faster vents CO₂ and pulls it further right.
Carbonic anhydraseEnzyme catalyzing carbonic acid ⇄ water + CO₂.
logP (octanol:water)Lipid-solubility measure. Uncharged = positive logP, crosses membranes. Charged = negative logP, doesn't.
Ion trappingUncharged form crosses the membrane; on the side where it ionizes, it gets stuck and accumulates.
Weak acid · when ionizedAt pH ABOVE its pKa (A⁻).
Weak base · when ionizedAt pH BELOW its pKa (BH⁺).
Weak acid drugs (examples)Aspirin 3.5, warfarin 5.0, amoxicillin 2.4, phenytoin 8.4.
Weak base drugs (examples)Lidocaine 7.9, codeine 8.2, epinephrine 8.7, atropine 9.7.
Body fluid pH (examples)Stomach 1.5 · urine 5.8 · intracellular 6.8 · CSF 7.3 · blood 7.4 · pancreatic 8.0 · bile 8.5.
Aspirin in the stomachpKa 3.5, pH 2 → mostly protonated (uncharged) → absorbed.
Aspirin in extracellular fluidpH 7 → mostly ionized → trapped there.
Aspirin practice ratios (worked out)Base/acid: stomach 10^(2−3.5) ≈ 0.03; extracellular 10^(7−3.5) ≈ 3,160 (he rounded to ~3,000).
Aspirin in class (1 mM version)He used 1 mM uncharged on both sides → stomach total ≈ 1.03 mM; extracellular ≈ 3,000 mM (~3 M). Slide bonus uses 1 µM → ≈ 3.16 mM.
NormalityConcentration in equivalents per liter — the unit pH is based on ([H⁺] in Eq/l).
10 mM strong acidpH 2 (10⁻² M). Higher acid concentration → lower pH.
Very dilute strong acid (10 nM HCl)NOT pH 8 — water already supplies 10⁻⁷ M H⁺, so pH stays just under 7. Water acts as a buffer.
Pure water[H⁺] = [OH⁻] = 10⁻⁷ M → pH 7; water ≈ 55 M.
The 7 strong acidsChloric, hydrobromic, hydrochloric, hydroiodic, nitric, perchloric, sulfuric — 'I don't expect you to know them.'
H₂SO₄ second protonFirst H⁺ strong, second weak → real pH slightly LOWER than the one-proton estimate.
Weak base protonatedCHARGED (NH₃ + H⁺ → NH₄⁺). Weak acid protonated = NEUTRAL.
Histidine as a bufferpKa ~6–6.5, close to physiological pH → good buffer; ~38 exposed histidines on deoxyhemoglobin.
AlbuminMajor plasma pH buffer (spoken): without it, plasma pH is hard to keep stable.
AcetazolamideBlocks carbonic anhydrase → urine pH goes UP; urine pH can be manipulated to trap drugs.
Acidosis typesMetabolic (H⁺ production/handling) vs respiratory (CO₂ elimination) acidosis.
Acetic acid / acetatepKa 4.76; acetic acid P = 0.67 (logP −0.17) — negative but measurable crossing; acetate (charged) won't cross.
Ammonia / ammoniumpKa 9.3; NH₃ P = 0.2 (logP −0.7) crosses slowly; below 9.3 mostly NH₄⁺ → ~no permeability.
Negative logP but still crosses?Yes if close to zero (slow but measurable); a charge or bigger size drops it to −2/−3. Fentanyl = very high logP → very fast.
Same pH on both sidesAt equilibrium the TOTAL drug concentration is equal on both sides — no trapping.
Different pH on the two sidesUncharged form equalizes; drug accumulates on the side where it ionizes (trapped).
Rabbit / herbivore urineAlkaline (~9; precipitate "snow globe" bladder); carnivores more acidic.
Duodenum pH~7 — pancreatic bicarbonate neutralizes gastric acid.
Male reproductive tractEpithelium can shift pH 6.5 → 7.5 in ~5 min (bicarbonate secretion).
Enzyme pH optimaPepsin works in the stomach (~2); pancreatic amylase and trypsin in the duodenum (~7).

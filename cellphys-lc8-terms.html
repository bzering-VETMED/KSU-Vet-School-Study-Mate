⚠️ Precision traps

  • Weak ACID ionized ABOVE pKa · weak BASE ionized BELOW pKa
  • Uncharged crosses membranes; drug piles up where it is MORE ionized
  • Henderson–Hasselbalch: 10^(pH − pKa) = [A⁻]/[HA] (deprotonated over protonated)
  • pH = pKa → 50:50, not fully dissociated
  • "Strong" = fully dissociated, not concentrated
  • Bicarbonate pKa 6.4 · phosphate 7.2 · plasma 7.4 · cytosol 7.2
  • Each pH unit = 10×; watch exponent/decimal slips

🧪 LC08 · Acids, bases & ion trapping (Wed 10/21)

pHNegative log of the H⁺ concentration: pH = −log₁₀[H⁺].
pH example1 mM (10⁻³ M) HCl → pH 3.
Strong acidDissociates FULLY in water (HCl). H₂SO₄ is strong for its first proton.
Weak acidOnly partly dissociates; e.g., HSO₄⁻ (pKa 1.9).
Acid (Brønsted–Lowry)Any molecule that DONATES a proton (H⁺).
Base (Brønsted–Lowry)Any molecule that ACCEPTS a proton (H⁺).
Conjugate pairAcid form is always the protonated one; one of the pair carries a net charge.
KaAcid dissociation constant = [H⁺][A⁻] / [HA].
pKa−log Ka; the pH where [HA] = [A⁻] (50:50).
pH below pKaProtonated form (HA) dominates.
pH above pKaDeprotonated form (A⁻) dominates.
Henderson–HasselbalchpH − pKa = log([A⁻]/[HA]) → 10^(pH − pKa) = [A⁻]/[HA].
Each pH unitA 10-fold change in the ratio (log scale).
Buffer pKa on a titration curveThe inflection point.
Water (Kw)Weak acid and weak base: [H₃O⁺][OH⁻] = 10⁻⁷ × 10⁻⁷ = 10⁻¹⁴.
Normal pHPlasma/extracellular fluid ~7.4; cytosol ~7.2.
Mass actionChange one component of a reaction → the others shift.
Bicarbonate bufferHCO₃⁻ / H₂CO₃, pKa 6.4.
Phosphate bufferHPO₄²⁻ / H₂PO₄⁻, pKa 7.2.
Deoxyhemoglobin bufferpKa 7–7.7, from its histidines.
Ideal bufferpKa near the target pH + high capacity (many H⁺ sites).
Why body buffers work anywayPlentiful, and their components can be adjusted physiologically.
Bicarbonate response to added H⁺H⁺ + HCO₃⁻ → H₂CO₃ → H₂O + CO₂; breathing faster vents CO₂ and pulls it further right.
Carbonic anhydraseEnzyme catalyzing carbonic acid ⇄ water + CO₂.
logP (octanol:water)Lipid-solubility measure. Uncharged = positive logP, crosses membranes. Charged = negative logP, doesn't.
Ion trappingUncharged form crosses the membrane; on the side where it ionizes, it gets stuck and accumulates.
Weak acid · when ionizedAt pH ABOVE its pKa (A⁻).
Weak base · when ionizedAt pH BELOW its pKa (BH⁺).
Weak acid drugs (examples)Aspirin 3.5, warfarin 5.0, amoxicillin 2.4, phenytoin 8.4.
Weak base drugs (examples)Lidocaine 7.9, codeine 8.2, epinephrine 8.7, atropine 9.7.
Body fluid pH (examples)Stomach 1.5 · urine 5.8 · intracellular 6.8 · CSF 7.3 · blood 7.4 · pancreatic 8.0 · bile 8.5.
Aspirin in the stomachpKa 3.5, pH 2 → mostly protonated (uncharged) → absorbed.
Aspirin in extracellular fluidpH 7 → mostly ionized → trapped there.
Aspirin practice ratios (worked out)Base/acid: stomach 10^(2−3.5) ≈ 0.03; extracellular 10^(7−3.5) ≈ 3,160.

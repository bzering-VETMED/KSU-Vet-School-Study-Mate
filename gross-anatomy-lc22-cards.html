⚠️ Precision traps

  • Ureteric (ureter) vs uterine (uterus) vs urethral (urethra) orifice: spelling matters.
  • Hilus = the indented border (door); sinus = the space behind it (garage).
  • Renal sinus is a 'recess', but pelvic recesses are diverticula of the renal pelvis.
  • Renal pelvis is a dilated ureter, not bone.
  • Renal papilla = apex of pyramid; renal crest = fused papillae.
  • Median section shows the crest; paramedian shows pyramids and papillae.
  • Right kidney is cranial and fixed; left is caudal ('left behind') and pendulous.
  • Right kidney medial border faces caudal vena cava; left faces aorta.
  • Lateral ligament → pelvic wall (round ligament = free edge); median ligament → pelvic symphysis and linea alba.
  • Ureters enter the bladder dorsally; the urethra leaves from the neck.

💧 Overview & function

Four parts of the urinary system?Paired kidneys, paired ureters, urinary bladder, urethra
Path of urine?Kidney → ureter → bladder (enters dorsal aspect) → urethra
Cavities the urinary system lies in?Abdominal and pelvic cavities
Why is it called the urogenital system?Shares the urethra: runs through the penis in males, joins the vagina in females
Main function of the kidneys?Form urine from the blood
Large dog: daily renal blood flow and urine output?1000 to 2000 liters of blood → 1 to 2 liters of urine
Renin: what does it do?Regulates blood pressure (renin-angiotensin system); spelled with a single n
Erythropoietin: what does it do?Increases red blood cell production in bone marrow

📍 Location & position

Where are the kidneys found?Pressed against the abdominal roof, lumbar region, right and left of median plane
Kidneys: intraperitoneal or retroperitoneal?Retroperitoneal (behind the peritoneum)
Which kidney surface has peritoneum, and which layer?Ventral surface only, parietal peritoneum (no visceral peritoneum)
Which kidney is fixed, which is pendulous?Right is fixed (less variable); left is pendulous (more variable)
Which kidney is more cranial, and by how much?Right, by about half a kidney length
Right kidney vertebral level?Thirteenth thoracic or first lumbar vertebra to third lumbar vertebra
Left kidney vertebral level?Second lumbar to fourth lumbar vertebra
Phrase for remembering kidney position?“Left behind”: the left kidney is more caudal
★ Professor's own mnemonic for cranial-versus-caudal kidney questions
How to palpate the right kidney on physical exam?Find the last (floating) rib, then go dorsally
Dog and cat kidney type?Unilobar (smooth, papillae fused into a crest)
Kidney type with lobes visible on the surface?Multilobar (cow)
Embryologically, all kidneys start as…Multilobar; fused lobes become unilobar

🫘 Borders, surfaces & relations

Lateral border: shape and relations?Convex; abdominal wall, and spleen on the left
Medial border: shape?Concave (indented, forms the hilus)
Right kidney medial border faces the…Caudal vena cava
Left kidney medial border faces the…Abdominal aorta
Dorsal surface: contacts and peritoneum?Contacts lumbar hypaxial muscles; free of peritoneum
Ventral surface: faces and covering?Faces the abdominal cavity; covered by parietal peritoneum
Gland at the cranial pole of both kidneys?Adrenal (suprarenal) gland
Right kidney cranial pole contacts the…Caudate process of the liver (renal impression) and right adrenal gland
Left kidney cranial pole contacts the…Left lobe of the pancreas and left adrenal gland
Right kidney caudal pole contacts the…Ascending colon
Left kidney caudal pole contacts the…Descending colon, and mesovarium in females
Mesovarium?Part of the broad ligament suspending the ovary

🔍 Internal structure

Renal hilus?Indented medial border of the kidney
Structures entering or leaving at the hilus?Renal artery, renal vein, lymphatics, nerves, ureter
Renal sinus?Recess (space) the hilus leads into
Contents of the renal sinus?Renal pelvis, fat, proximal branches of renal vessels and nerves
Garage analogy?Driveway = vessels · garage door = hilus · garage = sinus
Renal pelvis?Funnel-shaped dilatation of the ureter inside the renal sinus (the “sea”)
Renal pelvis receives urine from… and passes it to…From papillary ducts; into the ureter
Pelvic recesses?Curved diverticula of the renal pelvis (the “rivers” feeding the sea)
Where do pelvic recesses project?Into the parenchyma between renal pyramids
How many pelvic recesses?5 to 6 from each border of the pelvis
Fibrous capsule covers…Entire kidney surface (not peritoneum)
Fibrous capsule composition?Mostly collagen, few elastic fibers
Consequence of capsule composition?Kidney cannot swell well when internal pressure rises
Why does the capsule strip easily from a healthy kidney?Attached by loose connective tissue
Capsule that won't peel off at necropsy means…Adhesions or pathology (fibrosis)
Renal cortex?Outer parenchyma, just inside the fibrous capsule
Why does the cortex look granular?Renal corpuscles (glomeruli), found only in the cortex
Renal medulla?Inner, darker parenchyma
Why does the medulla look striated?Medullary rays
Median section?Cut through the midline dividing the kidney into equal halves
Renal crest?Free (shelf-like) edge of the medulla facing the pelvis
Renal crest is formed by…Fusion of the renal papillae
Where do papillary ducts open?On the border of the renal crest facing the pelvis
Openings of the papillary ducts?Papillary foramina (not visible grossly)
Renal papilla?Apex of the renal pyramid
Renal pyramid?Pyramid-shaped medullary substance; represents a kidney lobe
Seen ONLY in a paramedian section?Renal pyramids and renal papillae
★ Professor put a star on these two: paramedian sections only
Paramedian section?Cut parallel to and slightly off the midline

❤️ Blood supply

Full arterial sequence?Aorta → renal → interlobar → arcuate → interlobular → afferent arteriole → efferent arteriole
Two capillary plexuses?Cortical capillary plexus and medullary capillary plexus
Vessels supplying the medullary capillary plexus?Straight arteries; drained by straight veins
Venous drainage sequence?Interlobular → arcuate → interlobar → renal vein → caudal vena cava
Blood supply chain the professor cares about?Abdominal aorta → renal artery → arcuate arteries … renal vein → caudal vena cava
★ “These you must know” / “that's all I care about” (outlined vessels)
Arcuate arteries are located at the…Corticomedullary junction
★ The Kahoot question
Why “arcuate”?They form an arc

🚰 Ureters & bladder

Ureter?Narrow muscular tube carrying urine from kidney to bladder
How does the ureter move urine?Smooth muscle peristalsis (little help from gravity in quadrupeds)
Ureter length depends on…Size of the animal
Two parts of the ureter?Abdominal part and pelvic part
Abdominal part of the ureter: start and course?Begins at renal pelvis, runs caudally on a sagittal course
Pelvic part: direction on entering the pelvic cavity?Bends medially
Ureter enters what in the male? In the female?Genital fold (male); broad ligament, specifically mesometrium (female)
Where do ureters open into the bladder?Dorsal surface of the neck of the bladder
Why do ureters penetrate the bladder wall obliquely?Prevents urine reflux into the ureter when intravesical pressure rises
Why doesn't oblique entry block bladder filling?Ureteric peristalsis overcomes the resistance
Intravesical pressure?Pressure within the bladder (vesica = bladder)
Bladder function?Distensible urine storage organ
Smooth muscle of the bladder wall?Detrusor muscle (not identified in lab)
Bladder epithelium?Transitional epithelium (allows distension)
Three parts of the bladder (cranial → caudal)?Apex, body, neck
Bladder apex?Cranial blind end; has scar tissue
Scar on the bladder apex is a remnant of the…Urachus
Urachus in the fetus?Connects the primitive bladder to the allantoic sac
Bladder neck?Narrow caudal part leading into the urethra
Why is the bladder easy to reach from the ventral abdominal wall?Not covered by greater omentum; directly contacts abdominal wall
Openings of the ureters into the bladder?Ureteric orifices
Three orifices not to confuse?Ureteric (ureter), uterine (uterus), urethral (urethra)
★ “This is where the spelling matters”
Trigone of the bladder is formed by…Two ureteric orifices plus the urethral crest
Urethral crest?Median ridge; male ductus deferens and prostate empty secretions there
Bladder structures in order (slide 22)?Apex scar, lumen, ureteric orifices, trigone, urethral crest, urethra
Number of bladder ligaments?Three: two lateral and one median
Lateral ligament connects…Lateral surface of the bladder to the pelvic wall
Round ligament of the bladder?Free edge of the lateral ligament
Round ligament contains the remnant of the…Umbilical artery (may stay patent and supply the bladder)
Median ligament connects…Ventral surface of the bladder to the pelvic symphysis and linea alba
Median ligament in the fetus contains…Urachus and umbilical arteries
Umbilical cord vessels and their ligament homes?Umbilical arteries (bladder ligaments) + umbilical vein (falciform ligament free edge)

🚪 Urethra

Female urethra carries…Urine only
Male urethra carries…Urine, semen, and seminal (accessory gland) secretions
Male urethra extent?Internal opening at bladder neck → external opening at end of penis
Two parts of the male urethra?Pelvic part (internal) and penile part (external)
Female external urethral orifice opens into the…Vagina
Male external urethral orifice?Opening of the penile urethra at the end of the penis

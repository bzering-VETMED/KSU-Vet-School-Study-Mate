🧭 🔀 Hormone switches

7 pathways · tap one arrow at a time · ★ must know · 💭 understanding only

Glucagon → glucose out of the liverStart: Low blood sugar (glucagon) or stress/exercise (epinephrine)
⬇️ What is released, and by whom?
★ Pancreas alpha cells release glucagon
⬇️ Where does glucagon bind?
💭 Its G-protein-coupled receptor on the liver cell (sits in the membrane)
⬇️ What does the receptor switch on?
★ The Gs protein (relay inside the membrane)
⬇️ What does Gs turn on?
💭 Adenylyl cyclase: converts ATP → cyclic AMP
⬇️ Which second messenger rises?
★ Cyclic AMP ↑ (carries the signal inside the cell)
⬇️ What does cyclic AMP activate?
★ Protein kinase A (a kinase = ADDS phosphates)
⬇️ Middle-man kinase?
💭 Protein kinase A phosphorylates phosphorylase kinase → ON
⬇️ Which breakdown enzyme turns ON?
★ Glycogen phosphorylase ON (phosphorylated) · glycogen synthase OFF
⬇️ What does phosphorylase cut off?
★ One glucose at a time → glucose-1-phosphate
⬇️ How does it become glucose-6-phosphate?
💭 Phosphoglucomutase moves the phosphate (carbon 1 → 6)
⬇️ What frees the glucose? Where?
★ Glucose-6-phosphatase removes the phosphate → free glucose · LIVER ONLY (muscle lacks it)
⬇️ How does it leave the liver?
★ Through GLUT2 (glucose transporter 2) → blood sugar ↑

Insulin → glucose stored as glycogenStart: High blood sugar
⬇️ What is released, and by whom?
★ Pancreas beta cells release insulin
⬇️ Where does insulin bind?
💭 The insulin receptor on liver, muscle and fat cells
⬇️ How does glucose get into muscle and fat?
★ GLUT4 (insulin-dependent glucose transporter) moves to the membrane → glucose enters
⬇️ Which enzyme does insulin turn on?
★ Protein phosphatase 1 → REMOVES phosphates
⬇️ Breakdown enzyme?
★ Glycogen phosphorylase OFF (dephosphorylated) → breakdown stops
⬇️ Building enzyme?
★ Glycogen synthase ON (dephosphorylated)
⬇️ How is glucose activated?
💭 Glucose → glucose-6-phosphate → glucose-1-phosphate → UDP-glucose (activated glucose)
⬇️ What adds it to the chain?
★ Glycogen synthase adds glucose → glycogen stored
⬇️ What makes the branches?
★ Glycogen branching enzyme adds the branch points

Glucagon → gluconeogenesis switchStart: Low blood sugar → glucagon (or epinephrine) released
⬇️ Signal inside the liver cell?
★ Gs protein → cyclic AMP ↑ → protein kinase A ON (adds phosphates)
⬇️ What does protein kinase A phosphorylate?
★ The two-job enzyme: phosphofructokinase-2 / fructose-2,6-bisphosphatase (one enzyme, two halves)
⬇️ Which half turns off, which turns on?
★ Phosphofructokinase-2 half OFF (stops MAKING the signal) · fructose-2,6-bisphosphatase half ON (DESTROYS the signal)
⬇️ What happens to the signal?
★ Fructose-2,6-bisphosphate ↓ (the "fed flag" drops)
⬇️ Effect on glycolysis?
★ Phosphofructokinase-1 loses its activator → glycolysis ↓ (stop burning glucose)
⬇️ Effect on gluconeogenesis?
★ Fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase loses its inhibitor → gluconeogenesis ↑ (start making glucose)
⬇️ Result?
★ Liver makes new glucose → blood sugar ↑
⬇️ Rule + trap?
★ Fructose-2,6-bisphosphate ACTIVATES phosphofructokinase-1 and INHIBITS fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase · phosphofructokinase-1 ≠ phosphofructokinase-2 · bisphosphaTASE = enzyme, bisphosphaTE = signal molecule

Fat burning (lipolysis → β-oxidation)Start: Low blood sugar (glucagon) or stress/exercise (epinephrine)
⬇️ Signal inside the cell?
★ Gs protein → cyclic AMP ↑ → protein kinase A ON
⬇️ Breakdown enzyme?
★ Protein kinase A phosphorylates hormone-sensitive lipase → ON (the main enzyme to know)
⬇️ Building enzyme?
★ …and phosphorylates acetyl-CoA carboxylase → OFF (stop building fat)
⬇️ What does hormone-sensitive lipase cut?
★ Triglyceride → 3 fatty acids + glycerol (in fat cells)
⬇️ Where do they go?
★ Fatty acids + glycerol go into the blood
⬇️ What happens to glycerol?
💭 Glycerol → liver → gluconeogenesis (new glucose)
⬇️ Activated where?
💭 In the tissue cell cytosol: fatty acid + coenzyme A → fatty acyl-CoA (costs 2 ATP)
⬇️ How does it enter the mitochondria?
★ Fatty acyl-CoA can't cross → the carnitine shuttle carries it in
⬇️ What does β-oxidation do?
★ In the mitochondria: chops off 2 carbons per round → acetyl-CoA + FADH₂ + NADH
⬇️ The 4 steps?
💭 4 steps per round → 1 FADH₂ + 1 NADH + 1 acetyl-CoA ("Don't Hurry, Dad's Tired" = dehydrogenase, hydratase, dehydrogenase, thiolase)
⬇️ Where do the products go?
★ Acetyl-CoA → TCA cycle · FADH₂ + NADH → electron transport chain → ATP

Fat storage (lipogenesis)Start: High blood sugar → beta cells release insulin
⬇️ Which enzyme does insulin turn on?
★ Protein phosphatase 2A → REMOVES a phosphate
⬇️ Building vs breakdown enzyme?
★ Acetyl-CoA carboxylase ON · hormone-sensitive lipase OFF (stop burning fat)
⬇️ Second ON switch?
★ Citrate (high when fed) binds acetyl-CoA carboxylase → ON (allosteric, no phosphate; feed-forward)
⬇️ Citrate's other job?
💭 Citrate shuttle: acetyl-CoA rides out of the mitochondria inside citrate → cytoplasm (where fat is built)
⬇️ What does acetyl-CoA carboxylase do?
★ Adds a carbon (CO₂, uses 1 ATP): acetyl-CoA (2C) → malonyl-CoA (3C) = committed + rate-limiting step
⬇️ What starts the chain?
★ Starter = 1 plain acetyl-CoA (2 carbons)
⬇️ How does the chain grow?
★ Malonyl-CoA (3C, "the brick") is added → 1 CO₂ leaves → chain +2 carbons (2 → 4 → 6 …)
⬇️ Which carrier reduces it?
★ NADPH (not NADH) reduces each new 2-carbon unit: oxygen removed → plain CH₂ fat chain
⬇️ When does it stop? Where?
★ Repeat → stops at palmitate (16 carbons) · liver + adipose
⬇️ Where does the backbone come from?
★ Dihydroxyacetone phosphate (from glycolysis) → reduced by NADH → glycerol-3-phosphate
⬇️ How is triglyceride made?
★ Glycerol-3-phosphate + 3 fatty acids (e.g., palmitate, as fatty acyl-CoA) added at positions 1 → 2 → 3 → triglyceride
⬇️ Stop after 2 fatty acids?
💭 Diacylglycerol phosphate → membrane lipids
⬇️ Where does it go?
★ Adipose: stores the triglyceride · liver: ships it out in VLDL (very low density lipoprotein)

KetogenesisStart: Fasting / starvation / diabetes → low insulin, high glucagon
⬇️ Fat burning gives?
★ β-oxidation → tons of acetyl-CoA in the liver
⬇️ What does gluconeogenesis use up?
★ Oxaloacetate (to make glucose)
⬇️ Why does acetyl-CoA need it?
★ Acetyl-CoA + oxaloacetate → citrate (TCA step 1)
⬇️ No oxaloacetate →
★ Acetyl-CoA can't enter the TCA cycle → piles up
⬇️ What runs out?
★ Free coenzyme A (acetyl-CoA = acetate stuck to coenzyme A)
⬇️ The fix?
★ Liver (and kidney) combine extra acetyl-CoAs → ketone bodies → frees coenzyme A
⬇️ Steps?
💭 2 acetyl-CoA → acetoacetyl-CoA → HMG-CoA → acetoacetate
⬇️ The 3 ketone bodies?
★ Acetoacetate · β-hydroxybutyrate (acetoacetate reduced by NADH) · acetone (acetoacetate breaks down spontaneously → CO₂ + acetone)
⬇️ Who uses ketones?
★ Heart + brain (ketone → acetyl-CoA → TCA cycle → ATP)
⬇️ Why can't the liver?
★ It lacks 3-oxoacid CoA transferase
⬇️ Cutoffs?
★ Normal ~0.1 mM → ketosis > 0.5 mM (normal, fasting) → ketoacidosis > 1.5 mM (pathological, pH < 7.35)

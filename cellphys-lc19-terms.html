🥩 LC19 · Amino acid catabolism & nitrogen waste

Glucogenic amino acidCan be converted to glucose (most of them; e.g., alanine → pyruvate).
Ketogenic-only amino acidsLeucine and lysine.
Both glucogenic and ketogenicPhenylalanine, tyrosine, threonine, isoleucine, tryptophan.
Why acetyl-CoA can't make glucoseIts 2 carbons leave as CO₂ in the TCA cycle; no net carbon for oxaloacetate.
TransaminationAmine moves from an amino acid onto α-ketoglutarate → glutamate + an α-keto acid.
DeaminationGlutamate releases NH₄⁺ (regenerating α-ketoglutarate).
α-keto acidAmino acid with the amine swapped for oxygen = a carbohydrate skeleton.
Branched-chain amino acidsLeucine, isoleucine, valine; up to ⅓ of muscle protein; broken down in muscle, not liver.
Aromatic amino acidsPhenylalanine, tyrosine; preferentially broken down by the liver.
BCAA:AAA ratioLiver-function marker: normal ~3:1; liver disease can drop to ~1.5:1.
Ammonia toxicityBase (↑pH); NH₄⁺ is K⁺-sized → K⁺ dysregulation in brain; ↑glutamine.
Urea cycleLiver: 2 NH₄⁺ + HCO₃⁻ + 3 ATP → urea (safer, not basic). Slightly energy-positive when TCA runs.
Carbamoyl phosphate synthetaseRegulatory step of the urea cycle.
N-acetylglutamateAllosteric activator of carbamoyl phosphate synthetase; made when glutamate is high.
CreatineAlternate nitrogen waste (from glycine + urea-cycle nitrogen); stores high-energy phosphate in muscle.
CreatinineSpontaneous breakdown product of creatine/phosphocreatine; always entering blood.
Hepatic encephalopathyBrain dysfunction from liver failure: ↑ammonia + low BCAA:AAA.
Wolfhound case↑NH₄⁺, ↑serum bile acids, ↓BCAA:AAA → patent ductus venosus (portosystemic shunt).
Hepatic encephalopathy dietHigh-quality, low-protein diet enriched in branched-chain amino acids.
GlutamateProduct of transamination; carries amino groups; high glutamate → N-acetylglutamate.
Ornithine / citrulline / arginineUrea cycle intermediates (names only; steps not required).
Serum bile acidsRise when the liver is bypassed or failing (shunt marker).
Patent ductus venosusPortosystemic shunt: portal blood bypasses the liver (Irish wolfhounds).

1.Olfactory (first cranial) nerve
👉 highlighted on the photo
CN I. Not dissected: dozens of tiny fibers pass from the nasal mucosa through the cribriform plate to the olfactory bulb. Smell only.
📚 course slide · Lab 33 slides #3
2.Optic (second cranial) nerve
👉 highlighted on the photo
CN II. The thick nerve behind the eyeball, inside the retractor bulbi cone. It leaves through the optic canal and joins the optic chiasm. Vision.
📚 course slide · Lab 33 slides #5
3.Oculomotor (third cranial) nerve
👉 highlighted on the photo
CN III. Goes through the orbital fissure. It supplies the dorsal, medial and ventral recti, the ventral oblique and the levator palpebrae superioris. Its parasympathetic fibers synapse in the ciliary ganglion.
📚 course slide · Lab 33 slides #7
4.Ciliary ganglion
👉 highlighted on the photo
A small swelling at the end of the oculomotor nerve. It holds parasympathetic cell bodies whose fibers constrict the pupil.
📚 course slide · Lab 33 slides #9
5.Trochlear (fourth cranial) nerve
👉 highlighted on the photo
CN IV. Goes through the orbital fissure and supplies only the dorsal oblique. It is the only cranial nerve that leaves the dorsal brainstem.
📚 course slide · Lab 33 slides #11
6.Trigeminal (fifth cranial) nerve
👉 "Trigeminal nerve V" (bottom of the drawing)
CN V, from the pons. It splits into 3: ophthalmic (V1) through the orbital fissure, maxillary (V2) through the round foramen, and mandibular (V3) through the oval foramen.
📖 lab guide · Lab 33 notes, Fig. 5-56 (orbit nerves, dorsal view)
7.Ophthalmic nerve
👉 "Ophthalmic nerve of V"
V1, sensory only. It goes through the orbital fissure and splits into the frontal, infratrochlear, ethmoidal and long ciliary nerves.
📖 lab guide · Lab 33 notes, Fig. 5-56 (orbit nerves, dorsal view)
8.Frontal nerve
👉 "Frontal nerve"
A branch of the ophthalmic nerve. It runs rostrally between the dorsal oblique and the dorsal rectus to the upper eyelid and forehead skin.
📖 lab guide · Lab 33 notes, Fig. 5-56 (orbit nerves, dorsal view)
9.Infratrochlear nerve
👉 "Infratrochlear nerve"
A branch of the ophthalmic nerve. It passes under the trochlea to the medial corner of the eye.
📖 lab guide · Lab 33 notes, Fig. 5-56 (orbit nerves, dorsal view)
10.Ciliary nerves
👉 highlighted on the photo
Long ciliary nerves (from V1) run along the optic nerve to the eyeball. They carry sensation from the cornea and sympathetic fibers to the dilator pupillae.
📚 course slide · Lab 33 slides #17
11.Ethmoidal nerve
👉 "Ethmoidal nerve"
A branch of the ophthalmic nerve. It goes through the ethmoidal foramen and cribriform plate to the nasal mucosa.
📖 lab guide · Lab 33 notes, Fig. 5-56 (orbit nerves, dorsal view)
12.Maxillary nerve
👉 highlighted on the photo
V2, sensory. It runs through the round foramen into the alar canal and comes out in the pterygopalatine fossa, where it gives off zygomatic, pterygopalatine and infraorbital branches.
📚 course slide · Lab 33 slides #20
13.Zygomatic nerve
👉 highlighted on the photo
The first branch of the maxillary nerve. It enters the periorbita and carries sensation from the lateral eyelids and skin.
📚 course slide · Lab 33 slides #22
14.Pterygopalatine ganglion
👉 at the green highlight (the ganglion sits right on this nerve)
A parasympathetic ganglion dorsal to the maxillary nerve, on the medial pterygoid muscle. Its fibers go to the lacrimal, nasal and palatine glands.
📚 course slide · Lab 33 slides #24
15.Pterygopalatine nerve
👉 highlighted on the photo
Comes off the ventral side of the maxillary nerve and draws fibers from the pterygopalatine ganglion. It splits into the minor palatine, major palatine and caudal nasal nerves.
📚 course slide · Lab 33 slides #24
16.Minor palatine nerve
👉 "Minor palatine nerve" (bottom of the drawing)
A branch of the pterygopalatine nerve going to the soft palate.
📖 lab guide · Lab 33 notes, Fig. 5-55 (maxillary nerve branches)
17.Major palatine nerve
👉 "Major palatine nerve"
A branch of the pterygopalatine nerve. It runs through the palatine canal to the hard palate via the major palatine foramen.
📖 lab guide · Lab 33 notes, Fig. 5-55 (maxillary nerve branches)
18.Caudal nasal nerve
👉 "Caudal nasal nerve"
A branch of the pterygopalatine nerve. It goes through the sphenopalatine foramen to the nasal mucosa.
📖 lab guide · Lab 33 notes, Fig. 5-55 (maxillary nerve branches)
19.Infraorbital nerve
👉 highlighted on the photo
What the maxillary nerve becomes after the pterygopalatine nerve leaves it. It runs through the maxillary foramen, infraorbital canal and infraorbital foramen to the upper lip and nose.
📚 course slide · Lab 33 slides #29
20.Superior alveolar branches
👉 "Superior alveolar nerve" (bottom of the drawing)
Branches off the infraorbital nerve inside the infraorbital canal. They supply the upper teeth.
📖 lab guide · Lab 33 notes, Fig. 5-55 (maxillary nerve branches)
21.Abducent (sixth cranial) nerve
👉 highlighted on the photo
CN VI. Goes through the orbital fissure and supplies the lateral rectus and the retractor bulbi ("LR6").
📚 course slide · Lab 33 slides #32
22.Facial (seventh cranial) nerve
👉 labeled branches on the slide
CN VII. It enters the internal acoustic meatus and exits the stylomastoid foramen. It supplies the muscles of facial expression (auriculopalpebral, dorsal and ventral buccal branches).
📚 course slide · Lab 33 slides #33
23.Vestibulocochlear (eighth cranial) nerve
👉 "VIII" (left side of the brainstem)
CN VIII. It enters the internal acoustic meatus with CN VII and ends in the inner ear, so you can't dissect it. It carries hearing and balance.
📖 lab guide · Lab 33 notes, Fig. 5-53 (brain, ventral view)
24.Distal ganglion of the vagus
👉 highlighted on the photo
Just outside the tympano-occipital fissure, caudal to the cranial cervical ganglion. It holds visceral sensory cell bodies. The transcript and Pre-Lab 33 call it parasympathetic.
📚 course slide · Lab 33 slides #36
25.Cranial cervical ganglion
👉 highlighted on the photo
The most cranial sympathetic ganglion. It sits next to the distal vagal ganglion; together they lead into the vagosympathetic trunk.
📚 course slide · Lab 33 slides #38
26.Glossopharyngeal (ninth cranial) nerve
👉 highlighted on the photo
CN IX. Goes through the jugular foramen and tympano-occipital fissure to the tongue (caudal third) and the pharynx. Expose it by cutting the longus capitis.
📚 course slide · Lab 33 slides #40
27.Vagus (tenth cranial) nerve
👉 highlighted on the photo
CN X. Goes through the jugular foramen and tympano-occipital fissure and joins the sympathetic trunk as the vagosympathetic trunk down the neck.
📚 course slide · Lab 33 slides #42
28.Proximal ganglion of the vagus
👉 not visible: it sits inside the jugular foramen (only the distal ganglion shows here)
The vagus has 2 ganglia. The proximal ganglion sits inside the jugular foramen, so you can't see it. The distal ganglion is outside.
📖 lab guide · Lab 33 notes, Fig. 5-54 (nerves at the tympano-occipital fissure)
29.Pharyngeal plexus
👉 highlighted on the photo
A network formed by branches of the glossopharyngeal + vagus (plus cranial cervical ganglion fibers). It supplies the pharyngeal muscles.
📚 course slide · Lab 33 slides #47
30.Cranial laryngeal nerve
👉 highlighted on the photo
Leaves the vagus at the distal ganglion. It supplies the cricothyroid muscle and the laryngeal mucosa.
📚 course slide · Lab 33 slides #49
31.Caudal laryngeal nerve
👉 highlighted on the photo
The end of the recurrent laryngeal nerve. It supplies every laryngeal muscle except the cricothyroid. The left recurrent loops around the aortic arch; the right loops around the right subclavian artery.
📚 course slide · Lab 33 slides #51
32.Accessory (eleventh cranial) nerve
👉 highlighted on the photo
CN XI. Goes through the jugular foramen. Its ventral branch supplies the sternocephalicus (mastoid part) and its dorsal branch supplies the trapezius.
📚 course slide · Lab 33 slides #53
33.Hypoglossal (twelfth cranial) nerve
👉 highlighted on the photo
CN XII. Goes through the hypoglossal canal and is motor to the tongue muscles. It crosses lateral to the external carotid artery; IX runs medial to it.
📚 course slide · Lab 33 slides #55
34.Ansa cervicalis
👉 "Ansa cervicalis" (right side of the drawing)
A loop between the hypoglossal nerve and C1. Don't search for it; just know where it is.
📖 lab guide · Lab 33 notes, Fig. 5-54 (nerves at the tympano-occipital fissure)

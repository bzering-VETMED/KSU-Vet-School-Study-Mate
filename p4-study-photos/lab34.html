1.Meninges
👉 #5 dura, #6 arachnoid, #8 pia
The 3 connective-tissue coverings of the brain and spinal cord, from outside in: dura mater, arachnoid, pia mater.
📖 lab guide · Lab 34 notes, Fig. 6-1 (meninges, median plane)
2.Dura mater
👉 highlighted on the photo
The thickest, outermost meninx, fused to the inside of the cranium. Its folds are the falx cerebri and the tentorium cerebelli.
📚 course slide · Lab 34 slides #4
3.Longitudinal cerebral fissure
👉 highlighted on the photo
The deep midline groove that separates the left and right cerebral hemispheres. The falx cerebri sits in it.
📚 course slide · Lab 34 slides #6
4.Falx cerebri
👉 highlighted on the photo
A dura fold in the longitudinal fissure between the hemispheres. Its attached dorsal edge holds the dorsal sagittal sinus.
📚 course slide · Lab 34 slides #8
5.Pia mater
👉 #8 (the thin layer on the brain surface)
The innermost meninx, stuck directly to the brain. It follows the brain down into every sulcus.
📖 lab guide · Lab 34 notes, Fig. 6-1 (meninges, median plane)
6.Arachnoid
👉 #6 (arachnoid + its trabeculae)
The middle meninx, lining the dura. It bridges over the sulci and does not dip into them. Under it is the subarachnoid space full of cerebrospinal fluid (#7).
📖 lab guide · Lab 34 notes, Fig. 6-1 (meninges, median plane)
7.Leptomeninges
👉 the thin layer the probe is lifting
Arachnoid + pia mater together. In embalmed brains they are collapsed onto each other, which is what the probe lifts here.
📚 course slide · Lab 34 slides #11
8.Pachymeninges
👉 highlighted on the photo
Another name for the dura mater. Think "pachy = thick."
📚 course slide · Lab 34 slides #13
9.Cerebellomedullary cistern
👉 #14 (between the cerebellum and the medulla)
The largest subarachnoid cistern, in the angle between the cerebellum and medulla. This is where you do a cisterna magna CSF tap.
📖 lab guide · Lab 34 notes, Fig. 6-1 (meninges, median plane)
10.Basilar artery
👉 highlighted on the photo
The single artery on the ventral midline of the brainstem, formed by the vertebral arteries joining. It runs rostrally to the arterial circle.
📚 course slide · Lab 34 slides #16
11.Vertebral arteries
👉 highlighted on the photo
Branches of the subclavian that run up the transverse foramina and enter the vertebral canal. They form the basilar artery.
📚 course slide · Lab 34 slides #18
12.Ventral spinal artery
👉 highlighted on the photo
Continues caudally from the vertebral/basilar junction along the ventral midline of the spinal cord.
📚 course slide · Lab 34 slides #20
13.Internal carotid arteries
👉 highlighted on the photo
The other main blood supply to the brain (besides the vertebrals). They enter the skull and join the arterial circle beside the hypophysis.
📚 course slide · Lab 34 slides #22
14.Caudal communicating arteries
👉 highlighted on the photo
They come off the internal carotid and run caudally to join the basilar artery, closing the back half of the circle.
📚 course slide · Lab 34 slides #24
15.Cerebral arterial circle
👉 highlighted on the photo
The arterial ring around the hypophysis (circle of Willis): internal carotids + caudal communicating arteries + basilar artery + their branches.
📚 course slide · Lab 34 slides #26
16.Rostral cerebral artery
👉 highlighted on the photo
Runs forward into the longitudinal fissure and over the corpus callosum. It supplies the medial cerebrum.
📚 course slide · Lab 34 slides #28
17.Middle cerebral artery
👉 highlighted on the photo
Runs laterally, rostral to the piriform lobe, then spreads over the whole lateral cerebrum. It is the biggest branch.
📚 course slide · Lab 34 slides #30
18.Caudal cerebral artery
👉 highlighted on the photo
Comes off the caudal communicating artery, rostral to the oculomotor nerve, and dives deep to the caudal cerebrum.
📚 course slide · Lab 34 slides #32
19.Rostral cerebellar artery
👉 highlighted on the photo
Leaves the caudal third of the arterial circle, caudal to the oculomotor nerve, and runs along the pons to the cerebellar hemisphere. Watch the spelling: cerebellar vs cerebral.
📚 course slide · Lab 34 slides #34
20.Caudal cerebellar artery
👉 highlighted on the photo
A branch of the basilar artery going to the caudal cerebellum.
📚 course slide · Lab 34 slides #36
21.Dorsal sagittal sinus
👉 highlighted on the photo
The venous sinus in the attached dorsal edge of the falx cerebri. It drains caudally into the transverse sinuses.
📚 course slide · Lab 34 slides #38
22.Transverse sinus
👉 highlighted on the photo
Paired sinuses that run transversely in the bony transverse canal. Each one splits into the temporal sinus and the sigmoid sinus.
📚 course slide · Lab 34 slides #40
23.Temporal sinus
👉 highlighted on the photo
From the transverse sinus to the retroarticular foramen. There it becomes an emissary vein that joins the maxillary vein.
📚 course slide · Lab 34 slides #42
24.Sigmoid sinus
👉 highlighted on the photo
The S-shaped continuation of the transverse sinus. It curves over the petrous temporal bone and passes through the jugular foramen into the tympano-occipital fissure.
📚 course slide · Lab 34 slides #44
25.Cavernous sinus
👉 highlighted on the photo
On each side of the floor of the middle cranial fossa, beside the hypophysis. It drains caudally as the ventral petrosal sinus.
📚 course slide · Lab 34 slides #48
26.Ventral petrosal sinus
👉 highlighted on the photo
Continues caudally from the cavernous sinus through the petro-occipital canal and joins the sigmoid sinus.
📚 course slide · Lab 34 slides #46
27.Ventral internal vertebral venous plexuses
👉 highlighted on the photo
Paired venous channels on the floor of the vertebral canal. They collect blood from the skull sinuses and run down the spine.
📚 course slide · Lab 34 slides #50
28.Intervertebral veins
👉 "Intervertebral vein III"
At each intervertebral foramen they connect the internal vertebral plexus to the vertebral vein. Only find them if they are already exposed.
📖 lab guide · Lab 34 notes, Fig. 6-4 (cervical vertebral veins)

1.Caudal gluteal artery
👉 #1 on the photo
A terminal branch of the internal iliac artery. It leaves the pelvis over the greater ischiatic notch to the caudal hip and thigh muscles.
🌐 UMN · UMN Lab 20 · lateral rump
2.Cranial gluteal artery
👉 highlighted on the photo
From the caudal gluteal artery. It crosses the cranial part of the greater ischiatic notch, between the middle and deep gluteals.
📚 course slide · Lab 26 slides #5
3.Cranial gluteal nerve
👉 #2 on the photo
Travels with the cranial gluteal artery between the middle and deep gluteals, which it supplies, plus the tensor fasciae latae.
🌐 UMN · UMN Lab 21 · gluteal dissection (cat)
4.Iliolumbar artery
👉 highlighted on the photo
A branch of the caudal gluteal artery that runs cranially along the ilium to the lumbar muscles.
📚 course slide · Lab 26 slides #9
5.External iliac artery
👉 highlighted on the photo
Leaves the aorta at about L6–L7 and becomes the femoral artery after passing through the vascular lacuna.
📚 course slide · Lab 26 slides #11
6.Vascular lacuna
👉 #1 on the photo
The opening between the inguinal ligament and the pelvis where the external iliac artery leaves the abdomen.
🌐 UMN · UMN Lab 20 · external iliac → femoral branches
7.Deep femoral artery
👉 highlighted on the photo
The only branch of the external iliac, given off inside the abdomen near the vascular lacuna.
📚 course slide · Lab 26 slides #15
8.Caudal epigastric artery
👉 highlighted on the photo
Comes off the pudendoepigastric trunk (from the deep femoral) and runs cranially on the dorsal surface of the rectus abdominis.
📚 course slide · Lab 26 slides #17
9.Femoral triangle
👉 on the photo
The space bounded by the sartorius (cranial), vastus medialis + rectus femoris (lateral) and pectineus + adductor (caudal). It holds the femoral artery, vein and saphenous nerve.
🌐 UMN · UMN Lab 20 · femoral triangle
10.Medial circumflex femoral artery
👉 highlighted on the photo
The continuation of the deep femoral artery after it leaves the abdomen, running caudally between the quadriceps and pectineus into the adductor.
📚 course slide · Lab 26 slides #21
11.Deep branch (medial circumflex femoral artery)
👉 highlighted on the photo
One of the 2 branches of the medial circumflex femoral artery; it runs deep toward the hip joint.
📚 course slide · Lab 26 slides #23
12.Transverse branch (medial circumflex femoral artery)
👉 highlighted on the photo
The other branch of the medial circumflex femoral artery; it runs transversely into the adductor.
📚 course slide · Lab 26 slides #25
13.Femoral artery
👉 #1 on the photo
The continuation of the external iliac after the vascular lacuna. It runs down the femoral triangle (pulse site) and becomes the popliteal artery.
🌐 UMN · UMN Lab 20 · branches of the femoral artery
14.Superficial circumflex iliac artery
👉 highlighted on the photo
The first branch of the femoral artery, going cranially to the sartorius and tensor fasciae latae.
📚 course slide · Lab 26 slides #29
15.Lateral circumflex femoral artery
👉 highlighted on the photo
A femoral branch that dives between the rectus femoris and vastus medialis into the quadriceps.
📚 course slide · Lab 26 slides #31
16.Proximal caudal femoral artery
👉 highlighted on the photo
A femoral branch to the caudal (adductor/hamstring) thigh muscles, the first of 3 caudal femorals.
📚 course slide · Lab 26 slides #33
17.Saphenous artery
👉 highlighted on the photo
Leaves the femoral artery in the middle of the thigh and runs down the medial leg; it splits into cranial and caudal branches.
📚 course slide · Lab 26 slides #35
18.Saphenous nerve
👉 #6 on the photo
A branch of the femoral nerve. It runs with the femoral artery in the femoral triangle, then along the medial leg (skin of the medial limb).
🌐 UMN · UMN Lab 21 · lumbosacral plexus (dog)
19.Medial saphenous vein
👉 #1 on the photo
The superficial vein on the medial crus and thigh, draining into the femoral vein. Common blood-draw site in cats.
🌐 UMN · UMN Lab 20 · medial limb (cat)
20.Cranial branch (saphenous artery)
👉 highlighted on the photo
The branch of the saphenous artery running down the dorsal (cranial) paw.
📚 course slide · Lab 26 slides #39
21.Caudal branch (saphenous artery)
👉 highlighted on the photo
The branch of the saphenous artery running down the plantar (caudal) side of the tarsus and paw.
📚 course slide · Lab 26 slides #41
22.Lateral saphenous vein
👉 #1 on the photo
The superficial vein on the lateral crus, crossing the lateral hock. Classic dog catheter/blood-draw site.
🌐 UMN · UMN Lab 20 · lateral saphenous vein (dog)
23.Cranial branch (lateral saphenous vein)
👉 highlighted on the photo
The branch of the lateral saphenous vein on the dorsal (cranial) side of the tarsus.
📚 course slide · Lab 26 slides #47
24.Caudal branch (lateral saphenous vein)
👉 highlighted on the photo
The branch of the lateral saphenous vein on the plantar (caudal) side of the tarsus.
📚 course slide · Lab 26 slides #49
25.Descending genicular artery
👉 highlighted on the photo
A small femoral branch running to the medial side of the stifle.
📚 course slide · Lab 26 slides #51
26.Middle caudal femoral artery
👉 highlighted on the photo
The 2nd caudal femoral artery, into the caudal thigh muscles (adductor, semimembranosus).
📚 course slide · Lab 26 slides #53
27.Distal caudal femoral artery
👉 highlighted on the photo
The last femoral branch before the popliteal, to the gastrocnemius and caudal thigh.
📚 course slide · Lab 26 slides #55
28.Popliteal artery
👉 #12 on the photo
The continuation of the femoral artery behind the stifle, between the heads of the gastrocnemius. It ends as the cranial tibial artery.
🌐 UMN · UMN Lab 21 · sciatic division (cat)
29.Cranial tibial artery
👉 #1 on the photo
The continuation of the popliteal artery. It runs down the cranial crus with the deep fibular nerve and becomes the dorsal pedal artery.
🌐 UMN · UMN Lab 21 · cranial crus + pes

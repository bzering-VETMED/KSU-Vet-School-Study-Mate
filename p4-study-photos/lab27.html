Lab 27: Pelvic limb nerves

1.Lumbosacral plexus
👉 on the photo
The ventral branches of L4–S3 that form the nerves of the pelvic limb.
🌐 UMN · UMN Lab 21 · lumbosacral plexus (dog)
2.Obturator nerve
👉 highlighted on the photo
From L4–L6. It goes through the obturator foramen to the adductors (pectineus, gracilis, adductor). Cut it and the leg slides out sideways.
📚 course slide · Lab 27 slides #4
3.Femoral nerve
👉 #4 on the photo
From L4–L6, inside the iliopsoas. It supplies the quadriceps (stifle extension) and gives off the saphenous nerve; autonomous zone = medial limb skin.
🌐 UMN · UMN Lab 21 · medial thigh nerves
4.Pudendal nerve
👉 #3 on the photo
From S1–S3. It runs to the perineum, giving the caudal rectal, perineal and dorsal nerve of the penis/clitoris.
🌐 UMN · UMN Lab 20 · lateral rump
5.Caudal rectal nerve
👉 highlighted on the photo
A pudendal branch to the external anal sphincter.
📚 course slide · Lab 27 slides #10
6.Perineal nerves
👉 #3 on the photo
Pudendal branches to the perineal skin and muscles.
🌐 UMN · UMN Lab 20 · lateral rump
7.Dorsal nerve of the penis
👉 highlighted on the photo
The end of the pudendal nerve, along the dorsum of the penis (or clitoris in females). Sensory to the glans.
📚 course slide · Lab 27 slides #14
8.Caudal cutaneous femoral nerve
👉 highlighted on the photo
From the sacral plexus to the skin of the caudal thigh.
📚 course slide · Lab 27 slides #16
9.Lumbosacral trunk
👉 #1 on the photo
The joined L6–S2 roots in the pelvis that become the sciatic nerve.
🌐 UMN · UMN Lab 21 · gluteal dissection (cat)
10.Caudal gluteal nerve
👉 highlighted on the photo
From the lumbosacral trunk to the superficial gluteal muscle.
📚 course slide · Lab 27 slides #19
11.Cranial gluteal nerve
👉 highlighted on the photo
From the lumbosacral trunk. It runs with the cranial gluteal artery between the middle and deep gluteals and also supplies the tensor fasciae latae.
📚 course slide · Lab 27 slides #21
12.Sciatic nerve
👉 #2 on the photo
The biggest nerve, from the lumbosacral trunk. It leaves over the greater ischiatic notch, runs caudal to the hip, and splits into the common fibular + tibial nerves.
🌐 UMN · UMN Lab 20 · gluteal + perineal region
13.Lateral cutaneous sural nerve
👉 highlighted on the photo
A branch of the common fibular nerve to the skin of the lateral crus.
📚 course slide · Lab 27 slides #25
14.Caudal cutaneous sural nerve
👉 highlighted on the photo
A branch of the tibial nerve to the skin of the caudal crus.
📚 course slide · Lab 27 slides #27
15.Common fibular nerve
👉 #4 on the photo
The lateral branch of the sciatic. It crosses the lateral gastrocnemius and splits into superficial + deep fibular nerves. Flexes the tarsus, extends the digits.
🌐 UMN · UMN Lab 21 · cranial crus + pes
16.Superficial fibular nerve
👉 #4 on the photo
Runs under the fibularis longus to the dorsal paw skin. That dorsal skin is the fibular nerve's autonomous zone.
🌐 UMN · UMN Lab 21 · cranial crus + pes
17.Deep fibular nerve
👉 highlighted on the photo
Runs with the cranial tibial artery under the long digital extensor and cranial tibial muscles.
📚 course slide · Lab 27 slides #33
18.Perforating branch
👉 #6 on the photo
The vessel that perforates between metatarsals II and III, connecting the dorsal and plantar arteries of the paw.
🌐 UMN · UMN Lab 21 · arterial cast, crus + pes
19.Tibial nerve
👉 #9 on the photo
The medial/caudal branch of the sciatic. It supplies the gastrocnemius + digital flexors (extends the tarsus) and the plantar paw skin (its autonomous zone).
🌐 UMN · UMN Lab 20 · branches of the femoral artery
20.Tarsal tunnel
👉 highlighted on the photo
The channel on the plantar-medial tarsus where the tibial nerve branches and flexor tendons pass to the paw.

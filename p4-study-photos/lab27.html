The ventral branches of L4–S3 that form the nerves of the pelvic limb.

Obturator nerve
From L4–L6. It goes through the obturator foramen to the adductors (pectineus, gracilis, adductor). Cut it and the leg slides out sideways.

Femoral nerve
From L4–L6, inside the iliopsoas. It supplies the quadriceps (stifle extension) and gives off the saphenous nerve; autonomous zone = medial limb skin.

From S1–S3. It runs to the perineum, giving the caudal rectal, perineal and dorsal nerve of the penis/clitoris.

Cranial gluteal nerve

Sciatic nerve
The biggest nerve, from the lumbosacral trunk. It leaves over the greater ischiatic notch, runs caudal to the hip, and splits into the common fibular + tibial nerves.

Common fibular nerve
The lateral branch of the sciatic. It crosses the lateral gastrocnemius and splits into superficial + deep fibular nerves. Flexes the tarsus, extends the digits.

Superficial fibular nerve
Runs under the fibularis longus to the dorsal paw skin. That dorsal skin is the fibular nerve's autonomous zone.

Perforating branch
The vessel that perforates between metatarsals II and III, connecting the dorsal and plantar arteries of the paw.

19.Name it: highlighted on the photo
📚 course slide · Lab 27 slides #38
Tibial nerve
The medial/caudal branch of the sciatic. It supplies the gastrocnemius + digital flexors (extends the tarsus) and the plantar paw skin (its autonomous zone).
💗 TAP TO REVEAL

Tarsal tunnel
The channel on the plantar-medial tarsus where the tibial nerve branches and flexor tendons pass to the paw.

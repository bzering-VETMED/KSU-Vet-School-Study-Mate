1.Epiglottic cartilage
👉 #12 on the photo
The most rostral laryngeal cartilage. It flips back over the laryngeal inlet when the dog swallows, so food goes to the esophagus instead.
🌐 UMN · UMN · pharynx, bisected head
2.Aryepiglottic fold
👉 highlighted on the photo
A mucosal fold running from the epiglottis to the arytenoid cartilage. It forms the side wall of the laryngeal inlet.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #5
3.Thyroid cartilage
👉 #6 on the photo
The biggest laryngeal cartilage: a ventral U-shaped trough made of two laminae. Its laryngeal prominence is what you palpate on the neck.
🌐 UMN · UMN · hyoid apparatus, ventrolateral
4.Rostral cornu
👉 highlighted on the photo
Projection at the dorsal edge of the thyroid cartilage that points rostrally and articulates with the thyrohyoid bone.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #9
5.Caudal cornu
👉 highlighted on the photo
Projection at the dorsal edge of the thyroid cartilage that points caudally and articulates with the cricoid cartilage.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #11
6.Caudal thyroid incisure
👉 highlighted on the photo
The ventral midline notch in the caudal border of the thyroid cartilage, where the cricothyroid ligament attaches.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #13
7.Cricothyroid ligament
👉 highlighted on the photo
Fills the ventral gap between the thyroid and cricoid cartilages, attaching at the caudal thyroid incisure.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #14
8.Cricoid cartilage
👉 highlighted on the photo
A complete signet ring: narrow ventral arch, wide dorsal lamina. It sits caudal to the thyroid cartilage and in front of the trachea.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #16
9.Arytenoid cartilage
👉 highlighted on the photo
Paired cartilages that sit on the rostral border of the cricoid lamina. Their 4 processes are muscular, vocal, corniculate and cuneiform.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #18
10.Muscular process
👉 highlighted on the photo
The lateral process of the arytenoid. The muscles that open and close the glottis attach here (cricoarytenoideus dorsalis, cricoarytenoideus lateralis, thyroarytenoideus).
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #20
11.Vocal process
👉 highlighted on the photo
The ventral process of the arytenoid, where the vocal fold attaches.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #22
12.Vocal fold
👉 highlighted on the photo
Runs from the vocal process of the arytenoid to the midventral thyroid cartilage. It is the caudal of the two folds and sits caudal to the ventricle.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #24
13.Corniculate process
👉 highlighted on the photo
The dorsal horn of the arytenoid. It forms the caudal edge of the laryngeal inlet.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #26
14.Cuneiform process
👉 highlighted on the photo
Attached to the arytenoid rostral to the corniculate process. The vestibular fold attaches to it.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #28
15.Vestibular fold
👉 highlighted on the photo
The rostral fold, running from the cuneiform process. Order down the airway: vestibular fold, then ventricle, then vocal fold.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #30
16.Laryngeal ventricle
👉 highlighted on the photo
The pouch between the vestibular fold and the vocal fold.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #32
17.Glottis
👉 highlighted on the photo
The voice-producing part of the larynx: both vocal folds + the vocal processes + the rima glottidis.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #34
18.Rima glottidis
👉 highlighted on the photo
Just the gap through the glottis, between the vocal folds. The cricoarytenoideus dorsalis widens it.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #36
19.Cricothyroid muscle
👉 highlighted on the photo
The "bow tie" on the ventral larynx, from the cricoid to the thyroid cartilage. It tenses the vocal folds and is the only laryngeal muscle supplied by the cranial laryngeal nerve.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #38
20.Cricoarytenoideus dorsalis
👉 highlighted on the photo
Runs from the dorsal cricoid lamina to the muscular process. It is the only abductor: it opens the rima glottidis. When it is paralyzed, you get laryngeal paralysis.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #40
21.Cricoarytenoideus lateralis
👉 highlighted on the photo
Runs from the lateral cricoid arch to the muscular process. It adducts, which closes the rima glottidis.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #42
22.Thyroarytenoideus
👉 highlighted on the photo
The parent muscle from the thyroid cartilage to the muscular process. It splits into vocalis (medial) and ventricularis (rostral) and adducts the glottis.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #44
23.Vocalis
👉 highlighted on the photo
The medial part of the thyroarytenoideus. It lies inside the vocal fold, next to the vocal ligament.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #46
24.Vocal ligament
👉 highlighted on the photo
The ligament that forms the free edge of the vocal fold, from the vocal process to the thyroid cartilage.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #48
25.Auricular cartilage
👉 #3 on the photo
The cartilage that shapes the pinna (ear flap) and rolls into a funnel for the vertical ear canal.
🌐 UMN · UMN · ear muscles, dorsal view
26.Tragus
👉 highlighted on the photo
A thick, square plate of auricular cartilage on the rostral side of the ear canal opening, opposite the antihelix.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #54
27.Marginal cutaneous sac
👉 highlighted on the photo
A small skin pouch on the lateral margin of the ear. It is easier to find on your cadaver than in pictures.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #56
28.Tympanic membrane
👉 highlighted on the photo
The eardrum, at the deep end of the external acoustic meatus. It separates the outer ear from the middle ear (tympanic bulla).
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #58
29.Annular cartilage
👉 #6 on the photo
A ring of cartilage linking the auricular cartilage to the bony external acoustic meatus.
🌐 UMN · UMN · hyoid bones + ear, lateral
30.Temporalis muscle
👉 #2 on the photo
The biggest jaw-closer. It fills the temporal fossa and inserts on the coronoid process of the mandible.
🌐 UMN · UMN · before removing the zygomatic arch
31.Masseter muscle
👉 #1 on the photo
A jaw-closer on the lateral side of the mandible, from the zygomatic arch to the masseteric fossa.
🌐 UMN · UMN · arch removed, masseter reflected
32.Zygomatic salivary gland
👉 #5 on the photo
Sits in the ventral orbit, medial to the zygomatic arch. You see it once the arch is removed. Its ducts open into the mouth near the last upper molar.
🌐 UMN · UMN · lateral orbit, periorbita removed
33.Medial pterygoid muscle
👉 #4 on the photo
A jaw-closer from the pterygopalatine fossa to the medial side of the mandible ramus and angular process.
🌐 UMN · UMN · deep dissection, maxillary artery
34.Lateral pterygoid muscle
👉 at the arrow / probe
A smaller jaw-closer next to the medial pterygoid, from the pterygopalatine fossa to the condylar process. Your lab does not ask you to separate the two.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #69
35.Digastricus
👉 #1 on the photo
The only jaw-opener. It runs from the paracondylar process to the ventral border of the mandible and has a tendinous intersection in the middle.
🌐 UMN · UMN · ventrolateral, digastricus
36.Styloglossus
👉 #1 on the photo
An extrinsic tongue muscle from the stylohyoid bone to the tongue. It retracts the tongue.
🌐 UMN · UMN · styloglossus + hyoglossus
37.Hyoglossus
👉 highlighted on the photo
An extrinsic tongue muscle from the basihyoid and thyrohyoid to the tongue. It retracts and depresses the tongue. The hypoglossal nerve runs on its lateral surface.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #75
38.Genioglossus
👉 #1 on the photo
A fan-shaped extrinsic tongue muscle from the chin (medial mandible) into the tongue. It protrudes and depresses the tongue.
🌐 UMN · UMN · medial view, genioglossus
39.Sternohyoideus
👉 highlighted on the photo
A strap muscle from the sternum to the basihyoid. It pulls the hyoid and tongue caudally.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #79
40.Thyrohyoideus
👉 highlighted on the photo
From the thyroid cartilage to the thyrohyoid bone. It pulls the larynx and hyoid toward each other.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #81
41.Mylohyoideus
👉 highlighted on the photo
A sling under the tongue between the two halves of the mandible. It raises the floor of the mouth and tongue. The hypoglossal nerve runs deep to it.
📚 course slide · Lab 30 slides #83
42.Geniohyoideus
👉 #1 on the photo
From the chin to the basihyoid, deep to the mylohyoideus. It pulls the hyoid rostrally.
🌐 UMN · UMN · sagittal head, larynx + tongue

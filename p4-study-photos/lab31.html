1.Periorbita
👉 #1 on the photo
The fibrous cone around the eye muscles, nerves and vessels, open toward the orbital fissure.
🌐 UMN · UMN · orbit in periorbita
2.Lacrimal gland
👉 #2 on the photo
Sits dorsolateral to the eyeball, under the orbital ligament, inside the periorbita. It makes most of the tear film.
🌐 UMN · UMN · lateral orbit, periorbita removed
3.Levator palpebrae superioris
👉 #1 on the photo
A thin muscle on the dorsal surface of the dorsal rectus that lifts the upper eyelid (CN III). Easy to destroy.
🌐 UMN · UMN · cat orbit, dorsolateral
4.Obliquus muscles
👉 on the photo
The 2 oblique eye muscles: dorsal oblique (CN IV) and ventral oblique (CN III).
🌐 UMN · UMN · cat orbit, dorsal oblique + trochlea
5.Rectus muscles
👉 on the photo
The 4 recti (dorsal, medial, ventral, lateral) run from around the optic canal to the sclera. Lateral rectus = CN VI; the rest = CN III.
🌐 UMN · UMN · cat orbit, extrinsic eye muscles
6.Retractor bulbi
👉 highlighted on the photo
The cone of muscle right around the optic nerve that pulls the eye back (CN VI).
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #17
7.Dorsal rectus
👉 highlighted on the photo
Rotates the eye up. Nerve: CN III.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #10
8.Medial rectus
👉 #1 on the photo
Rotates the eye medially (toward the nose). Nerve: CN III.
📖 lab guide · Fig. 5-36 Schema of extrinsic ocular muscles and their action on the l
9.Ventral rectus
👉 highlighted on the photo
Rotates the eye down. Nerve: CN III.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #13
10.Lateral rectus
👉 highlighted on the photo
Rotates the eye laterally. Nerve: CN VI ("LR6"). Don't cut it in lab.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #15
11.Dorsal oblique
👉 on the photo
Its tendon runs through the trochlea, then turns laterally onto the eyeball. It rotates the top of the eye medially. Nerve: CN IV.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #18
12.Trochlea
👉 at the arrow / probe
The cartilage pulley on the medial orbit wall that the dorsal oblique tendon slides through.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #19
13.Ventral oblique
👉 #1 on the photo
The only eye muscle that doesn't start at the back of the orbit; it starts on the medial orbit wall near the lacrimal fossa. Nerve: CN III.
🌐 UMN · UMN · cat orbit, lateral rectus reflected
14.Superficial gland of the third eyelid
👉 highlighted on the photo
Wraps the base of the third-eyelid cartilage. When it prolapses, that's "cherry eye."
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #22
15.External fibrous coat
👉 highlighted on the photo
The outer tunic of the eyeball = cornea + sclera.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #24
16.Cornea
👉 #9 on the photo
The clear front of the fibrous coat. No blood vessels.
🌐 UMN · UMN · eyelids (top) + enlarged eye (bottom)
17.Sclera
👉 highlighted on the photo
The white, opaque back part of the fibrous coat, where the eye muscles insert.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #28
18.Limbus
👉 highlighted on the photo
The junction of the cornea and sclera.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #30
19.Middle vascular coat
👉 highlighted on the photo
The middle tunic = the uvea: iris + ciliary body + choroid.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #32
20.Uvea
👉 labeled on the photo
Another name for the middle vascular coat (iris, ciliary body, choroid).
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #32
21.Iris
👉 highlighted on the photo
The colored diaphragm in front of the lens. Its opening is the pupil.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #34
22.Pupil
👉 highlighted on the photo
The opening in the iris. Dilator pupillae (sympathetic) widens it; sphincter (parasympathetic) narrows it.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #36
23.Choroid
👉 highlighted on the photo
The vascular layer between the sclera and retina at the back of the eye; it holds the tapetum.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #38
24.Ora serrata
👉 highlighted on the photo
The jagged line where the light-sensitive retina ends and the ciliary part begins.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #40
25.Fundus
👉 on the photo
The back of the eye you see with an ophthalmoscope (retina, optic disk, tapetum).
📚 lecture slide · Dr. Malreddy eye lecture, slide 29
26.Tapetum lucidum
👉 highlighted on the photo
The reflective layer in the dorsal choroid that makes dogs' eyes shine at night.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #43
27.Ciliary body
👉 #1 on the photo
The uvea ring between the iris and choroid. Its muscle changes lens shape, and its processes make aqueous humor.
📚 lecture slide · Dr. Malreddy eye lecture #36
28.Ciliary processes
👉 highlighted on the photo
The folds of the ciliary body that secrete aqueous humor and anchor the zonular fibers.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #46
29.Zonule
👉 highlighted on the photo
The suspensory ligament of the lens (all the zonular fibers together).
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #48
30.Zonular fibers
👉 highlighted on the photo
Fibers from the ciliary processes to the lens equator that hold the lens in place.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #49
31.Lens
👉 #1 on the photo
A clear disc behind the iris, held by the zonule: capsule, soft cortex, hard nucleus.
🌐 UMN · UMN · eyeball incised
32.Vitreous body
👉 highlighted on the photo
The clear gel that fills the vitreous chamber behind the lens.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #53
33.Vitreous chamber
👉 highlighted on the photo
The big space behind the lens, filled by the vitreous body.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #54
34.Aqueous humor
👉 labeled on the photo
Made by the ciliary processes. It flows posterior chamber → pupil → anterior chamber → iridocorneal angle.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #57
35.Anterior chamber
👉 highlighted on the photo
The space between the cornea and iris.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #57
36.Posterior chamber
👉 #1 on the photo
The thin space between the iris and lens. It is NOT behind the lens.
📚 lecture slide · Dr. Malreddy eye lecture #36
37.Iridocorneal angle
👉 highlighted on the photo
The angle between the iris and cornea, where aqueous humor drains (blocked in glaucoma).
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #61
38.Scleral sinus
👉 highlighted on the photo
The venous channel in the sclera near the angle that carries aqueous humor away.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #63
39.Pectinate ligament
👉 #8 on the photo
Strands spanning the iridocorneal angle from the iris base to the cornea.
📖 lab guide · Fig. 5-39 Section of eyeball at iridocorneal angle.
40.Internal coat
👉 labeled on the photo
The inner tunic of the eyeball = the retina.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #24
41.Retina
👉 on the photo
The inner tunic. Its parts are pars optica (light-sensitive), pars ciliaris and pars iridica.
📚 lecture slide · Dr. Malreddy eye lecture, slide 29
42.Pars optica retinae
👉 highlighted on the photo
The light-sensitive part of the retina, behind the ora serrata.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #69
43.Pars ciliaris retinae
👉 highlighted on the photo
The non-sensory retina lining the ciliary body.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #71
44.Pars iridica retinae
👉 highlighted on the photo
The non-sensory retina lining the back of the iris.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #73
45.Dilator pupillae
👉 #1 on the photo
The intrinsic iris muscle that widens the pupil. It is sympathetic.
📚 lecture slide · Dr. Malreddy eye lecture, slide 38
46.Optic disk
👉 highlighted on the photo
Where the optic nerve leaves the eye. There are no photoreceptors there (the blind spot).
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #78
47.External jugular vein
👉 #1 on the photo
The big superficial neck vein formed by the linguofacial + maxillary veins. Blood-draw and catheter site.
🌐 UMN · UMN · superficial veins of the head
48.Lingual vein
👉 highlighted on the photo
Drains the tongue and joins the facial vein to form the linguofacial vein.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #83
49.Linguofacial vein
👉 highlighted on the photo
Formed by the lingual + facial veins. It joins the maxillary vein to form the external jugular.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #85
50.Facial vein
👉 #5 on the photo
Runs across the side of the face from the medial eye corner; it joins the lingual vein.
🌐 UMN · UMN · lips + cheek muscles
51.Dorsal nasal vein
👉 highlighted on the photo
Runs along the top of the muzzle into the facial vein.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #89
52.Angularis oculi vein
👉 #1 on the photo
Drains the medial corner of the eye into the facial vein.
🌐 UMN · UMN · superficial veins, rostrolateral
53.Deep facial vein
👉 #1 on the photo
A deep tributary of the facial vein, coming from under the zygomatic arch.
📖 lab guide · Fig. 5-42 Superﬁcial veins of head, right lateral aspect.
54.Maxillary vein
👉 #5 on the photo
Comes from behind the mandible and joins the linguofacial vein to form the external jugular.
🌐 UMN · UMN · superficial salivary glands
55.Facial (seventh cranial) nerve
👉 highlighted on the photo
CN VII leaves the stylomastoid foramen. Its branches (auriculopalpebral, dorsal and ventral buccal) supply the muscles of facial expression.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #96
56.Auriculopalpebral nerve
👉 #1 on the photo
A facial nerve branch to the ear muscles and eyelids (blinking).
📚 course slide · Dr. Wilhite head + eye deck #22

Auriculopalpebral branches to the rostral ear muscles.
📚 course slide · Lab 31 slides #99
58.Palpebral branches
👉 highlighted on the photo
Auriculopalpebral branches to the orbicularis oculi (closing the eyelids).

59.Buccal branches

The dorsal + ventral buccal branches of the facial nerve, running across the masseter to the lips and cheek.

69.Pharyngoesophageal limen
👉 #10 on the photo
The boundary between the laryngopharynx and the esophagus.

70.Trachea
👉 #8 on the photo
The airway down the neck, held open by C-shaped cartilages; the trachealis muscle closes them dorsally.

👉 highlighted on the photo
The C-shaped rings, open dorsally, that keep the trachea from collapsing.

Basel · calcium & phosphate handling · Mon 11/9 · LC59+60 S1–24 · 24 terms · say it, then tap the pink box

Calcium filtered vs excreted (human)~10,000 mg/day filtered, ~100 mg/day lost → must reabsorb ~99% (S4)
Hormones that set plasma Ca²⁺PTH (↑ Ca²⁺) · calcitonin (↓ Ca²⁺) · active vitamin D (S5)
★ Lumen-positive charge and Ca²⁺Positive lumen in the late proximal tubule + thick ascending limb pushes CATIONS (Na⁺, Ca²⁺, K⁺) out paracellularly (S6–8)
★ Proximal tubule Ca²⁺~65% · PASSIVE, paracellular, lumen-positive charge · not hormone-controlled (S9)
★ Thick ascending limb Ca²⁺~25% · PASSIVE, paracellular, lumen-positive charge · through CLAUDIN-16 (S10)
★ Distal convoluted tubule Ca²⁺~8% · ACTIVE + CONTROLLED (ECaC/TRPV5) · the decision point (S11)
★ Calcium totalsPassive ~90% always (65 + 25) · DCT decides the last ~8% → ~90% (high Ca²⁺) to ~98% (low Ca²⁺) (S12)
★ Claudin-16 vs claudin-14Claudin-16 = makes tight junctions leaky to Ca²⁺ · claudin-14 INHIBITS claudin-16 (S10, S16)
★ PTH effect #1 · distal tubule↑ ECaC/TRPV5 number + open probability, ↑ calbindin → more Ca²⁺ reabsorbed (S13)
★ PTH effect #2 · proximal tubule↑ CYP27B1 (cytochrome P450 enzyme) → turns 25-hydroxy vitamin D into ACTIVE 1,25-dihydroxy vitamin D (S14)
Active vitamin D targetsGUT (↑ Ca²⁺ absorption — how PTH reaches the gut) + distal tubule (synergizes with PTH on ECaC/calbindin) (S14–15)
★ PTH effect #3 · thick ascending limbBLOCKS claudin-14 → claudin-16 freer → more Ca²⁺ reabsorbed (S16)
Kidney vs bone as PTH targetBasel: you could argue the KIDNEY is the main target (so much Ca²⁺ passes through it)
Calcitonin in the kidneyMostly opposes PTH: blocks claudin-16 (less TAL Ca²⁺), ↓ distal Ca²⁺ pumps · oddly, slightly ↑ CYP27B1 · weaker than PTH (S18)
★ Loop diuretics and Ca²⁺Block NKCC → less K⁺ back-leak → less lumen-positive charge → less Ca²⁺ reabsorbed → CALCIUM-WASTING (S19)
★ Thiazides and Ca²⁺Block NCC → less Na⁺ enters the DCT cell → cell more negative → Ca²⁺ enters through ECaC more easily → CALCIUM-SPARING (S19)
Diuretic choice and calciumHypocalcemic patient → think twice about loop diuretics · monitor Ca²⁺ on long-term loops
Phosphate role in bloodNon-volatile buffer (bicarbonate is the volatile one)
★ Phosphate handlingFreely filtered · 80–95% reabsorbed in the PROXIMAL tubule · 5–20% excreted (needed for acid buffering) (S22)
★ NaPi-IIaSodium-phosphate cotransporter (apical, proximal tubule) · secondary active (S23)
★ FGF23 and phosphateFrom osteocytes · REMOVES NaPi from the membrane → less phosphate reabsorbed, more excreted (S23)
PTH and phosphateAlso ↓ NaPi (weaker) + ↑ FGF23 via bone → ↑ Ca²⁺ but phosphate stable or ↓ (S23–24)
Acidosis and phosphateH⁺ inhibits NaPi → more phosphate left in filtrate to buffer acid
Why too LITTLE phosphate excretion is badNot enough phosphate reaches the collecting duct → can't buffer secreted H⁺ → acidosis

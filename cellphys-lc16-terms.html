🍬 LC16 · Glucose transport, glycolysis, anaerobic metabolism

GLUTGlucose transporter family; passive (facilitated diffusion) DOWN the glucose gradient.
GLUT4The insulin-dependent GLUT (muscle, adipose). The one GLUT you must know.
GLUT1 / GLUT3 tissuesBrain and red blood cells: tissues with an absolute glucose requirement.
SGLTSodium-glucose cotransporter: secondary active, uses the Na⁺ gradient to move glucose UP its gradient (gut, kidney).
Glucose trappingHexokinase/glucokinase adds a phosphate → glucose-6-phosphate is negative and can't leave; keeps free glucose low inside.
HexokinaseLow Km (<1 mM, high affinity), most tissues, constitutive, inhibited by glucose-6-phosphate.
GlucokinaseHigh Km (~15 mM), liver + pancreatic β-cells, inducible, NOT inhibited; glucose sensor.
4 → 7.5 mM glucoseHexokinase activity +1.7%; glucokinase activity +58%.
Glycolysis first half (steps 1–5)"Add a phosphate, change around, add a phosphate, break it down."
Glycolysis second half (steps 6–10)"NADH, ATP, switch it, dry it, ATP."
Phosphofructokinase-1Main regulatory, irreversible, rate-limiting step of glycolysis (step 3).
Fructose-1,6-bisphosphatePFK-1 product; feed-forward activates pyruvate kinase.
Pyruvate kinaseLast step (PEP → pyruvate); irreversible, regulated, makes ATP.
Irreversible glycolysis stepsHexokinase/glucokinase, phosphofructokinase-1, pyruvate kinase.
Glycolysis net yield+2 ATP and +2 NADH per glucose (~7 ATP with oxygen).
Lactate dehydrogenaseReduces pyruvate → lactate to regenerate NAD⁺ (or reverses to clear lactate).
Why make lactate?To refill NAD⁺ so glycolysis step 6 keeps running when oxygen is low.
Lactic acid drawbacksOnly 2 ATP, loses TCA energy, builds in blood → metabolic acidosis.
Cori cycleMuscle lactate → liver → glucose → back to muscle. Net cost 4 ATP (+2 muscle, −6 liver).
Lactate in the heartOxidative muscle takes up lactate → pyruvate → TCA; up to 70% of heart energy in intense exercise.
Yeast fermentationPyruvate decarboxylase + alcohol dehydrogenase → ethanol, regenerating NAD⁺.
Volatile fatty acidsAcetate, propionate (+ butyrate) from rumen bacteria = main ruminant energy source.
Hindgut fermentersHorses, rabbits.
Pyruvate kinase deficiency (Westie)Red cells have no mitochondria → glycolysis only → no ATP → hemolytic anemia. No cure; transfusions.
Grain overload (ruminant)Fast fermentation acidifies rumen → Lactobacillus takes over → lactic acid → ↓ rumen contractions, acidosis.
Metabolic acidosisLow blood pH from acid build-up (e.g., lactic acid).
Fibrobacteres / MegasphaeraRumen bacteria that refill NAD⁺ by making succinate / propionate.
Facilitated diffusionCarrier-assisted movement DOWN a gradient, no energy (GLUTs).
Secondary active transportUses another ion's gradient (Na⁺) to move something UP its gradient (SGLTs).

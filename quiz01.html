Question 2
What is the mass in kg of the Boer buck from the previous question?
✏️ Your work: solve it cold before revealing.
Tap to reveal answer
≈ 127 kg

Direction check: kilograms are the bigger unit, so the number must get smaller. If you got 616, you multiplied.
Question 3
Physiological saline is ~0.9% (wt/vol) NaCl in water. Assume the molecular weight of NaCl is 58 g/mol. How many milligrams of NaCl are needed to make one deciliter of physiological saline?
✏️ Your work: solve it cold before revealing.
Tap to reveal answer
900 mg
0.9% w/v = 0.9 g / 100 mL and 1 dL = 100 mL, so one deciliter needs 0.9 g = 900 mg.

The trap: the molecular weight is superfluous. Percent weight/volume is already a mass-per-volume statement; nothing here converts to moles.
Question 4
📷 Photo: Percheron.

The Percheron weighs 1,950 pounds. Estimate the volume of total body water in liters.
✏️ Your work: solve it cold before revealing.
Tap to reveal answer
≈ 532 L
1,950 lb ÷ 2.2 = 886 kg → 886 × 0.60 = 532 L.

TBW is 60% of body mass — the first number in 60·40·20·15·5.
Question 5
The same 1,950 lb Percheron. Estimate the volume of its plasma in liters.
✏️ Your work: solve it cold before revealing.
Tap to reveal answer
≈ 44 L
886 kg × 0.05 = 44.3 L.

Plasma is the intravascular compartment, 5% of body mass. Cross-check: ECF = 886 × 0.20 = 177 L; 177 ÷ 4 ≈ 44 L.

Worth noticing: 5% is plasma; the 8% figure is whole blood (cells + plasma). Read the noun in the question.
Question 6
You have concerns about kidney function in a 22 lb Beagle and must determine creatinine (113.12 g/mol) clearance. Urine output in 12 hours is 410 mL and the creatinine concentration in that urine is 119 mg/dL. What is the creatinine elimination in mg/day? (must be correct to ±1%)
✏️ Your work: solve it cold before revealing.
Tap to reveal answer
≈ 976 mg/day
119 mg/dL = 1.19 mg/mL.
410 mL × 1.19 mg/mL = 488 mg per 12 h.
488 × 2 = 976 mg/day.

Two decoys: the dog's 22 lb weight and creatinine's molecular weight are both unused. The question asks for an amount in mg, not a dose per kg and not moles.
Question 7 · Select all that apply
Which of the following are used to determine the diffusion coefficient (i.e., components in the calculation)?
  • Concentration difference
  • Cross-sectional area
  • Solute size (molecular radius)
  • Solvent viscosity
  • Temperature
✏️ Your work: solve it cold before revealing.
Tap to reveal answer
Solute size · Solvent viscosity · Temperature
D = kBT / 6πηr — the relevant components are temperature, viscosity, and radius.

Concentration difference and cross-sectional area are Fick's terms, not Einstein's. Einstein = one particle; Fick = population crossing a boundary.
Question 8
Which component in Fick's Law of Diffusion do we manipulate in a clinical setting to maximize drug or nutrient delivery (i.e., flux) to a targeted location?
  • Concentration difference
  • Cross-sectional area
  • Diffusion coefficient
  • Diffusion distance
✏️ Your work: solve it cold before revealing.
Tap to reveal answer
Cross-sectional area
This is the routes-of-administration idea: oral gives you the enormous intestinal surface; nebulized delivery uses the lung. Increasing surface area is the clinical lever emphasized in the practice copy.

D is treated as fixed by temperature/viscosity in this context; diffusion distance is set by anatomy.
Question 9
Vertex is marketing a new first-in-class pain medication (suzetrigine) with a molecular mass of 473.4 g/mol. Which statement is true?
  • Suzetrigine will diffuse through water more rapidly than glucose, but not as fast as cyclosporin.
  • Suzetrigine will diffuse through water more rapidly than oxytocin, but not as fast as codeine.
  • Suzetrigine will diffuse through water at a rate similar to lidocaine or aspirin.
  • Suzetrigine will diffuse through water more rapidly than it will diffuse through 1% dextrose in water.
✏️ Your work: solve it cold before revealing.
Tap to reveal answer
Faster than oxytocin, slower than codeine
Place 473 on the size ladder: codeine 299 → suzetrigine 473 → oxytocin ~1,000. Bigger diffuses slower, so it beats oxytocin and loses to codeine.

Glucose and aspirin are ~180, while cyclosporin is ~1,200. The original practice copy notes that lidocaine's molecular weight was not in the uploaded materials.

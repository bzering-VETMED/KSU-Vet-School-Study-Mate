🔥 LC28 · Eicosanoids quiz

23 questions · Dr. Basel style · Exam 4

Exam-style questions on LC28 only, written from the slides (not an official key). Tap an answer to check it; select-all needs ✓ Check. Keep your FIRST answer unless you can name the fact that proves it wrong. 📖 = slide number.

Q1A horse receives a synthetic glucocorticoid, which reduces eicosanoid production by acting through annexin A1. Which enzyme does this inhibit?

✅ D · Glucocorticoids act via annexin A1 on phospholipase A2, the first shared step, so arachidonic acid release is blocked.📖 S15

Q2Which eicosanoid class is reduced by a synthetic glucocorticoid but NOT by a first-generation non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID)?

✅ C · Glucocorticoids block phospholipase A2 upstream of every branch; NSAIDs block only COX-1/COX-2, leaving the 5-LOX leukotriene branch intact.📖 S15, S18

Q3Prostaglandin E2 (PGE2) binds the EP3 receptor. Which event follows? (cAMP = cyclic adenosine monophosphate; IP3 = inositol trisphosphate)

✅ B · EP3 recruits Gi; Gαi inhibits adenylyl cyclase, so cAMP-dependent pathways decrease.📖 S10

Q4Prostaglandin E2 (PGE2) causes different, even opposite, effects in different tissues. Which explanation does the lecture give?

✅ A · Main Point of the receptor table: different G protein couplings (e.g. EP2/Gs vs EP3/Gi) explain tissue-specific effects.📖 S9

Q5Which tissue – eicosanoid – effect pairing matches the eicosanoid functions table?

✅ A · Uterus PGF2α causes luteolysis; kidney uses PGE2, endothelium PGI2, platelets TxA2.📖 S7

Q6A dog on a first-generation non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) develops a gastric ulcer. Loss of which effect best explains it?

✅ D · Gastric mucosa relies on COX-1-derived PGE2 and PGI2 for cytoprotection; blocking COX-1 removes it.📖 S7, S21, S22

Q7A patient taking a COX-2 selective inhibitor (coxib) has a thrombotic cardiovascular event. Which imbalance best explains it?

✅ C · Endothelial PGI2 is COX-2 derived and keeps platelets resting; platelet TxA2 is COX-1 derived and unaffected, so aggregation/vasoconstriction dominate.📖 S26, S27

Q8Which pairing of a cardinal sign of inflammation and its cause is NOT as stated in lecture?

✅ B · Rubor is redness due to vasodilation; vascular permeability causes tumor (swelling).📖 S13

Q9A hypovolemic dog is given a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) and glomerular filtration rate drops. Which change contributes?

✅ D · NSAIDs reduce macula densa and endothelial PGE2, preventing afferent dilation (and, via less renin, efferent contraction).📖 S12, S30 (slides only, not yet lectured)

Q10Macula densa cells sense low NaCl and produce prostaglandin E2 (PGE2) via PTGS1/2. What does this PGE2 stimulate?

✅ A · Macula densa PGE2 stimulates renin exocytosis from the granular cells of the juxtaglomerular apparatus.📖 S28 (slides only, not yet lectured)

Q11Which patients does the lecture identify as at increased risk of acute kidney injury (AKI) after non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) treatment? (select all that apply)

✅ A, C, D · Most healthy patients are fine; hypovolemic, hypotensive, or angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor/angiotensin receptor blocker (ARB) patients are at risk.📖 S29, S30 (slides only, not yet lectured)

Q12Thromboxane A2 (TxA2) binds the TPβ receptor in the pathway shown in lecture. What happens next? (DAG = diacylglycerol; IP3 = inositol trisphosphate)

✅ C · TPβ recruits Gq; Gαq activates phospholipase C to make DAG and IP3, which activate downstream pathways.📖 S11

Q13Which statement about how eicosanoids usually signal is correct?

✅ B · Eicosanoids are very hydrophobic, so they are usually autocrine/paracrine; a few can be endocrine.📖 S5

Q14A trainer wants a racehorse's osteoarthritis pain treated while avoiding digestive-tract ulcers. Which drug class fits, and which risk remains? (NSAID = non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug)

✅ D · Coxibs target COX-2 only, sparing COX-1 gut cytoprotection, but push toward clotting (adverse cardiovascular events).📖 S12, S23, S27; transcript

Q15A horse receives long-term synthetic glucocorticoids. Which metabolic side effect is described in lecture?

✅ A · Glucocorticoids are catabolic in muscle, connective tissue, skin, and bone, releasing amino acids that the liver uses for gluconeogenesis.📖 S17

Q16A dehydrated dog with arthritis needs pain control. Compare first-generation NSAIDs (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs) and coxibs for kidney risk.

✅ B · Kidney PGE2 is made by PTGS1/2, and the slide states NSAIDs and coxibs may both cause AKI in fragile patients.📖 S28, S29 (slides only, not yet lectured)

Q17In the eicosanoid functions table, which pairing of tissue and synthesizing enzyme is correct?

✅ C · Colon cancer PGE2 is COX-2; gastric mucosa is COX-1, airways 5-LOX, joints COX-2.📖 S7

Q18Which statement correctly contrasts prostaglandin-G/H synthase 1 (PTGS1) and prostaglandin-G/H synthase 2 (PTGS2)?

✅ A · PTGS1 is always expressed (gut, platelets); PTGS2 is induced by PAMPs/DAMPs to start inflammation.📖 S7; transcript

Q19How do glucocorticoids reduce phospholipase A2 (PLA2) activity, as described in lecture?

✅ D · Glucocorticoids activate transcription factors that raise annexin A1; annexin A1 binds and inhibits PLA2 (an indirect block).📖 S15; transcript

Q20A horse on a first-generation NSAID develops ulcers. Besides losing PGE2, what else promotes them? (NSAID = non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug; PGE2 = prostaglandin E2; 5-LOX = 5-lipoxygenase; LTB4 = leukotriene B4)

✅ B · With PTGS1 blocked, arachidonic acid all goes down the 5-LOX path; more LTB4 = more neutrophil, enzyme, and acid damage.📖 S21; transcript

Q21A man who had a heart attack is told to take low-dose aspirin daily. Which explanation matches the lecture? (COX = cyclooxygenase)

✅ C · Blocking COX-1 removes platelet TxA2, pushing away from clotting; blocking COX-2 would push toward clotting.📖 S26; transcript

Q22Which drug did the lecture name as the only approved coxib in veterinary medicine?

✅ B · Firocoxib is the only approved veterinary coxib and shows fewer cardiovascular effects than human coxibs.📖 S23; transcript

Q23A racehorse with osteoarthritis receives repeated glucocorticoid injections into the joint. What outcome did the lecture predict?

✅ D · Glucocorticoids relieve pain but are catabolic in peripheral tissue, including cartilage, so the joint degrades further.📖 S17; transcript

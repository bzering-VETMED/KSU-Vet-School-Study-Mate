Fick's law of diffusion⭐ emphasized

V̇gas = A/T × D × (P₁ − P₂) · D ∝ solubility ÷ √molecular weight

💬 More gas crosses with a BIG area, THIN wall, and BIG pressure difference. Every lung disease breaks one of these.

Example: CO₂: D ∝ 0.067/√44 ≈ 0.010 · O₂: D ∝ 0.003/√32 ≈ 0.0005 → CO₂ diffuses ~20× more easily, but its push is only 46 − 40 = 6 vs O₂ 100 − 40 = 60.

Dalton's law🔹 mentioned

Pgas = P_barometric × fraction of gas

💬 Each gas's pressure = total pressure × its share. At altitude the 21% stays the same; the total drops.

Example: Sea level: 760 × 0.21 = 159 mmHg. Altitude (P_B 240): 240 × 0.21 ≈ 50 mmHg.

Inspired PO₂ (in the trachea)⭐ emphasized

PIO₂ = (P_B − 47) × FIO₂

💬 Air gets humidified; water vapor (47 mmHg) takes its share first. This is your 159 → 149 step.

Example: (760 − 47) × 0.21 = 149 mmHg.

Alveolar gas equation⭐ emphasized

PAO₂ = PIO₂ − (PACO₂ ÷ R)

💬 Alveolar O₂ = what you breathe in, minus what CO₂ displaces. R = 0.8 at rest (more O₂ in than CO₂ out).

Example: 149 − (40 ÷ 0.8) = 149 − 50 = ~99–100 mmHg. Hypoventilation (PaCO₂ 80): 149 − 100 = 49.

Deck 3 slide 9 · deck 5 slide 10

Respiratory exchange ratio🔹 mentioned

R = V̇CO₂ ÷ V̇O₂

💬 CO₂ out ÷ O₂ in. ~0.8 at rest on a mixed diet → less CO₂ leaves than O₂ enters.

Example: 250 mL/min O₂ in, 200 mL/min CO₂ out → R = 0.8.

Deck 3 slide 10

Alveolar–arterial difference⭐ emphasized

A–a difference = PAO₂ (calculated) − PaO₂ (measured)

💬 How much worse the blood is than the alveolar air. Big gap = ventilation-perfusion mismatch (or shunt/diffusion). Pure hypoventilation = normal gap.

Example: Dog on room air: PaO₂ 49, PaCO₂ 48, R 0.8 → PAO₂ = 149 − 48/0.8 = 89 → 89 − 49 = 40 mmHg = severe mismatch.

Deck 5 slide 23

Bohr equation (dead space)⭐ emphasized

V_D ÷ V_T = (PaCO₂ − PECO₂) ÷ PaCO₂

💬 How much of each breath is wasted. Dead-space air has no CO₂, so it dilutes exhaled CO₂.

Example: (40 − 20) ÷ 40 = 0.5 → half of each breath is dead space.

Ventilation equations🔹 mentioned

V̇E = V_T × f · V_T = V_D + V_A · V̇E = (V_D × f) + (V_A × f)

💬 Total breathing = breath size × rate. Every breath pays the dead-space "tax" first.

Example: V̇E 8 L/min, V_D 0.15 L: slow (f 16, V_T 0.5) → V̇A = 16 × 0.35 = 5.6. Fast (f 32, V_T 0.25) → 32 × 0.10 = 3.2. Fast shallow wastes air.

Deck 2 slides 16–17

Alveolar ventilation equation⭐ emphasized

V̇A = (V̇CO₂ × 0.863) ÷ PaCO₂

💬 PaCO₂ is set by breathing vs CO₂ production. Breathe more → CO₂ down. Make more CO₂ → CO₂ up.

Example: Same V̇CO₂, double V̇A → PaCO₂ 40 → 20. Halve V̇A → 80.

Deck 2 slide 21 · Quiz 9

Red blood cell transit time⭐ emphasized

Transit time = pulmonary capillary blood volume ÷ cardiac output

💬 How long a red cell stays in the lung capillary to load O₂.

Example: Rest: 70 mL ÷ 83 mL/s = 0.84 s. Exercise: 140 mL ÷ 500 mL/s = 0.28 s. 🟠 Notes 3 rounds rest to ~0.75 s.

Deck 2 slide 7 · deck 3 slide 22

Fick principle🔹 mentioned

V̇O₂ = Q̇ × (CaO₂ − CvO₂)

💬 O₂ used = blood flow × how much O₂ each bit of blood drops off.

Example: Rest: 5,000 mL/min × (20 − 15)/100 = 250 mL/min. Exercise: 24,000 × (20 − 4)/100 = 3,840 mL/min.

Deck 4 slides 21–22 · deck 7 slide 32

Pulmonary vascular resistance⭐ emphasized

PVR = (mean pulmonary artery P − left atrial P) ÷ cardiac output

💬 Pressure drop across the lung ÷ flow. The lung moves the SAME flow with ~1/10 the pressure drop → very low resistance.

Example: Lung: (15 − 5) ÷ 5 L/min = 2. Body: (100 − 2) ÷ 5 = ~20 → lung ≈ 1/10.

Deck 4 slides 3–4

Henry's law⭐ emphasized

Dissolved gas = partial pressure × solubility (O₂ 0.003 · CO₂ 0.067 mL/100 mL/mmHg)

💬 Gas dissolves in proportion to its pressure. O₂ barely dissolves → we need hemoglobin.

Example: O₂ at 100 mmHg: 100 × 0.003 = 0.3 mL/100 mL. CO₂ at 46: 46 × 0.067 ≈ 3.1 (~20× more soluble).

Deck 3 slide 11 · your Quiz 9 Q13 miss

Boyle's law🔹 mentioned

P₁V₁ = P₂V₂ (constant temperature)

💬 Make the space bigger → pressure drops. That's how inspiration pulls air in.

Example: 2 L at 760 mmHg squeezed to 1 L → 1,520 mmHg (halve volume = double pressure).

Deck 3 slides 12–13

Law of Laplace⭐ emphasized

Alveolus: P = 2T ÷ r (soap bubble with 2 surfaces: 4T ÷ r)

💬 Small bubbles have higher collapsing pressure, so they empty into big ones. Surfactant lowers T to stop this.

Example: Halve the radius → pressure doubles → small alveolus would collapse into a big one without surfactant.

Notes 6 · deck 6 slide 22

Poiseuille's law (bonus)🔹 mentioned

R = 8ηl ÷ πr⁴ · V̇ = (P_atm − P_alv) ÷ R

💬 Resistance depends on radius to the 4th power. Narrow a tube a little → resistance goes up a LOT.

Example: Halve the radius → resistance × 2⁴ = 16×.

Deck 6 slides 30–31

Blood O₂ content⭐ emphasized

CaO₂ = (Hb × 1.34 × SaO₂) + (PO₂ × 0.003)

💬 O₂ on hemoglobin + the tiny dissolved bit. He likes to give a NEW hemoglobin value.

Example: Hb 15, 97%, PO₂ 100: 19.5 + 0.3 = 19.8 mL/100 mL. Hb 10: 10 × 1.34 × 0.97 = 13.0 + 0.3 = 13.3.

Deck 7 slide 15 · notes 7

Nitrogen washout (FRC)🔹 mentioned

FRC = volume of N₂ exhaled ÷ 0.79

💬 Breathe 100% O₂ until all N₂ (79% of lung air) is washed out; collect it.

Example: 5% × 40 L = 2 L N₂ → 2 ÷ 0.79 = ~2.5 L.

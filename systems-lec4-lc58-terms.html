★ Slide 19 — Basel: "know this slide very, very well"Collecting-duct filtrate is very HYPOtonic (NKCC + NCC) · medulla is very HYPERtonic (countercurrent + urea) · if water has a PATHWAY it moves out · shrink that difference = diuresis (S19, S32)
★ Why collecting-duct filtrate is hypotonicNKCC (thick ascending limb) and NCC (distal convoluted tubule) pull out salt where water cannot follow (S19)
★ Why the medulla is hypertonicCountercurrent multiplication (NaCl) + urea concentration (S19)
Filtrate osmolarity along the nephron (S19)Bowman's 300 · proximal tubule 300 · loop tip ~1000 · end of thick ascending ~200 · distal tubule/early collecting duct ~100
NCCNa⁺/Cl⁻ cotransporter in the DISTAL convoluted tubule (S19)
Where ADH is made vs releasedMade in the HYPOTHALAMUS (magnocellular neurons) · released from the POSTERIOR pituitary (S20)
ADH = AVPAntidiuretic hormone = arginine vasopressin
★ OsmoreceptorsMagnocellular neurons with many aquaporins → water moves in/out easily → cell size tracks ECF osmolarity (S20)
★ High ECF osmolarity → ADH (steps)1 Water leaves the neuron · 2 neuron shrinks · 3 stretch-inactivated TRPV1 channels depolarize it · 4 more action potentials · 5 ADH released (S20)
★ Main control of ADH releaseSystemic OSMOLARITY · high = ↑ ADH · low = ↓ ADH (S20)
ADH half-life~20 minutes
★ ADH in the collecting ductInserts aquaporins (AQP2) + urea transporters on the LUMINAL side → water reabsorbed + urea recycling ↑ (S22)
AVPR2 (V2) vs AVPR1 (V1)V2 = collecting duct, water retention · V1 = vessels, constriction (S29–30)
★ ADH changes what in urine?WATER excreted, not solute excreted (S23)
High ADH → urine?Small volume, very CONCENTRATED = antidiuresis (S23)
Low ADH → urine?Large volume, very DILUTE = water diuresis (S23)
★ Diabetes insipidus (DI)Loss of ADH signaling · "insipidus" = tasteless · persistent polyuria/polydipsia (PU/PD) (S24)
★ DI lab pictureLOW urine specific gravity (near 1.000) · HIGH plasma osmolarity · urine won't concentrate with water deprivation (S24)
Urine specific gravity (USG)Density of urine vs water (water = 1.000) · higher = more concentrated · normal roughly 1.030–1.060 (🔹 ranges later)
Water deprivation testPsychogenic overdrinking → USG RISES · true DI → USG stays LOW
★ Central DIHypothalamus/posterior pituitary fails to make or secrete ADH (S25)
★ Nephrogenic DICollecting duct fails to RESPOND to ADH (S25)
★ Desmopressin response testCentral DI → urine concentrates (USG ↑) · nephrogenic DI → no change (S25)
DesmopressinADH analog · much longer half-life (about twice-daily dosing) · targets kidney more than blood pressure (S25)
Central DI treatmentDesmopressin (replaces missing ADH) (S25)
Nephrogenic DI treatmentHarder · thiazide diuretic (mild hypovolemia → less urine) · lots of water · low-sodium/low-protein diet (S25)
★ Two reasons to reabsorb water (S26)Lower OSMOLARITY (water without solute) vs raise VOLUME (water + Na⁺)
★ ADH alone regulates…OSMOLARITY (water without much solute) (S26)
★ RAAS / aldosterone regulates…VOLUME (Na⁺ + water) (S26–29)
Aldosterone in the collecting duct↑ number + open probability of ENaC in principal cells → Na⁺ reabsorbed, K⁺ lost (via ROMK) (S28)
★ Angiotensin II and ADHAngiotensin II also stimulates ADH release → aldosterone (↑ Na⁺) + ADH (↑ water) → ↑ ECF/blood volume (S29)
ANP and volumeInhibits renin/RAAS, aldosterone, AND ADH → less Na⁺ and water retained (S30)
★ How diuretics work (general rule)RAISE filtrate osmolarity (often also lower medullary osmolarity) → less pull on water → more urine (S32)
Carbonic anhydrase inhibitorsAcetazolamide · block bicarbonate reabsorption in the proximal tubule → ↑ HCO₃⁻ in filtrate → ↑ osmolarity · rarely used (acid-base effects) (S33–34)
★ Loop diureticsFurosemide · block NKCC in the thick ascending limb → ↑ Na⁺ in the collecting duct AND wash out the medullary gradient ("double whammy") → STRONGEST (S35–36)
★ Thiazide diureticsBlock NCC in the distal tubule → ↑ Na⁺ in the collecting duct · medullary gradient mostly intact → WEAKER (S37–38)
★ Why loop + thiazide diuretics waste K⁺NOT a direct effect · more Na⁺ reaches the collecting duct → more Na⁺ through ENaC → more K⁺ secreted through ROMK (S36, S38)
ROMKRenal outer medullary K⁺ channel · K⁺ secretion in the collecting duct (S36)
Long-term loop/thiazide patientsRecheck bloodwork for a slowly falling K⁺
★ Potassium-sparing diureticsAmiloride = blocks ENaC directly · spironolactone = blocks the mineralocorticoid (aldosterone) receptor → less ENaC → no K⁺ loss via ROMK (S39–40)
★ ENaC rule for K⁺↑ ENaC activity = K⁺-WASTING · ↓ ENaC activity = K⁺-SPARING (S40)

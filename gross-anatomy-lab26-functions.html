⚠️ Precision traps

  • Supply ≠ course: the transverse branch of the medial circumflex femoral supplies the adductor but ENDS in the semimembranosus.
  • Lateral circumflex femoral = biggest single supply to the coxal (hip) joint capsule — not the medial circumflex femoral.
  • Main blood to the pes = cranial tibial → dorsal pedal → dorsal metatarsal artery II → perforating branch → deep plantar arch → plantar metatarsals. The saphenous branches are collateral routes.
  • Cranial gluteal NERVE supplies middle + deep gluteals AND continues to the tensor fasciae latae.
⚠️ Source disagreement · femoral branch order · Slide 26 lists: superficial circumflex "femoral" (typo for iliac), proximal caudal femoral, saphenous, descending genicular, middle caudal femoral, distal caudal femoral (no lateral circumflex femoral). Slide 50: descending genicular arises DISTAL to the saphenous. The video names the descending genicular before the saphenous. Learn the slide order; confirm in lab.
⚠️ Source disagreement · lateral saphenous venipuncture · Slides 44–48 + video: the CRANIAL BRANCH is the most common site. Lab 26 notes #8: the straight section PROXIMAL to where the cranial + caudal branches join is best for larger needles or fluid volume (it may contain valves).
⚠️ Variation · dorsal perineal artery · Not in the lab-guide figure. Size is inversely related to the ventral perineal; it usually comes off the caudal gluteal just distal to the lateral caudal artery and can reinforce or replace the caudal rectal artery (Lab 26 notes #2).

🔴 Gluteal region

Internal pudendal arteryPelvic viscera (smaller terminal branch of the internal iliac)
Caudal gluteal arteryLarger terminal branch of the internal iliac → gluteal region + caudal thigh; gives the iliolumbar + cranial gluteal arteries
Iliolumbar arteryDeep surface of the cranial end of the middle gluteal muscle (ilium region)
Cranial gluteal arteryMiddle + deep gluteal muscles
Cranial gluteal nerveMotor → middle gluteal, deep gluteal + tensor fasciae latae
Umbilical arteryRuns in the lateral ligament of the bladder

🟣 Deep femoral system

Deep femoral arteryOnly branch of the external iliac → pudendoepigastric trunk, then continues as the medial circumflex femoral
Pudendoepigastric trunkShort common trunk → external pudendal + caudal epigastric arteries
External pudendal arteryLeaves the abdomen via the inguinal canal (superficial inguinal ring) → inguinal region
Caudal epigastric arteryCaudal half of the rectus abdominis + central parts of the oblique + transverse abdominal muscles
Medial circumflex femoral arteryAdductor region, medial + caudal thigh muscles (via deep + transverse branches)
Deep branch (medial circumflex femoral)Descends between the adductor + vastus medialis → deep thigh muscles
Transverse branch (medial circumflex femoral)Supplies the adductor; terminates in the semimembranosus

🟢 Femoral artery branches

Femoral arteryMain arterial supply of the pelvic limb; pulse palpated in the femoral triangle
Femoral triangleClinical: palpation site for the femoral pulse
Superficial circumflex iliac arterySartorius, tensor fasciae latae, rectus femoris
Lateral circumflex femoral arteryQuadriceps (between rectus femoris + vastus medialis) + more blood to the coxal joint capsule than any other single vessel
Proximal caudal femoral arteryCaudal thigh muscles (mid-thigh)
Saphenous arterySkin of the medial stifle; continues as cranial + caudal branches to the paw
Cranial branch (saphenous artery)Medial crus → flexor surface of the tarsus → dorsal common digital arteries
Caudal branch (saphenous artery)Through the tarsal tunnel → deep branches → plantar surface of the paw (plantar common digital arteries)
Descending genicular arteryMedial surface of the stifle
Middle caudal femoral arterySemimembranosus + caudal thigh muscles
Distal caudal femoral arteryLarge vessel → gastrocnemius + caudal thigh/stifle region

🟠 Popliteal + crus

Popliteal arteryContinuation of the femoral after the distal caudal femoral → stifle → splits into cranial + caudal tibial
Caudal tibial arteryTiny branches into the popliteus (rarely found)
Cranial tibial arteryCraniolateral crus muscles → continues as the dorsal pedal artery = MAIN supply to the pes

🔵 Veins + nerves

Medial saphenous veinSatellites the saphenous artery; venipuncture site in CATS
Lateral saphenous veinNo satellite artery; cranial branch = most common venipuncture site in DOGS
Saphenous nerveSensory (branch of the femoral nerve) to the skin of the medial thigh, stifle, crus + paw
Caudal cutaneous sural nerveNerve-biopsy site near the lateral saphenous vein — mixed fibers, partial plantar motor → no obvious deficit
Deep branch (common peroneal nerve)Runs with the cranial tibial artery under the cranial tibial + long digital extensor muscles

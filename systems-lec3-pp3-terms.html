3 processes for gas exchangeVentilation → diffusion → blood flow
Active transport of O₂/CO₂?NEVER · purely passive diffusion (fish swim bladder claim disproven)
★ Gases move down their…PRESSURE gradient, not concentration (saline vs olive oil example)
Oxygen cascadePO₂ falls step by step: atmosphere → alveoli → arterial blood → tissue → mitochondria (~1–5 mmHg)
Dalton's lawPartial pressure = total (barometric) pressure × fraction of the gas
PO₂ of dry air at sea level760 × 0.21 ≈ 159 mmHg
At altitude, what changes?Barometric pressure falls · O₂ FRACTION stays ~21%
Everest summit barometric pressure~1/3 of sea level → PO₂ ~1/3
Water vapor pressure at body temp47 mmHg
Inspired (tracheal) PO₂(760 − 47) × 0.21 ≈ 149 mmHg
★ Alveolar gas equationAlveolar PO₂ = inspired PO₂ − (PaCO₂ ÷ R) = 149 − (40 ÷ 0.8) ≈ 100 mmHg
★ Why alveolar PO₂ < atmospheric PO₂Air is warmed + moistened (water vapor), diluted by alveolar CO₂, and R < 1
Respiratory exchange ratio (R)CO₂ output ÷ O₂ uptake · ~0.8 at rest (mixed diet) · up to ~1.3 in exercise
Henry's lawDissolved gas concentration = partial pressure × solubility
O₂ solubility0.003 mL/100 mL/mmHg → very low (why we need hemoglobin)
CO₂ solubility0.067 mL/100 mL/mmHg → ~20–22× more soluble than O₂
CO₂ stored in the body~20 L (can be blown off by hyperventilating)
Boyle's lawP₁V₁ = P₂V₂ (constant temperature) · halve volume → double pressure
Boyle's law in inhalationDiaphragm contracts → chest volume ↑ → alveolar pressure falls below atmospheric → air flows in
Boyle's law in exhalationDiaphragm relaxes → lungs recoil → alveolar pressure above atmospheric → air flows out (passive)
Only active muscle in quiet breathingDiaphragm
★ Fick's lawGas flow = (Area ÷ Thickness) × D × (P₁ − P₂)
D (diffusion constant) ∝ …Solubility ÷ √molecular weight
Pressure gradient for O₂ across barrierAlveolar 100 − venous 40 = ~60 mmHg
Pressure gradient for CO₂ across barrierVenous 46 − alveolar 40 = ~6 mmHg (~1/10 of O₂'s)
★ Normal pressuresAlveolar: O₂ 100, CO₂ 40 · Venous: O₂ 40, CO₂ 46 · Arterial: O₂ 100, CO₂ 40
Venous blood saturation at PO₂ 40~75% (lost a lot of pressure, not much content)
EquilibrationBlood leaving a healthy lung has the same gas pressures as alveolar gas
★ Time to equilibrate (healthy lung)~0.25 s, out of ~0.75 s transit → big reserve
Diffusion limitationThick barrier or less area → blood doesn't reach alveolar PO₂ → hypoxemia
Alveolar proteinosis / silicosisThickens blood-gas barrier → diffusion limitation; macrophages won't eat silica
★ Dog with low diffusing capacity + exerciseCardiac output ↑ → transit time ↓ → hypoxemia WORSENS → exercise intolerance
DyspneaDiscomfort with breathing
★ Mild lung disease: O₂ vs CO₂Hypoxemia FIRST; CO₂ stays normal (chemoreceptors ↑ breathing to defend PaCO₂ 40)
Which variable is regulated?Arterial PCO₂ (40) · not PO₂
Racehorse during a runCardiac output ↑ ~10× → transit too fast → hypoxemic + slightly hypercapnic (blue-black arterial blood)
Horse spleen~14 kg, contracts in exercise → "turbocharger" ↑ O₂ content
★ At altitude, O₂ becomes…Diffusion-limited → arterial saturation falls as exercise ↑ cardiac output
★ Carbon monoxide test (tested on Quiz 9!)Measures lung DIFFUSING CAPACITY · CO binds hemoglobin so tightly that blood PCO ≈ 0
Nitrous oxide testPerfusion-limited → measures pulmonary BLOOD FLOW

⚠️ Precision traps

  • Gases follow PRESSURE, not concentration gradients
  • Dalton: altitude lowers PRESSURE, not the 21% O₂ fraction
  • O₂ gradient ~60 mmHg vs CO₂ only ~6 mmHg (CO₂ is ~20× more soluble)
  • Mild disease → hypoxemia first; PaCO₂ is defended by breathing
  • 🟠 Sources disagree: Poole (lecture) said CO₂ equilibrates easily · his notes say CO₂ takes about the SAME time as O₂ (gradient is 1/10), so disease can impair both → know both points

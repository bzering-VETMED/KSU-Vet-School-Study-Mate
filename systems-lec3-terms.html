LC37 · Respiratory System 1 (Basel) · upper airways & pleura

Upper airwaysNares, nasal cavity, pharynx
Lower airwaysLarynx → trachea → bronchial tree → alveolar sacs
Mammal lung vs bird lungMammal = blind-ended, mixes air (alveolar PO₂ ~100 vs air ~150) · Bird = one-way flow through lung via air sacs → more efficient
Reptile breathing (3 ways)Diaphragm (crocodilians) · chest-wall muscles · buccal pumping (small lizards)
Amphibian gas exchangeMostly through thin moist skin; some lungs or gills
★ Obligate nasal breatherHorse; epiglottis position. Nares collapse at high flow → more negative pressure → contributes to EIPH (exercise-induced pulmonary hemorrhage)
Elephant pleuraNo pleural space (connective tissue instead) · helps snorkeling
Marine mammal lungsCollapsible; water pressure on dives raises lung O₂ pressure
3 tunics of airwayTunica mucosa → tunica submucosa → tunica adventitia (or serosa)
Tunica mucosa = 3 partsEpithelium + lamina propria + muscularis mucosa
Tunica muscularis in airways?No (it appears in the gut)
Adventitia vs serosaAdventitia blends into surroundings · serosa is bounded by a serosal membrane (in a body cavity)
Respiratory epitheliumPseudostratified columnar with cilia + goblet cells
Nasal conchaeThin bones, huge surface area → warm, filter, and especially MOISTEN air
Olfactory epitheliumThicker; contains olfactory sensory neurons
Olfactory (Bowman's) glandsMucus dissolves odorants, then washes them away
LarynxFirst portion 100% devoted to the airway
False vocal foldRespiratory epithelium + glands; does not vibrate
True vocal foldStratified squamous non-keratinized epithelium + muscle (vibrates)
Tracheal cartilageC-shaped rings in the adventitia → prevent collapse during inhalation
Trachealis muscleCloses back of the rings; relaxes so the esophagus can expand when swallowing
Diffuse neuroendocrine cellsRelease serotonin; dysregulation → asthma (not visible on H&E)
Visceral vs parietal pleuraVisceral = on the lung · parietal = on the chest wall
Pleural space pressureNegative → holds lungs open; more negative on inhalation
★ PneumothoraxAir in pleural space → negative pressure lost → lung collapses
Spontaneous pneumothoraxRuptured blebs/bullae on lung surface (case: Siberian husky)

LC38 · Respiratory System 2 (Basel) · lower airways & alveoli

Conducting airwaysBronchi + bronchioles · thick walls · NO gas exchange = dead space
Exchange airwaysStart at respiratory bronchioles → alveolar ducts → alveolar sacs
Total cross-sectional area going deeperIncreases → resistance drops + air slows
Highest resistanceTrachea + early bronchi
★ Poiseuille's lawResistance ∝ 1 / radius⁴ (small constriction → huge resistance)
2 types of breathing workResistive (airway diameter) + elastic (stretching lungs)
Bronchus IDCartilage PLATES in the adventitia + glands + pseudostratified epithelium
Bronchiole IDNO cartilage · thick smooth muscle · simple cuboidal/columnar epithelium
What replaces cartilage in bronchioles?Smooth muscle (muscularis mucosa)
Club cellsBronchioles · break down mucus, detoxify, start making surfactant
Surface tensionWater shrinks its surface (entropy) → pulls lungs closed
SurfactantSurface-acting agent → lowers surface tension → less elastic work
Respiratory bronchiole IDBronchiole wall with a FEW alveoli opening off the side
Alveolar duct IDWall is all alveoli · septa end in smooth-muscle KNOBS
Alveolar sac IDBlind end · septa end in sharp POINTS
Type I pneumocyteSquamous · lines alveoli · forms the blood-air barrier
Type II pneumocyteCuboidal, at corners · secretes surfactant + stem cell for Type I
Alveolar macrophagePhagocytic; keeps alveoli clean (in lumen or wall)
★ Blood-air barrier = 3 layersType I pneumocyte → fused basement membrane → capillary endothelial cell
Not part of the barrierType II pneumocytes + macrophages
Why pulmonary pressure stays lowBarrier is so thin, high pressure ruptures capillaries (EIPH)
AsthmaOverreactive airways → bronchoconstriction + excess mucus → ↑ resistive work
Asthma x-rayBright branching pattern (bronchial inflammation/fluid)
AlbuterolBeta-2 agonist (mimics sympathetic) → bronchodilation; rescue inhaler
Long-term asthma treatmentCorticosteroids (reduce inflammation)

Pulmonary Physiology 1 (Poole) · structure

★ What does breathing regulate?Arterial PCO₂ (~40 mmHg) and pH
Oxygen uptake equation(Inspired ventilation × inspired O₂ fraction) − (expired ventilation × expired O₂ fraction)
% of inspired O₂ the lung extracts~25%, even during exercise (defends alveolar PO₂; horse = exception)
Body size vs resting O₂ use per kgSmaller animal → higher → more risk under anesthesia
Hyperventilation effect↓ CO₂ → ↑ pH → cerebral arterioles constrict → ↓ brain blood flow
Lungs vs kidneys for acid–baseLungs: fast, ~10,000 mmol CO₂/day · kidneys: low capacity, days–weeks
★ Fick's lawGas flow = Area / Thickness × Diffusing capacity × (P alveolar − P blood)
Gases move down their…PRESSURE gradient (not concentration)
Blood-gas barrier thickness~0.3 µm (human)
Number of alveoli (human)~300 million
Unicameral lungOne big sac (tegu lizard) → tiny surface area, low capacity
Bird lung exchangeUnidirectional, cross-current; no mixing → blood PO₂ near atmospheric (bar-headed geese over Everest)
Mammal lung = ?Mixing-pool (uniform pool) exchanger
Intrapleural space~100 µm of fluid; lubricates lung sliding; lungs have no muscles of their own
Particle >5 µmImpacts in large airways
Particle 1–5 µmSediments in small airways
Particle ~0.1 µmReaches alveoli by diffusion (e.g., asbestos)
Mucociliary escalatorGoblet-cell mucus + cilia move particles toward mouth; only in non-exchange airways
Small airway diameter depends on…Alveolar tethering → varies with lung volume
Pores of KohnHoles in alveolar walls; Poole: macrophages climb through
Type I cell (Poole)~90% of alveolar surface; metabolically inert
Surfactant moleculeDipalmitoyl phosphatidylcholine (lecithin), from Type II cells
Macrophages won't eat…Silica + asbestos
Collateral ventilationDog/cat = high · horse = intermediate · pig/cattle = none
Conducting zone volume (human)~150 mL vs respiratory zone 2–5 L
Greatest total cross-sectional areaRespiratory zone (gas moves mostly by diffusion)
★ Obstruction in large airway vs parenchymaLarge airway = noticed early (small total area) · deep lung = silent for long
% of airway resistance in large airways~70%

Pulmonary Physiology 2 (Poole) · volumes & ventilation

3 steps to get O₂ into bloodVentilation → diffusion → blood flow
Tidal volumeAir per breath (~500 mL human; horse ~7.5 L)
★ Red blood cell transit timePulmonary capillary blood volume ÷ cardiac output (~0.84 s rest → ~0.28 s exercise)
Why transit time falls in exerciseCardiac output ↑ 6–10× but capillary volume only ~2×
Exercise-induced hypoxemiaRacehorses, greyhounds, elite humans (red cells move too fast)
★ Functional residual capacityEnd of normal exhale; NO muscles acting; intrapleural ≈ −5 cm H₂O
Total lung capacityMaximum air after deepest breath in
Residual volumeAir left after maximal exhale (never 0)
Vital capacityTotal lung capacity − residual volume
Can a spirometer measure residual volume?No → use nitrogen washout, helium dilution, or plethysmography
Nitrogen washoutBreathe 100% O₂, collect N₂ → N₂ volume ÷ 0.79 = lung volume
Exhalation at restPassive
EmphysemaObstructive; airways collapse (crackles); ↑ functional residual capacity + residual volume
Ventilation equationTotal ventilation = dead space ventilation + alveolar ventilation
★ Deep-slow vs rapid-shallowDeep, slow = more alveolar ventilation · rapid, shallow wastes air in dead space
Panting dogShallow breaths for heat loss, NOT hyperventilation
Dead space / tidal volume ratioHuman ~0.3 · horse/cattle 0.5–0.75 · falls with exercise
★ Bohr equationDead space / tidal volume = (PaCO₂ − expired PCO₂) ÷ PaCO₂
Cow: PaCO₂ 40, expired PCO₂ 20Dead space / tidal volume = 0.5
Anatomic vs physiologic dead spaceAnatomic = conducting zone · physiologic adds ventilation-perfusion mismatch
★ Alveolar ventilation equationAlveolar ventilation = (CO₂ output ÷ PaCO₂) × 863
Alveolar ventilation doubles, CO₂ output unchangedPaCO₂ halves
Ventilation-perfusion mismatchAlways bad → hypoxemia; severe → hypercapnia too
★ Which region gets most ventilation?BASE (gravity)
Apex alveoliStretched, stiff → expand less
Base alveoliSmall, compliant → fill easily
ComplianceVolume change ÷ pressure change (lung ~10× a balloon)
Diseased lungs + exerciseRespiratory muscles steal cardiac output from limbs → exercise intolerance

⚠️ Precision traps

  • Adventitia ≠ serosa · trachea switches to serosa once inside the thorax
  • False vocal fold = respiratory epithelium · TRUE vocal fold = stratified squamous
  • Bronchus = cartilage PLATES · trachea = C-shaped RINGS · bronchiole = NONE
  • Alveolar duct septa = KNOBS · alveolar sac septa = POINTS
  • Type II pneumocyte is NOT part of the blood-air barrier
  • Residual volume vs functional residual capacity (no muscles acting = functional residual capacity)
  • Total cross-sectional area is SMALLEST in large airways (individual airways are biggest)
  • Basel: premature surfactant deficit before ~36 weeks · Poole: ~34 weeks → use Poole for his questions
  • Basel said bird lung "CO₂ ~150" → he meant O₂

⚠️ Precision traps

  • ★ G0 ≠ exited: G0 (quiescent) cells can be reactivated with the right signals; terminally non-dividing (exited) cells normally cannot (transcript, S5).
  • ★ G1/S = divide or die: once DNA is duplicated the cell can't go back to resting. G1/S is "probably the most important decision point" (transcript).
  • CDK activation needs ALL three: bind cyclin + ADD a phosphate + REMOVE a phosphate. Not add OR remove (S9, transcript).
  • SOS is a GEF (guanine nucleotide exchange factor): GDP out, GTP in on Ras. Ras is a monomeric GTPase (not heterotrimeric) (transcript, S11).
  • Order: EGF → RTK dimerize + cross-phosphorylate → adapter → SOS → Ras → MAPKKK → MAPKK → MAPK → nucleus → jun/myc/fos → G1 cyclin, E2F, G1-CDK. Don't put Ras before SOS (S11).
  • G1 = 1 set of chromosomes, G2 = 2 sets. Interphase = G1 + S + G2 = most of the cycle (S5–S6).
  • Nuclear envelope dissolves in prometaphase, reforms in telophase. Prophase = condense (S6, transcript).
  • G1/S checks growth signals; G2/M checks DNA replication quality (breaks, chromosome number); M checks spindle attachment before anaphase. Size checked at both G1/S and G2/M (S8, transcript).
  • EGF: slide and Basel say "epithelial"; standard = epidermal (OUTSIDE). Accept the slide wording.
  • ★ pRb binds E2F (not p53). Phosphorylating pRb RELEASES E2F = go. p53 acts through p21, which inhibits CDK (S15, transcript).
  • "Checkpoint permanently on" (S16) = E2F always free → always into S phase. NOT permanent arrest (S18, transcript).
  • ★ AKT = removing the BRAKES (blocks apoptosis + cell cycle arrest); Ras/MAPK = putting on the GAS. Don't flip them. EGF does both (S11–S12, transcript).
  • ★★ Wnt ON → beta-catenin NOT phosphorylated → nucleus → cyclin D. Wnt OFF → Axin/GSK3/APC phosphorylates beta-catenin → proteasome (S13, transcript).
  • Dishevelled binds Axin/GSK3 more strongly than APC. APC is a kinase only when bound to Axin/GSK3 (transcript).
  • CDK phosphorylates its OWN cyclin → proteasome → sharp drop. Phosphorylation here = degradation, not activation (S14, transcript).
  • Cyclin peaks (Basel): E = G1/S, A = G2/M, B = M (metaphase); D = master (up to start, down at end). S14 figure puts A near S/G2 — use transcript (S14).
  • PDPK1 = PIP3-dependent protein kinase 1; AKT = protein kinase B (not A, C, or G) (transcript).
  • pRb mutation drives many, not all, canine osteosarcomas (transcript).

Cell cycleS4–S5; transcriptGap 1 (G1) → Synthesis (S) → Gap 2 (G2) → Mitosis (M) + cytokinesis; mitosis is only a small, final part.
Why regulate divisiontranscriptToo few cells → degenerative disease; too many → cancer; "both end up at the same place" (death).
G1 (Gap 1)S5; transcriptRecently divided cell grows and builds nutrient surplus; 1 set of chromosomes.
S phase (Synthesis)S5; transcriptDNA synthesis: DNA polymerase copies DNA, 1 copy → 2 copies.
G2 (Gap 2)S5; transcriptChecks DNA synthesis went well + enough nutrients/size before division; 2 sets of chromosomes.
M phase (Mitosis)S5; transcriptSeparates sister chromatids to opposite sides, then cytokinesis splits the cell.
G0 (Gap 0)S5; transcriptQuiescence: stable, not actively dividing, BUT can be reactivated to divide with the right signals.
Exited (terminally non-dividing) cellsS5; transcriptMature cells that have turned off cell cycling; NOT G0 — normally no signal can make them divide; replaced by other cells when they die.
InterphaseS6; transcript★ G1 + S + G2 — "between the steps of mitosis"; most of the cell cycle.
ProphaseS6; transcriptChromosomes condense (slide also: centrosomes duplicate).
PrometaphaseS6; transcriptNuclear envelope dissolves; centrosomes move to opposite sides (poles).
MetaphaseS6; transcriptSister chromatids line up on the metaphase plate.
Metaphase plateS6; transcriptMidline of the cell where chromosomes align during metaphase.
AnaphaseS6; transcriptDaughter chromosomes (chromatids) pulled/pushed toward each side.
TelophaseS6; transcriptNuclear membranes reform on both sides; chromatids on opposite sides.
CytokinesisS6; transcriptContractile ring pinches the membrane → two daughter cells; microtubules (MT) form interphase array.
Contractile ringS6Ring that pinches the cell in two during cytokinesis.
CentrosomeS6; transcriptOrganizes mitosis; moves to opposite sides in prometaphase (slide: duplicates in prophase).
CheckpointS7–S8; transcriptDecision point where the cycle pauses and asks questions; three: G1/S, G2/M, M.
G1/S checkpointS8; transcript★ "Probably the most important decision point": checks cell size, environmental growth signals, intact DNA. Proteins: pRb, p53, E2F, AKT.
Divide or dietranscript★ Once past G1/S and DNA is duplicated, the cell must DIVIDE OR DIE — it can't return to resting.
G2/M checkpointS8; transcriptChecks replication quality (no breaks, right chromosome number) + enough nutrients and size for mitosis.
M checkpointS8; transcriptChecks spindle attachment and chromosomes on the metaphase plate BEFORE anaphase.
Cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK)S7, S9; transcriptKinase that needs a cyclin; integrates multiple signals = trigger switch for moving through a checkpoint.
CDK activation requirementsS9; transcript★ 3 inputs: cyclin bound + phosphate REMOVED at one site + phosphate ADDED at another. Missing any one = inactive.
PhosphataseS9Enzyme that removes a phosphate; required step to activate CDK.
CyclinS7, S9; transcriptProtein CDK depends on; rising cyclin concentration is one activating input; 'time-keeper' of the cycle.
Growth signalsS7, S10; transcriptRequired inputs (usually more than one) for passing G1/S and starting the cycle; say "go into S phase."
Growth factorsS10; transcriptUsually signal through receptor tyrosine kinases (Dr. Schultz): EGF, IGF-1, PDGF, EPO.
EGF (epithelial growth factor)S10–S11; transcriptSlide AND Basel say "epithelial" (standard: epidermal). Tyrosine kinase receptor ligand → SOS → Ras → MAPK.
IGF-1 (insulin-like growth factor 1)S10Growth factor listed as a CDK input.
PDGF (platelet derived growth factor)S10Growth factor listed as a CDK input.
EPO (erythropoietin)S10Growth factor listed as a CDK input.
Steroid hormones as growth signalsS10; transcriptSteroid hormone receptors can act as growth factors for certain tissues.
EstrogenS10Steroid hormone; signal for female reproductive cell growth.
TestosteroneS10Steroid hormone; signal for male reproductive cell growth.
Receptor tyrosine kinase (RTK) activationS11; transcriptLigand binds → receptor DIMERIZES → CROSS-PHOSPHORYLATES → recruits adapter proteins.
EGFR (epidermal growth factor receptor)S11; transcriptTyrosine kinase receptor for EGF; dimerizes and cross-phosphorylates (P P) → adapter → SOS.
SOS (son of sevenless)S11; transcriptGuanine nucleotide exchange factor (GEF) for Ras: kicks out GDP so GTP binds → active Ras. Named for the Drosophila 7th-eye mutant.
RasS11; transcriptMonomeric GTPase: inactive with GDP, active with GTP → activates MAPKKK.
MAPKKK → MAPKK → MAPKS11; transcriptKinase cascade: each phosphorylates the next; MAPK goes to the nucleus.
MitogentranscriptSomething that makes a cell go into mitosis rapidly; MAP kinase = mitogen-activated protein kinase.
MAPK (mitogen-activated protein kinase)S11; transcriptEnters nucleus; acts as or phosphorylates transcription factors → jun, myc, fos → G1/S progression.
Myc / Jun / FosS11; transcriptTranscription factors switched on via MAPK; raise G1 cyclin, E2F, and G1-CDK.
G1 cyclinS11; transcriptCyclin that binds the G1/S cyclin-dependent kinase; increased by jun/myc/fos.
G1-CDKS11; transcriptThe CDK that must be activated to pass the G1/S checkpoint; increased by jun/myc/fos.
E2FS11, S15; transcript★ Transcription factor for S-phase genes (DNA polymerase, helicase, topoisomerase); held by pRb. Freeing E2F = "final decision point."
G1-CDK–cyclinS15; transcriptActivated by cyclin D + cyclin E + dephosphorylation + phosphorylation; integrates Wnt and growth factor inputs; main target = pRb.
PI3-K (phosphoinositide 3-kinase)S12; transcriptSecond arm off the receptor tyrosine kinase (Schultz plug-and-play): recruited to EGFR, converts PIP2 → PIP3.
PIP2 → PIP3S12; transcriptPhosphatidylinositol bisphosphate → trisphosphate (made by PI3-K); rising PIP3 activates PDPK1.
PDPK1 (PIP3-dependent protein kinase 1)S12; transcript"Tells you exactly what it does": active when PIP3 rises; activates AKT.
AKT (protein kinase B, PKB)S12; transcript★ Historical name protein kinase B. Blocks apoptosis + prevents cell cycle arrest = "removing the brakes."
Brakes vs gasS11–S12; transcript★ AKT arm = REMOVES THE BRAKES (blocks apoptosis/arrest); Ras/MAPK arm = PUTS ON THE GAS. EGF does both.
WntS13; transcript★★ Growth factor ("wingless," Drosophila naming), highly conserved; binds Frizzled + LRP. "Super important pathway. Expect me to ask questions about it."
FrizzledS13; transcript★ Wnt receptor (mutant flies look "frizzled"); with co-receptor LRP recruits Dishevelled.
LRPS13; transcriptCo-receptor (cofactor) recruited with Frizzled when Wnt binds. Expansion not given (OUTSIDE: low-density lipoprotein receptor-related protein — confirm).
Dishevelled (DVL)S13; transcript★ Recruited to Frizzled/LRP; binds Axin/GSK3 MORE STRONGLY than APC does → pulls APC off the complex.
Axin / GSK3 / APC complexS13; transcript★ A kinase ONLY when all bound together; phosphorylates beta-catenin → proteasome. APC alone is not a kinase.
ProteasomeS13–S14; transcriptDegrades phosphorylated beta-catenin (and phosphorylated cyclins).
Beta-catenin (β-catenin)S13; transcript★ Spared when Wnt is on → enters nucleus as transcription factor (with LEF/TCF) → cyclin D → push through G1/S.
Beta-catenin: adherens junction roletranscriptCatenins are also adapter proteins for adherens junctions (adhesion belts) — second job of beta-catenin.
LEF/TCFS13Transcription factor partners of beta-catenin on S13 (figure label; Basel did not name them).
Cyclins rise and fallS14; transcript★ Named because they cycle: go up, then sharply down, at set points in the cycle.
Cyclin DS14; transcriptGeneral/master regulator: rises to START the cycle, falls at the END; Wnt/beta-catenin output.
Cyclin ES14; transcriptGets really high right about the G1/S checkpoint.
Cyclin AS14; transcriptBasel: gets really high right about the G2/M checkpoint (S14 figure reads near S/G2 — see flag).
Cyclin BS14; transcriptThe M-checkpoint cyclin: rises through interphase, peaks at metaphase, then drops sharply.
Mitosis promoting factor (MPF)S14; transcriptCDK–cyclin B complex; activity rises exponentially, peaks at metaphase, then drops dramatically.
Cyclin 'clock' (prevents reversal)S14; transcript★ Active CDK phosphorylates its OWN cyclin → proteasome → sharp drop: "close the door behind us" so the cycle can't go back.
pRb (retinoblastoma protein)S15; transcript★ Critical control component: main target of G1-CDK; binds E2F ("not today"); phosphorylated pRb releases E2F.
Final decision pointS15; transcript★ Phosphorylate pRb or not: once pRb is phosphorylated and E2F released, the cell moves into S phase.
ATMS15; transcriptSenses many types of DNA damage; phosphorylates p53. Expansion not given (OUTSIDE: ataxia-telangiectasia mutated — confirm).
p53S15; transcript★ Critical control component; "guardian of the genome," strong tumor suppressor; phosphorylated by ATM → transcription factor → p21.
p21S15; transcriptMade by p53 (transcription); INHIBITS cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK) → no pRb phosphorylation.
RetinoblastomaS18; transcriptAggressive childhood eye tumor ("retina" + "blastoma"); most had pRb mutations → source of the name.
Mutated RbS18; transcript★ Cannot bind E2F → E2F always free → cell always moves into S phase regardless of other signals → tumor.
Constitutively activeS16, S18; transcriptAlways on, regardless of upstream signals (here: E2F); "permanently on" in S16 = this.
Canine osteosarcomaS18; transcriptpRb mutation = one of the main drivers in many (NOT all) canine osteosarcomas; ↑ osteoblast cell cycling.
pRb-mutant tumorstranscriptUsually faster-growing; whether aggressive depends on other mutations (student question).

OsteosarcomaS3, S17–S18; transcriptDiagnosis of the greyhound case. Etiology: likely pRb mutation → E2F always free → osteoblasts keep cycling.
Periosteal proliferationS3New bone reaction from the periosteum seen on the case radiograph, with palisading cortical bone.

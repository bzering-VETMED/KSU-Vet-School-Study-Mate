⚠️ Precision traps

  • Abdomen = END OF EXPIRATION · thorax = peak INSPIRATION (opposite!)
  • Left lateral (left side DOWN) → gas rises into the RIGHT-side pylorus + duodenum · right lateral → gas in the LEFT-side fundus
  • Barium/fluid does the OPPOSITE of gas (it sinks): right lateral → barium in pylorus/duodenum · left lateral → barium in fundus
  • VD (on its back): fundus is dorsal → fills with FLUID; body + pylorus fill with GAS · DV = the reverse
  • Caudoventral liver margin: DOG = LEFT lateral lobe · CAT = RIGHT lateral lobe
  • Kidney length on the VD: dog 2.5–3.5 × L2 · cat 2–3 × L2 (not L1, not L3)
  • LEFT kidney is more CAUDAL ("left is left behind") · RIGHT kidney sits in the renal fossa of the caudate lobe of the liver
  • Serosal detail comes from intra-abdominal FAT, not gas. Fluid/effusion LOWERS detail (border effacement)
  • Soft tissue and fluid are the SAME opacity
  • Spleen HEAD is fixed (gastrosplenic ligament); the TAIL is mobile

🌗 Opacities + technique

5 opacities, least → most opaqueGas → fat → soft tissue = fluid → mineral → metal
When do you expose an abdominal film?End of EXPIRATION (pause, least motion blur) · thorax is the opposite (peak inspiration)
Field boundaries of an abdominal filmDiaphragm → coxofemoral joints · pelvic limbs extended caudally · take a 2nd film if it does not fit
Standard views at K-StateLeft lateral + right lateral + ventrodorsal (VD) · minimum = 2 orthogonal views
Why both laterals?Different structures fill with gas on each side (left lateral → pylorus + duodenum highlighted)
Why orthogonal views?A 3-D animal is squashed into a 2-D shadow → need 2 views to localize

📸 Additional views

Compression viewWooden spoon presses the abdomen → moves bowel off a region → less superimposition (e.g. small kidney, mass)
Pneumogastrogram / pneumocolonogramAir into the stomach (orogastric tube) or colon (red rubber catheter) → outlines the organ, tells colon from small bowel
Perineal lateral (pelvic limbs pulled forward)Shows the region of the urethra for urinary stones (femurs + fabellae moved off it)
Horizontal beamBeam shot ACROSS the animal → detects free gas in the abdomen (surgical emergency) or thorax

🧭 Systematic read

Her reading orderExtra-abdominal → serosal detail + contour → large parenchymal organs (liver, spleen) → urogenital → GI tract LAST
Why the GI tract last?Most superimposition/most complex · and if you find the foreign body first you stop looking at everything else
Extra-abdominal structuresNon-abdominal soft tissues (nipples, prepuce, masses), subcutis, pelvic limb + lumbar muscles, skeleton, body wall, diaphragm, THORAX on the edge
Nipples / skin tags / prepuceLook like masses → confirm on the orthogonal view
Epaxial vs hypaxial on a filmEpaxial = DORSAL to the transverse processes · hypaxial = VENTRAL · they make confusing lines
Why trace the body wall?Look for herniation/disruption (trauma, bite wounds)

🫁 Diaphragm, contour + serosal detail

Which side of the diaphragm do you normally see?Thoracic side only (gas in the lungs); the abdominal side border-effaces with liver/stomach
When do you see the ABDOMINAL side?Lots of falciform fat (normal variant, cats) OR free peritoneal gas (pathologic unless post-surgery)
Diaphragm parts seenCupola (dome) + right and left crura
Abdominal contourBreed shape: greyhound = tucked up · English bulldog = pendulous · most dogs in between
What gives serosal detail in the abdomen?Intra-abdominal (visceral) FAT around organ margins
6 causes of poor serosal detailEmaciation · young age (brown fat = more water) · peritoneal effusion · serosal disease (peritonitis, carcinomatosis) · mass effect / organomegaly (e.g. pregnancy) · big patient / technique (scatter)

🫀 Liver + spleen

Liver bordersCranial: diaphragm · caudal: stomach, right kidney, small bowel · lateral: body wall · ventral: falciform fat
Easiest liver margin to seeCaudoventral margin · DOG = left lateral lobe · CAT = right lateral lobe
Gastric axisLine fundus → pylorus · normal = between perpendicular to the spine and parallel to the ribs
Normal caudal extent of the liverWithin or just past the costal arch · NOT out to the kidney poles (= too big)
Big liver vs small liver on filmBig: margins past the costal arch, rounded/lumpy, axis shifted caudally · small: axis displaced cranially
Spleen headLeft cranial abdomen, FIXED, triangle on VD / small fusiform shape on lateral (often seen in cats)
Spleen tailMobile · dog: usually seen ventrally on the lateral · cat: rarely (smaller spleen)
What attaches the spleen to the stomach?Gastrosplenic ligament (short gastric arteries run in it) · NOT the greater omentum

🫘 Urogenital + retroperitoneum

Kidney positionCranial + dorsal, retroperitoneal · LEFT more caudal + more mobile ("left is left behind")
Right kidney hard to see because…Superimposed liver, bowel, stomach, spleen · it sits in the renal fossa of the caudate lobe
Kidney size (VD)Dog 2.5–3.5 × length of L2 · cat 2–3 × L2
Retroperitoneal structures you seeLumbar muscles + kidneys (sometimes aorta/vessels) · NOT ureters, adrenals, lymph nodes unless abnormal
Round dots ventral to the spineDeep circumflex iliac arteries seen end-on (mistaken for lymph nodes)
Urinary bladder regionsApex · body · neck/trigone · easier on the LATERAL (spine, pelvis, colon overlap on VD)

🍽️ GI tract

Stomach shape: dog vs catDog: left → right across the whole abdomen · cat: J-shaped, pylorus on midline
4 regions of the stomachCardia · fundus · body · pylorus
Right lateral: where is the gas?Fundus (left-sided, now up)
Left lateral: where is the gas?Pylorus + duodenum (right-sided, now up)
Barium on a right lateralPools in the pylorus + duodenum (right side, down)
VD: fundus has…Fluid / barium (fundus is dorsal) · body + pylorus get the gas · DV = reverse
Small intestine on filmTubular, uniform diameter, gas/fluid/mixed, smooth margins, everywhere · can NOT judge wall thickness
Fat cat quirkSmall bowel clusters on the right side
Cecum: dog vs catDog: C-shaped / corkscrew, mid-abdomen, gas-filled · cat: comma/cone, rarely seen
Colon partsAscending, transverse, descending (descending is mobile) → rectum in the pelvic canal

👻 Not seen in health

GallbladderBorder-effaces with the liver · seen only if mineralized · ventral right liver
PancreasBody at the pyloroduodenal angle · right lobe along the descending duodenum (bigger in dogs) · left lobe along the greater curvature (bigger in cats; seen in obese cats)
Ureters + urethraOnly with contrast or when huge/stones · male urethra: prostatic, membranous (pelvic), penile
Adrenal glandsRight: medial + slightly cranial to the right kidney · left: more cranial to its kidney · enlarged → kidney pushed caudolaterally
UterusDorsal to the bladder, ventral to the colon · seen only when enlarged (gravid from ~25–30 days)
ProstateIntact males (not neutered, not cats) · < 70% of pelvic-inlet height · fat triangle separates it from the bladder
Medial iliac lymph nodesVentral to L6–L7, dorsal to the descending colon → push it ventrally when enlarged
Mesenteric lymph nodesCentral · drain abdominal viscera · push bowel peripherally when enlarged

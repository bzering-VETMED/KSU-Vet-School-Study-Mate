🗣️ LC39 · Physiology 1 · Structure

Poole, explained simply · read this first, then the terms page

💡 The lung is built to move oxygen in and CO₂ out, and its whole design is about making that easy: huge surface, paper-thin wall.
🖼️ Picture it: Picture a tennis court of tissue folded into your chest, with blood spread over it in a sheet thinner than a soap bubble.

The plain-English version

  • Why we breathe: mainly to get rid of CO₂ and keep blood pH ~7.4. Getting O₂ in matters too, but CO₂ is what the body controls tightly (arterial PCO₂ ~40).
  • Fick's law in plain words: more gas crosses when the surface is BIG, the wall is THIN, and the pressure difference is LARGE. Every lung disease breaks one of these.
  • Gases go from high pressure to low pressure. No pumps. Ever.
  • Airways = two zones: the pipes (trachea → bronchioles) just carry air (dead space). Gas exchange only happens deep in the lung (respiratory zone).
  • Big pipes, small total area: one trachea vs millions of tiny airways. So the trachea and big bronchi are where most of the resistance is. A blockage there is noticed fast; a blockage deep in the lung can hide.
  • The lung barely uses O₂ from the air: it only pulls out ~25%, even in exercise. That keeps alveolar O₂ high so blood can load.
  • Birds are better: air flows one way through their lungs. Mammals breathe in and out of dead-end sacs, so fresh air mixes with stale air.
  • 3 alveolar cells: Type I (the thin wall), Type II (makes surfactant, repairs Type I), macrophages (cleaning crew; they won't eat silica or asbestos).

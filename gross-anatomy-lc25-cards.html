⚠️ Precision traps

  • Abdomen at END OF EXPIRATION; thorax at peak INSPIRATION.
  • Soft tissue = fluid opacity → effusion REDUCES serosal detail.
  • Right lateral → gas in FUNDUS. Left lateral → gas in PYLORUS/DUODENUM. Barium does the opposite.
  • Ventrodorsal: fundus = fluid/barium; dorsoventral: fundus = gas.
  • Easiest liver margin: dog LEFT lateral lobe, cat RIGHT lateral lobe.
  • Kidney size: dog 2.5–3.5 × L2; cat 2–3 × L2 — on VENTRODORSAL.
  • Splenic head → stomach = GASTROSPLENIC LIGAMENT, not greater omentum.
  • Epaxial = DORSAL to transverse processes; hypaxial = VENTRAL.
  • Compression = reduce superimposition; horizontal beam = free gas; perineal view = urethral stones.
  • Pancreas: right lobe bigger in DOGS; left lobe bigger in CATS.
  • Deep circumflex iliac arteries (end-on dots) ≠ lymph nodes.
  • Fetal mineralization: cats 35–39 d, dogs 42–45 d (cats first).

⚪ Opacities & taking the radiograph

Opacities, least → most opaque?Gas, fat, soft tissue (= fluid), mineral, metal
★ Opening review question
Gas appears?Black
Fat appears?Darker gray
Soft tissue vs fluid opacity?Same opacity — medium gray
★ Fluid = soft tissue → border effacement
Iodine and barium contrast are which opacity?Metal (very white)
Abdominal radiographs are taken at?End of expiration — minimizes motion blur
★ Abdomen = breathe OUT; thorax = peak inspiration
Thoracic radiographs are taken at?Peak inspiration (fill lungs with gas)
Pelvic limbs positioned?Extended caudally, out of the abdomen
Boundaries of an abdominal radiograph?Diaphragm to the coxofemoral joints
Why two views (orthogonal)?3-D object squished to 2-D; need both to localize
Standard views at K-State?Left lateral, right lateral, ventrodorsal
★ 3 standard views
Why take both laterals?Different structures fill with gas on each side (e.g., pylorus/duodenum on left lateral)

📸 Optional views & indications

Compression view?Wooden spoon presses the abdomen — reduces superimposition over an area of interest
★ Review Q: small kidney hidden by gut → compression view
Pneumogastrogram?Air into the stomach via orogastric tube — distends it to find foreign material/mass
Pneumocolonogram?Air into the colon via red rubber catheter — tells colon from small intestine
Lateral with pelvic limbs pulled forward (perineal view)?Visualizes the region of the urethra — urinary stones
★ Removes femur/fabella superimposition
Horizontal beam radiography?Beam shot across the patient — highlights free gas
★ Free abdominal gas = possible surgical emergency

🧭 Systematic approach

Her evaluation order?Extra-abdominal structures → serosal detail & contour → liver & spleen → urogenital → gastrointestinal tract
★ Same way every time = 'physical exam of radiology'
Why GI tract last?Most superimposition; and you'll stop looking once you find the foreign body
Extra-abdominal structures include?Non-abdominal soft tissues, body wall, diaphragm, skeleton, thorax
External structures mistaken for masses?Nipples, skin tags, masses, prepuce
★ Prepuce is often mistaken for a mass
Epaxial muscles location?Dorsal to the transverse processes
★ Epaxial = dorsal; hypaxial = ventral
Hypaxial muscles location?Ventral to the transverse processes
Why identify lumbar muscles?They create confusing lines on radiographs
Thoracic structures seen on an abdominal radiograph?Lungs, caudal vena cava, aorta, cardiac silhouette
Review: lung lobe near the cardiac silhouette on right side?Right middle lung lobe (cardiac apex points left)
Why trust but verify markers?Mislabeled sides can create false pathology / wrong-side surgery
Why look at the body wall?Find herniation, especially after trauma or bite wounds

🫁 Diaphragm

Which side of the diaphragm is normally visible?Thoracic surface only (gas in lungs)
★ Abdominal side border-effaces with the liver
Border effacement?Two touching soft tissue structures can't be told apart
Parts of the diaphragm seen?Cupola (dome), right crus, left crus
When is the abdominal side of the diaphragm seen?Excessive falciform fat (normal variant) or peritoneal free gas (pathologic)
★ Free gas = worry (e.g., ruptured bowel) unless post-surgery

🌫️ Serosal detail & contour

Abdominal contour?Shape of the abdomen — breed-related
Greyhound contour?Tucked up
English bulldog contour?Pendulous / potbelly — normal for them
Serosal detail?Ability to see the serosal margins of abdominal organs
Main natural contrast in the abdomen?Intra-abdominal (visceral) fat
★ Thorax = gas; abdomen = fat
Fatter animal → serosal detail?Better (usually)
Causes of poor serosal detail?Emaciation, young age, peritoneal effusion, serosal disease, mass effect/organomegaly, technique/patient factors
★ List of 6
Why do puppies have poor detail?Brown fat — higher water content
Serosal diseases that reduce detail?Peritonitis and carcinomatosis
Carcinomatosis?Neoplasia spread throughout the peritoneal cavity
Why do large dogs/horses have poor detail?More scatter of the x-ray beam

🟤 Liver & spleen

Liver borders?Cranial diaphragm; caudal stomach, right kidney, small intestine; lateral body wall; ventral falciform fat
Easiest liver margin to see?Caudoventral margin
★ Dog = LEFT lateral lobe; cat = RIGHT lateral lobe
3 indicators of liver size?Gastric axis, shape of margins, caudal extent
Gastric axis line?Fundus to pylorus
Normal gastric axis?Between perpendicular to the spine and parallel to the ribs
Normal caudal extent of the liver?Within or slightly past the costal arch
Hepatomegaly signs?Extends well past the costal arch, rounded/lumpy margins, gastric axis displaced caudally
Microhepatica sign?Gastric axis displaced cranially
Splenic head location?Left cranial abdomen; immobile
Splenic tail?Mobile
Splenic tail on lateral: dog vs cat?Dog: usually visible ventrally; cat: usually not (smaller spleen)
Why is spleen size variable?Hemodynamics, hydration, sedation
Review: what connects the splenic head to the stomach?Gastrosplenic ligament (short gastric arteries run in it)
★ Not the greater omentum — class missed it

🫘 Kidneys, retroperitoneum & bladder

Kidney location?Cranial and dorsal abdomen, retroperitoneal space
Which kidney is more caudal?Left — 'left is left behind'; may be pendulous
Dog vs cat kidney shape?Dog more elongate; cat more rounded
Kidneys on the lateral view?Superimposed on each other; cranial pole of right kidney obscured
Kidney harder to see?Right — superimposed; sits in the renal fossa of the caudate lobe of the liver
Kidney size assessed on which view?Ventrodorsal
Normal dog kidney length?2.5–3.5 × length of L2
★ Memorization number
Normal cat kidney length?2–3 × length of L2
★ Memorization number
Retroperitoneal structures normally seen?Lumbar muscles and kidneys
Retroperitoneal structures occasionally seen?Blood vessels (aorta, deep circumflex iliac arteries)
Retroperitoneal structures not normally seen?Ureters, adrenal glands, lymphatic vessels, lymph nodes
Deep circumflex iliac arteries mistaken for?Lymph nodes — round dots because seen end-on
Urinary bladder 3 regions?Apex, body, neck/trigone
Bladder best evaluated on?Lateral — on ventrodorsal it's covered by pelvis, spine, colon

🍽️ Gastrointestinal tract

Positive contrast?Barium or iodine (white)
Negative contrast?Gas (air)
Dog stomach shape?Left body wall to right body wall
Cat stomach shape?J-shaped, left cranial quadrant, pylorus on midline
Stomach position?Caudal to liver; cranial to colon and small intestine
4 regions of the stomach?Cardia, fundus, body, pylorus
Right lateral: what fills with gas?Fundus (left-sided structure is up)
★ Gas rises; fluid/barium sinks
Left lateral: what fills with gas?Pylorus and duodenum (right-sided structures are up)
★ Left lateral = pylorus gas
Right lateral with barium: highlighted?Pylorus and duodenum (dependent side)
Left lateral with barium: highlighted?Fundus
Ventrodorsal: gas vs fluid?Fundus (dorsal) fills with fluid/barium; body & pylorus (ventral) fill with gas
Dorsoventral: gas vs fluid?Fundus fills with gas; body & pylorus fill with fluid/barium
Small intestine appearance?Tubular, uniform diameter, gas/fluid/mixed, smooth serosal margins, throughout the abdomen
Fat cats: small intestine?May cluster in the right abdomen
Can you measure intestinal wall thickness on radiographs?NO — luminal fluid is same opacity as the wall
★ Caps-lock on the slide
Key size rule for bowel?Compare loops — one segment much bigger than others is the problem (don't memorize numbers)
Colon segments?Ascending, transverse, descending; heterogeneous content; mobile
Dog cecum?Right mid abdomen; C-shaped, compartmentalized, usually gas-filled
★ Mistaken for abnormal small intestine
Cat cecum?Rarely seen; cone-shaped; may need barium
Rectum?Continuation of the colon within the pelvic canal

👻 Structures not usually seen

Gallbladder on radiographs?Border-effaced with liver; seen if mineralized; ventral and right aspect of liver
Pancreas — when seen?Left limb sometimes in obese cats
Pancreas body location?Pyloroduodenal angle
Right lobe of the pancreas?Along descending duodenum — larger in dogs
Left lobe of the pancreas?Along greater curvature of stomach — larger in cats
Ureters seen when?Only with contrast, massive distension, or ureteral stones
Urethra seen when?Only with contrast
3 parts of the male urethra?Prostatic, membranous/pelvic, penile
Right adrenal gland location?Medial and slightly cranial to right kidney
Left adrenal gland location?Relatively more cranial to its kidney than the right
Adrenal enlargement does what?Displaces the adjacent kidney caudally and laterally
Uterus location?Dorsal to bladder, ventral to colon
★ Normally NOT seen
Uterus appearance when seen?Tubular, homogeneous soft tissue: pregnant or diseased
Gravid uterus enlargement visible?25–30 days
Fetal mineralization: cats vs dogs?Cats 35–39 days; dogs 42–45 days
Prostate not seen in?Neutered males and cats
Prostate seen in?Intact male dogs (benign prostatic hyperplasia)
Prostate location?Ventral to rectum, dorsal to pubis
Prostate size limit?<70% height of the pelvic inlet
Landmark separating prostate from bladder?Triangle of fat
★ "Important landmark"
Medial iliac (sublumbar) lymph nodes location?Ventral to L6–L7, dorsal to the descending colon
Enlarged medial iliac nodes do what?Displace the descending colon ventrally
Medial iliac nodes drain?Pelvic limbs and perineal region
Mesenteric lymph nodes?Central; drain abdominal viscera; enlarged → push small intestine peripherally

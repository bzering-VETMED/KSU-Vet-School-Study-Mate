⚠️ Precision traps

  • Secondary = backbone hydrogen bonds · tertiary = hydrophobic side chains (S9 mislabels this row)
  • Immunohistochemistry: antibody probes TISSUE · agglutination: antigen probes SERUM
  • ELISA is the sensitive one (more false positives) → confirm with Coggins, not the reverse
  • Positive = lysine, arginine, histidine · negative = aspartate, glutamate. Don't flip the charges.

🧬 LC14 · Amino acids & protein structure

Amino acid partsAmine group + carboxylic acid group + a side chain (R group) that sets its class.
Positively charged amino acidsLysine, arginine, histidine.
Negatively charged amino acidsAspartate and glutamate.
Polar uncharged amino acidsSerine, threonine, tyrosine (alcohol groups) + asparagine and glutamine.
Phosphorylatable amino acidsSerine, threonine, tyrosine: the OH (alcohol) groups take the phosphate.
Nonpolar (hydrophobic) amino acidsEverything else (e.g., phenylalanine, leucine, valine); they hide in the protein core.
Primary structureThe amino acid sequence; direct translation of mRNA. Written N-terminus left → C-terminus right.
Secondary structureLocal folds (alpha helix, beta sheet) held by hydrogen bonds along the backbone.
Tertiary structureWhole chain collapses into one 3D shape, driven mainly by hydrophobic interactions between side chains.
Disulfide bondCovalent bond between two cysteines that locks (stabilizes) tertiary structure.
Quaternary structureSeveral polypeptide chains forming one protein (hemoglobin = 2 alpha + 2 beta). Only some proteins.
Posttranslational modificationChanging a side chain after translation (e.g., phosphorylation) to change its properties.
AntibodyDefense protein that recognizes the unique 3D surface of one specific protein (its antigen).
AntigenThe molecule (usually a protein) an antibody recognizes.
ImmunohistochemistryAn antibody probes a tissue section for a specific protein.
Direct fluorescent antibody testType of immunohistochemistry: fluorescent anti-rabies antibody on brain tissue → glows if rabies protein is present.
Agglutination assayAn antigen probes serum for antibodies; antibodies cross-link it → clumps (precipitate).
Tube agglutination (Brucella suis)Brucella antigen + pig serum → clumping if anti-Brucella antibodies. Titer > 1/160 = infection.
TiterHighest serum dilution that still reacts; higher titer = more antibody.
ELISAEnzyme-linked immunosorbent assay: an enzyme multiplies the signal → very sensitive, but more false positives.
Coggins testAgar immunodiffusion (agglutination-type) test for equine infectious anemia; used to confirm a positive ELISA.
Western blotUsed when ELISA and Coggins disagree on equine infectious anemia.
BackboneRepeating amine–carbon–carbonyl chain of the protein; side chains hang off it.
Hydrogen bondWeak attraction between H and O/N; holds secondary structure along the backbone.
Hydrophobic interactionNonpolar side chains cluster away from water; main driver of tertiary folding.
Charge (ionic) interactionAttraction between + and − side chains; helps tertiary structure.
Alpha helixCoiled secondary structure held by backbone hydrogen bonds.
Beta sheetPleated, side-by-side strands held by backbone hydrogen bonds.

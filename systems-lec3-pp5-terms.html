HypoxemiaDecreased arterial O₂ content
★ 5 causes of hypoxemia1 ↓ inspired PO₂ · 2 hypoventilation · 3 diffusion limitation · 4 right-to-left shunt · 5 ventilation-perfusion mismatch
Healthy arterial PO₂A little below alveolar (small normal shunt + mismatch) · still ~97% saturated
1 · ↓ inspired PO₂ causesAltitude (low barometric pressure, normal O₂ fraction) or low inspired O₂ fraction (wrong gas settings)
1 · ↓ inspired PO₂ blood gasesLow PaO₂ AND low PaCO₂ (stimulates breathing)
2 · Hypoventilation causesAirway obstruction · respiratory muscle weakness/paralysis · CNS depression (anesthetics, barbiturates, owner's narcotics)
Hypoventilation means…Too little breathing RELATIVE to CO₂ production
2 · Hypoventilation blood gasesLow PaO₂ + HIGH PaCO₂
★ Hypoventilation vs low inspired PO₂Check PaCO₂: HIGH = hypoventilation · LOW = low inspired PO₂
Definition of hypoventilationElevated PaCO₂ (hyperventilation = reduced PaCO₂)
Halve alveolar ventilation (same CO₂ output)PaCO₂ doubles (40 → 80)
PaCO₂ 80 → alveolar PO₂?149 − (80 ÷ 0.8) = ~49 mmHg
3 · Diffusion limitation causeThickened blood-gas barrier (alveolar proteinosis, edema, pneumonia)
3 · Diffusion limitation at rest vs exerciseRarely a problem at rest · exercise → low PaO₂ (shorter transit time)
Diffusion limitation: mild vs severeMild → low O₂, normal CO₂ (breathing ↑) · severe → high CO₂ even at rest
3 · Diagnose diffusion limitationMeasure lung diffusing capacity · improves with ↑ inspired O₂
4 · Right-to-left shuntVenous blood reaches arterial side without passing ventilated alveoli
Normal (physiologic) shuntsBronchial veins → pulmonary veins · Thebesian veins → left ventricle · only a couple mmHg effect
Pathologic shuntsPatent ductus arteriosus · septal defect · patent foramen ovale (with high right-heart pressure) · atelectasis
Atelectasis under anesthesiaLarge dogs · dependent lung compressed + breathing depressed · worse on high O₂
★ Diagnose shuntA few breaths of 100% O₂ → arterial sample · PaO₂ >500 ≈ 5% shunt · ~250 ≈ 20% shunt (iso-shunt graph)
Shunt severity depends on…Amount of venous blood reaching arterial side
High right-heart pressure + shuntIncreases right-to-left shunting
5 · Ventilation-perfusion mismatchVentilation and blood flow don't match · most common cause in lung disease
★ Why high-ratio units can't fix low-ratio unitsHigh ratio adds only a little extra O₂ (hemoglobin already ~full) → mixed blood is always low in O₂
Ventilation-perfusion ratio = 0Shunt (perfused, not ventilated)
Ventilation-perfusion ratio = infinityDead space (ventilated, not perfused)
★ Mismatch always causes…Hypoxemia · NOT always CO₂ retention
★ 100% O₂: mismatch vs shuntMismatch → PaO₂ rises a lot · shunt → barely rises
Gold-standard test for mismatchMultiple inert gas elimination technique
Assess mismatch severityAlveolar−arterial PO₂ difference: ideal alveolar PO₂ (alveolar gas equation) − measured PaO₂
Large alveolar−arterial differenceSubstantial mismatch
★ Example: PaO₂ 49, PaCO₂ 48, R 0.8Ideal alveolar = 149 − 48/0.8 = 89 → difference = 40 mmHg → severe mismatch
Rule out shunt in a hypoxemic animal100% O₂ → PaO₂ ~600 = no shunt → hypoxemia on air = mismatch
Dyspnea in lung disease comes from…Chemoreceptors: low O₂ (peripheral) + high CO₂ (central)
Higher ventilation-perfusion ratio in a lung unitAlveolar PO₂ ↑, PCO₂ ↓

⚠️ Precision traps

  • ★ Poole exam Q: hypoventilation = HIGH PaCO₂ · low inspired PO₂ = LOW PaCO₂ (hyperventilating)
  • 100% O₂ fixes mismatch, NOT shunt
  • Mismatch ALWAYS → hypoxemia, but NOT always → hypercapnia
  • Ratio 0 = shunt · ratio infinity = dead space

🗣️ LC45 · Physiology 7 · Blood gas transport

Poole, explained simply · read this first, then the terms page

💡 Hemoglobin is the O₂ delivery truck; blood can barely dissolve O₂ on its own. CO₂ mostly travels home as bicarbonate.
🖼️ Picture it: Hemoglobin = a 4-seat bus. In the lungs almost every seat fills (~97%). At rest, the body drops off ~1 passenger per bus (→ 75% full). In exercise, it drops off ~3.

The plain-English version

  • Without hemoglobin you'd need an impossible heart: dissolved O₂ is only 0.3 mL/100 mL → you'd need ~83 L/min of cardiac output at rest.
  • O₂ content (the recipe): hemoglobin × 1.34 × saturation, plus a tiny dissolved bit (PO₂ × 0.003). Normal ≈ 20 mL/100 mL. He likes to give a new hemoglobin number and have you calculate it.
  • The S-shaped curve: flat on top (lungs: even if PO₂ drops a bit, you stay ~full) and steep in the middle (tissues: small PO₂ drop = lots of O₂ released).
  • Normal points: arterial ~100 mmHg / 97% · venous at rest ~40 mmHg / 75% · P50 = the PO₂ where the bus is half full (~27 in humans).
  • Right shift = "let go": hot, acidic, high-CO₂ tissue (exercising muscle) makes hemoglobin let go of O₂ more easily. Hot, Acid, CO₂ → right → release. A small shift can double what's dropped off.
  • Left shift = "hold on": myoglobin (muscle storage), carbon monoxide, fetal hemoglobin, the bar-headed goose (helps load O₂ at altitude).
  • Small animals have right-shifted curves (higher P50) because they burn O₂ faster.
  • "Oxy-shot" for horses? Doesn't work. Blood is already ~97% full and O₂ barely dissolves.
  • CO₂ goes home 3 ways: mostly as bicarbonate (carbonic anhydrase makes it), some stuck to hemoglobin (carbamino), a little dissolved.
  • Haldane effect: hemoglobin that just dropped off O₂ can pick up more CO₂. Perfect teamwork in the tissues.
  • Why mismatch hurts O₂ but not always CO₂: the O₂ curve is flat on top, so extra breathing can't add O₂. The CO₂ curve is steep, so extra breathing CAN blow off extra CO₂.

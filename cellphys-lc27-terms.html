⚠️ Precision traps

  • CPI-17 vs MYPT-1: CPI-17 is phosphorylated by PKC and INHIBITS MLCP → MLC stays phosphorylated → contraction maintained (S12). MYPT-1 is phosphorylated by PKG and STIMULATES myosin phosphatase → relaxation (S16). Slide says: compare them.
  • Gq ≠ always contraction: α1 + Gq/IP3/DAG → contraction (S9–S12); H1 + Gq/NO/cGMP → relaxation/vasodilation (S14–S18). Same G protein, same PLC, opposite tone.
  • Same ligand, different receptor: acetylcholine → M2/Gi heart (↓ rate); M1/M3/Gq/IP3/DAG bronchi (constriction); M3/Gq/NO/cGMP vascular endothelium (vasodilation) (S26, S29). Norepinephrine: α1 (Gq) vessels vs β1 (Gs) heart.
  • α vs βγ: α carries most signaling and owns the GTPase (S4–S5). βγ activates GIRK in the Gi pathway (S26). On S22 the Gs line 'βγ can activate GIRK/Kir3' is struck through; do not assign GIRK to Gs.
  • GDP = OFF, GTP = ON: activation = GDP leaves, GTP binds (exchange), not phosphorylation of GDP. Off-switch = α's own GTPase hydrolyzes GTP → GDP (S5).
  • IP3 vs DAG: IP3 → endoplasmic reticulum channel → Ca²⁺ → CaM → MLCK (S11). DAG + Ca²⁺ → PKC → CPI-17 (S12). Breakdown: IP3 → inositol, DAG → phosphatidic acid ('respectively', S13).
  • -ase precision: guanylyl cyclase makes cGMP from GTP; adenylyl cyclase makes cAMP from ATP; phosphodiesterase breaks cAMP/cGMP to AMP/GMP; phosphatase removes phosphate from proteins; MLCK (kinase) contracts vs MLCP (phosphatase) relaxes.
  • Direct vs kinase-mediated: cAMP binds HCN directly (faster If); cAMP acts on Cav3.1 via PKA phosphorylation (S22). In Gi, Cav3.1 is changed by phosphatases dephosphorylating it (S26).
  • Gi lowers cAMP two ways in sequence: αi inhibits adenylyl cyclase, then ongoing phosphodiesterase activity actually drops cAMP (S26). Gi does not destroy cAMP itself.
  • Kinase per pathway (S6): IP3/DAG → PKC; NO/cGMP → PKG; Gs cAMP ↑ → PKA ↑; Gi cAMP ↓ → PKA ↓. Watch arrows ↑/↓ flipped in options.

🔀 LC27 · G Protein-Coupled Receptors (Wed 10/28)

G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR)S1, S2, S30Most common cell-surface receptor type; ligands for and effects of many still undetermined. Also called G-protein-linked receptor.
GPCR drug targetsS2>800 human GPCR genes; 475 approved drugs (34%) target 108 GPCRs; 27% of global drug market.
Seven-pass transmembrane proteinS3, S30GPCR topology: crosses the membrane 7 times ('seven-pass' underlined on slide); N-terminus outside, C-terminus in cytosol.
Extracellular ligand-binding siteS3, S30Domain on the interstitial side that binds the first messenger (e.g., norepinephrine, histamine, acetylcholine).
G protein binding siteS3, S30Intracellular (cytosolic) receptor domain that binds the heterotrimeric G protein.
Phosphorylation sites (receptor)S3Sites on the cytosolic C-terminal tail of the GPCR (shown in S3 figure; function not stated on slide).
Heterotrimeric G proteinS1, S3, S4Three-subunit (α, β, γ) guanine-nucleotide binding protein coupling the GPCR to cytosolic targets: commonly ion channels or enzymes.
α (alpha) subunitS4, S5Binds GDP/GTP; has GTPase activity; mediates signaling in many (most) pathways (αq, αs, αi).
βγ (beta-gamma) subunitS4, S26Dissociates from α when GTP binds; mediates signaling in some pathways (e.g., activates GIRK in Gi pathway).
Molecular switchS5G protein toggles off (GDP-bound) and on (GTP-bound).
GDP-bound = 'switched off'S5Guanosine diphosphate on α: trimer intact (α with βγ), inactive.
GTP-bound = 'switched on'S5, S30Activated receptor causes GTP-for-GDP exchange; G protein switches on and α and βγ dissociate.
GTPase activity (of α)S5α subunit hydrolyzes its own GTP to GDP after a short period: switches off and re-associates with βγ.
Monomeric G protein (e.g., RAS)S5Single-subunit G protein that is also a molecular 'switch'.
GqS6, S10, S15G protein whose αq stimulates phospholipase C; drives both Gq/IP3/DAG and Gq/NO/cGMP pathways.
Gs ('stimulatory')S6, S22G protein whose αs stimulates adenylyl cyclase: cAMP ↑, protein kinase A ↑.
Gi ('inhibitory')S6, S26G protein whose αi inhibits adenylyl cyclase: cAMP ↓, protein kinase A ↓.
Other G proteins (GOlf, GGust, transducin, G12, G13)S6Additional G proteins to be presented in cell and systems physiology, not in this lecture.
Second messengerS6, S30Intracellular signal made after G protein activation; the 4 prototypical pathways are named for the G protein + second messenger(s).
Phospholipases (PLA2, PLA1, PLD, PLC)S8Enzymes cutting different bonds of a membrane phospholipid; PLC is the one in the Gq pathway.
Phospholipase C (PLC)S8, S10, S15Stimulated by αq; cleaves PIP2 into IP3 + DAG (bold on S10).
PIP2: phosphatidylinositol bisphosphateS8, S10, S15Membrane lipid broken down by PLC into IP3 and DAG (S15 calls it 'phosphoinositol diphosphate').
IP3: inositol trisphosphateS10, S11, S15Second messenger: opens receptor-gated channels on endoplasmic reticulum to release Ca²⁺ (S15: 'phosphoinositol triphosphate').
DAG: diacylglycerolS10, S12Second messenger that, together with Ca²⁺, activates protein kinase C.
α1-adrenergic receptorS10, S29Norepinephrine receptor on vascular smooth muscle; Gq/IP3/DAG → vasoconstriction.
Norepinephrine (NE)S10, S22Ligand for α1 (Gq, vascular smooth muscle) and β1 (Gs, pacemaker) receptors.
Endoplasmic / sarcoplasmic reticulumS11, S15Intracellular Ca²⁺ store emptied through IP3-activated receptor-gated channels.
Calmodulin (CaM)S11Ca²⁺-binding protein that imparts Ca²⁺ sensitivity to proteins it interacts with.
Myosin light chain kinase (MLCK)S11Activated by Ca²⁺–CaM; phosphorylates MLC → contraction.
Myosin light chain (MLC)S11, S13Phosphorylated MLC raises myosin head affinity for actin; cross-bridge cycling needs ATP hydrolysis.
Protein kinase C (PKC)S6, S12Activated by DAG + Ca²⁺ together; phosphorylates CPI-17.
CPI-17S12C-kinase potentiated protein phosphatase inhibitor, 17 kDa; phosphorylated by PKC → INHIBITS MLCP → contraction maintained.
Myosin light chain phosphatase (MLCP)S12, S13, S16Dephosphorylates MLC → relaxation; inhibited by CPI-17, stimulated via MYPT-1.
Inositol / phosphatidic acidS13, S17Breakdown products of IP3 and DAG, respectively (termination).
Ca²⁺ removalS13, S17Termination: transport into intracellular stores (Ca²⁺-ATPase) + export out of cell (exchangers and pumps).
H1 histaminergic receptorS15, S18, S29Histamine receptor; Gq → PLC → IP3 → Ca²⁺ → NO/cGMP → vasodilation (S29: on vascular endothelium).
Histamine (His)S15, S18, S28Released from mast cells (S18); ligand for H1 (Gq/NO/cGMP) and H2 (Gs, stomach acid).
Nitric oxide synthase (NOS)S16Activated by Ca²⁺; makes NO from the amino acid arginine.
Nitric oxide (NO)S16, S17Gaseous second messenger; diffuses very short distances, then breaks down to nitrite/nitrate (NO2⁻/NO3⁻).
Guanylyl cyclaseS16Stimulated by NO; makes cGMP from GTP.
cGMP: cyclic guanosine monophosphateS16, S17Second messenger that stimulates PKG; broken down by phosphodiesterases to GMP.
Protein kinase G (PKG)S6, S16cGMP-activated kinase; phosphorylates MYPT-1.
MYPT-1: myosin phosphatase target 1S16Phosphorylated by PKG → STIMULATES myosin phosphatase → relaxation (opposite of CPI-17).
Endothelial cell → smooth muscle (S18)S18S18 figure: H1 + NOS in endothelial cell; NO diffuses to smooth muscle → guanylyl cyclase → PKG → vasodilation.
β1-adrenergic receptorS22, S28Norepinephrine receptor in cardiac pacemaker cells; Gs/cAMP → ↑ heart rate.
Adenylyl cyclaseS22, S26Stimulated by αs, inhibited by αi; makes cAMP from ATP.
cAMP: cyclic adenosine monophosphateS22Second messenger; binds HCN directly and the regulatory subunit of PKA.
Protein kinase A (PKA)S22cAMP binds regulatory subunit → catalytic subunit active; phosphorylates Cav3.1.
HCN channelS20, S22Hyperpolarization and cyclic nucleotide gated cation channel; cAMP binding speeds its activation.
Funny current (If)S20, S22'Pacemaker' current through HCN channels; drives phase 4 potential toward threshold.
Cav3.1 / ICa,TS20, S22T-type Ca²⁺ channel/current; activates at threshold and generates the action potential upstroke (phase 0).
Pacemaker action potential phases 4, 0, 3S19, S20Phase 4 slow depolarization (If) → phase 0 upstroke (ICa,T) → phase 3 repolarization.
Sinoatrial (SA) nodeS19, S24Cardiac pacemaker / sinus node: the example tissue for the Gs and Gi pathways.
Phosphodiesterases (PDE)S17, S23, S26Break down cAMP → AMP (many types) and cGMP → GMP; ongoing activity lowers cAMP in Gi.
PhosphatasesS13, S17, S23, S26Dephosphorylate proteins (Cav3.1, CPI-17, MYPT-1); many types.
M2 muscarinic receptorS26, S29Acetylcholine receptor in cardiac pacemaker cells; Gi/cAMP + βγ-GIRK → ↓ heart rate.
Acetylcholine (ACh)S24, S26, S29Released from parasympathetic fibers; M2 (Gi, heart), M1/M3 (Gq, bronchi, pancreatic β-cell), M3 (Gq/NO, endothelium).
GIRK (Kir3) channelS24, S26, S27G protein-gated inwardly rectifying K⁺ channel; activated by βγ in Gi pathway → hyperpolarization.
Pathway bifurcationS10, S30One pathway can branch to multiple effects in a cell (e.g., IP3 arm + DAG arm).
One ligand, many receptor subtypesS30Same ligand can act via different receptor subtypes/pathways in different tissues (e.g., ACh: M2 vs M3).
Different ligands, common cascadeS30Different ligands acting on their own receptors in one tissue can activate the same second-messenger cascade.
β2-adrenergic receptor (appendix)S28Gs/cAMP: arteries vasodilation, bronchi bronchodilation, muscle glycogen breakdown.
V1 vs V2 vasopressin receptors (appendix)S28, S29V2 = Gs, kidney distal nephron water reabsorption; V1 = Gq/IP3/DAG, arterial vasoconstriction.
Gs appendix receptors: H2, glucagon, ACTH-R, TSH-RS28H2 stomach acid; glucagon liver glycogen breakdown; ACTH-R cortisol; TSH-R thyroid hormone.
α2-adrenergic receptor (appendix)S29Gi/cAMP: epinephrine on neurons decreases neurotransmitter release.
M1, M3 / AT1, AT2 (appendix)S29Gq/IP3/DAG: ACh bronchoconstriction and insulin secretion; angiotensin arterial vasoconstriction.

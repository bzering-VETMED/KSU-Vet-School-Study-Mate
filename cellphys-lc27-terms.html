🔀 LC27 · G Protein-Coupled Receptors (Wed 10/28)

G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR)S1, S2, S30; transcriptMost common cell-surface receptor type; >800 in humans; some act as monomers, some as dimers (transcript). Also called G-protein-linked receptor.
Why membrane receptors?transcriptWater-soluble ligands (low octanol:water partition coefficient) can't enter cells; steroids (lipid-soluble, carriers) use cytosolic/nuclear receptors.
GPCR ligand classesS2; transcriptAmines (serotonin, ACh, dopamine, histamine), peptides (angiotensin, bradykinin, CCK, oxytocin, vasopressin), chemokines, lipids, glucagon, VIP, GABA/glutamate.
Olfactory and orphan receptorsS2; transcriptDogs ~1000 olfactory GPCRs; orphan receptors = GPCRs whose ligands are unknown.
GPCR historytranscriptDiscovered 1971; cholera and pertussis toxins identified Gs and Gi; Nobel 1994 (Rodbell, Gilman); 2012 crystal structure Nobel.
GPCR drug targetsS2; transcript>800 human GPCR genes; 475 approved drugs (34%, 'a third of all drugs') target 108 GPCRs; 27% of global drug market.
Seven-pass transmembrane proteinS3, S30; transcript7 transmembrane segments = the SIGNATURE of a GPCR (transcript); N-terminus outside, C-terminus in cytosol.
Extracellular ligand-binding siteS3, S30Domain on the interstitial side that binds the first messenger (e.g., norepinephrine, histamine, acetylcholine).
G protein binding siteS3, S30Intracellular (cytosolic) receptor domain that binds the heterotrimeric G protein.
Phosphorylation sites (receptor tail) / feedbackS3; transcriptLong cytoplasmic tail with phosphorylation sites for FEEDBACK regulation; e.g., β-adrenergic receptor kinase changes receptor affinity for norepinephrine.
Heterotrimeric G proteinS1, S3, S4Three-subunit (α, β, γ) guanine-nucleotide binding protein coupling the GPCR to cytosolic targets: commonly ion channels or enzymes.
α (alpha) subunitS4, S5; transcriptBinds GDP/GTP; its GTPase is the TIMER (how long signaling lasts); signals in most pathways (αq, αs, αi).
βγ (beta-gamma) subunitS4, S26Dissociates from α when GTP binds; mediates signaling in some pathways (e.g., activates GIRK in Gi pathway).
Molecular switchS5; transcript'Critical slide': G proteins are TIMERS/molecular switches: off (GDP-bound) and on (GTP-bound).
GDP-bound = 'switched off'S5Guanosine diphosphate on α: trimer intact (α with βγ), inactive.
GTP-bound = 'switched on'S5, S30; transcriptLigand-bound GPCR acts as an EXCHANGE FACTOR: swaps GTP for GDP; G protein switches on; α and βγ dissociate.
GTPase activity of α = the timerS5; transcriptα hydrolyzes its own GTP → GDP; α re-associates with βγ → off. Signal lasts as long as GTP is bound.
Monomeric G proteins (Ras, Rab, Rho)S5; transcriptSingle-subunit G proteins (~170 known) that are also switches; NOT part of this lecture (transcript).
GqS6, S10, S15; transcriptαq stimulates phospholipase C; ONE pathway with a branch point: IP3/DAG vs NO/cGMP. Historically 'Go (other)'.
Gs ('stimulatory')S6, S22G protein whose αs stimulates adenylyl cyclase: cAMP ↑, protein kinase A ↑.
Gi ('inhibitory')S6, S26G protein whose αi inhibits adenylyl cyclase: cAMP ↓, protein kinase A ↓.
Other G proteins (GOlf, GGust, transducin, G12, G13)S6; transcriptAdditional G proteins for cell and systems physiology; transducin (vision) was the first characterized (transcript).
Second messengerS6, S30Intracellular signal made after G protein activation; the 4 prototypical pathways are named for the G protein + second messenger(s).
Phospholipases (PLA2, PLA1, PLD, PLC)S8; transcriptCut different bonds of a phospholipid → different products: A1/A2 release fatty acids (arachidonic acid, Basel LC28); C → IP3 + DAG; D.
Phospholipase C (PLC)S8, S10, S15; transcriptStimulated by αq; hydrolyzes PIP2 → IP3 (water-soluble, into cytosol) + DAG (stays in membrane).
PIP2: phosphatidylinositol bisphosphateS8, S10, S15; transcriptPhosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate: membrane lipid cut by PLC into IP3 + DAG (S15 calls it 'phosphoinositol diphosphate').
IP3: inositol trisphosphateS10, S11, S15; transcriptSecond messenger: opens Ca²⁺ channels on endoplasmic reticulum (slide: receptor-gated channels; transcript says 'ryanodine receptor': see flag).
DAG: diacylglycerolS10, S12; transcriptStays in the membrane; with Ca²⁺ activates protein kinase C.
α1-adrenergic receptorS10, S29; transcriptNorepinephrine receptor on vascular smooth muscle; Gq/IP3/DAG → contraction = mechanism for NE ↑ vascular resistance (transcript).
Generalized Gq pathway (know this)S9, S10; transcriptGq α → phospholipase C → IP3 + DAG → ER Ca²⁺ → Ca²⁺-dependent effect (calmodulin); Ca²⁺ + DAG → PKC → phosphorylation effect.
Norepinephrine (NE)S10, S22Ligand for α1 (Gq, vascular smooth muscle) and β1 (Gs, pacemaker) receptors.
Endoplasmic / sarcoplasmic reticulumS11, S15; transcriptIntracellular Ca²⁺ store emptied through IP3-gated channels; refilled by SERCA pumps (transcript).
Calmodulin (CaM)S11; transcriptCa²⁺ sensor giving proteins Ca²⁺ sensitivity; 4 Ca²⁺ sites with COOPERATIVITY → steep curve → essentially all-on/all-off.
Myosin light chain kinase (MLCK)S11Activated by Ca²⁺–CaM; phosphorylates MLC → contraction.
Myosin light chain (MLC)S11, S13Phosphorylated MLC raises myosin head affinity for actin; cross-bridge cycling needs ATP hydrolysis.
Protein kinase C (PKC)S6, S12; transcriptCa²⁺-dependent kinase activated by DAG + Ca²⁺; phosphorylates CPI-17 → phosphorylation-dependent effect.
CPI-17 (concept, not full name)S12; transcriptPhosphorylated by PKC → INHIBITS MLCP → MLC stays phosphorylated → contraction maintained. Full name NOT required (transcript).
Myosin light chain phosphatase (MLCP)S12, S13, S16; transcriptDephosphorylates MLC → relaxation; inhibited via CPI-17 (Gq/IP3/DAG), activated via MYPT-1 (Gq/NO/cGMP).
Inositol / phosphatidic acid (termination)S13, S17; transcriptIP3 → inositol (slide; transcript: inositol bisphosphate); DAG → phosphatidic acid (or monoacylglycerol, transcript) → ↓ PKC.
Ca²⁺ removalS13, S17; transcriptTermination: SERCA pumps (Ca²⁺-ATPase) refill the ER + export out of cell (exchangers and pumps).
H1 histaminergic receptorS15, S18, S29; transcriptHistamine receptor on vascular ENDOTHELIUM (transcript); Gq → PLC → IP3 → Ca²⁺ → NO → smooth muscle relaxation.
Histamine (His)S15, S18, S28; transcriptFrom mast cells (S18); H1 (Gq/NO/cGMP) → ↓ vascular resistance; H2 (Gs) → stomach acid.
Nitric oxide synthase (NOS)S16Activated by Ca²⁺; makes NO from the amino acid arginine.
Nitric oxide (NO)S16, S17, S18; transcriptGaseous messenger; goes to ANOTHER CELL (endothelium → smooth muscle); very short diffusion; oxidized to nitrite/nitrate.
Guanylyl cyclaseS16; transcriptStimulated by NO; makes cGMP from GTP (same GTP pool the G protein uses, transcript).
cGMP: cyclic guanosine monophosphateS16, S17Second messenger that stimulates PKG; broken down by phosphodiesterases to GMP.
Protein kinase G (PKG)S6, S16cGMP-activated kinase; phosphorylates MYPT-1.
MYPT-1 (concept, not full name)S16; transcriptPhosphorylated by PKG → ACTIVATES myosin light chain phosphatase → relaxation (opposite of CPI-17). Full name NOT required.
NO acts on a different cell (S18)S14, S18; transcriptEndothelial cell (H1, NOS) → NO diffuses → vascular smooth muscle (guanylyl cyclase, PKG) → relaxation. S14 just draws one cell.
DAG in the NO pathwayS14, S18; transcript'Standing around watching': DAG → PKC → CPI-17 in the same cell would oppose relaxation, so NO acts in another cell.
β1-adrenergic receptorS22, S28Norepinephrine receptor in cardiac pacemaker cells; Gs/cAMP → ↑ heart rate.
Adenylyl cyclaseS22, S26Stimulated by αs, inhibited by αi; makes cAMP from ATP.
cAMP: cyclic adenosine monophosphateS22; transcriptSecond messenger; binds HCN directly and the regulatory subunit of PKA ('huge effect on heart rate').
Protein kinase A (PKA)S22; transcriptcAMP-dependent kinase; phosphorylates Cav3.x → ↑ open probability → faster depolarization.
HCN channelS20, S22; transcriptHyperpolarization- and cyclic nucleotide-gated NON-SELECTIVE CATION channel → depolarization (phase 4); cAMP speeds it.
Funny current (If)S20, S22; transcriptHCN current; 'funny' because it was measured at a non-physiological holding potential (transcript); drives phase 4.
Cav3.x (often Cav3.1) / ICa,TS20, S22; transcriptVoltage-sensitive T-type (transient) Ca²⁺ channel; phase 0 upstroke; also in brainstem pacemakers (transcript).
Pacemaker action potential phases 4, 0, 3S19, S20; transcriptPhase 4 slow depolarization (HCN) → phase 0 upstroke (Cav3.x) → phase 3 repolarization.
Sinoatrial (SA) nodeS19, S24; transcriptPacemaker: SLOPE of phase 4 sets T-to-P duration → heart rate. Pacemaker cells ≠ contracting cells (transcript).
Phosphodiesterases (PDE)S17, S23, S26; transcriptBreak down cAMP → AMP and cGMP → GMP; still active in Gi, so cAMP falls when adenylyl cyclase is inhibited.
Kinases vs phosphatases (concept)S13, S17, S23, S26; transcriptKinases ADD and phosphatases REMOVE phosphates, making enzymes more/less active: 'know that' (transcript).
M2 muscarinic receptorS26, S29; transcriptAcetylcholine receptor in SA node pacemaker cells; Gi: ↓cAMP (HCN, PKA) + βγ → GIRK: three mechanisms → ↓ heart rate.
Acetylcholine (ACh)S24, S26, S29; transcriptFrom the VAGUS (parasympathetic); M2 (Gi, heart), M1/M3 (Gq, bronchi, pancreatic β-cell), M3 (Gq/NO, endothelium).
GIRK (Kir3) channelS24, S26, S27; transcriptG protein-gated inwardly rectifying K⁺ channel; βγ opens it → K⁺ out → HYPERPOLARIZATION. 'Put a period on' its importance.
Three mechanisms slow the heart (Gi)S24, S26; transcript↓cAMP → ↓HCN; ↓cAMP → ↓PKA → ↓Cav3.x phosphorylation; βγ → GIRK → hyperpolarization.
Pathway bifurcationS10, S30; transcript'Four pathways, actually three, one with a branch point' (Gq → IP3/DAG vs NO/cGMP); IP3 arm + DAG arm in one cell.
One ligand, many receptor subtypesS30; transcriptSame ligand on different receptor subtypes/pathways in different tissues (ACh: M2 vs M3; NE: α1 vs β1).
Different ligands, common cascadeS30Different ligands acting on their own receptors in one tissue can activate the same second-messenger cascade.
Modular systemS28, S29, S30; transcriptMix and match ligand × receptor × G protein pathway → myriad responses; more branch points exist (e.g., arrestin, LC25 RAAS).
β2-adrenergic receptor (appendix)S28Gs/cAMP: arteries vasodilation, bronchi bronchodilation, muscle glycogen breakdown.
V1 vs V2 vasopressin receptors (appendix)S28, S29V2 = Gs, kidney distal nephron water reabsorption; V1 = Gq/IP3/DAG, arterial vasoconstriction.
Gs appendix receptors: H2, glucagon, ACTH-R, TSH-RS28H2 stomach acid; glucagon liver glycogen breakdown; ACTH-R cortisol; TSH-R thyroid hormone.
α2-adrenergic receptor (appendix)S29Gi/cAMP: epinephrine on neurons decreases neurotransmitter release.
M1, M3 / AT1, AT2 (appendix)S29Gq/IP3/DAG: ACh bronchoconstriction and insulin secretion; angiotensin arterial vasoconstriction.

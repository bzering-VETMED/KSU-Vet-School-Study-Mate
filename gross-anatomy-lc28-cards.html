👄 Oral cavity: spaces & borders

Oral cavity extends from… to…Lips (rostral) to the pharynx (caudal) (slides 3–4)
Angle where the upper and lower lips meet?Commissure (left + right) — like the dorsal/ventral commissures of the vulva
Two subdivisions of the oral cavity?Vestibule + oral cavity proper (divided by the teeth and jaws)
Vestibule = ?Space rostral + lateral to (outside) the teeth
Oral cavity proper = ?Space medial to (inside) the teeth; occupied by the tongue when the mouth is closed
Labial vestibule?Rostral: between the lips and the (incisor) teeth (slide 6)
Buccal vestibule?Lateral: between the cheeks and the teeth (slide 6)
Vestibule and oral cavity proper communicate through…Interdental spaces (between the teeth)
Large space between the teeth in horses?Diastema (next semester)
Dorsal border of the oral cavity proper?Hard palate (slide 7)
Ventral border of the oral cavity proper?Floor of the mouth (where the tongue is)
Rostral + lateral borders of the oral cavity proper?Dental arcade (teeth) (slide 7)
Clinical: grass awn between cheek and teeth is in the…Buccal vestibule (a bone medial to the teeth = oral cavity proper)
Structures numbered on slide 3 (oral cavity view)?Vestibule, canine tooth, philtrum, hard palate, soft palate, tongue, sublingual caruncle, palatoglossal arch, palatine tonsil, frenulum

🦷 Hard palate & vomeronasal organ

Hard vs soft palate — which is rostral?Hard palate (bony) is rostral; soft palate (soft tissue) is caudal
Three bones forming the hard palate?Palatine process of the maxilla + incisive bones + horizontal plate of the palatine bone (slide 9)
Transverse ridges of the hard palate mucosa?Rugae — 8–10 transverse ridges on the oral side (slide 9)
Cleft palate = ?Failure of the left and right palatine bones to fuse along the midline → abnormal oral–nasal opening
Cleft lip vs cleft palate — shared problem?Both create an abnormal opening between the oral and nasal cavities
Median palatine raphe?Midline seam dividing the hard palate into two symmetrical halves; less distinct in dogs (slide 11)
Incisive papilla?Small median swelling caudal to the upper central incisors (slide 11)
Incisive ducts open… and lead to…On either side of the incisive papilla → nasal cavity + vomeronasal organ
Vomeronasal organ = ? (other name)Jacobson's organ
Vomeronasal organ location and shape?Cigar-shaped, directly dorsal to the palate (floor of the nasal cavity)
Vomeronasal organ function?Sexual behavior + kin recognition via pheromones; lined with olfactory receptor cells (slides 12–13)
Flehmen response?Lip curl that moves pheromones/scents to the vomeronasal organ

👅 Soft palate, arches & muscles

Soft palate continues caudally to the pharynx at the…Intrapharyngeal opening
Intrapharyngeal opening is formed by…Left + right palatopharyngeal arches
Palatoglossal arch extends from…Palate to the tongue
Mucosa on the ventral (oral) surface of the soft palate?Oral mucosa (faces the oral cavity)
Mucosa on the dorsal surface of the soft palate?Respiratory mucosa (faces the nasopharynx)
What lies between the two mucosal layers of the soft palate?Salivary glands + muscles
Tensor veli palatini action?Tenses the soft palate (slide 16)
Levator veli palatini action?Raises the soft palate (slide 16)
Palatinus action?Shortens the soft palate — slide calls it 'palatine muscle' (slide 16)

⚡ Palate innervation

Three cranial nerves that innervate the palate?Trigeminal (V), vagus (X), glossopharyngeal (IX)
Three branches of the trigeminal n.?Ophthalmic (V1), maxillary (V2), mandibular (V3)
Major + minor palatine nerves come from… and supply…Maxillary n. (V2): MINOR palatine → soft palate; MAJOR palatine → hard palate (slide 17)
Nerve to the tensor veli palatini comes from…Mandibular n. (V3)
Pharyngeal plexus is formed by…Vagus (X) + glossopharyngeal (IX)
Pharyngeal plexus innervates…Levator veli palatini + palatinus
Maxillary n. passes through the…Maxillary foramen, then continues rostrally
Ganglion closely associated with the maxillary n.?Pterygopalatine ganglion (minor palatine n. appears to leave near it)
Other nerves labeled with the maxillary n. on slide 18?Pterygopalatine ganglion + nerve, nerve of the pterygoid canal, infraorbital n., caudal nasal n., accessory palatine n.

❤️ Arteries to the palate (pathway)

Aortic arch gives off…Brachiocephalic trunk + left subclavian a.
Brachiocephalic trunk gives off…Left common carotid, right common carotid, right subclavian
Common carotid a. branches into…External + internal carotid aa.
Internal carotid a. goes to…The brain
External carotid a. terminates as…Superficial temporal a. + maxillary a.
First palatine branch of the maxillary a.?Minor palatine a.
Common trunk from the maxillary a. gives…Major palatine a. (ventral) + sphenopalatine a. (dorsal) (slide 19)
Maxillary a. changes its name to…Infraorbital a.
Infraorbital a. route?Maxillary foramen → infraorbital canal → infraorbital foramen
Where is the infraorbital foramen?Below the eye (infra = below, orbit = eye socket)
Choanae vs conchae?Choanae = caudal openings of the nasal cavity; conchae = bony scrolls in the nasal cavity
Other branches labeled on the palate artery slide (slide 19)?Infraorbital → dorsal nasal + lateral nasal aa.; rami of the major palatine aa. run rostrally on the palate

👅 Tongue: regions, lyssa & papillae

Tongue tissue type?Skeletal muscle
Regions of the tongue, rostral → caudal?Apex (tip), body, root
Fold anchoring the tongue to the floor of the mouth?Lingual frenulum
Ankyloglossia = ?Tongue tie: shortened lingual frenulum → trouble nursing/lapping water; fixed by cutting the frenulum
Lyssa?Fibrous cord with fat + muscle on the ventral apex; stiffens the apex for lapping water (slides 20, 22)
Four types of tongue papillae?Filiform, fungiform, foliate, vallate (= circumvallate)
Non-gustatory (no taste) papillae?Filiform — mechanical only
Filiform papillae in cats are used for…Grooming (rough, 'pokey' tongue)
Vallate papillae location?Form a V on the root of the tongue
Fungiform papillae look like…Little white dots ('fungus')
Foliate papillae location?Sides of the tongue (leaf-like)
Structures numbered on the tongue figure (slide 20)?Apex, body, root (floor of the oropharynx), median groove, vallate papillae, fungiform papillae, palatoglossal arch, palatine tonsil in the tonsillar fossa, epiglottis, frenulum

💪 Tongue muscles

Intrinsic tongue muscles = ?Origin AND insertion within the tongue; no hyoid attachment; fibers in many directions (movement, avoid biting, speech in humans)
Extrinsic tongue muscles share what in their names?'-glossus'; the prefix names the bone of origin
Styloglossus — origin and action?Stylohyoid bone → middle portion of the tongue; shortens the tongue, elevates the apex, pulls the mid portion laterally (slide 28)
Hyoglossus — origin and action?Basihyoid bone → caudal 2/3 of the tongue; RETRACTS + depresses the tongue (slides 29–30)
Hyoglossus relationships?Passes between the lingual a. (deep/medial) and the hypoglossal n. (superficial/lateral) (slides 29–30)
Genioglossus — origin and action?Mandibular symphysis (chin) → ventral surface of the tongue; PROTRUSION of the tongue (slide 31 action is wrong)
Mandibular symphysis = ?Fusion of the left and right mandibles (like the pubic symphysis)

🩸 Tongue blood supply & venous drainage

Lingual a. is a branch of…External carotid a.
Sublingual a. is a branch of…Facial a. (itself a branch of the external carotid)
Vein draining around the angle of the eye?Angularis oculi v.
Vein on the dorsal aspect of the nose?Dorsal nasal v.
Angularis oculi + dorsal nasal vv. drain into…Facial v.
Sublingual v. drains into…Lingual v.
Facial v. + lingual v. form…Linguofacial v. (slide 33)
Linguofacial v. + maxillary v. form…External jugular v. (slide 33)
Full venous chain of the face/tongue?Angularis oculi + dorsal nasal → facial; sublingual → lingual; facial + lingual → linguofacial; linguofacial + maxillary → external jugular
Branches labeled on the lingual a. (slide 32)?Hyoid branch + tonsillar a.

🧠 Tongue innervation

General sensation to the rostral 2/3 of the tongue?Lingual n.
Taste from the rostral 2/3 of the tongue?Chorda tympani (passes through the middle ear)
Taste from the caudal 1/3 of the tongue?Glossopharyngeal n. (CN IX)
Only motor nerve to the tongue muscles?Hypoglossal n. (CN XII)

💧 Salivary glands

Major salivary glands?Parotid, mandibular, sublingual — serous secretion, most saliva, longer ducts (slide 35)
Minor salivary glands?Zygomatic, buccal — mucous secretion, shorter ducts; studied less (slide 35)
Parotid gland location (every species)?Paired, triangular, mixed gland ventral to the auricular cartilage (ear) (slide 37)
Structures associated with the parotid gland?Superficial temporal a., facial + trigeminal nerves (dissect under it); maxillary v. runs superficial to it
Parotid duct opens where?Buccal vestibule opposite upper premolar 4 (parotid papilla) (slide 37)
Mandibular gland location?Paired, ovoid, mixed; between the maxillary and linguofacial veins, caudal to the mandible (slide 38)
Mandibular gland shares a capsule with…MAJOR (monostomatic) sublingual gland (slides 38–39)
Mandibular duct opens at…Sublingual caruncle (under the tongue)
Sublingual gland location?Under the tongue
Monostomatic vs polystomatic?Major sublingual = monostomatic (one duct → sublingual caruncle); minor sublingual = polystomatic (many small lobules, many ducts into the oral cavity) (slide 39)
Zygomatic gland location?Medial to the rostral end of the zygomatic arch, ventral to the periorbita, lateral to the medial pterygoid m. (slide 40)
Zygomatic duct opens where?Buccal vestibule, 1 cm caudal to the parotid papilla (slide 40)
Mandibular gland neighbors (slide 38)?Digastricus m., medial retropharyngeal lymph node, hypoglossal n., external carotid a.
Order of the 4 salivary glands numbered on slide 36?1 parotid, 2 mandibular, 3 sublingual, 4 zygomatic
Zygomatic gland is the dog's version of…Dorsal buccal glands (slide 39 legend)

Transverse cervical nerve
📝 Slide note (professor) Note:
The transverse cervical nerve is another cutaneous branch of C2 spinal nerve. It travels ventrally and innervates the cranioventral part of the neck.

See if you can find this branch that is heading ventrally from C2.

Much like the other nerves in the area, this nerve is very superficial and there is a high likelihood it was removed with the skin or fat in the area. This dissection image was taken from VAP806 dissection Pre-Lab videos

❓ Identify the highlighted structure.
Lateral thoracic vein
📝 Slide note (professor) Note:
The lateral thoracic artery, vein and nerve emerge from the axilla between the latissimus dorsi and the deep pectoral.

The nerve is the motor to the cutaneous trunci muscle and can be found on the ventral and deep surface of cutaneous trunci.

The nerve is very superficial and is easily removed with fat and fascia in this area, see if you can find the nerve without transecting it. The artery and vein are deeper and not readily identified, if you do not see them now, we will identify them later with the brachial plexus. Here the artery and vein are barely visualized deep in the fat on this cadaver This dissection image was taken from VAP806 dissection Pre-Lab videos

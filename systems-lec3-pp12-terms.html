Poole · exercise-induced pulmonary hemorrhage (EIPH) · "will be on your exam" · 36 terms · say it, then tap the pink box

★ pink row = professor emphasized
★ The 3 exam questionsWhat is EIPH? What causes it? How can we prevent it?
★ EIPHExercise-induced pulmonary hemorrhage: bleeding into the lungs from RUPTURED PULMONARY CAPILLARIES during intense exercise
How commonEssentially every racehorse (scope 3 races → >95%; lavage → bleeding after every race)
Old belief vs nowUsed to blame large airways · microsphere studies + electron microscopy prove PULMONARY CAPILLARY rupture
★ Cause #1: HIGH INTRAvascular pressureHuge heart → cardiac output 400–500 L/min → pulmonary artery mean pressure >120 mmHg
★ Cause #2: very NEGATIVE alveolar (EXTRAvascular) pressureBig inspiratory flow through high-resistance airways → alveolar pressure ~ −40 cmH₂O
★ Why the capillary rupturesPositive inside + negative outside ADD together across a blood-gas barrier <1 µm thick
Why the barrier is so thinNeeded for gas exchange, but inherently fragile
Undersized horse lungModest capillary blood volume → blood must go fast AND at high pressure
★ Where EIPH startsCAUDODORSAL lung lobes (near the diaphragm) → spreads forward with more race starts
★ Altitude makes EIPH worse because…Low alveolar O₂ → HYPOXIC VASOCONSTRICTION → higher pulmonary artery pressure at the same cardiac output
High hematocrit in racing horseUp to ~70% → more viscous blood → adds to pressure
Horse = obligate nasal breatherDoesn't mouth-breathe when running · ~60–70 L/s through each nostril
★ Nasal resistanceNasal cavity = 50–70% of total airway resistance
★ Why nostrils collapseSkin over the nasoincisive notch region has NO muscle to hold it open → sucked in on inspiration
★ Resistance and airway radiusResistance rises with the 4th POWER of narrowing → small collapse = big resistance jump
Other negative-pressure contributorsLong trachea = big dead space · laryngeal hemiplegia ("roarer") → more obstruction → more bleeding (tie-back helps)
Diagnosis: endoscopyGrades blood in trachea · keep head UP after the race (head down drains blood out) · not quantitative
EpistaxisBlood from the nostrils · only ~2–3% of horses
★ Best quantitative testBRONCHOALVEOLAR LAVAGE: ~0.5 L saline into the caudodorsal lobe, count red cells
Healing of capillary breaksSmall breaks seal within ~1–2 hours (rabbit studies)
★ Who clears blood from the alveoliALVEOLAR MACROPHAGES → become HEMOSIDEROPHAGES (iron-loaded)
★ Why EIPH is PROGRESSIVEHemosiderophage oxidative bursts + iron → inflammation, SCARRING (stiff lung) · boundary of stiff vs normal lung tears · venous remodeling
Scar tissue and complianceLOWER compliance (stiffer)
★ Venous remodelingAfter many starts, small veins get thick walls/cuffing → can't be pulled open → ↑ pulmonary vascular resistance → even higher pressure
Gross appearanceOld EIPH: dark blue/black caudodorsal lobes · fresh: petechial hemorrhages
★ Furosemide (Salix/Lasix)Diuretic → ↓ blood volume → ↓ pulmonary artery pressure → EIPH ↓ ~50% (fixes INTRAvascular pressure)
Furosemide side effects / why trainers like itHorse loses ~20 L (lighter, faster), alkalizing, brief bronchodilation, used to mask drugs → banned race-day in many places
★ Nasal stripHolds nostrils open → ↓ nasal resistance → LESS negative alveolar pressure → EIPH ↓ ~50% (fixes EXTRAvascular pressure)
Nasal strip placementOver the nasoincisive notch region (not high on the face)
Nasal strip extra benefit~5% less O₂ used by breathing in severe exercise → more O₂ for the legs
Nasal strips in humansDon't help: we can mouth-breathe
Inhaled corticosteroids (long term)Weak evidence they may slow progression (target inflammation)
Other theoriesToo much flow to caudodorsal lung · foot-strike pressure waves summing there (jumpers bleed more) · coagulation defect (no good evidence)
Catastrophic EIPHRare sudden death on the track
GeneticsBleeders in the foundation Thoroughbred gene pool (Bartlett's "Bleeding" Childers)

⚠️ Precision traps

  • Bleeding source = PULMONARY CAPILLARIES, not large airways
  • Furosemide = INTRAvascular (blood volume/pressure) · nasal strip = EXTRAvascular (alveolar pressure)
  • Alveolar pressure is NEGATIVE in inspiration · capillary pressure POSITIVE · they ADD
  • Starts CAUDODORSAL (not cranioventral)
  • Scarring = LOWER compliance (stiffer) ≠ emphysema
  • Endoscopy = screening · bronchoalveolar lavage = quantitative
  • Altitude worsens EIPH via hypoxic VASOCONSTRICTION (↑ pulmonary artery pressure)

Ascending Aorta
📍 Where? From the left ventricle, medial to the pulmonary trunk; very short
🌿 Branches? Right + left coronary aa.
📝 Slide note (professor) Note:
The aorta has 3 segments we want you to identify in the thoracic cavity. The ascending aorta, aortic arch and descending aorta.
The ascending aorta extends cranially from the left ventricle medial to the pulmonary trunk, this segment typically is very short and is covered by thymus tissue and fat/fascia and other vessels, you will likely need to reflect these to better visualize the area (not visible in image)
The aortic arch begins when the ascending aorta makes a sharp turn dorsally and to the left. You also can identify this area as this is where the main arteries branch off the thoracic aorta. (partially visible in image)
The descending aorta runs caudally and is located ventral to the vertebrae, your intercostal arteries branch off the descending aorta.

Thoracic duct
🧭 Course? Cisterna chyli → dorsal thorax (aorta–azygos) → crosses the left esophagus → left brachiocephalic v. / venous angle
⭐ Key fact? Straw/red-brown content, beaded walls; often double
📝 Slide note (professor) Note:
The thoracic duct is the chief channel for the return of lymph from the lymphatic capillaries and ducts of the venus system. It begins in the sublumbar region and travels cranially. Along the way it collects from the abdomen, pelvic viscera and pelvic limbs. It crosses between the crura of the diaphragm where now it should be able to be visualized. It will be found near the thoracic aorta and the ventral border of the azygous vein, it continues cranially to the left brachiocephalic vein where it usually terminates.

This is a very delicate structure, tears very easily. Compare the color difference of the thoracic vein to the nearby sympathetic nerve trunk.

You will need to remove fat and fascia near the descending aorta in order to see the thoracic duct, use caution to avoid inadvertently tearing the duct.

Cranial vena cava
🩸 Formed by? Right + left brachiocephalic vv. (+ costocervical vv.)
➡️ Ends? Right atrium
📝 Slide note (professor) Note:
The cranial vena cava drains into the right atrium. Follow from the base of the heart cranially, a large vein should be on the right side, this is the cranial vena cava.

You may need to reflect thymus and other fat and fascia in the area to help better visualize cranial to the heart.

Depending on the size of your cadaver, cranial vena cava may only span a short distance before other veins converge to form the cranial vena cava.

Phrenic nerve may be overlying here, do not transect this nerve.

Subclavian vein
➡️ Joins? External jugular v. → brachiocephalic v.
📝 Slide note (professor) Note:
The subclavian vein converges with the external jugular vein to form the brachiocephalic vein. The subclavian drains the blood of the forearms.

There are left and right subclavian veins for each forearm.

Descending Aorta
📍 Where? Ventral to the vertebrae, caudally
🌿 Branches? Dorsal intercostal aa. (4th onward), bronchoesophageal a.
📝 Slide note (professor) Note:
The aorta has 3 segments we want you to identify in the thoracic cavity. The ascending aorta, aortic arch and descending aorta.
The ascending aorta extends cranially from the left ventricle medial to the pulmonary trunk, this segment typically is very short and is covered by thymus tissue and fat/fascia and other vessels, you will likely need to reflect these to better visualize the area (not visible in image)
The aortic arch begins when the ascending aorta makes a sharp turn dorsally and to the left. You also can identify this area as this is where the main arteries branch off the thoracic aorta. (partially visible in image)
The descending aorta runs caudally and is located ventral to the vertebrae, your intercostal arteries branch off the descending aorta.

Left subclavian artery
📍 Arises from? Aortic arch directly (2nd branch)
📝 Slide note (professor) Note:
The left subclavian artery differs from nearby arteries as it branches off of the aortic arch further caudally. It passes obliquely to the left across the ventral surface of the esophagus as it heads towards the left forearm.

It then gives off multiple branches to other arteries, the right subclavian gives off the same branches but to the right side.

Again, there will be nerves in this area wrapping around the left subclavian artery, be very careful not to transect these nerves.

Superficial cervical artery
📍 Arises from? Subclavian a.
🎯 Supplies / drains? Superficial neck + shoulder (superficial cervical LN region)
📝 Slide note (professor) Note:
The superficial cervical artery arises from the subclavian arteries opposite to the internal thoracic artery. It travels from the thoracic inlet to supply the base of the neck and the adjacent scapular region.

In this image, the distal portion has been transected and it is lying out of its normal anatomical position.

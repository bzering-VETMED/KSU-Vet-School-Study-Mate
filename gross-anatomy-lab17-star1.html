Right coronary artery
📍 Arises from? Ascending aorta (aortic sinuses): the first branches of the aorta
📝 Slide note (professor) Note:
The right coronary artery leaves the coronary sinus of the aorta. It encircles the right side of the heart in the coronary groove and often extends to the subsinuosal interventricular groove.

The coronary arteries can be difficult to find as they branch off the ascending aorta immediately after the aortic valve. You will need to dig through the fat of the coronary groove to find these arteries.

Notice how the right auricle has been reflected back to help visualize this area.

Cardiac notch
📍 Where? Right lung, between the cranial + middle lobes
🩺 Clinical? Heart touches the thoracic wall here (right side) → pericardiocentesis/auscultation window
📝 Slide note (professor) Note:
The cardiac notch is an area of clinical importance. It is found on the right side.

The right cranial and right middle lung lobes form this natural notch, and in this area lies direct access to the heart. This area is found by finding the 4th and 5th intercostal space. This is the site for pericardiocentesis procedures.

If you already removed the lungs, place the right lungs back into the thoracic cavity to visualized how this space is formed.

Principal bronchi
🧩 Are? Right + left primary bronchi from the tracheal bifurcation
📝 Slide note (professor) Note:
As the trachea is descending in the thoracic cavity, it bifurcates into a right and left principal bronchi to supply the right and left lungs.

At this time, the lungs have already been removed, so you will be looking for where the principal bronchi was transected to allow for removal. Remember, there are several very important nerves and vessels in this area, use caution to not inadvertently transect them.

If you are struggling to find this through all of the tissue, palpate cranially for the trachea rings, and slowly follow that caudally until you feel the bifurcation.

💊 LC30 · Intro to Receptor Pharmacology (Wed 11/4)

Pharmacokinetics (PK)S5"What the body does to the drug": absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination (ADME).
Pharmacodynamics (PD)S5"What the drug does to the body": binding, activation, systemic response.
ADMES6Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Elimination: the four pharmacokinetic processes.
AbsorptionS6How the drug gets into plasma (injection, transdermal, oral).
Intravenous / subcutaneous / intramuscularS6Injection routes listed under absorption on the S6 figure ("Intravenous, Subcutaneous, Intramuscular, etc.").
TransdermalS6Absorption route through the skin.
OralS6Absorption route by mouth.
DistributionS6Where the drug goes in the body: bound in plasma, free in plasma, lipid, tissues A/B/C (S6 figure).
Bound vs free in plasmaS6Distribution states on the slide: drug carried bound in plasma versus free in plasma.
MetabolismS6How the body processes the drug, in liver (or other tissues).
Oxidation, reduction, hydrolysis, conjugationS6Metabolism reactions listed on the slide (liver or other tissues).
EliminationS6How the drug exits the body ("out of the body"); S6 figure pictures kidney and intestine; the S5 diagram labels this step excretion.
Half-lifeS4OUTSIDE — confirm: time for plasma drug concentration to fall by 50%. Listed in objective terms, not defined on slides.
Area under the curve (AUC)S4OUTSIDE — confirm: area under the plasma concentration–time curve; reflects total drug exposure. Listed, not defined on slides.
CmaxS4OUTSIDE — confirm: maximum (peak) plasma concentration after a dose. Listed, not defined on slides.
TmaxS4OUTSIDE — confirm: time after dosing at which Cmax is reached. Listed, not defined on slides.
Minimum effective concentration (MEC)S4OUTSIDE — confirm: lowest plasma concentration that produces the desired effect. Listed, not defined on slides.
MACS4OUTSIDE — confirm: meaning not given on slides; in anesthesia = minimum alveolar concentration. Confirm which meaning Dr. Basel intends.
Minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC)S4OUTSIDE — confirm: lowest antimicrobial concentration that inhibits visible bacterial growth. Listed, not defined on slides.
Therapeutic windowS4OUTSIDE — confirm: concentration range between minimum effective and toxic levels. Listed, not defined on slides.
Binding (PD step 1)S7How the drug binds the receptor; described by KD.
Activation (PD step 2)S7What the drug does at the receptor.
Systemic response (PD step 3)S7What the drug does to the body.
Binding studyS9, S12Cell-free study of drug affinity for a receptor, ignores effect; [DR] plotted against [D]; described by KD.
k1 (kon)S10Association rate constant: how fast the drug binds the receptor.
k-1 (koff)S10Dissociation rate constant: how fast the drug leaves the receptor.
Dissociation constant (KD)S9, S10, S12KD = k-1/k1 (a ratio); drug concentration (M) at which 50% of receptors are bound.
AffinityS10How tightly a drug binds its receptor. Low KD = high affinity; high KD = low affinity.
Michaelis-Menten equationS8Listed in S8 terms; not explained on slides. OUTSIDE — confirm: saturation equation with the same form as the binding curve.
Concentration response studyS11, S12In vitro study of how much signal a ligand causes; [drug] vs cellular effect; depends on binding + signaling; described by EC50.
Half-maximal effective concentration (EC50)S11, S12Drug concentration (M) that elicits 50% of the maximal response in vitro.
ConcentrationS14Mass/volume or moles/volume; good for in vitro, where drug concentration at the receptor is measurable.
DoseS14Mass/weight or mass/surface area; better for in vivo; brings pharmacokinetic principles into play.
Dose response studyS15, S19In vivo study; drug dose vs % population with therapeutic effect; combines PD and PK; described by ED50.
Median effective dose (ED50)S15, S19Drug dose (μg/m2) at which 50% of the population shows a therapeutic effect.
ED99S19Drug dose at which 99% of the population shows a therapeutic effect.
Median toxic dose (TD50)S16, S19Drug dose at which 50% of the population shows a toxic effect.
Median lethal dose (LD50)S16, S19Drug dose at which 50% of the population shows a lethal effect.
LD1S19Drug dose at which 1% of the population shows a lethal effect.
Therapeutic index (TI)S17, S19, S27LD50/ED50, a comparison of doses. Larger TI = bigger gap between therapeutic and toxic doses = generally safer drug.
Relatively safe drugs (TI)S18Amoxicillin (>>100:1), penicillin (>>100:1).
Midrange drugs (TI)S18Morphine (70:1), diazepam (100:1).
Dangerous drugs (TI)S18Cocaine 15:1, amphotericin 4:1, 5-fluorouracil 2.5:1, digoxin 2:1, cisplatin 1.5:1.
PotencyS21Strength of the response a drug produces; S21 compares potency of the response (EC50) with binding affinity (KD).
EC50 = KDS21, S22No spare receptors: 1:1 receptors to pathways; response tracks % receptors bound; binding is the limiting step.
EC50 < KDS21, S22Spare receptors: more receptors than pathways; response exceeds % bound; signaling is the limiting step. S22: effect curve sits left of % bound curve.
Spare receptors (receptor reserve)S21, S23Receptors beyond what is needed for full signal; create redundancy if receptors are deactivated.
Maximal effect (Emax)S23, S25Maximum response of the pathway; lowered by an irreversible inhibitor once receptor reserve is gone.
Irreversible antagonistS23Permanently inactivates receptors; with spare receptors the curve shifts right at full effect until reserve is used up, then Emax falls (S23 figure).
Competitive antagonistS25Shifts the curve right (raises EC50) without lowering Emax.
Non-competitive antagonistS23, S25Lowers Emax no matter the agonist dose; spare receptors can make it look competitive.
PheochromocytomaS2, S24Adrenal tumor secreting catecholamines; surgical danger is hemodynamic instability from massive norepinephrine/epinephrine release.
CatecholaminesS24Norepinephrine and epinephrine, released by the pheochromocytoma.
Alpha-adrenergic receptor (α-adrenoceptor)S24, S26Receptor on smooth muscle mediating catecholamine response; spare α-adrenoceptors exist in many smooth muscle types.
PhenoxybenzamineS2, S25Irreversible, non-competitive alpha-adrenergic antagonist given preoperatively for pheochromocytoma resection.
Hypertensive crisisS26Risk when massive epinephrine overwhelms low-dose phenoxybenzamine because receptor reserve is not depleted.

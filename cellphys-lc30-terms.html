Pharmacokinetics (PK)S4–S5; transcript★ "What the BODY does to the DRUG": absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination (ADME). Basel: brief primer only; pharmacology covers it next year.
Pharmacodynamics (PD)S5, S7; transcript★ "What the DRUG does to the BODY": does it bind? what does it do when it binds? what systemic effects?
ADMES4, S6; transcriptAbsorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Elimination: the four pharmacokinetic processes (Basel: "admi").
AbsorptionS6; transcriptHow (and how fast) the drug gets into plasma: oral (gut), intravenous injection, transdermal (skin), inhalation (lung capillaries).
Intravenous / subcutaneous / intramuscularS6; transcriptInjection routes on the S6 figure; IV is easiest — "I put it in your plasma."
TransdermalS6; transcriptAbsorption route across the skin.
OralS6; transcriptAbsorbed through the stomach or small intestine; may need modification before absorption.
InhalationtranscriptAbsorption through lung capillaries (transcript; not on S6 figure).
DistributionS6; transcriptWhere the drug goes once in blood: bound vs free in plasma, fat (lipid) vs water, tissues A/B/C.
Bound vs free in plasmaS6Distribution states on the slide: drug carried bound in plasma versus free in plasma.
MetabolismS6; transcriptChemical processing by liver (or other tissues): glucuronidation, oxidation, sulfation, reduction, hydrolysis, conjugation.
GlucuronidationtranscriptLiver adds a big sugar (glucuronide) → more water-soluble → kidney excretes it. Basel's acetaminophen example.
EliminationS6; transcriptHow the drug exits: kidney filtration, bile, exhaled by lungs, sweated through skin (S5 diagram says "excretion").
Acetaminophen (PK example)transcriptLiver glucuronidates it → kidney filters and won't reabsorb it → washes out. Mechanism of action unclear (maybe "COX-3"). Overdose (~10,000 mg) lethal.
Half-lifeS4OUTSIDE — confirm: time for plasma drug concentration to fall by 50%. Listed on S4, NOT lectured (pharmacology next year).
Area under the curve (AUC)S4OUTSIDE — confirm: area under the plasma concentration–time curve; total drug exposure. Listed, not lectured.
CmaxS4OUTSIDE — confirm: maximum (peak) plasma concentration after a dose. Listed, not lectured.
TmaxS4OUTSIDE — confirm: time after dosing at which Cmax is reached. Listed, not lectured.
Minimum effective concentration (MEC)S4OUTSIDE — confirm: lowest plasma concentration that produces the desired effect. Listed, not lectured.
MACS4OUTSIDE — confirm: not expanded on slides or in lecture; in anesthesia = minimum alveolar concentration.
Minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC)S4OUTSIDE — confirm: lowest antimicrobial concentration that inhibits visible bacterial growth. Listed, not lectured.
Therapeutic windowS4; transcriptRange between therapeutic and toxic/lethal doses; Basel uses it as the window the therapeutic index describes ("narrow window" for chemotherapeutics).
Binding (PD question 1)S7; transcriptDoes it bind to the receptor, and how well? → affinity, KD. In vitro, even cell-free.
Activation (PD question 2)S7; transcriptWhen it binds, what does it do? → EC50, Emax. In vitro, on cells.
Systemic response (PD question 3)S7; transcriptIn a whole body, what systemic effects? → dose, ED50, TD50, LD50.
Binding studyS9, S12; transcriptCell-free study of affinity only — "does not care about any effects, just binding"; vary [drug] at constant receptor; Michaelis-Menten-shaped curve; described by KD.
k1 (kon)S10Association rate constant: how fast the drug binds the receptor.
k-1 (koff)S10Dissociation rate constant: how fast the drug leaves the receptor.
Dissociation constant (KD)S9, S10, S12; transcript★ KD = k-1/k1; drug CONCENTRATION (M) at which 50% of receptors are bound.
AffinityS10; transcriptHow tightly a drug binds. ★ LOW KD = HIGH affinity; high KD = low affinity. "Affinity isn't necessarily good or bad."
Michaelis-Menten equationS8; transcriptBinding curve follows Michaelis-Menten dynamics again: many open receptors → fast binding; as they fill, fewer binding events; 100% only at infinite concentration.
Concentration response studyS11, S12; transcriptIn vitro (cells): [drug] vs cellular effect; depends on binding + signaling; described by EC50 and Emax ("last lecture" = LC29).
Half-maximal effective concentration (EC50)S11, S12; transcriptDrug concentration (M) that elicits 50% of the maximal response in vitro.
KD and EC50 units + idealS12; transcript★ Both ALWAYS have CONCENTRATION units. In a perfect world KD = EC50 (bind 50% → 50% response).
ConcentrationS14; transcriptMass/volume or moles/volume; needs a defined volume (petri dish, cell-free) — good for in vitro.
DoseS14; transcriptMass/weight (mg/kg) or mass/surface area (mg/m²; slides: μg/m²); no need to know local concentration; PK comes into play. Better for in vivo.
Dose per surface areatranscriptUsed for more dangerous drugs: toxicity scales better with surface area (squared) than weight (cubed).
Dose response studyS15, S19; transcriptIn vivo: y = % of POPULATION responding as wanted; x = log dose; S-shaped curve; combines PD and PK; described by ED50.
Median effective dose (ED50)S15, S19; transcript★ Dose at which 50% of the population shows a therapeutic (positive) effect.
ED99S19; transcriptDose at which 99% of the population shows a therapeutic effect.
Median toxic dose (TD50)S16, S19; transcript★ Dose at which 50% of the population shows a toxic (negative side) effect.
Median lethal dose (LD50)S16, S19; transcript★ Dose at which 50% of the population would be expected to die; ESTIMATED from side effects + animal studies, not dosed to death.
LD1S19; transcriptDose at which 1% of the population shows a lethal effect.
Therapeutic index (TI)S17, S19, S27; transcript★ Classic = LD50/ED50 (use unless otherwise defined). Larger = safer. ED50 ≈ LD50 → "not a drug, it's a poison."
LD1/ED99S19; transcript★ Stronger therapeutic index: treat ~everyone before killing anyone. Want it > 1.
Relatively safe drugs (TI)S18; transcriptAmoxicillin, penicillin (>>100:1; ~100× dose without deaths). Allergy is a separate confounder (immune reaction).
Midrange drugs (TI)S18; transcriptMorphine (~70:1), diazepam (~100:1).
Dangerous drugs (TI)S18; transcriptCocaine 15:1 (cough suppressant), amphotericin 4:1 (antifungal), 5-fluorouracil 2.5:1, digoxin 2:1, cisplatin 1.5:1.
Amphotericin BS18; transcriptAntifungal; fungi are "not all that different" from us → few selective targets → TI ~4; resistant fungi leave no room to raise dose.
Cocaine (pharmaceutical use)S18; transcriptStrong cough suppressant (not a pain reliever); TI ~15.
ChemotherapeuticsS18; transcriptNarrowest TI: given by controlled infusion in hospital with close monitoring.
PotencyS21Strength of the response a drug produces (EC50); S21 compares potency of the response (EC50) with binding affinity (KD).
EC50 = KDS21, S22; transcriptNo spare receptors: 1:1 receptors to pathways (4 receptors, 4 pathways); response tracks % bound; receptor binding is the limiting step.
EC50 < KDS21, S22; transcript★ SPARE RECEPTORS: more receptors than pathways (8 receptors, 4 pathways); 50% bound → 100% response; signaling is the limiting step.
Spare receptors (receptor reserve)S21, S23; transcript★ More receptors than signaling pathways; makes the cell more sensitive to drug and lets it survive receptor loss.
Why spare receptors existS23; transcriptCritical receptors (adrenoceptors) — lose them and "you're basically dead"; reserve lets you absorb a toxin (e.g. snake bite) without losing max response.
Maximal effect (Emax)S23, S25Maximum response; lowered by an irreversible inhibitor only once the receptor reserve is gone.
Irreversible antagonist + spare receptorsS23; transcript★ Shifts EC50 RIGHT with full Emax (LOOKS COMPETITIVE) until the receptor reserve is depleted; then ↓ Emax.
Competitive antagonistS25Shifts the curve right (raises EC50) without lowering Emax.
Non-competitive antagonistS23, S25Lowers Emax no matter the agonist dose; spare receptors can make it look competitive.
PheochromocytomaS2, S24; transcriptAdrenal tumor secreting catecholamines; surgical danger is hemodynamic instability from massive norepinephrine/epinephrine release.
CatecholaminesS24Norepinephrine and epinephrine, released by the pheochromocytoma.
Alpha-adrenergic receptor (α-adrenoceptor)S24, S26; transcriptSmooth muscle receptor for catecholamines; many SPARE α-adrenoceptors in smooth muscle.
PhenoxybenzamineS2, S25–S27; transcriptIrreversible, non-competitive α-adrenoceptor antagonist given pre-op; low doses look competitive (spare receptors); high doses toxic (narrow TI) → still used to "take the edge off."
Hypertensive crisisS26Risk when massive epinephrine overwhelms low-dose phenoxybenzamine because receptor reserve is not depleted.

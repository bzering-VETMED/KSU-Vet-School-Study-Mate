🗣️ LC42 · Physiology 4 · Pulmonary blood flow

Poole, explained simply · read this first, then the terms page

💡 The lung gets ALL the blood the heart pumps, at very low pressure, because high pressure would rip its paper-thin walls.
🖼️ Picture it: A garden hose made of tissue paper: it can carry lots of water only if you keep the pressure low and open more hoses when flow goes up.

The plain-English version

  • Low pressure: pulmonary artery ~25/8 (mean ~15) vs body ~120/80. Proof: pulmonary arteries have thin walls and little muscle. ⭐ Why low? To protect the fragile barrier.
  • Resistance is very low: same blood flow as the body with ~1/10 the pressure drop.
  • Exercise: blood flow goes up 5×, but pressure only ~doubles because the lung OPENS closed vessels (recruitment) and WIDENS open ones (distension). Horses go up ~10× → vessels burst (exercise-induced pulmonary hemorrhage).
  • Two kinds of vessels: capillaries in alveolar walls get SQUEEZED when alveoli are full/pressurized; bigger vessels outside the alveoli get PULLED OPEN when the lung inflates.
  • J-curve: resistance is lowest at normal resting lung volume. Too empty → big vessels narrow. Too full → capillaries stretched thin. ⭐ That's one reason animals don't breathe near full lungs.
  • Ventilator warning: too much positive pressure squeezes capillaries shut → no blood → blood gases get WORSE.
  • Hypoxic vasoconstriction: a poorly ventilated spot shuts its own blood supply so blood goes where the air is (good). At altitude the WHOLE lung does it → pressure up → right heart fails → brisket disease in cattle. ⭐ Fix: go down or give O₂.
  • Gravity: most blood goes to the bottom of the lung (like most air). The top gets more air than blood → higher O₂ there → tuberculosis likes the top.

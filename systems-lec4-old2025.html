Q1Good approximation of the INTRACELLULAR fluid volume in a 1000 kg giraffe?

✅ D · 🔁 Also on the other year's exam. Total body water 60% = 600 L · intracellular = ⅔ = 400 L · extracellular = ⅓ = 200 L (2024 asked extracellular)

Q2A horse gets a hypertonic saline infusion. Resulting fluid shift?

✅ A · Salt stays outside cells → extracellular fluid gets saltier → water leaves cells

Q3A cat becomes hyponatremic after drinking excess water. What explains confusion or seizures?

✅ B · 🔁 Also on the other year's exam. Dilute plasma → water moves INTO cells → brain swells in a closed skull

Q4A dog with liver failure develops ascites. Which Starling change explains it?

✅ B · Liver makes albumin · low albumin = low capillary oncotic pressure (the main in-force) → fluid leaks out

Q5What would you find in the pars radiata (medullary rays)? (select ALL that apply)

✅ A, D, E · Basel (R1): pars radiata = the STRAIGHT tubes running to/from the medulla: thin + thick loops and collecting ducts. Glomeruli, proximal and distal convoluted tubules = pars convoluta

Q6Which component of the filtration barrier provides the PRIMARY selectivity?

✅ C · 🟠 Basel (R2): 'the REAL filter is the fused basement membrane.' Some textbooks say slit diaphragm. Use Basel's answer on his exam

Q7Which would MOST increase GFR?

✅ C · Efferent constriction = ↑ glomerular pressure, ↓ flow, ↑ GFR

Q8LOW sodium at the macula densa causes: (select ALL that apply)

✅ B, E · Low NaCl = 'GFR too low' → afferent DILATES (prostaglandin E2) + renin released (→ angiotensin II constricts the EFFERENT)

Q9Why can NSAIDs cause acute kidney injury in hypotensive animals?

✅ B · Low pressure → kidney relies on prostaglandins to dilate the afferent · NSAIDs block them → GFR and O₂ delivery crash

Q10A patient gets an ACE inhibitor. Likely glomerular effects? (select ALL that apply)

✅ B, C · Less angiotensin II → efferent relaxes → ↓ glomerular pressure → ↓ GFR (B) · less aldosterone → less Na⁺/water kept → ↓ plasma volume → ↓ GFR (C)
⚠️ Not 100% sure. See the note.

Q12Which would be PRESENT in the filtration space (concentration > 0)? (select ALL that apply)

✅ A, B, C, D, E · 🔁 Also on the other year's exam. Basel (R2/R4): <15 kDa = freely filtered (filtrate = blood) · 15–70 kDa = partially filtered (>0 but less than blood) · >70 kDa = none. Hemoglobin (64) is partially filtered → small amount, but > 0

Q13Which would be at EQUAL concentration in the filtration space and blood? (select ALL that apply)

✅ A, B, C, E · 🔁 Also on the other year's exam. Basel (R2/R4): <15 kDa = freely filtered (filtrate = blood) · 15–70 kDa = partially filtered (>0 but less than blood) · >70 kDa = none. Everything under 15 kDa

Q14Which can be measured in blood to estimate GFR? (select ALL that apply)

✅ B, E · 🔁 Also on the other year's exam. Creatinine + SDMA (also cystatin C; BUN is a weaker marker)

Q15Which can be found in the renal MEDULLA? (select ALL that apply)

✅ A, D · 🔁 Also on the other year's exam. Medulla = loops + collecting ducts · glomeruli and convoluted tubules stay in the cortex

Q16Gentamicin causes selective proximal tubule injury primarily because:

✅ B · Filtered aminoglycosides get swallowed by megalin/cubilin and pile up in proximal cells

Q17Which kidney process is ISOSMOTIC?

✅ B · Proximal tubule: water follows solute 1-to-1, so filtrate stays ~300

Q18Which solutes are ACTIVELY absorbed in the proximal convoluted tubule? (select ALL that apply)

✅ A, B, D · Sodium-coupled (secondary active): glucose, amino acids, bicarbonate (via Na⁺/H⁺ exchange) · Ca²⁺ and K⁺ = passive paracellular

Q19Penicillin is an organic anion that binds OAT1 and OAT3. Expect:

✅ A · OATs pull organic anions from blood into the tubule = secretion → cleared fast

Q20Other than Na⁺, water and urea, what happens to solute concentration from the start to the end of the thin loop?

✅ B · Descending limb loses water (not solute) → everything else concentrates; ascending thin limb loses NaCl, not other solutes

Q23A mutation impairing NKCC would cause:

Q24Which diuretic acts on the DISTAL CONVOLUTED TUBULE to inhibit NaCl reabsorption?

Q25How does PTH affect calcium handling in the DISTAL convoluted tubule?

Q27A dog on chronic thiazides has mild HYPOcalciuria. Mechanism?

✅ C · Block NCC → less Na⁺ enters → cell more negative → Ca²⁺ enters via ECaC more easily (Ca²⁺-sparing)

✅ A · α = Acid secretion (H⁺ out) · β = Base (bicarbonate) secretion

Q31The vasa recta preserves the medullary gradient by:

✅ C · Passive hairpin: blood picks up salt going down, gives it back coming up

Q32Which would MOST decrease medullary tonicity?

Q33Which segment has the greatest vasopressin-sensitive UREA permeability?

Q35A dog becomes HYPERnatremic after water deprivation. Hypothalamic response?

✅ B · High osmolarity → osmoreceptors (TRPV1) → thirst + ADH

Q36Best diuretic class for a patient with hypertension AND hypokalemia?

✅ D · 🔁 Also on the other year's exam. Loops and thiazides WASTE K⁺; ENaC blockers / spironolactone spare it

Q37A dairy cow with milk fever (hypocalcemia). Which renal responses are stimulated? (select ALL that apply)

✅ A, C, D · PTH: ↑ CYP27B1 (active vitamin D), ↑ ECaC + calbindin, ↓ claudin-14 · PTH makes the kidney DUMP phosphate

Q38Loop diuretics can worsen hypocalcemia primarily by:

✅ B · No NKCC → no K⁺ back-leak → less lumen-positive charge → less paracellular Ca²⁺

Q39Which hormone gives the FAST drop in plasma K⁺ after a meal?

✅ D · 🔁 Also on the other year's exam. Insulin pushes K⁺ into muscle (fast) · aldosterone = slow, long-term

Q40Which signal causes the LARGEST increase in K⁺ secretion in the collecting duct?

✅ C · High flow opens BK channels (cilia/TRPV4), the 'emergency valve' that dumps K⁺ 'hand over fist' · aldosterone/ROMK is the everyday main regulator
⚠️ Not 100% sure. See the note.

Q41Ethylene glycol toxicity: metabolic acidosis with a HIGH anion gap. The high gap means:

Q42In the distal nephron, ammonia trapping sustains acid secretion by:

✅ A · NH₃ grabs secreted H⁺ → NH₄⁺ (trapped, can't diffuse back) · RhCG moves NH₃, not H⁺

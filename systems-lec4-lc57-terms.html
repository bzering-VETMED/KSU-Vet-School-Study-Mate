Basel · countercurrent multiplication & urea recycling · Fri 11/6 · LC57+58 S4–17 · 28 terms · say it, then tap the pink box

★ NKCC (NKCC2) — Basel: "memorize, have it off the top of your head"Na⁺/K⁺/2Cl⁻ cotransporter in the THICK ascending limb · secondary active NaCl reabsorption (K⁺ leaks back to the lumen) (S5)
★ Why the medulla is salty (3 parts)1 Active NaCl transport in the thick ascending limb (NKCC + Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase) · 2 Different permeabilities of the thin limbs · 3 Countercurrent flow + vasa recta exchange (S5–6)
Gradient NKCC alone can make~200 mOsm across the thick ascending limb wall (S5)
Thin descending limb permeabilityWATER yes · salt no → water leaves into the salty medulla, filtrate concentrates (S5)
Thin ascending limb permeabilityNa⁺ yes (passive) · water no (S5, S7)
★ Thick ascending limbWater-IMPERMEABLE + pumps NaCl out → filtrate becomes HYPOtonic ("diluting segment") (S7)
Countercurrent multiplicationOpposite-direction flow in the two loop limbs multiplies the small 200 mOsm step into a large top-to-bottom gradient (S5–6)
★ What the whole gradient system depends onNKCC · no active NaCl pumping = no gradient at all
Vasa rectaMedullary capillaries running OPPOSITE to the loop · countercurrent EXCHANGE: carry Na⁺ down, water up · preserve the gradient while returning reabsorbed water/solute (S6)
Multiplication vs exchangeMultiplication = the LOOP, powered by NKCC, BUILDS the gradient · exchange = the VASA RECTA, passive, PRESERVES it
★ How far NaCl mechanisms alone get the medulla~600 mOsm (half of the ~1200 maximum)
★ Where the other ~600 mOsm comes fromUREA (up to half the inner-medulla osmoles) → total ~1200 (S14)
Gradient needs constant powerIf NKCC or flow slows, the gradient washes out · very hydrated (diuresis) → less salty medulla
Flow effect on loop reabsorptionSlower flow = more efficient reabsorption + more hypotonic filtrate · faster = less (S7)
Net effect of the loop (S7)Thin loop = water + Na⁺ reabsorbed together (isotonic) · thick ascending = Na⁺ only → hypotonic filtrate to the distal tubule
High vs low GFR at the macula densaHigh GFR = fast flow = HIGH Na⁺ at the distal tubule · low GFR = slow flow = LOW Na⁺ (S9)
UreaNitrogen waste from protein metabolism · freely filtered (S11)
Urea along the nephron (S11)Proximal tubule reabsorbs ~50% · thin loop SECRETES ~60% (more than filtered) · collecting duct reabsorbs ~70% · ~40% excreted
BUN and GFRUrea is filtered but also reabsorbed + secreted; diet and metabolism change it → BUN only roughly tracks GFR (S12)
★ Urea transport is always…PASSIVE · it cannot build a gradient by itself (S13)
Urea in the proximal tubuleFollows water reabsorption (S13)
Urea transporter, thin descending limbUT-A2 (S13)
★ Urea transporters, inner medullary collecting ductUT-A1 and UT-A3 · controlled by ADH (vasopressin) (S13)
★ Urea recyclingWater leaves the collecting duct → urea concentrates → inner-medullary collecting duct lets urea out into the interstitium → urea enters the thin loop → comes back around → gradient builds (S14)
★ High ADH and ureaMore UT-A1/A3 → more urea in the medulla → higher osmolarity → more water reabsorbed → ANTIdiuresis (S15–16)
★ Low ADH and ureaFewer UT-A1/A3 → less medullary urea → weaker gradient → less water reabsorbed → diuresis (S15–16)
What the kidney actually tunes to set the gradientUREA (via ADH) · the NaCl part just depends on flow + NKCC
Medullary osmolarity ruleHigher medullary osmolarity = more driving pressure to reabsorb water (S16)

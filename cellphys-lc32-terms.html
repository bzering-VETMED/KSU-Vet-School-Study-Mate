💀 LC32 · Apoptosis and Oncogenes (Mon 11/9)

Tissue cell numbertranscriptDepends on two things, like birth vs death rate: cells going through the cell cycle vs cells dying.
ApoptosisS5; transcriptProgrammed cell death; most common form (~12 types now described). Controlled, signaled; DNA fragmented, contents not spilled.
Programmed cell deathtranscriptCells told to die (vs cells dying unbidden); apoptosis principles apply to most of its ~12 types.
BlebsS5; transcriptMembrane-wrapped bubbles of cytoplasm the apoptotic cell packages itself into, after DNA is catalytically fragmented.
No inflammation (apoptosis)S5; transcript★ Contents wrapped in membrane, not spilled into the extracellular matrix (ECM) → immune system not triggered: "non-inflammatory form of cell death."
Development (apoptosis role)S5; transcriptNormal apoptosis shaping the body: digit formation (absent → webbed or joined fingers) and neuron pruning (brain/spinal circuits).
Surveillance (apoptosis role)S5; transcriptNormal apoptosis removing damaged or diseased cells.
Maintenance (apoptosis role)S5; transcriptNormal apoptosis retiring cells, e.g. liver cells that get damaged detoxifying are replaced every few months.
Regulation (apoptosis role)S5; transcriptNormal apoptosis removing immune cells after a response, e.g. excess CD8+ (cluster of differentiation 8) T cells.
NecrosisS6; transcript★ Uncontrolled death from acute/severe stress; cell ruptures, cytoplasm leaks → damage signals → inflammation.
DAMPs (damage-associated molecular patterns)transcriptReleased intracellular contents from ruptured cells that signal "something is wrong" → immune activation, inflammation.
HomeostasisS7; transcriptMitosis and apoptosis balanced; tissue cell number stable.
NeoplasiaS7; transcriptMitosis ↑ and/or apoptosis ↓: more cells than the tissue needs (pathology term), e.g. cancers.
DegenerationS7; transcriptMitosis ↓ and/or apoptosis ↑, when pathological = degenerative disease, e.g. neuron loss in Parkinson's.
Apoptosis triggersS8; transcriptCell fate (embryology), removal of trophic/growth factors, DNA damage, immune death signals, extreme Ca2+ release → all converge on caspases.
CaspasesS8; transcriptIntracellular proteases made as inactive procaspases (zymogens); once activated, cut up DNA and package the cell.
ZymogentranscriptInactive enzyme precursor that must be activated (here: procaspase → caspase).
Initiator caspasesS8; transcript★ Caspase 8 or 9 — two initiators = two pathways (9 = internal, 8 = external); activate the effectors.
Effector caspasesS8; transcriptCaspase 3, 6, 7; take apart the nucleus and cell, package it for removal.
Internal pathwayS9–S10; transcript★ Cell monitors itself ("is everything okay?"): Bax vs Bcl-2 → cytochrome C → caspase 9.
External pathwayS12–S13; transcript★ Outside signal (immune system: "you have become dangerous"): FasL → Fas → FADD → caspase 8.
BaxS10; transcript★ "Bax is bad": in the mitochondrial membrane, OPENS pores → cytochrome C escapes → death. ↑ by p53 (DNA damage).
Bcl-2S10; transcript★ Opposite of Bax ("live"): in the mitochondrial membrane, CLOSES pores. ↑ by growth/trophic factors (and AKT).
Cytochrome C (Cyt-C)S10; transcriptNormally in the mitochondrial intermembrane space carrying electrons complex III → IV; in the cytoplasm = "something is really wrong."
Apaf-1S10; transcriptBinds released cytochrome C to build the apoptosome. Expansion not given (OUTSIDE: apoptotic protease activating factor 1 — confirm).
ApoptosomeS9–S10; transcriptLarge cytochrome C + Apaf-1 complex; cleaves procaspase 9 → caspase 9.
Procaspase 9 / caspase 9S9–S10; transcriptInternal-pathway initiator; procaspase 9 cleaved by the apoptosome → caspase 9 → caspases 3, 6, 7.
Trophic (survival) factorsS8–S10; transcriptGrowth/survival signals that ↑ Bcl-2; their REMOVAL ("I must not be needed") triggers apoptosis.
Bax pathway triggersS9; transcriptDamage sensing (DNA damage → p53 → ↑ Bax), removal of trophic factors; figure also: oxidative stress + Ca2+ overload.
Tug of war (Bax vs Bcl-2)S11; transcript★ Constant tug of war; the RATIO decides how close the cell is to apoptosis. Many signals integrate here.
Bax:Bcl-2 balanceS11; transcript↑ Bcl-2/↓ Bax → tissue grows (proliferation); balanced → homeostasis; ↑ Bax/↓ Bcl-2 → regression/degeneration. Not always pathological.
Superoxide (student question)transcriptNot the internal-pathway trigger — it crosses membranes anyway; CYTOCHROME C release is what triggers it.
FasS12–S13; transcriptDeath receptor expressed by almost all cells; FasL binding → Fas HOMOtrimerizes → binds FADD.
FasL (Fas ligand)S12–S13; transcriptAgonist for Fas; expressed by cytotoxic T cells (and some tumors).
FADDS13; transcriptFas-associated death domain; recruited by trimerized Fas; activates procaspase 8.
"DD" = death domaintranscript★ Any protein ending in DD (FADD, TRADD) probably triggers an apoptotic pathway.
TRADDtranscriptTNF-alpha (tumor necrosis factor alpha) receptor-associated death domain; another DD protein.
Procaspase 8 / caspase 8S12–S13; transcriptExternal initiator; FADD → caspase 8, which cleaves MORE procaspase 8 (self-amplifying loop) → 3, 6, 7.
T-cell cytotoxicityS14; transcriptCytotoxic CD8+ T cell recognizes a bad cell (via MHC I, major histocompatibility complex class I), expresses FasL → target dies. One of several weapons.
T-cell homeostasisS14; transcript★ Newly activated CD8+ T cells DON'T express Fas → expand freely; as they mature they express Fas → kill each other → contraction.
Tumor anti-immunityS14; transcriptTumor gains a mutation to express FasL → triggers Fas on CD8+ T cells → kills them faster than they kill it.
OncogeneS16; transcript★ Mutated or overexpressed gene that can cause cancer (the already-mutated form).
Proto-oncogeneS16; transcript★ Normal gene (signaling, cell cycle, apoptosis) that becomes an oncogene when it GAINS activity (mutated, misregulated, overexpressed).
Tumor-suppressor geneS16; transcript★ Gene (damage surveillance, negative cell cycle regulation) that causes cancer when it LOSES activity.
Gain vs loss ruletranscript★ "Look at these pathways and decide": gain of activity → cancer = proto-oncogene; loss of activity → cancer = tumor suppressor.
RasS17–S18; transcriptProto-oncogene, monomeric GTPase; its GTPase activity is the natural off switch. Mutated Ras can't hydrolyze GTP → always on.
MAPK pathwayS17–S18; transcriptMitogen-activated protein kinase cascade downstream of Ras; activates Myc/Jun/Fos → G1/S.
Myc / Jun / FosS18Transcription factors made constantly by mutated Ras signaling; push the cell repeatedly into the cell cycle.
AKT (protein kinase B, PKB)S19–S20; transcriptProto-oncogene; normally on only when PDPK1 activates it; ↑ Bcl-2. Mutated AKT always on → ↑ Bcl-2 → blocks apoptosis.
PI3-K / PIP2 → PIP3 / PDPK1S19; transcriptUpstream of AKT: phosphoinositide 3-kinase makes PIP3; PDPK1 (PIP3-dependent protein kinase 1) activates AKT (LC31).
p53S21–S22; transcript★ Tumor suppressor; critical control component. Normally stops the cycle (p21) AND potentiates apoptosis (↑ Bax). Mutated = can't be activated.
ATMS21; transcriptDNA damage-sensing kinase that phosphorylates (activates) p53.
p21S21–S22Made by active p53; inhibits cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK) so the damaged cell can't advance.
Rb / pRbS21Retinoblastoma protein; binds and inactivates E2F. CDK phosphorylates Rb → E2F released.
E2FS21Transcription factor for DNA replication genes; free E2F goes to nucleus and drives the cell cycle.
APCS23–S24; transcriptTumor suppressor; a kinase only when bound to Axin/GSK3 → phosphorylates beta-catenin. Mutated APC can't bind → no kinase.
Axin / GSK3S23–S24; transcriptPartners APC must bind to act as a kinase on beta-catenin. OUTSIDE (confirm): GSK3 = glycogen synthase kinase 3.
Beta-catenin (Catenin B)S23–S24; transcriptNever phosphorylated when APC is mutated → always to nucleus → way too much cyclin D.
Wnt / LEF/TCFS23Wnt signaling stabilizes beta-catenin; beta-catenin acts with LEF/TCF transcription factors (figure labels).
Cyclin DS24; transcriptCell cycle regulator transcribed by beta-catenin; constant production pushes the cell through the cycle.
Viral oncogeneS25Oncogene delivered by a virus that causes tumorigenesis (NOT TESTED — Basel cut the Viral Oncogenes objective).
vRasS27Viral Ras: constitutively active, cannot hydrolyze GTP (NOT TESTED — Basel cut the Viral Oncogenes objective).
HPV (human papilloma virus)S25Carries viral oncogenes E6 and E7; cause of cervical and other cancers (NOT TESTED — Basel cut the Viral Oncogenes objective).
E6S29HPV protein → ubiquitination and degradation of p53 (NOT TESTED — Basel cut the Viral Oncogenes objective).
E7S29HPV protein that binds Rb so it cannot inhibit E2F (NOT TESTED — Basel cut the Viral Oncogenes objective).
German Shepherd clinical caseS2, S31; transcript9-year-old dog: weight loss, diarrhea, anorexia, nodular colon masses, high mitotic index. Basel: "she has colon cancer ... that's easy."
Anisocytosis / anisokaryosisS2Variation in cell size / nucleus size; biopsy signs of neoplasia in the case.
Colon cancer stepwise mutationsS32 · slides onlyNormal epithelium → APC → Ras (adenoma) → p53 (carcinoma) → metastasis. Slides only; not walked through — likely low priority.
Multiple-mutation principleS32 · slides onlyBecause cell cycle regulation is redundant, cancers rarely come from a single mutation (slides only).

⚠️ Precision traps

  • Bax OPENS mitochondrial pores (pro-apoptotic); Bcl-2 CLOSES them (anti-apoptotic). Don't flip them. (S10)
  • Internal (Bax) pathway → caspase 9; external (Fas/FasL) pathway → caspase 8. Both converge on effector caspases 3, 6, 7. (S8, S9, S12)
  • Initiator caspases = 8 or 9; effector caspases = 3, 6, 7. Initiator activates effector, never the reverse. (S8)
  • Cytochrome C + Apaf-1 = apoptosome. The apoptosome activates procaspase 9, not procaspase 8. (S10)
  • Trophic factors ↑ Bcl-2 (survival). Removal of trophic factors triggers Bax. DNA damage → p53 → ↑ Bax. (S9, S10)
  • More Bax → degeneration; more Bcl-2 → proliferation. Mitosis > apoptosis = neoplasia; apoptosis > mitosis = degeneration (Parkinson's). (S7, S11)
  • Protooncogene = NORMAL gene (Ras, AKT); oncogene = MUTATED version; tumor-suppressor = p53, APC. Ras and AKT are protooncogenes, NOT tumor suppressors. (S16–S24)
  • Mutated Ras cannot HYDROLYZE GTP (stuck ON). It is not 'cannot bind GTP'. Mutated p53 cannot be PHOSPHORYLATED. Mutated APC cannot BIND Axin/GSK3. (S18, S22, S24)
  • E6 → p53 (ubiquitination, degradation); E7 → Rb (binds, frees E2F). Easy pair to flip. (S29)
  • Colon cancer order: APC → Ras → p53 (adenoma after Ras, carcinoma after p53). Mnemonic: A-R-P, alphabetical. (S32)

ApoptosisS5Programmed cell death: DNA fragmented, cytoplasm blebs, contents NOT spilled into extracellular matrix (ECM), so no inflammation.
BlebsS5Bubble-like pieces the cytoplasm splits into during apoptosis, after DNA is catalytically fragmented.
No inflammation (apoptosis)S5Apoptotic cell contents are not spilled into the extracellular matrix, so no inflammatory response.
Development (apoptosis role)S5Normal apoptosis that shapes the body, e.g. formation of digits and neuron pruning.
Surveillance (apoptosis role)S5Normal apoptosis removing damaged or diseased cells.
Maintenance (apoptosis role)S5Normal apoptosis removing old cells, e.g. old liver cells.
Regulation (apoptosis role)S5Normal apoptosis removing excess cells, e.g. excessive CD8+ (cluster of differentiation 8) T cells.
NecrosisS6Uncontrolled cell death from acute stress; cell ruptures, contents spill into ECM, inflammatory. 'Messy.'
HomeostasisS7Mitosis and apoptosis balanced; tissue size stays stable.
NeoplasiaS7Mitosis outweighs apoptosis; new abnormal growth, e.g. cancers.
DegenerationS7Apoptosis outweighs mitosis; tissue loss, e.g. Parkinson's disease.
Initiator caspasesS8Caspase 8 or 9; every apoptosis trigger activates one, which then activates the effector caspases.
Effector caspasesS8Caspase 3, 6, 7; activated by initiator caspases; carry out apoptosis.
Apoptosis triggers (S8 figure)S8Cell fate (embryology), removal of survival factors, DNA damage, death signal, calcium (Ca2+) release; all converge on initiator caspases.
Caspase 9S9Initiator caspase of the INTERNAL (Bax/mitochondrial) pathway; activated from procaspase 9 by the apoptosome.
Caspase 8S12Initiator caspase of the EXTERNAL (Fas/FasL) pathway; activated from procaspase 8 by FADD.
BaxS10Pro-apoptotic protein that OPENS mitochondrial membrane pores, releasing cytochrome C.
Cytochrome C (Cyt-C)S10Mitochondrial protein released through Bax pores; combines with Apaf-1 to form the apoptosome.
Apaf-1S10Partner that combines with cytochrome C to form the apoptosome. OUTSIDE (confirm): apoptotic protease activating factor 1.
ApoptosomeS10Cytochrome C + Apaf-1 complex; activates procaspase 9 into caspase 9.
Procaspase 9S10Inactive precursor of caspase 9; activated by the apoptosome.
Bcl-2S10Anti-apoptotic protein that CLOSES mitochondrial pores, so no cytochrome C release; opposes Bax.
Trophic (survival) factorsS9Survival signals that increase Bcl-2 transcription; their REMOVAL triggers the Bax pathway.
Bax pathway triggersS9Listed: damage sensing (DNA damage via p53) and removal of trophic factors; figure also shows oxidative stress + calcium (Ca2+) overload at mitochondria.
Bax:Bcl-2 balanceS11More Bax → degeneration; balanced → homeostasis; more Bcl-2 → proliferation.
FasS13Membrane death receptor; activated by FasL, trimerizes, recruits FADD.
FasL (Fas ligand)S13Ligand that binds Fas and triggers the external apoptosis pathway.
FADDS13Fas-associated death domain (slide wording); recruited by trimerized Fas; activates procaspase 8.
Procaspase 8S13Inactive precursor of caspase 8; activated by FADD.
T-cell cytotoxicityS14Cytotoxic CD8+ T cell recognizes foreign antigen on major histocompatibility complex class I (MHC I) and uses FasL to kill the cell.
T-cell homeostasisS14During clonal expansion T cells show FasL but no Fas (no signal); old T cells express both Fas and FasL and kill each other.
Tumor anti-immunityS14Tumor cell expresses FasL; it binds Fas/FADD on the attacking cytotoxic CD8+ T cell, so the T cell undergoes apoptosis.
OncogeneS16Mutated/overexpressed gene that can cause cancer.
ProtooncogeneS16Normal gene in apoptosis or cell cycle that mis-regulation or mutation could turn into an oncogene.
Tumor-suppressor geneS16Gene for damage surveillance and negative cell cycle regulation.
RasS18Protooncogene GTPase; mutated Ras cannot hydrolyze guanosine triphosphate (GTP), so it stays active without growth signal.
MAPK pathwayS18Mitogen-activated protein kinase cascade downstream of Ras; activates Myc/Jun/Fos.
Myc / Jun / FosS18Transcription factors made constantly by mutated Ras signaling; push the cell repeatedly into the cell cycle.
AKT (PKB)S20Protooncogene kinase (protein kinase B) downstream of PI3-K; mutated AKT is constantly active → overproduces Bcl-2; figure: also blocks apoptosis and cell cycle arrest.
PI3-K / PIP2 → PIP3 / PDPK1S19Upstream of AKT on figure: phosphoinositide 3-kinase converts PIP2 to PIP3; PDPK1 activates AKT (figure labels).
p53S21Tumor-suppressor; critical control component of cell cycle regulation. Mutated p53 cannot be phosphorylated → no p21.
ATMS21Damage-sensing kinase that phosphorylates (activates) p53 on the figure.
p21S22Made by active p53; inhibits CDK (cyclin-dependent kinase) so the damaged cell cannot advance.
Rb / pRbS21Retinoblastoma protein; binds and inactivates E2F. CDK phosphorylates Rb → E2F released.
E2FS21Transcription factor for DNA replication genes; free E2F goes to nucleus and drives the cell cycle.
APCS24Tumor-suppressor; with Axin/GSK3 targets beta-catenin for destruction. Mutated APC cannot bind Axin/GSK3.
Axin / GSK3S24Partners APC binds to ubiquitinate beta-catenin. OUTSIDE (confirm): GSK3 = glycogen synthase kinase 3.
Beta-catenin (Catenin B)S24Wnt-pathway protein; high when APC is mutated → LEF/TCF transcription of cyclin D.
Wnt / LEF/TCFS23Wnt signaling stabilizes beta-catenin; beta-catenin acts with LEF/TCF transcription factors (figure labels).
Cyclin DS24Cell cycle regulator transcribed by beta-catenin; constant production pushes the cell through the cycle.
Viral oncogeneS25Oncogene delivered by a virus that causes tumorigenesis.
vRasS27Viral Ras: mutated, constitutively active Ras; cannot hydrolyze GTP; signals through MAPK without growth signal.
HPV (human papilloma virus)S25Carries two viral oncogenes, E6 and E7; a significant cause of cervical and other cancers.
E6S29HPV protein that causes ubiquitination of p53 → proteasome degradation; cell loses damage control.
E7S29HPV protein that binds Rb so Rb cannot inhibit E2F; E2F pushes the cell into the cycle.
Colon cancer stepwise mutationsS32Normal epithelium → APC → Ras (adenoma) → p53 (carcinoma) → further mutations → metastasis.
Multiple-mutation principleS32Because cell cycle regulation is redundant, cancers rarely come from a single mutation; they build up multiple.
German Shepherd clinical caseS29-year-old dog: weight loss, diarrhea, anorexia, nodular colon masses, neoplastic cells on biopsy.
Anisocytosis / anisokaryosisS2Variation in cell size / nucleus size; biopsy signs of neoplasia in the case.

The portal vein carries venous blood to the liver from the abdominal viscera such as the stomach, the small intestine, the cecum, the colon, the pancreas and the spleen.

Caudal mesenteric vein
The cranial and caudal mesenteric veins are the distal terminal branches of the portal vein. The cranial mesenteric veins arborizes in the mesentery and collects blood from the jejunum, ileum, caudal duodenum, and the right lobe of the pancreas. The caudal mesenteric vein in the mesocolon drains the cecum and the colon.

Cranial mesenteric vein
The cranial and caudal mesenteric veins are the distal terminal branches of the portal vein. The cranial mesenteric veins arborizes in the mesentery and collects blood from the jejunum, ileum, caudal duodenum, and the right lobe of the pancreas. The caudal mesenteric vein in the mesocolon drains the cecum and the colon. Typically the cranial mesenteric vein is thicker than the caudal mesenteric vein.

Gastroduodenal vein
The gastroduodenal vein is a small, proximal branch of the portal vein in the mesoduodenum. It enters the portal vein from the right side near the body of the pancreas and drains the pancreas, the stomach, the duodenum, and the greater omentum.

Left gastric vein
The left gastric vein dumps into the splenic vein. The left gastric receives blood from the lesser curvature of the stomach. This vein will likely be the easiest to identify if you reflect all of the mesentery to the right side like how you did to follow the splenic artery. Ventral vagal trunk
The ventral vagal trunk is formed by the right and left ventral vagus nerve branches uniting. The ventral trunk unites further cranially than the dorsal trunk, making it much easier to identify in the thoracic cavity. This trunk is found on the ventral surface of the esophagus.

⭐ Nephron osmolarity map (Basel: "know this slide very, very well") · LC57+58 S19 / S32

Tap a pink number (or its row) to reveal.

1 · Filtrate in Bowman's space300 mOsm · freely filtered, so it matches plasma (Renal 2, Rule 1)
2 · Filtrate leaving the proximal tubule300 · salt and water leave TOGETHER, so the concentration doesn't change
3 · Filtrate at the loop tip~1000–1200 · thin descending limb lets WATER out into the salty medulla (salt stays in)
4 · Filtrate at the top of the thick ascending limb~200 · NKCC pumps salt out and water can't follow → hypotonic
5 · Filtrate in the distal tubule~100 · NCC pulls out MORE salt, still no water → very hypotonic
6 · Collecting-duct filtrate at the bottomLow ADH: stays ~100 (dilute urine, diuresis) · high ADH: water leaves until it nears the medulla (~1100–1200, concentrated urine)
7 · Cortex interstitium300 · same as plasma
8 · Outer-medulla interstitium~500–800 · climbing (countercurrent multiplication: NKCC + thin-limb permeability + vasa recta)
9 · Inner-medulla tip interstitium~1100–1200 · about half NaCl (~600) + half UREA (~600, needs ADH)
10 · The arrow: what lets water leave the collecting duct?A PATHWAY: aquaporins (AQP2) inserted by ADH · no pathway = water stays in = diuresis · diuretics shrink the 100-vs-1200 difference = less pull = diuresis

How to read it

  • Purple tube = the nephron; background darkens as the medulla gets saltier.
  • Badges 1–6 = osmolarity INSIDE the tube (filtrate). Badges 7–9 = OUTSIDE (interstitium).
  • The exam idea: very dilute filtrate (100) next to a very salty medulla (1200) = a huge pull on water, used only if ADH opens a pathway.

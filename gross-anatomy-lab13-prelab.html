Stifle · femoropatellar ligament

7To free the lateral fabella and apply caudal traction so the lateral femoropatellar ligament becomes distinct, which cuts preserve the needed attachment?

  1. Cut cranial, distal, and dorsal to the fabella; retain the fascia caudal to it.
  2. Cut distal, caudal, and dorsal to the fabella; retain the fascia cranial to it.
  3. Cut cranial, caudal, and dorsal to the fabella; retain the fascia distal to it.
  4. Cut cranial, distal, and caudal to the fabella; retain the fascia dorsal to it.
Tap to reveal answer
Cut distal, caudal, and dorsal to the fabella; retain the fascia cranial to it.
The femoropatellar ligament is a fascial band between the patella and the gastrocnemial sesamoid (fabella). Preserving the cranial fascia keeps the connection toward the patella so caudal traction on the fabella makes the ligament taut. The exact three-cut instruction is an inference from this attachment relationship rather than wording stated verbatim in the PPT.

Stifle · meniscus

8Why does the medial meniscus move less than the lateral meniscus when the stifle is flexed?

  1. Its attachment to the lateral femoral condyle through the meniscofemoral ligament
  2. Its attachment to the patella through the medial femoropatellar ligament
  3. Its attachment to the medial collateral ligament along its peripheral margin
  4. Its attachment to the fibular head through the lateral collateral ligament
Tap to reveal answer
Its attachment to the medial collateral ligament along its peripheral margin
The medial meniscus is attached to the medial collateral ligament, so its movement is more restricted.
Stifle · cruciate ligament

9With the femur held stationary, the tibia moves excessively caudally. Which tibial attachment should be inspected?

  1. The cranial intercondylar area
  2. The lateral condyle beside the fibular head
  3. The medial edge of the popliteal notch
  4. The medial surface distal to the medial condyle
Tap to reveal answer
The cranial intercondylar area
The cranial cruciate ligament attaches to the cranial intercondylar area of the tibia and limits excessive craniocaudal motion.

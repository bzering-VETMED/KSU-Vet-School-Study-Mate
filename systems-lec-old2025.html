Q1A dog is given a β-blocker. Which change is most likely observed in the SA node?

✅ D · β-blocker → less cAMP → less funny current (HCN) → flatter phase 4 → slower heart rate

Q2In a normal heart, why does the SA node act as the pacemaker rather than the AV node or Purkinje fibers?

✅ A · Fastest spontaneous phase 4 depolarization reaches threshold first and sets the rate (overdrive suppression)

Q3Acetylcholine signaling will cause which of the following in nodal cells?

✅ D · ACh → M2 → ↓cAMP + ↑K⁺ permeability → flatter phase 4 → slower rate

Q4A depolarization wave moving perpendicular to the axis of a lead will produce what ECG effect?

✅ C · Perpendicular = isoelectric lead

Q5Which of the following represents the depolarization of the AV node?

✅ E · AV node depolarization is too small to see; it sits in the PR segment

Q6The bizarre complexes in the ECG marked “C” are likely:

✅ D · Wide, bizarre QRS = ventricular origin (premature ventricular complex). (Answer from the stem; the ECG image isn't in the upload.)

Q7You have a patient with atrial fibrillation. Which could be appropriate treatments? (select all that apply)

✅ A, C, D · LC21–22: rate control with β-blockers (class 2) and Ca²⁺ channel blockers (class 4); cardioversion is used for atrial fibrillation. Lidocaine = ventricular arrhythmias

Q8On a well-prepared canine blood smear, the central pallor of erythrocytes is most evident because:

✅ A · Biconcave disc → thinner center → paler

Q9When comparing fields of a canine smear, an increased number of polychromatophilic cells would indicate:

✅ D · Polychromatophils = reticulocytes = marrow is responding

Q10What is a significant difference in bovine blood compared to most mammals?

✅ B · Ruminants: lymphocyte-predominant leukogram

Q11What is a significant difference in camelid erythrocytes compared to most mammals?

✅ A · Camelid RBCs = small, elliptical, no nucleus

Q12A parrot’s blood smear shows abundant heterophils with rod-shaped granules. This leukocyte population functions analogously to mammalian:

✅ D · Heterophil = bird/reptile neutrophil

Q13Bone marrow smear: a cell with basophilic cytoplasm and a smaller but relatively euchromatic, central nucleus. This cell is likely a:

✅ E · LC33: rubricyte = smaller nucleus + basophilic cytoplasm · metarubricyte = small DENSE nucleus, more eosinophilic

Q14Bone marrow smear: a cell with eosinophilic granules and an oblong (but not dented) nucleus. This cell is likely a:

✅ D · Myelocyte = round/oblong nucleus + specific granules · metamyelocyte = dented (kidney) · band = U-shaped

Q15CBC from a dog: very low total neutrophils and increased band cells. This is consistent with:

✅ B · Bands > or with LOW segs = degenerative left shift (marrow can't keep up)

Q16Low hematocrit, increased MCV, decreased MCHC. Which could cause this? (select all that apply)

✅ C, D · Big, pale cells = reticulocytes = REGENERATIVE anemia → blood loss or hemolysis. Marrow/EPO problems are non-regenerative

Q17In what locations can hematopoiesis occur (including extreme/pathological conditions)? (select all that apply)

✅ A, C · Bone marrow normally; spleen (and liver) = extramedullary hematopoiesis. Kidney makes EPO but not blood cells. ⚠️ Confirm thymus with Dr. Basel
⚠️ Not 100% sure. Confirm with the instructor.

Q18Which signaling molecule is an antithrombotic signal that is lost when the endothelium is damaged?

✅ B · Healthy endothelium makes PGI2 (and NO) to keep platelets away

Q19Which binds to GPIb to facilitate strong adhesion of platelets to damaged vessel walls?

✅ E · vWF bridges exposed collagen ↔ platelet GPIb

Q20Which clotting factor pathway is activated by release of tissue factor (TF, III)?

✅ C · TF + VIIa = extrinsic

Q21Which protein is important for clot degradation and formation of fibrin degradation products?

✅ B · Plasmin = fibrinolysis

Q22Corgi with repeated epistaxis and petechiae. Buccal mucosal bleeding time is prolonged; prothrombin time is normal. Likely:

✅ B · Petechiae + long BMBT + normal PT = platelet/vWF problem (primary). Corgi → think von Willebrand disease

Q23Which cytokine most directly stimulates eosinophil proliferation and differentiation?

✅ C · IL-5 = eosinophils · G-CSF = neutrophils · IL-3 = basophils

Q24Which immune cell type primarily kills virus-infected or tumor cells without prior antigen exposure?

✅ B · NK = innate; cytotoxic T cell needs prior activation

Q25Which innate immune cell secretes numerous cytokines to regulate most other immune cells?

✅ B · LC35: eosinophil functions = killing parasites + REGULATING IMMUNE CELLS

Q26Which cell type is the only antigen-presenting cell able to activate naïve T-cells?

✅ B · Dendritic cells prime naïve T cells

Q27In which region of the lymph node would you find the highest concentration of B-cell follicles and germinal centers?

✅ C · Nodules = outer cortex

Q28What is the predominant lymphocyte type in the paracortex of the lymph node?

✅ C · Paracortex = T-cell zone (thymus-dependent)

Q29Why is it normal for lymphocytes to invade the surface epithelium of the tonsil?

✅ B

Q30What is the predominant lymphocyte type within the PALS of the spleen?

✅ D · Periarteriolar lymphoid sheath = T cells

Q31What is the function of the rod-shaped endothelial cells lining splenic sinusoids?

✅ A · Gaps between rod cells filter out old, stiff RBCs

Q32Where does negative selection of T-cells occur in the thymus?

✅ C · LC37: medullary epithelial cells + dendritic cells delete self-reactive T cells. Positive selection = cortex

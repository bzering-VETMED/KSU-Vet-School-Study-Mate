Amino acid → ureaStart: An amino acid to break down
⬇️ First step?
★ Transamination: amine moves onto α-ketoglutarate
⬇️ Products?
★ Glutamate + an α-keto acid (→ glucose or ketones)
⬇️ Second step?
★ Glutamate is deaminated → NH₄⁺ (ammonium)
⬇️ Why is NH₄⁺ dangerous?
★ Base (↑pH) · K⁺-sized → K⁺ dysregulation · ↑glutamine
⬇️ Where is it detoxified?
★ Liver urea cycle
⬇️ Regulatory enzyme?
★ Carbamoyl phosphate synthetase
⬇️ Its activator?
★ N-acetylglutamate (made when glutamate is high)
⬇️ Output?
★ Urea → blood → kidney excretes it

Liver shunt → hepatic encephalopathyStart: Irish wolfhound with a patent ductus venosus
⬇️ What is bypassed?
★ Portal blood skips the liver (portosystemic shunt)
⬇️ Which amino acids aren't removed?
★ Aromatic amino acids (phenylalanine, tyrosine)
⬇️ What happens to ammonia?
★ NH₄⁺ isn't made into urea → ammonia ↑
⬇️ Ratio change?
★ BCAA:AAA (branched-chain : aromatic) falls, 3:1 → ~1.5:1
⬇️ Other lab sign?
★ Serum bile acids ↑
⬇️ Why brain signs?
★ Neurotransmitter + K⁺ dysregulation → ataxia, seizures
⬇️ Treatment?
★ High-quality, low-protein diet enriched in branched-chain amino acids

Glucocorticoid receptorStart: Cortisol enters the cell
⬇️ Where is the receptor at rest?
★ Cytoplasm, bound to heat shock proteins (HSP70/HSP90), inactive
⬇️ Cortisol binds →
★ Shape (conformational) change
⬇️ What is released?
★ The heat shock protein complex
⬇️ Next?
★ Glucocorticoid receptor forms a dimer
⬇️ Where does it go?
★ Into the nucleus
⬇️ What does it do?
★ Turns on immunosuppressive gene transcription
⬇️ Off switch?
★ Ubiquitinated → proteasome degrades it

Vasopressin (antidiuretic hormone) V1 and V2Start: ↑ Blood osmolality or ↓ blood volume
⬇️ Who senses it?
★ Hypothalamic neurosecretory cells
⬇️ Where is it released?
★ Posterior pituitary → vasopressin into the blood
⬇️ V1 receptor location?
★ Vascular smooth muscle
⬇️ V1 signal?
★ IP3 (inositol trisphosphate) + DAG (diacylglycerol) → contraction → ↑ blood pressure
⬇️ V2 receptor location?
★ Kidney collecting duct
⬇️ V2 signal?
★ ↑ Cyclic AMP → aquaporins inserted → water retained

Histamine → vasodilationStart: Inflammation releases histamine
⬇️ Which receptor, where?
★ H1 receptor on endothelial cells
⬇️ What do endothelial cells make?
★ Nitric oxide (NO), a gas
⬇️ Where does NO go?
★ Diffuses into nearby smooth muscle (paracrine; no surface receptor)
⬇️ Its target?
★ Guanylyl cyclase (intracellular enzyme)
⬇️ Second messenger?
★ Cyclic GMP ↑
⬇️ Result?
★ Smooth muscle relaxes → vasodilation

Bicarbonate bufferStart: Extra H⁺ added to the blood
⬇️ H⁺ combines with?
★ Bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻) → carbonic acid (H₂CO₃)
⬇️ Which enzyme?
★ Carbonic anhydrase
⬇️ Products?
★ Water + CO₂
⬇️ Body's response?
★ Breathe faster → vent CO₂
⬇️ Effect on the reaction?
★ Pulls it further right → more H⁺ removed
⬇️ Bicarbonate pKa?
★ 6.4 (works because it's plentiful and adjustable, not because pKa ≈ 7.4)

Ion trapping (aspirin)Start: Aspirin = weak acid, pKa 3.5
⬇️ Stomach pH 2 vs pKa?
★ Below pKa → mostly protonated → UNCHARGED
⬇️ Can it cross membranes?
★ Yes, uncharged (positive logP) → crosses into cells
⬇️ Extracellular fluid pH 7 vs pKa?
★ Above pKa → mostly ionized (A⁻) → CHARGED
⬇️ Can it cross back?
★ No → trapped → accumulates in extracellular fluid
⬇️ Rule?
★ Weak acid ionized ABOVE pKa · weak base ionized BELOW pKa
⬇️ Ratio (worked out)?
💭 10^(pH − pKa): stomach ≈ 0.03 · extracellular ≈ 3,160 (calculated, not on slide)

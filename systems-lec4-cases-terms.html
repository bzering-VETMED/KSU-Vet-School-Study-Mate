🫘 Renal 11–13 · Case-Based Learning

Basel · Case Sets 1–3 · Wed 11/11–Fri 11/13 · "end of the kidney section" · 48 terms · say it, then tap the pink box

★ Prerenal vs renal vs postrenal azotemiaPRErenal = before the kidney (dehydration) · RENAL = kidney itself loses GFR · POSTrenal = urine can't leave (blockage)
Azotemia vs uremiaAzotemia = ↑ BUN/creatinine · uremia = azotemia causing TOXIC signs (vomiting, nausea)
★ Granular castsTubule cells sloughing off in sheets → one specific tubule cell type is DYING (tubular necrosis)
Tubular epithelial cells in urineSubstantial direct damage to kidney cells (no bacteria = not infection)
★ Fanconi syndromeProximal tubule loses nearly all function → glycosuria (normal blood glucose) + aminoaciduria + proteinuria + bicarbonate loss · acquired, genetic, or idiopathic (Basel: "now I expect you to know that")
★ Case: dog after tetracyclineNormal GFR · everything low in blood, high in urine · granular casts → acquired FANCONI (proximal tubule death)
★ Why tetracycline kills the proximal tubuleOrganic ANION · actively pumped IN from the blood side (basolateral) · only passively leaves into filtrate → very high cell concentration → blocks our 30S ribosomes → cells die · not an overdose
Fanconi treatmentKidney support: fluids ± diuretics to flush · regrowth takes days–weeks
★ Case: Arabian horse, pH 7.05Metabolic acidosis (bicarbonate 8) + high chloride + low pCO₂ (compensation) + NORMAL anion gap + urine pH 8 → TYPE 1 (distal) renal tubular acidosis
★ Anion gapNa⁺ − (Cl⁻ + HCO₃⁻) · NORMAL gap + high Cl⁻ = losing bicarbonate directly (not an added acid)
★ Renal tubular acidosis (RTA) types1, 2, and 4 ("because we can't count")
★ Type 1 (distal) RTACollecting duct ALPHA-intercalated cells can't secrete H⁺ → can't make new bicarbonate → very HIGH urine pH despite acidosis · worst type
★ Type 2 (proximal) RTAProximal tubule can't reclaim bicarbonate (e.g., secondary to proximal tubule death in Fanconi/gentamicin)
Horse tachycardia/arrhythmiaPotassium at the BOTTOM of normal = effectively low (and low K⁺ in urine)
Horse mild creatinine riseMild GFR drop from dehydration (not eating/drinking) · not the main problem
Horse RTA treatmentBicarbonate drip (fluids + bicarbonate) · fixing pH helps fix potassium
Herbivore vs carnivore intercalated cellsHerbivores: more BETA than alpha · carnivores: more ALPHA · both have both
★ IsosthenuriaUSG ≈ plasma (~1.010) · urine not concentrated at all · classic CKD · rules OUT prerenal azotemia
★ Why CKD causes isosthenuria1 ↓ GFR · 2 ↓ tubular flow · 3 medullary gradient washes out (needs flow) · 4 can't reabsorb water
★ HyposthenuriaUSG BELOW plasma (e.g., 1.003, near water 1.000) · points to diabetes insipidus
Normal USG range (Basel)~1.035–1.060
★ Case: Siamese cat, months of PU/PDAzotemia (BUN 50, creatinine 3.0) + isosthenuria (1.010) + high phosphorus + hypertension (165) → CHRONIC KIDNEY DISEASE
Blood pressure cutoff (Basel)150 mmHg = highest you should ever accept
★ Why CKD → high phosphate~80% reabsorbed, ~20% excreted daily · NO secretion pathway · ↓ GFR = can't dump it
★ High phosphate → calcium → PTHPhosphate binds calcium → normal total but LOW ionized Ca²⁺ ("pseudo-hypocalcemia") → ↑ PTH → BONE wasting · soft-tissue calcification
★ CKD and potassium (Basel)Trends HYPERkalemic · low flow = BK channels can't work + ROMK equilibrates fast → K⁺ retained
Flow and potassium ruleToo high flow = WASTE K⁺ · too low flow = RETAIN K⁺ (hyperkalemia)
★ ACE inhibitor / ARB in CKDYES, first choice for hypertension · but ↓ aldosterone adds to HYPERkalemia → monitor K⁺, stop if it climbs
★ CKD diet (Basel)LOW sodium · LOW phosphate · HIGH, high-quality protein (cat is wasting/losing amino acids) · NO extra calcium · NO extra potassium
Phosphate bindersAluminum hydroxide · lanthanum carbonate
★ Case: boxer, USG 1.003Blood values high-normal (concentrated) + hyposthenuria → DIABETES INSIPIDUS
★ DI work-up order1 Water deprivation test · 2 desmopressin: responds = CENTRAL · no response = NEPHROGENIC
High cholesterol in the DI boxerCould hint at Cushing's, but Cushing's shouldn't cause USG this low → DI
★ Treat central vs nephrogenic DICentral = desmopressin · nephrogenic = water + THIAZIDES
★ Why thiazides help nephrogenic DIMatch Na⁺ loss to water loss (lowers hypernatremia) · ↓ blood volume → ↓ GFR → less water lost (counterintuitive)
★ Case: Labrador on gentamicinAcute azotemia + uremia (vomiting) + low urine output + metabolic acidosis + HYPERkalemia + glycosuria/proteinuria + casts → proximal tubule necrosis → ACUTE KIDNEY INJURY
★ Gentamicin vs tetracycline concentrationGentamicin: binds MEGALIN/cubam from the FILTRATE side · tetracycline: OAT from the BLOOD side · both pile up in proximal tubule cells
★ Why the proximal tubule is so sensitive to antibioticsVery ACTIVE cells with high protein turnover → poisoning ribosomes hurts them most · plus they concentrate the drug
★ Why GFR drops in gentamicin AKICasts CLOG the tubules → ↑ Bowman's space hydrostatic pressure → opposes filtration (Starling)
Gentamicin dog acidosisDead proximal tubule = bicarbonate lost → secondary TYPE 2 RTA
Gentamicin dog hyperkalemiaAcidosis (shifts K⁺ out of cells) + low tubular flow
★ Gentamicin AKI treatment1 STOP gentamicin · 2 bicarbonate fluids · 3 glucose + insulin (drive K⁺ into cells) · 4 diuretics to flush casts
Fast potassium-lowering hormoneINSULIN (give with glucose so blood sugar doesn't crash)
★ Case: blocked catUrethral obstruction → POSTrenal azotemia
★ Why a blockage stops filtrationBack pressure: urethra → ureters → tubules → Bowman's space · ↑ PBS → GFR tanks · RAAS activates → vicious cycle
Blocked cat lab pictureAzotemia + hyperkalemia + hyperphosphatemia + acidosis (acute)
Why the blocked cat vomitsUremia + severe metabolic acidosis
★ Blocked cat: what to treat FIRSTHyperkalemia + acidosis (bicarbonate, glucose + insulin) → THEN unblock · the K⁺ can kill it before the unblock

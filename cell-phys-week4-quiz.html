1During mitosis, which dynamic behavior must microtubules undergo to successfully form the mitotic spindle and separate chromosomes?

  1. They must exclusively undergo rapid polymerization to extend the spindle fibers.
  2. They must exclusively undergo rapid depolymerization to pull the chromosomes apart.
  3. They must continuously alternate between polymerization and depolymerization.
  4. They must remain structurally stable and locked in place throughout cell division.
Tap to reveal answer
They must continuously alternate between polymerization and depolymerization.
Mitosis depends on carefully regulated microtubule assembly and disassembly. Either locking them in place or preventing growth disrupts spindle function.

2Why does actin "waste" cellular energy by hydrolyzing ATP after it is incorporated into a filament?

  1. To drive a non-equilibrium state that destabilizes older parts of the filament, allowing the network to be dynamically regulated and recycled.
  2. To provide the necessary thermodynamic energy required to force new actin monomers to bind to the growing filament tip.
  3. To generate a mechanical power stroke that allows the microfilament to contract independently of myosin.
  4. To permanently lock the actin subunits together so that the established cytoskeleton never disassembles.
Tap to reveal answer
To drive a non-equilibrium state that destabilizes older parts of the filament, allowing the network to be dynamically regulated and recycled.
ATP-bound actin is incorporated at the growing end; hydrolysis makes older filament regions less stable and supports treadmilling/recycling.

3A newborn with Epidermolysis Bullosa Simplex has severe skin blistering after mild friction because skin cells tear under low shear stress. Which cytoskeletal system is mutated?

  1. Intermediate filaments
  2. Microtubules
  3. Motor proteins
  4. Microfilaments
Tap to reveal answer
Intermediate filaments
Intermediate filaments provide strong resistance to shearing and deformation, especially in epithelia.

4A Holstein calf with BLAD has severe recurrent infections, poor wound healing, and no pus formation. What is the core cellular deficiency?

  1. A mutation in chemical receptors prevents neutrophils from sensing tissue chemokines.
  2. A structural defect in the neutrophil membrane blocks phagocytosis.
  3. A mutation in CD18 prevents the expression of functional β2 integrins, making neutrophils unable to establish firm adhesion to the blood vessel wall.
  4. A defect in selectins prevents the initial carbohydrate-binding step and rolling.
Tap to reveal answer
A mutation in CD18 prevents the expression of functional β2 integrins, making neutrophils unable to establish firm adhesion to the blood vessel wall.
CD18 is part of β2 integrin/LFA. In BLAD, leukocytes can roll but fail at firm (arrest) adhesion and therefore cannot extravasate normally.

5Which class of cell adhesion molecules strictly requires calcium ions to stabilize its extracellular structure and primarily facilitates homophilic interactions?

  1. Selectins
  2. Integrins
  3. Cadherins
  4. IgCAMs
Tap to reveal answer
Cadherins
Cadherins are calcium-sensitive and usually bind the same cadherin on a neighboring cell.

6Which structural feature best differentiates a hemidesmosome from a focal adhesion?

  1. Hemidesmosomes are highly dynamic while focal adhesions are highly stable.
  2. Hemidesmosomes connect to intermediate filaments, whereas focal adhesions connect to microfilaments (actin).
  3. Hemidesmosomes connect to microtubules, whereas focal adhesions connect to extracellular collagen.
  4. Hemidesmosomes rely on cadherins, whereas focal adhesions rely on selectins.
Tap to reveal answer
Hemidesmosomes connect to intermediate filaments, whereas focal adhesions connect to microfilaments (actin).
Both use integrins for cell–matrix attachment, but the intracellular cytoskeletal partner differs.

7A proline hydroxylase inhibitor is used experimentally in a fibrotic disease. What is the structural consequence for collagen at normal body temperature?

  1. Collagen forms a triple helix but becomes a hollow fluid-filled tube.
  2. Collagen alpha-chains are chopped into amino acids before translation ends.
  3. Individual collagen alpha-chains fail to fold and wrap around one another into a stable triple helix conformation.
  4. Mature triple helices form normally but cannot be secreted.
Tap to reveal answer
Individual collagen alpha-chains fail to fold and wrap around one another into a stable triple helix conformation.
Hydroxyproline helps stabilize the tight collagen triple helix. Blocking proline hydroxylation undermines stable helix formation.

8How does the chemical structure of GAGs produce their primary physical function in weight-bearing tissues such as cartilage?

  1. Alternating positive and negative charges create permanent covalent cross-links.
  2. Their hydrophobic nature excludes water and forms a greasy barrier.
  3. Their neutral charge allows dry crystalline packing.
  4. Their high density of negative charges attracts water, creating a hydrated gel that resists compressive forces.
Tap to reveal answer
Their high density of negative charges attracts water, creating a hydrated gel that resists compressive forces.
GAGs are highly negatively charged and highly hydrated; they act like sponges and resist compression.

9How do adherens junctions and desmosomes differ in their intracellular organization?

  1. Adherens connect to microtubules; desmosomes anchor extracellular collagen.
  2. Adherens join microfilament (actin) networks across cells; desmosomes join intermediate filament networks.
  3. Adherens form impermeable barriers; desmosomes form cytoplasmic channels.
  4. Adherens use IgCAMs; desmosomes use selectins.
Tap to reveal answer
Adherens join microfilament (actin) networks across cells; desmosomes join intermediate filament networks.
Adherens couple neighboring actin networks through cadherins/catenins; desmosomes couple intermediate filament networks through desmosomal cadherins and plaque proteins.

Renal 1

Q1A 600 kg horse. About how much INTERSTITIAL fluid does it have?

✅ A · Water 60% = 360 L · extracellular ⅓ = 120 L · interstitial ≈ ¾ of extracellular = 90 L (plasma ≈ 30 L)

Q2A dog gets a large IV volume of 5% dextrose (free water once the glucose is used). Once water finishes moving, which is true?

✅ D · Free water spreads into BOTH compartments (no salt to hold it outside) → both bigger, both more dilute

Q3Which statement about the hole-in-the-bucket model is INCORRECT?

✅ C · No sensor, no set point → steady state but NOT homeostasis
Renal 2

Q4Which change would MOST increase net filtration at the START of the glomerular capillary?

✅ B · Efferent constriction ↑ glomerular hydrostatic pressure · the other three all reduce filtration

Q5Which are true of the fused glomerular basement membrane? (select ALL that apply)

✅ B, C, D, E · Mesangial cells are support only · the membrane is negative, so it repels albumin and favors cations

Q6Which would be filtered, but at a LOWER concentration in the filtrate than in plasma?

✅ A · Basel: <15 kDa = equal · 15–70 kDa = partial (>0 but lower) · albumin essentially none (negative)

Q7Under anesthesia a dog's mean arterial pressure falls to 60 mmHg. Best description of the kidney?

✅ D · Autoregulation works ~80–170 · below 80 the afferent is already wide open

Q8Renal plasma flow falls by half while glomerular pressure is held constant. What happens?

✅ C · Fewer 'sips' per minute (GFR ↓), but each sip is filtered harder (filtration fraction ↑)

Q9Which statement about the efferent arteriole is INCORRECT?

✅ B · Myogenic autoregulation is mostly the AFFERENT arteriole
Renal 3

Q10A dog on carprofen (an NSAID) becomes hypotensive under anesthesia and is azotemic the next day. Best explanation?

✅ A · Hypotension → kidney relies on prostaglandin E2 to keep the afferent open → NSAID removes it → GFR + O₂ crash

Q11Why is BUN a poor marker of GFR? (select ALL that apply)

✅ A, B, D, E · Urea is freely filtered, but reabsorption, secretion, diet and hydration all change BUN

Q12Which GFR marker is freely filtered, then reabsorbed AND destroyed in the proximal tubule?

✅ D · Cystatin C never returns to blood, so its blood level tracks filtration

Q13The renal clearance of drug X is much HIGHER than GFR. The most likely explanation is that X is:

✅ C · Clearance = GFR only if not reabsorbed or secreted · secretion adds extra removal → clearance > GFR

Q14Which does ANP do? (select ALL that apply)

✅ A, B, C, D · ANP shuts down everything that keeps salt and water

Q15A dog vomited for 2 days. BUN 70 (7–27), creatinine 1.4 (0.5–1.5), USG 1.045. Most likely?

✅ B · BUN ↑ with normal creatinine + highly concentrated urine = the kidney works; it's just dry

Q16Why can efferent constriction put the MEDULLA at risk of hypoxia?

✅ A · Less blood (O₂) reaches the medulla while more filtered Na⁺ must be pumped (O₂ demand ↑)
Renal 4

Q17Which proximal tubule transporter uses ATP directly?

✅ D · Basel: the proximal tubule spends ATP on ONE pump · everything else is secondary active

Q18Why does the lumen of the LATE proximal tubule become positive?

✅ C · Lumen-positive #1 · (ROMK back-leak = #2, thick ascending limb)

Q19Which drug class is concentrated in proximal tubule cells by FILTRATE-side endocytosis?

✅ B · Megalin/cubam swallow filtered aminoglycosides · tetracyclines/penicillins enter by OAT from the blood side

Q20Expected effects of acetazolamide? (select ALL that apply)

✅ A, B, C, D · Blocks carbonic anhydrase → bicarbonate not reclaimed → lost in urine → blood acidic, urine alkaline

Q21On a slide you see pale cells with clear borders bulging into a wide, clean lumen, and the tubule branches. What is it?

✅ A · Only collecting ducts (and vessels) branch · proximal = tall, pink, messy lumen

Q22Which statement about the proximal tubule is INCORRECT?

✅ D · The proximal tubule never enters the medulla (its straight part is in the medullary ray)
Renal 5

Q23Filtrate osmolarity at the end of the proximal tubule, the tip of a long loop, and the end of the distal tubule is about:

✅ C · Slide 19: iso → hyper at the tip → most hypotonic at the distal tubule

Q24Which segment is water-impermeable AND contains PTH-regulated active calcium entry?

✅ B · ECaC + calbindin + NCX/PMCA · the thick ascending limb is waterproof too, but its Ca²⁺ is passive

Q25A racehorse receives furosemide before every race. Which disturbances are expected over time? (select ALL that apply)

✅ A, B, C · Blocking NKCC wastes Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ (less lumen-positive charge)

Q26Which statement about aquaporins in principal cells is INCORRECT?

✅ A · Apical = ADH Adds · Basolateral = Built-in

Q27A cow develops chronic respiratory acidosis. Over time, its collecting duct shifts toward:

✅ D · Kidney compensates by switching toward alpha cells (acid out, new bicarbonate in)
Renal 6

Q28Which is NOT required for countercurrent multiplication?

✅ C · The vasa recta is PASSIVE (exchange) · it preserves but doesn't build the gradient

Q29A dog is in a water diuresis (low ADH). What happens to medullary urea?

✅ B · Low ADH → fewer urea transporters → less urea recycling → weaker gradient

Q30Which statement about urea is INCORRECT?

✅ A · Urea is always passive; ADH just opens the transporters
Renal 7

Q31Per slide 19, which would cause a diuresis? (select ALL that apply)

✅ A, C, E · Shrink the filtrate–medulla difference or take away water's pathway → diuresis

Q32When hypothalamic osmoreceptor neurons shrink, which channel opens to trigger ADH release?

✅ D · TRPV4 = flow sensor for BK · TRPV5 = distal calcium channel

Q33Angiotensin II stimulates the release of which hormones? (select ALL that apply)

✅ B, C · Salt (aldosterone) + water (ADH) together → volume ↑

Q34A dog with PU/PD has USG 1.004. After supervised water deprivation, USG is 1.030. Best plan?

✅ C · Concentrates on its own = kidney and ADH work = primary polydipsia

Q35Why does uncontrolled diabetes mellitus cause polyuria?

✅ B · Above ~350 mg/dL, SGLTs saturate → glucose acts as an osmolyte in the filtrate

Q36An acidotic dog needs volume replacement. Which fluid is the best choice?

✅ A · 0.9% NaCl adds chloride → worsens acidosis · balanced fluids avoid it

Q37Which statement about ADH is INCORRECT?

✅ D · ENaC = aldosterone
Renal 8

Q38A dog has hypoparathyroidism (no PTH). Which renal changes are expected? (select ALL that apply)

✅ A, C, D, E · No PTH → nothing boosts ECaC/CYP27B1, nothing blocks claudin-14, nothing removes NaPi-IIa → more Ca²⁺ lost, phosphate kept

Q39Where is MOST filtered calcium reabsorbed, and is it hormone-regulated?

✅ C · ~65% passive (lumen-positive #1) · the distal 8% is the regulated part

Q40Which pairing of source and kidney effect is correct for FGF23?

✅ B · Bone's phosphate alarm → phosphate dumped

Q41Which is a CALCITONIN effect in the kidney?

✅ A · Calcitonin blocks the DOOR (claudin-16) · PTH blocks the BLOCKER (claudin-14)
Renal 9

Q42A dehydrated dog with low blood volume is also hypokalemic. Why does it lose very little potassium in the urine?

✅ D · Aldosterone is HIGH for volume, but low flow means BK stays off and ROMK equilibrates

Q43On a very low-potassium diet, which cell adds potassium reabsorption, and how?

✅ C · Lets the kidney keep up to ~98% of filtered K⁺

Q44Na⁺ 150, Cl⁻ 105, HCO₃⁻ 15. Anion gap and best interpretation?

✅ B · 150 − (105 + 15) = 30 → GOLD MARK

Q45Acidosis, normal anion gap, K⁺ 6.6 (high), urine pH 5.1, normal creatinine, no glucosuria. Most likely?

✅ A · High K⁺ + can still acidify = low aldosterone signaling

Q46Which are features of TYPE 1 (distal) renal tubular acidosis? (select ALL that apply)

✅ B, C, D, E · Glucosuria = type 2 (proximal/Fanconi)

Q47Why is ammonia the kidney's main buffer in CHRONIC acidosis?

✅ D · Phosphate is limited to what's filtered · ammonia production can be ramped up

Q48Which acidosis has a NORMAL anion gap?

✅ C · Nothing new added; chloride displaced bicarbonate

Q49An acidotic patient has a potassium at the LOW end of normal. What does this suggest?

✅ B · Acidosis should push K⁺ UP · a low-normal value means the body is actually short (type 1 RTA horse)
Renal 10

Q50During bladder FILLING, which receptor pairing is active?

✅ A · Storage = sympathetic: balloon relaxes (β₃), knot tied (α₁)

Q51A dog with a T13 spinal cord lesion has a small, spastic bladder that empties uncontrollably. Best drug?

✅ D · Upper motor neuron = Uncontrolled squeezing → block M3 (Oxybutynin = Off)

Q52A spayed female dog leaks urine while sleeping; neurologic exam is normal. Best drug?

✅ C · Weak sphincter → α₁ agonist tightens it

Q53Why is phase 2 of the cystometrogram nearly flat?

✅ B · Isobaric phase = storage (sympathetic β₃)
Cases

Q54A dehydrated dog has USG 1.010 on three samples, with high BUN and creatinine. Best explanation?

✅ A · Persistent isosthenuria in a dry animal = can't concentrate = lost nephrons

Q55A blocked male cat has K⁺ 7.4 and pH 7.08. What comes FIRST?

✅ D · Stabilize K⁺ and acidosis before unblocking (Basel)

Q56Why does GFR fall in gentamicin acute kidney injury even though the glomerulus isn't damaged?

✅ C · Starling: back pressure from clogged tubules opposes filtration

Q57Why does a CKD cat trend toward hyperkalemia?

✅ B · Basel: too little flow = retain K⁺

Q58In CKD, high phosphate eventually causes bone wasting. Which chain is correct?

✅ A · PTH then pulls calcium from bone

Q59Which belongs in Basel's CKD diet for a hypertensive, wasting cat? (select ALL that apply)

✅ A, B, C · No extra calcium (deposits) or potassium (already trending high)

Q60A Border Collie swam all afternoon; now Na⁺, K⁺ and Cl⁻ are all low and it is seizing. Best immediate treatment?

✅ D · Diuretics are too slow; pull water out of swollen neurons now

Q61A dog drank antifreeze 1 hour ago and looks normal. What is the most specific early treatment?

✅ C · Ethanol blocks conversion of ethylene glycol to its toxic metabolites

Q62A urinalysis shows protein ONLY: no glucose, no casts, normal USG. Most likely?

✅ B · Leaky filter delivers more protein than the proximal tubule can take back

Q63Urine shows very HIGH sodium with a low USG. Which are possible causes? (select ALL that apply)

✅ A, C, D · Natriuresis · hyperaldosteronism and a low-salt diet would make urine sodium LOW

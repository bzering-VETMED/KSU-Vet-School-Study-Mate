❤️ LO1 · Main arterial chain

Main arterial chain of the pelvic limb (slide 4)?Abdominal aorta → external iliac → femoral → popliteal → cranial tibial → dorsal pedal → perforating metatarsal → deep plantar arch → plantar metatarsal arteries
Abdominal aorta terminates as…2 external iliac, 2 internal iliac, 1 median sacral a.
External iliac a. terminates as…Femoral a. and deep femoral a.
Femoral a. becomes the popliteal a. after…The distal caudal femoral a. comes off (as it enters the popliteus region)
Popliteal a. divides into…Cranial tibial a. (main) + caudal tibial a. (small, often not injected)
Cranial tibial a. becomes…Dorsal pedal a. — name change only, no branching, at the tarsus
Dorsal pedal pulse is most useful in…Large animals, especially horses (also humans)
Main blood supply to the digits?Plantar metatarsal arteries (the bulk)
Thoracic limb arch vs pelvic limb arch?Thoracic = superficial palmar arch; pelvic = deep plantar arch

🌿 Deep femoral a. (slide 5)

First branch of the deep femoral a.?Pudendoepigastric trunk
Pudendoepigastric trunk branches?External pudendal a. ('pudendo-') + caudal epigastric a. ('-epigastric')
Deep femoral a. becomes ___ after the pudendoepigastric trunkMedial circumflex femoral a.
Medial circumflex femoral branches?Deep branch, transverse branch, obturator branch
What is a 'trunk' (arteries)?A short common stem that branches like a Y (e.g., costocervical, brachiocephalic)

🩸 Femoral a. & femoral triangle (slide 5)

Vascular lacuna = ?Base of the femoral triangle (think cone); femoral a. enters, femoral v. exits
Where do you take the femoral pulse?Femoral a. in the femoral triangle / vascular lacuna
Superficial circumflex iliac a. — origin and side?Femoral a., cranial aspect
Deep circumflex iliac a. — origin?Abdominal aorta (to the caudal lateral abdominal wall)
Lateral circumflex femoral a. — origin?Femoral a., caudal aspect, about the same level as the superficial circumflex iliac
Thoracic limb equivalent of the circumflex femorals?Cranial + caudal circumflex humeral aa. (caudal from subscapular, cranial from axillary)
Caudal femoral arteries?Proximal, middle, distal — all from the caudal aspect of the femoral a.
Descending genicular a. goes to…The stifle (genu = knee); from the cranial aspect
Saphenous a. — origin, size, travel partners?Femoral a. (cranial aspect); small, superficial, often cut when skinning; runs with the saphenous n. + MEDIAL saphenous v.
Saphenous a. divides into…Cranial branch + caudal branch

🐾 Distal limb & paw arteries (slides 6–7)

Dorsal pedal a. terminates as…Arcuate a. (runs in an arc)
Arcuate a. gives off…Dorsal metatarsal arteries
Which artery gives the perforating metatarsal a.?Dorsal metatarsal a. II (between metatarsals II and III)
Why 'perforating'?It perforates from the dorsal side to the plantar side
Perforating metatarsal a. ends as…Deep plantar arch → plantar metatarsal arteries
Superficial vs deep naming (both surfaces)?Superficial = common digital arteries; deep = metatarsal arteries
Four sets on slide 7?Dorsal common digital, dorsal metatarsal, plantar metatarsal, plantar common digital

💙 LO2 · Veins

Superficial veins?Lateral saphenous v. + medial saphenous v.
Deep veins?Femoral v. + deep femoral v.
Deep drainage, femoral v. → heart?Femoral v. (exits vascular lacuna) + deep femoral v. → external iliac v. + internal iliac v. → common iliac v. → caudal vena cava
Common iliac: vein, artery, or both?Vein only — no common iliac artery
Venipuncture site in the DOG (pelvic limb)?Cranial branch of the LATERAL saphenous v.
Forelimb vs hind limb venipuncture (dog)?Forelimb = cephalic v.; hind limb = cranial branch of the lateral saphenous v.
Lateral saphenous v. disappears where, into what?At the popliteal lymph node (caudal to the stifle) → joins the distal caudal femoral v.
Venipuncture site in the CAT?MEDIAL saphenous v. (preferred site)
Medial saphenous v. joins…Femoral v., in the femoral triangle
Two veins that form the cranial vena cava? (review)Right + left brachiocephalic vv. (each = external jugular + subclavian)
Two veins that form the portal vein? (review)Cranial mesenteric + caudal mesenteric vv.

🟢 LO3 · Lymphatics

Constant vs inconstant vs terminal lymph node?Constant = always present; inconstant = sometimes (varies by dog/breed); terminal = all lymph from a region passes through it
Constant lymph nodes?Popliteal, superficial inguinal, medial iliac
Palpable lymph nodes?Popliteal (immediately caudal to the stifle) + superficial inguinal
Inconstant lymph nodes?Iliofemoral (= deep inguinal) + femoral
Single terminal lymph node of the pelvic limb?Medial iliac lymph node
Single terminal lymph node of the THORACIC limb?None
Medial iliac ln. location?At the termination of the abdominal aorta, near the deep circumflex iliac a.
Medial iliac ln. also drains…Testes (male); ovaries + part of the uterine body/horns (female)
Where is the iliofemoral (deep inguinal) ln.?Entrance of the femoral triangle
Where is the femoral ln.?Medial thigh, between the popliteal and superficial inguinal nodes
Lymph route (slide 17)?Popliteal ln. → medial iliac ln. → cisterna chyli → thoracic duct → cranial vena cava
Cisterna chyli?Large lymph cistern caudal to the diaphragm; collects chyle + lymph from the medial iliac nodes
Chyle vs chyme?Chyle = fat-rich milky lymph in lacteals; chyme = partly digested food in the stomach
Thoracic duct runs where, ends where?Roof of the thoracic cavity → joins the cranial vena cava
Multicentric lymphoma (slide 18)?Multiple swollen lymph nodes — here, swollen popliteal nodes
Thoracic limb constant vs inconstant nodes?Axillary = constant; accessory axillary = inconstant

⚡ LO4 · Lumbosacral plexus (slides 20–21)

Lumbosacral plexus is made of…Ventral branches of spinal nerves L3–S3 (dorsal branches go to epaxial muscles)

S1–S3 nerve at the sacral end of slide 21?Pudendal n. — slide mislabels it 'superficial fibular'
Pudendal n. goes to…Pelvic viscera + external anal sphincter (via caudal rectal n.)
Ilioinguinal n. (seen where)?Lateral abdominal wall (L3)
Lateral cutaneous femoral n. runs with…Deep circumflex iliac a. + v.
Genitofemoral n. exits with…External pudendal a. + v. through the superficial inguinal ring
Femoral n. goes to…Quadriceps (disappears deep into the quads)
Obturator n. goes to…Adductor muscles, medial thigh
Biggest nerve of the plexus?Sciatic n.
Sciatic n. divides into…Common fibular (cranial division) + tibial n. (caudal division)
Common fibular = ?Common peroneal (old name) → superficial fibular + deep fibular
Caudal cutaneous femoral n. supplies…Skin of the caudal thigh
Dorsal n. of the penis runs with…Dorsal a. of the penis
Lumbosacral trunk?Common nerve stem giving cranial gluteal, caudal gluteal, caudal rectal + sciatic ('not sure you need to know')

Femoral n. →Iliopsoas, quadriceps, sartorius

Autonomous zone of the lateral cutaneous femoral n.?Lateral thigh, cranial to the caudal cutaneous femoral zone

🧬 LO1 · Brain development & divisions

3 parts of the brain (slide 4)?Cerebrum, cerebellum, brain stem
What connects the brain stem to the cerebrum and cerebellum (slide 4)?Peduncles
Brain stem is continuous with… and most cranial nerves arise from… (slide 4)Continuous with the spinal cord (shares some features with it); most cranial nerves arise from the brain stem
Brain and brainstem develop from… (slide 5)The neural tube
Lumen of the neural tube in the brain becomes… (slide 5)The ventricles and the mesencephalic aqueduct
3-vesicle stage, rostral → caudal (slide 5)?Prosencephalon (forebrain) → mesencephalon (midbrain) → rhombencephalon (hindbrain)
5-vesicle stage, rostral → caudal (slides 6–7)?Telencephalon → diencephalon → mesencephalon → metencephalon → myelencephalon
Prosencephalon divides into… (slide 6)Telencephalon (yellow) + diencephalon (peach)
Rhombencephalon divides into… (slide 6)Metencephalon (blue) + myelencephalon (pink)
Which 3-vesicle segment does NOT divide (slide 6)?Mesencephalon — remains one segment
Adult derivative of each vesicle (slides 7, 9, 18, 25, 28, 33)?Telencephalon = cerebrum (hemispheres, olfactory bulb) · diencephalon = epithalamus, thalamus, metathalamus, hypothalamus · mesencephalon = midbrain · metencephalon = cerebellum + pons · myelencephalon = medulla oblongata
Structures labeled on the adult-regions median section (horse, slide 7)?Cerebrum, commissure of the cerebral hemispheres, choroid plexus of the 3rd ventricle, epiphysis (pineal), interthalamic adhesion, olfactory bulb, optic nerve (II), hypophysis, mammillary body, pons, medulla oblongata, cerebellum, choroid plexus of the 4th ventricle

🧠 LO2 · Telencephalon (cerebrum)

Cerebrum: how many hemispheres and lobes each (slide 9)?2 hemispheres; each has 4 lobes
4 lobes of each cerebral hemisphere (slide 9)?Frontal, parietal, temporal, occipital
Lumen of each cerebral hemisphere (slide 9)?The lateral ventricle
Surface of the cerebrum has… (slide 9)Gyri (ridges) and sulci (grooves)
Numbered functional areas of the cerebrum, dorsal view (slide 10)?1 motor cortex · 2 overlap · 3 somatosensory cortex · 4 auditory cortex · 5 visual cortex
Named sulcus on the dorsal cerebrum (slide 10)?Cruciate sulcus (motor cortex lies around it rostrally; somatosensory caudal)
Which cortex is most caudal (occipital) on slide 10?Visual cortex (5)
Caudate nucleus is a… (slide 11)Basal nucleus
Definition of basal nuclei (slide 11)?Nuclei found deep within the white matter of the brain
Main role of basal nuclei (slide 12)?Motor control (subcortical neuron groups); also motor learning, executive functions and behavior, emotions
Slide 12 figure numbers 1, 2, 3?1 motor cortex · 2 cerebellum · 3 basal nuclei (4–8 other nuclei)
3 types of communication fibers in cerebral white matter (slide 13)?Projection, commissural, association
Projection fibers (slide 13)?Ascending/descending fibers to and from the brain stem and spinal cord
Commissural fibers (slide 13)?Go between hemispheres
Association fibers (slide 13)?Stay within the same hemisphere
Internal capsule: what is it and can you see it (slides 13–14)?Projection fibers connecting the cerebral hemisphere to the brain stem; can't see without cutting into the brain
Internal capsule is continuous with… (slide 14)Crus cerebri and cerebral peduncle (3 bottom figure)
Internal capsule figure numbers (slide 14)?7 on the left figure; 1 on the right figure
3 commissural structures visible on a sagittal section (slides 13, 15)?Fornix, corpus callosum, rostral commissure
Fornix connects… (slide 15)The hippocampus with the diencephalon and cerebrum
Hippocampal commissure = ? and where (slide 15)?Commissure of the fornix; at the caudal part of the fornix
Rostral commissure (RC) fibers connect… (slide 15)The olfactory components of each hemisphere
Corpus callosum (slide 15)?Axons crossing between hemispheres (largest commissure, dorsal on sagittal section)
Other labels on the sagittal white-matter photo (slide 15)?Frontal lobe, occipital lobe, commissure of the fornix, fornix, corpus callosum, rc (rostral commissure), lam (lamina terminalis — outside knowledge, confirm)
Hippocampus: what is it (slides 14, 16)?An inward fold (invaginated gyrus) of the cerebrum that projects into / ends up in the floor of the lateral ventricle
Hippocampus belongs to which functional system (slide 16)?Principal component of the limbic system
Labels on the sheep brain hippocampus dissection (slide 16)?Corpus callosum, caudate nucleus, fimbria, cerebral white matter, cerebral cortex, septal region, hippocampus (floor of lateral ventricle)

🧠 LO2 · Diencephalon

Diencephalon position (slide 18)?Rostral-most part of the brainstem; ventral to the fornix, medial to the internal capsule
4 parts of the diencephalon (slide 18)?Epithalamus, thalamus, metathalamus, hypothalamus
Lumen of the diencephalon (slide 18)?Third ventricle
3 epithalamus components (slide 19)?Stria habenularis; habenular nuclei + interconnecting fibers (habenular commissure); pineal gland
Habenular commissure location (slide 19)?TINY, rostroventral to the pineal (not shown)
Pineal gland other name and species note (slide 19)?Epiphysis; dog has a small pineal, sheep a very large one
Thalamus function (slide 21)?Important relay center
Interthalamic adhesion (slides 21, 49)?Connects right and left thalamic halves across the midline; surrounded by the third ventricle
Metathalamus major structures + functions (slide 22)?Lateral geniculate body (4) = visual relay · medial geniculate body (5) = auditory relay
3 hypothalamus surface structures, ventral view (slide 23)?Optic chiasm (7, 24) · infundibulum (10, 22; where pituitary (21) attaches) · mammillary bodies (11, 20)
Mammillary bodies position per slide 23?'Immediately dorsal to the pituitary' (see ⚠️ flag)
Pituitary attaches to the hypothalamus at… by… (slide 24)Tuber cinereum, by the infundibular stalk, just caudal to the optic chiasm
Why does the pituitary often stay in the skull when the brain is removed (slide 24)?Fold of dura covers it in the sella turcica
With what region of the brain is the pituitary associated (slide 34)?Diencephalon (hypothalamus)

🧠 LO2 · Mesencephalon (midbrain)

3 mesencephalon structures (slide 25)?Tectum, cerebral peduncle, mesencephalic aqueduct (lumen)
Lumen of the mesencephalon (slide 25)?Mesencephalic aqueduct
Tectum position (slide 26)?Dorsal to the mesencephalic aqueduct
Other name for the tectum (slide 26)?Corpora quadrigemina
Rostral colliculi function (slide 26)?Visual reflexes (2 rostral colliculi, #6)
Caudal colliculi function (slide 26)?Auditory reflexes (2 caudal colliculi, #7)
Cerebral peduncle continues into… (slide 27)The internal capsule, to connect to the cerebrum
3 structures of the cerebral peduncle (slide 27)?Crus cerebri, tegmentum, substantia nigra
Crus cerebri location (slide 27)?Ventral surface of the cerebral peduncle
Cerebral peduncle figure numbers (slide 27)?3 and 12

🧠 LO2 · Metencephalon & myelencephalon

Metencephalon parts (slide 28)?Cerebellum and pons
Pons = which surface feature (slide 29)?Large transverse bar on the ventral brainstem = transverse fibers of the pons (14)
Longitudinal fibers of the pons (slide 29)?Dorsal to the transverse fibers; continue the descending fibers of the crus cerebri; not visible on the surface
Cerebellum regions (slide 30)?Two lateral cerebellar hemispheres (2) + one central cerebellar vermis (1)
Cerebellar cortex vs medulla (slide 30)?Cortex = gray matter · medulla = white matter (mostly; contains some nuclei)
Cerebellar peduncles: what they do and how they're named (slide 31)?Connect cerebellum to brainstem; named for where they attach (not easy to distinguish)
Rostral cerebellar peduncle (slide 31)?Mainly efferent axons from cerebellum to brainstem
Middle cerebellar peduncle (slide 31)?Continuous with the transverse fibers of the pons
Caudal cerebellar peduncle (slide 31)?Connects the medulla oblongata and the cerebellum
Cerebellar peduncle figure numbers (slide 32)?Rostral (9) · middle (2) · caudal (3); they carry fibers in and out of the cerebellum
Myelencephalon = ? and connected to… (slide 33)Medulla oblongata; connected to the spinal cord
Medulla oblongata structures (slide 33)?Trapezoid body (15), pyramids (17)
Lumen of the myelencephalon (slide 33)?Caudal ½ of the 4th ventricle

🩸 LO3 · Cerebral & cerebellar arteries

Formation of the basilar artery (slide 36)?Ventral spinal a. (12) joined by the vertebral aa. (13) → basilar a.; continues rostrally and bifurcates at the midbrain
Which arteries join the arterial circle rostrally (slide 36)?Two internal carotid aa. (5); their rostral branches complete the circle
Is the arterial circle species-specific (slide 36)?No — circle and branches are very similar across species
Cerebral arteries off the circle (slide 37)?3: rostral, middle, caudal cerebral
Which cerebral arteries are visible on the medial surface (slide 37)?Rostral and caudal cerebral
Cerebellar arteries — source and names (slide 37)?2, from the basilar a.: rostral and caudal cerebellar
Territory of each cerebral artery, dorsal view (slide 37)?Rostral cerebral = dorsomedial strip (dark) · middle cerebral = lateral surface · caudal cerebral = caudomedial (occipital) strip
Other arteries labeled on the lateral/midline views (slide 37)?Internal ethmoidal, internal ophthalmic, internal carotid, labyrinthine, caudal communicating, ventral spinal, vertebral, basilar

🛡️ LO4 · Meninges & spaces

Meninges = ? and buffered by… (slide 39)Connective tissue layers covering brain and spinal cord; buffered by cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)
Three meningeal layers, outside → in (slide 39)?Dura mater → arachnoid (mater) → pia mater
Dura mater texture (slide 40)?Tough, dense connective tissue
Epidural space around the spinal cord (slide 40)?Fat-filled space separating dura from the periosteum of the vertebral canal
Epidural space over the brain (slide 40)?None — dura is fused to the periosteum of the cranial cavity
Where does the dura fuse to periosteum at the brain/cord junction (slide 43)?Foramen magnum (dotted circle) — no epidural space cranial to it
Spinal cord meninges histology labels, inner → outer (slide 41)?PM (pia mater) → SA (subarachnoid space) → SD (subdural space) → DM (dura mater) → ED (epidural space)
Arachnoid structure (slide 42)?Outer membrane + trabeculae reaching down to the pia ('spider-like')
Subarachnoid space location and contents (slide 42)?Below the outer arachnoid membrane; blood vessels surrounded by CSF
Pia mater (slide 42)?Inner membrane most closely associated with nervous tissue; follows blood vessels into the central nervous system (CNS)
Subdural space (slide 47)?A POTENTIAL space (open only as an artifact on the specimen)
Cerebellomedullary cistern = ? (slide 47)Cisterna magna — a clinically important enlargement of the subarachnoid space
Falx cerebri (slide 44)?Dura extending into the longitudinal cerebral fissure, between the cerebral hemispheres
Venous sinus in the falx cerebri (slide 44)?Dorsal sagittal sinus (a vein)
Tentorium cerebelli (slide 45)?Extends into the transverse cerebral fissure, between cerebral hemispheres and cerebellum
Tentorium cerebelli composition (slide 45)?Partly dura mater and partly bone
Labels on the falx/arachnoid figure (slide 44)?Arachnoid villi, dorsal sagittal sinus, bone, dura mater, arachnoid, trabeculae, subarachnoid space, pia mater, cerebral vein/venule, cerebral artery/arteriole, perivascular space, cerebrum, falx cerebri

💧 LO5 · Ventricles & connections

Ventricular system in order of CSF flow (slides 49, 56)?Lateral ventricles → (interventricular foramen) → third ventricle → mesencephalic (cerebral) aqueduct → fourth ventricle → central canal of spinal cord / lateral apertures → subarachnoid space
Ventricles are lined by… filled with… (slide 49)Ependymal cells; circulating cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)
Lateral ventricles location (slide 49)?Cerebral hemispheres (2)
Third ventricle location and communications (slide 49)?Diencephalon, surrounds the interthalamic adhesion; communicates with lumen of the infundibulum (5) and mesencephalic aqueduct
Mesencephalic aqueduct: location and leads to… (slide 49)Mesencephalon (6); leads to the fourth ventricle
Fourth ventricle location (slides 49, 51)?Spans the metencephalon and myelencephalon (7)
Fourth ventricle connections (slide 49)?Central canal of the spinal cord (9) and lateral apertures (8) to the subarachnoid space
Opening connecting lateral and third ventricles (figure label, slide 56)?Interventricular foramen (lateral + third ventricles communicate, slide 50)
Roof of the 4th ventricle rostral and caudal to the cerebellum (slide 51)?Rostral (15) and caudal (15') medullary velum — thin, membrane-like flaps
What is revealed when the cerebellum and medullary vela are removed (slide 52)?Floor of the 4th ventricle

💧 LO6 · Cerebrospinal fluid production, flow, drainage

CSF life cycle: produced → exits → reabsorbed (slides 55–61)?Choroid plexus (all ventricles) → flows through ventricles → exits via lateral apertures of 4th ventricle → subarachnoid space around brain + cord → arachnoid villi → dorsal sagittal sinus (venous blood)
What produces CSF (slide 55)?Choroid plexus — specialized membrane that secretes CSF
Where is choroid plexus found (slide 56)?Roof and walls of all ventricles; lateral + third ventricles share choroid plexus where they communicate
Where does CSF exit the ventricular system (slide 56)?Lateral apertures of the 4th ventricle → subarachnoid space
Choroid plexus development (slide 57)?Blood vessels invaginate into the pia mater and are covered with modified ependymal cells; tufts of capillaries protrude into the ventricles
What forms the blood–CSF barrier (slide 57)?Tight junctions between the (modified) ependymal cells of the choroid plexus
FYI choroid plexus functions beyond CSF (slide 58)?Eliminates waste, controls T-cell travel in CSF, regulates microglia/neural stem cells, neuroplasticity windows, synapse maintenance (FYI only)
Where is 4th-ventricle choroid plexus visible on a removed brain (slides 59–60)?Protruding through the lateral aperture into the subarachnoid space
Other labels on the lateral-aperture brain photo (slide 59)?Cingulate gyrus, corpus callosum, fornix, septum, fimbria, hippocampus, piriform lobe, middle cerebellar peduncle, choroid plexus, cranial nerves II, V, VII
CSF reabsorption route (slide 61)?Arachnoid villi — projections from the subarachnoid space into the dorsal sagittal sinus

🏥 Clinical practice focus

Hydrocephalus definition (slide 65)?CSF build-up enlarging the ventricles, usually the lateral ventricles
3 causes of hydrocephalus (slide 65)?Obstruction to circulation, impaired drainage, overproduction; acquired or developmental
Obstruction in the mesencephalon → result (slide 65)?CSF produced rostral to it can't reach the 4th ventricle to exit → fluid accumulates → compression of cerebral tissue
Rapid-onset hydrocephalus signs (slide 65)?Mania, head pressing, muscle tremor, convulsions
Slow-onset hydrocephalus signs (slide 65)?Paralysis, dullness, blindness — or no clinical signs (incidental in people)
Dilated lateral ventricles compress which region (slides 64, 66)?Cerebral cortex (telencephalon) — 'enlarged lateral ventricle', 'compressed cerebral cortex'
Epidural anesthesia — which space and why it works (slide 67)?Epidural space of the vertebral canal (dura and periosteum separated, fat-filled) → regional anesthesia of spinal nerves
Two CSF collection sites (slides 68–69)?Cisterna magna (cerebellomedullary cistern) and lumbosacral junction (lumbar cistern)
Pituitary tumors in horses — neighboring structure labeled (slide 70)?Optic chiasm (pituitary/tumor sits just caudal-ventral to it)

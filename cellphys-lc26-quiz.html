🧷 LC26 · Enzyme-linked Receptors quiz

20 questions · Dr. Schultz style · Exam 4

Exam-style questions on LC26 only, written from the slides (not an official key). Tap an answer to check it; select-all needs ✓ Check. Keep your FIRST answer unless you can name the fact that proves it wrong. 📖 = slide number.

Q1Binding of atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP) to its receptor directly activates which enzyme activity? (select one correct response)

✅ A · The ANP receptor has an intrinsic intracellular guanylyl cyclase domain: GTP → cGMP. (Asked on Quiz07.)📖 S2, S4

Q2A dog's plasma atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP) is measured at 150 pM. Using Dr. Schultz's cutoffs, how is this interpreted? (select one correct response)

✅ D · ANP ~10 pM is healthy; >100 pM is pathologic. 100 nM is a unit trap; 200 pM is the BNP number; 1 pM is BNP's normal.📖 transcript

Q3A cat's plasma B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) is 250 pM. Which interpretation fits the lecture? (select one correct response)

✅ B · BNP is historically 'brain' but comes from the ventricle; normal ≤1 pM; >200 pM = badly distended heart. (BNP source asked on Quiz07.)📖 transcript

Q4Sildenafil, tadalafil, and vardenafil inhibit phosphodiesterase 5. Besides erectile dysfunction, which clinical use did Dr. Schultz name? (select one correct response)

✅ C · PDE5 inhibitors keep cGMP high (vasodilation); used for erectile dysfunction, pulmonary arterial hypertension, prostatic hyperplasia.📖 transcript

Q5In adrenal cortisol-secreting cells, adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) raises cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP). How does atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP) reduce ACTH-stimulated cortisol secretion? (select one correct response)

✅ E · ANP → cGMP → ↑PDE2 activity → cAMP broken down → ↓cortisol (same mechanism modestly ↓aldosterone). (Asked on Quiz07.)📖 transcript

Q6Natriuretic peptides are released from the heart as a 126-amino-acid prohormone. Which enzyme activates it, and what is the active peptide's length? (select one correct response)

✅ A · Corin cleaves the 126-aa prohormone to the 28-aa active form (Schultz corrected himself from 128 to 126).📖 transcript

Q7Which effects result from activation of natriuretic peptide receptors A/B (NPR-A/B)? (select all that apply)

✅ B, C, D · Afferent dilation ↑GFR; kidney vasodilation ↓renin → ↓angiotensin II → ↓aldosterone; pituitary NP receptors ↓ADH; vessels dilate.📖 S6, S7; transcript

Q8Atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP) dilates the afferent arteriole while the efferent arteriole is unchanged. What happens to glomerular filtration rate (GFR)? (select one correct response)

✅ E · Afferent dilation without efferent change → ↑GFR → more fluid delivered to the nephron.📖 S7; transcript

Q9Fibroblast growth factor, vascular endothelial growth factor, and nerve growth factor receptors share which activation step? (select one correct response)

✅ C · Receptor tyrosine kinases: agonist → two halves together → intrinsic tyrosine kinase → autophosphorylation.📖 S8; transcript

Q10In skeletal muscle, the insulin receptor substrate 1 (IRS-1) adapter protein directly activates which enzyme? (select one correct response)

✅ B · Activated insulin receptor → IRS-1 → PI3K (PIP2 → PIP3) → GLUT4 vesicle fusion. (Asked on Quiz07.)📖 S9, S11

Q11Which steps lie between insulin binding and GLUT4 insertion through the IRS-1 arm? (select all that apply)

✅ A, C, E · IRS-1 arm: autophosphorylation → IRS-1 → PI3K → GLUT4 vesicle fusion. SOS/RAS/MAPK belong to the second arm (GLUT4 synthesis).📖 S9, S11

Q12Per Dr. Schultz's in-class correction, how does RAS-GTP act on MAP kinase kinase kinase (Raf)? (select one correct response)

✅ D · Slides (S10, S12) show 'phosphorylation of MAPKKK'; Schultz said a newer review shows RAS activates it by interaction. See flag.📖 transcript (vs S12)

Q13In the insulin receptor's mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) arm, which kinase translocates into the nucleus? (select one correct response)

✅ C · Only phosphorylated MAPK translocates into the nucleus and activates early genes (myc, jun, fos). (Trap asked on Quiz07.)📖 S12

Q14A RAS mutation abolishes its intrinsic GTPase activity, but son of sevenless (SOS) still functions. Which state does RAS stay in? (select one correct response)

✅ E · SOS loads GTP; without GTPase RAS can't hydrolyze GTP → stays on. GTPase is the timer.📖 S13; transcript

Q15Continuing the previous question (RAS lacking GTPase activity), what outcome does the lecture predict? (select one correct response)

✅ D · MAPK pathway always on → mitogenesis → tumors. 'Your first example of a proto-oncogene.'📖 S13; transcript

Q16In a pancreatic β cell, glucose enters via GLUT2 and raises the ATP:ADP ratio. What happens next? (select one correct response)

✅ B · ↑ATP:ADP ↓K⁺ channel activity → depolarization → insulin secretion.📖 S14; transcript

Q17A cat's resting plasma glucose is 30 mM. Using 5 mM = 90 mg/dL, what is the value in mg/dL and its S16 category? (select one correct response)

✅ A · 30 mM × 18 = 540 mg/dL. 30 mM falls in 22–44 mM = diabetes (after-dinner is 14–17 mM).📖 S16; transcript

Q18Which statement correctly describes the erythropoietin (EPO) receptor? (select one correct response)

✅ A · Tyrosine-kinase-associated receptor: cytosolic domain lacks kinase activity; EPO → dimerization → JAK2 binds. (EPO → JAK asked on Quiz07.)📖 S18

Q19Ferbitinib, an invented drug, selectively blocks MAP kinase kinase (MEK). In an erythroid progenitor stimulated by erythropoietin (EPO), which response is most directly reduced? (select one correct response)

✅ E · MEK sits downstream of SOS/RAS and upstream of MAPK → cyclin D. The JAK2/STAT5 → bcl-2 arm is untouched.📖 S18, S19

Q20Which events are part of erythropoietin (EPO) receptor signaling? (select all that apply)

✅ A, B, C, E · EPO receptor has no intrinsic kinase; JAK2 does the phosphorylating. Arms: STAT5 → bcl-2 and SOS/RAS/MAPK → cyclin D.📖 S18, S19

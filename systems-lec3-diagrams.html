1 · The oxygen cascade

Deck 3 slide 4 · deck 5 slide 4 · ⭐ "explain why alveolar PO₂ is lower than atmospheric"

  • 159 → 149: air is humidified in the airways. Water vapor (47 mmHg) takes up space: (760 − 47) × 0.21 = 149. ⭐ (your miss: this is water vapor, not CO₂)
  • 149 → 100: fresh air mixes with alveolar CO₂, and you take up more O₂ than you give back CO₂ (R = 0.8). Alveolar gas equation: 149 − 40/0.8 ≈ 100.
  • 100 → 100: healthy lung = arterial matches alveolar. A gap here = disease (alveolar–arterial difference).
  • 100 → 40: tissues pull O₂ off. Mitochondria run at only ~1–5 mmHg.

2 · O₂ equilibration time in the capillary

Deck 3 slide 18 · notes 3 · ⭐ thick barrier + exercise

━ normal━ thick barrier┅ grossly abnormal┆ exercise

  • Normal: red cell reaches alveolar PO₂ by ~0.25 s, with lots of spare time.
  • Thick barrier (fibrosis, alveolar proteinosis): slower climb, but still gets there at rest.
  • What changes in exercise: transit time drops to ~0.28 s (orange). The thick-barrier cell gets cut off at the pink dot → hypoxemia only on exercise ("Fluffy won't run").
  • 🟠 Source conflict: resting transit time = 0.84 s (deck 2 slide 7 / deck 3 slide 22 calculation) vs ~0.75 s (notes 3 text + this figure). Know both; show the calculation if asked.

3 · Compliance curves: healthy vs emphysema vs fibrosis

Deck 6 slide 16 · notes 6 (his hardest lecture)

━ healthy┅ emphysema━ fibrosis

  • Compliance = slope = change in volume ÷ change in pressure. Steeper = stretchier.
  • Emphysema (steeper): holes in the elastic weave → too stretchy, weak recoil → air won't come OUT (obstructive).
  • Fibrosis (flatter): scar tissue → stiff, strong recoil → air won't go IN (restrictive).
  • Trap: emphysema = INCREASED compliance (sounds "good," isn't). Fibrosis = DECREASED compliance.

4 · Pulmonary vascular resistance vs lung volume (J/U-curve)

Deck 4 slide 12 · Quiz 9 Q20

  • Bottom of the curve = FRC (normal resting volume): resistance is lowest.
  • Low volume (toward RV): the lung isn't pulling on the big extra-alveolar vessels (less radial traction) → they narrow → resistance up.
  • High volume (toward TLC): the capillaries in the alveolar walls get stretched and squeezed → resistance up.
  • ⭐ Why animals don't breathe near TLC: stiff lung (more work) + high vascular resistance → strain on the right heart.

5 · Regional ventilation and blood flow (apex vs base)

Deck 4 slides 27–29 · deck 2 slides 22–24 · notes 4

  • Both ventilation and blood flow are highest at the BASE (gravity). ⭐ your miss: base gets the most ventilation.
  • But blood flow falls off faster going up than ventilation does.
  • So the ratio is HIGHEST at the APEX → more air than blood → higher PO₂ at the apex (132 vs 89). Tuberculosis likes the apex.
  • Trap: "most ventilation" = base · "highest ratio / highest PO₂" = apex. Don't flip them.
  • Ratio values (~3.3 / ~0.6) are read off the slide graph, not numbers he stressed.

6 · O₂ dissociation curve (label it!)

Deck 7 slides 14, 18, 19, 21 · notes 7 · ⭐ "label the O₂ dissociation curve"

━ normal┅ right shift┅ left shift

  • 1 = arterial, rest (~100 mmHg, ~97%) · 2 = venous, rest (~40 mmHg, ~75%) · 3 = P50 (27 mmHg in humans) · 4 = arterial, exercise (may fall a bit in greyhounds/Thoroughbreds) · 5 = venous, strenuous exercise.
  • Flat top = safety for loading in the lung. Steep middle = small PO₂ drop unloads lots of O₂ in tissues.
  • Right shift (pink, higher P50) = ↑ temperature, ↑ H⁺ (↓ pH), ↑ PCO₂ (= Bohr effect), ↑ 2,3-DPG (chronic). Exercising muscle.
  • Left shift (purple, lower P50) = carbon monoxide, fetal hemoglobin, myoglobin, bar-headed goose.
  • 🟠 Source conflict: lecture said larger animals have higher P50; notes say SMALLER animals are right-shifted (higher P50; rat ~40). Use the notes.
  • Exercise points 4 and 5 are placed by shape only; he gave no numbers for them.

7 · CO₂ vs O₂ dissociation curves

Deck 7 slide 27 · notes 7

━ CO₂━ O₂

  • In the normal range, the CO₂ curve is far steeper than the O₂ curve.
  • So extra breathing CAN blow off extra CO₂ (steep line: lower PCO₂ = much less CO₂ content).
  • But extra breathing CAN'T add O₂ once hemoglobin is near full (flat top).
  • Why it matters: in ventilation-perfusion mismatch, good units fix CO₂ but can't fix O₂ → low PaO₂ with normal PaCO₂.
  • Curve shapes match his slide; exact CO₂ content values on this drawing are not exam numbers.

8 · The 5 causes of hypoxemia: decision flowchart

Deck 5 slides 3–23 · notes 5 · ⭐ inspired hypoxia vs hypoventilation; ⭐ shunt diagnosis with 100% O₂

  • Step 1, PaCO₂: HIGH = hypoventilation (not breathing enough). LOW = low inspired O₂ (the low O₂ makes the animal breathe harder). ⭐
  • Step 2, 100% O₂: shunt barely improves (shunted blood never sees the O₂). PaO₂ >500 on 100% O₂ ≈ small shunt; ~250 ≈ big shunt.
  • Mismatch vs diffusion: mismatch is the most common (most lung disease). Diffusion limitation usually shows only during exercise.
  • Severity of mismatch: alveolar–arterial difference (see 🧮 equations). Big gap = bad mismatch.
  • The order of steps is a study aid built from his 5 causes and diagnostic clues; he did not draw this flowchart.

Glomerulus"Ball of thread" · tuft of capillaries where filtration happens (S7)
Bowman's (capsular) spaceSpace the filtrate is pushed into (S6)
Bowman's (glomerular) capsuleSquamous cells that hold the initial filtrate (S6)
Renal corpuscleGlomerulus + Bowman's capsule (S5–6)
★ Afferent arterioleBrings blood INTO the glomerulus (S6)
★ Efferent arterioleTakes blood OUT · a second arteriole (not a vein) → the kidney controls BOTH ends of the capillary bed (S6)
Vascular poleWhere the afferent + efferent arterioles enter/leave (S6)
Urinary poleWhere Bowman's space joins the proximal convoluted tubule · FILTRATE leaves here (S6)
Section shows no pole?Plane-of-section issue · every glomerulus has both poles, usually on opposite sides
3 cell types in the glomerulusEndothelial cells · podocytes (epithelial "foot cells") · mesangial cells (connective tissue support)
Fenestrated endotheliumCapillary lining full of holes · first, coarse filter (S8)
★ The REAL filterFused basement membrane (basal lamina) (S8)
PodocytesFoot processes wrap the capillary · SUPPORT the basement membrane so it can take high pressure (like a mesh behind filter paper) (S8)
Filtration slitGap between podocyte foot processes where fluid exits (S8, S10)
★ Filtration barrier = 3 layersFenestrated endothelium → fused basement membrane → podocytes (S8)
★ Size (most important factor)Freely filtered < ~2 nm radius (~15 kDa) · not filtered approaching ~4 nm (~70 kDa) · in between = partial (S10)
ChargeBasement membrane is NEGATIVE (glycosaminoglycans, sulfate groups) → positive molecules filter faster, negative slower · matters most near the size cutoff (S10)
ShapeLeast important · smallest diameter matters; long thin molecules can slip through (S10)
★ Rule 1 · small molecules & ions (Basel: "star this 7 times")FREELY filtered → filtrate concentration = blood concentration EXACTLY (S11)
★ Rule 2 · small proteinse.g., myoglobin (17 kDa, ~2 nm) → PARTIALLY filtered → filtrate concentration < blood, but present (S11)
★ Rule 3 · large proteins & cellse.g., albumin (66.5 kDa, ~3.6 nm), RBCs → NOT filtered → filtrate ≈ 0 (S11)
Why albumin isn't filtered (just under cutoff)It is NEGATIVELY charged → repelled by the basement membrane (S11)
★ Glomerular filtration equationGFR = Kf × [(PGC + πBS) − (PBS + πGC)] (S12)
PGC (glomerular capillary hydrostatic)~50 mmHg, and it STAYS ~50 along the whole glomerulus (efferent resistance prevents the usual drop) (S12)
πBS (Bowman's space oncotic)~0 mmHg (almost no protein filtered) (S12)
PBS (Bowman's space hydrostatic)~10 mmHg · rises with urinary/tubular OBSTRUCTION → filtration stops (S12)
πGC (glomerular capillary oncotic)~25 mmHg in → up to ~40 by the end (protein concentrates as fluid leaves) (S12)
★ Ultrafiltration pressure (UFP)Start: 50 − (10 + 25) = 15 mmHg · end (low flow): 50 − (10 + 40) = 0 (S12)
Low flow rateBlood concentrates fast → oncotic pressure rises fast → UFP hits 0 early · efficient per unit blood, low total (S13)
High flow rateOncotic pressure rises slowly → UFP stays up across the glomerulus → more filtrate per minute (S13)
Which flow does the kidney prefer?HIGH · it cares about total filtrate per TIME, not per unit of blood
★ GFRFluid filtered per unit TIME (S14)
★ Filtration fraction (FF)GFR ÷ renal plasma flow = fluid filtered per unit BLOOD (efficiency) (S14)
Low flow → ?Low GFR → bad, fast (S14)
Very high flow → ?Diminishing GFR returns + strain on the barrier + low FF → bad, slowly (S14)
Body defends low or high pressure more?LOW · low = death in hours–days · high = damage over decades
Optimal human renal blood flow~600 mL/min (🔹 mentioned)
★ Afferent constriction↓ glomerular pressure · ↓ renal blood flow · ↓ GFR (S15)
★ Afferent dilation↑ glomerular pressure · ↑ renal blood flow · ↑ GFR (S15)
★ Efferent constriction↑ glomerular pressure · ↓ renal blood flow · ↑ GFR* (S15)
★ Efferent dilation↓ glomerular pressure · ↑ renal blood flow · ↓ GFR (S15)
*Severe efferent constrictionCan eventually LOWER GFR: very low blood flow → oncotic pressure climbs fast (S15)
Ratio of afferent : efferent tone sets…Glomerular PRESSURE
Total tone (both open or both closed) sets…FLOW rate (pressure barely changes)
★ AutoregulationAFFERENT arteriole constricts when pressure rises, dilates when it falls → constant renal blood flow + GFR over MAP ~80–170 mmHg (S17)
Autoregulation mechanismStretch-sensitive ion channels in the vessel wall (myogenic) (S17)
Afferent vs efferent autoregulationBoth can, but AFFERENT is much stronger
Below the minimum pressureAfferent can't dilate any more → autoregulation fails → GFR drops
"I Love Lucy" conveyor beltFaster tubular flow → less time to pull Na⁺ out → MORE Na⁺ reaches the macula densa

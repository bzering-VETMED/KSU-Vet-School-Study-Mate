🗣️ LC40 · Physiology 2 · Volumes & ventilation

Poole, explained simply · read this first, then the terms page

💡 Not every breath is useful: part of it just fills the pipes. What counts is the air that reaches the alveoli, and that is what sets your blood CO₂.
🖼️ Picture it: Think of a long snorkel: each breath first refills the snorkel with your old air before any fresh air gets to you. The snorkel is dead space.

The plain-English version

  • Tidal volume = one normal breath. Part fills the pipes (dead space), the rest reaches alveoli (alveolar ventilation).
  • Deep, slow breaths beat fast, shallow ones. Fast shallow breathing keeps re-filling the pipes and wastes air. A panting dog is cooling off, not clearing CO₂.
  • Bohr equation (plain): the more dead space you have, the more your exhaled CO₂ gets diluted. Compare exhaled CO₂ to blood CO₂ → tells you what fraction of each breath is wasted. Cows/horses waste more (long airways).
  • Alveolar ventilation equation (plain): blood CO₂ goes UP if you breathe less or make more CO₂, and DOWN if you breathe more. Double the useful breathing → CO₂ halves.
  • Lung volumes: functional residual capacity = where you rest after a normal breath out; no muscles working. Residual volume = what's left after blowing out as hard as you can (never zero). Vital capacity = the most you can move in one breath.
  • A spirometer can't see residual volume (you can't blow it out) → use nitrogen washout.
  • Red blood cell transit time: how long a red cell sits in the lung capillary. At rest ~0.75 s (🟠 source conflict: slide 7 and the notes calculation give 0.84 s; the diffusion notes and figure use ~0.75 s. Know both). Exercise speeds blood up → less time (0.28 s). Racehorses and greyhounds go so fast they don't fully load O₂.
  • Most air goes to the BASE of the lung: the top alveoli are already stretched and stiff; the bottom ones are floppy and fill easily.

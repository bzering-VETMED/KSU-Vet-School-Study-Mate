Q1Ruidoso Downs racetrack (7,000 ft): high incidence and severity of EIPH. A contributing mechanism?

✅ D · LC50: low alveolar O₂ at altitude → hypoxic vasoconstriction → ↑ pulmonary artery pressure → more capillary rupture

Q2In HEALTH, after exercise onset, VO₂ kinetics are limited by:

✅ C · LC47: capillary red cell flow rises within the first contraction → delivery is fast → mitochondria ('enzyme inertia') set the speed

Q3In HEART FAILURE, after exercise onset, VO₂ kinetics are limited by:

✅ D · LC47/49: poor cardiac output + non-flowing capillaries → delivery becomes the limit → slow kinetics, bigger O₂ deficit

Q4Horse on a ventilator: arterial PCO₂ 40, expired PCO₂ 20. Dead space ÷ tidal volume?

✅ C · Bohr: (40 − 20) ÷ 40 = 0.5

Q5Heifer with narcotic overdose: alveolar ventilation falls to ½ normal, CO₂ production unchanged. Arterial PCO₂?

✅ B · PaCO₂ ∝ 1 ÷ alveolar ventilation → halve ventilation = double PaCO₂ → 80

Q6Bobcat at 11,000 ft (barometric pressure 447). PaCO₂ falls to 20 (carotid body drive), R = 0.8. Alveolar PO₂?

✅ D · (447 − 47) × 0.2093 = 84 · 84 − (20 ÷ 0.8 = 25) = 59

Q7Fick: Thoroughbred at max, cardiac output 400 L/min, arterial O₂ 27, venous O₂ 2 mL/100 mL. VO₂?

✅ B · 400 × (27 − 2)/100 = 100 L/min

Q8Hypoxemic dog in surgery: instead of adding O₂ you raise peak inspiratory pressure and hyperventilate. Bad idea because:

✅ E · Low CO₂ → ↓ brain flow + left shift (alkalosis) · high pressure → barotrauma + squeezes alveolar capillaries (↑ resistance)

Q9Why don't central chemoreceptors respond directly to a systemic metabolic acidosis?

✅ B · LC48: charged H⁺/bicarbonate can't cross the blood–brain barrier acutely; only CO₂ diffuses in

Q10Humans and animals don't breathe close to total lung capacity, even at max exercise, because:

✅ D · Top of the compliance curve = stiff · high lung volume stretches/narrows alveolar capillaries → ↑ pulmonary vascular resistance

Q11Arterial pH at max exercise: lactate ↑ 15 mM, PaCO₂ falls to 25 mmHg.

✅ B · (24 − 15) ÷ (0.03 × 25 = 0.75) = 12 · log 12 = 1.08 · 6.1 + 1.08 = 7.18

Q12Pointer/heeler mauled by a coyote (femoral artery). Normal lungs, hemoglobin 6 g/100 mL, 97% saturated, PaO₂ 100. O₂ content?

✅ B · 6 × 1.34 × 0.97 = 7.8 + (100 × 0.003 = 0.3) = 8.1 mL/100 mL

Q13During REBREATHING (as in lab), which chemoreceptors give the intense urge to breathe?

✅ C · Rebreathing = ↑ CO₂ (central + peripheral) AND ↓ O₂ (peripheral) → both, with potentiation

Q14Why was BAL fluid sampled 10 min post-exercise bright red, not cyanotic like arterial blood during exercise?

✅ C · Blood sitting in the alveoli equilibrates with high alveolar PO₂ (and the horse over-breathes after stopping)

Q15Why could a student hold her breath ~3× longer after hyperventilating? She:

✅ B · Lower starting PCO₂ → takes longer to reach the CO₂ breaking point (hemoglobin is already ~97% saturated)

Q16Emphysema (increased compliance) decreases peak expiratory flow because:

✅ C · LC44: lost recoil → low driving (alveolar) pressure · lost radial traction → airways collapse → high resistance

Q17Blood gas: pH >7.4, bicarbonate 62, PaCO₂ 56. Best description?

✅ C · Alkalemia · high bicarbonate explains it = METABOLIC · high PCO₂ = respiratory compensation (hypoventilation) → can't time it

Q18Rhodesian ridgeback: PaO₂ <50 at rest, blue membranes; you suspect HYPOVENTILATION (partial bronchial obstruction). Confirm most simply?

✅ A · Hypoventilation = HIGH PaCO₂ (the defining sign)

Q19Sheepdog with overt hypoxemia; you suspect a RIGHT-TO-LEFT SHUNT. Confirm/quantify?

✅ B · Shunt is the only cause NOT fixed by 100% O₂ → PaO₂ stays low

Q20The 2 primary reasons Thoroughbreds become hypoxemic AND hypercapnic galloping?

✅ A · LC49: transit time (main cause of low O₂) + hypoventilation (high CO₂)

Q21Buffalo trachea is much WIDER than a similar-sized horse's. Identify the FALSE statement (buffalo vs horse):

✅ E · Wider airway → less resistance → less negative alveolar pressure → LESS EIPH (dead space does rise)

Q22Which chemoreceptors drive the hyperventilation of SEVERE exercise (most species except horse)?

✅ A · Lactic acid (H⁺) can't reach central receptors → carotid bodies drive it

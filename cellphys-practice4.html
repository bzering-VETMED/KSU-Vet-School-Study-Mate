LC14 · Protein structure & diagnostics

Q1By convention, a protein's primary structure is written:

✅ B · Standard: N-ter to the left, C-ter to the right.📖 LC14 S10

Q2Which brucellosis tube agglutination titer is indicative of infection?

✅ D · Higher titers = more antibody; > 1/160 indicates infection.📖 LC14 S21

Q3Which test uses an antibody to probe a TISSUE SECTION for a specific protein?

✅ A · IHC: antibody → tissue. Agglutination: antigen → serum antibody.📖 LC14 S15
LC15 · Protein regulation & enzymes

Q4Select all that apply. Which regulatory tools does the glucocorticoid receptor use?

✅ B, C, E · The GR shows 3 of the 4 tools; no zymogen step.📖 LC15 S4, S11–12

Q5A reaction at equilibrium has a ΔG of:

✅ C · At equilibrium ΔG = 0: no available energy, no net direction.📖 LC15 S15

Q6Glucose-6-phosphate activates glycogen synthase, the last enzyme of glycogen synthesis. This is:

✅ C · An earlier metabolite activating a later enzyme = feed-forward (the step EPSM1 horses lose).📖 LC15 S24, S26
LC16 · Glucose transport & glycolysis

Q7A 1.5-year-old West Highland white terrier has regenerative hemolytic anemia, high bilirubin and ferritin, and hepatosplenomegaly. Why does pyruvate kinase deficiency destroy its red cells?

✅ B · No mitochondria → glycolysis only → no pyruvate kinase = no ATP → lysis. NADPH is the G6PD trap.📖 LC16 S2–3, S15

Q8How do yeast regenerate NAD⁺ when oxygen is low?

✅ A · Yeast → ethanol. Succinate = Fibrobacteres; propionate = Megasphaera.📖 LC16 S23–25

Q9Which statement about hexokinase and glucokinase is correct?

✅ D · Hexokinase: feedback inhibition by G6P. Glucokinase: not inhibited (glucose sensor).📖 LC16 S9
LC17 · TCA cycle & electron transport

Q10Which carrier moves electrons from complexes I and II to complex III?

✅ B · Q before III; cytochrome c before IV.📖 LC17 S20–21

Q11A cat with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy has sudden hind-limb paralysis. A clot buster restores flow, and the cat dies within the day. Which intermediate built up during ischemia and drove the injury?

✅ D · Succinate accumulates in ischemia; on reperfusion it floods complex II with FADH₂ → superoxide.📖 LC17 S2, S12–13, S26

Q12Why is the electron transport system located in the inner mitochondrial membrane?

✅ C · An H⁺-tight membrane lets complexes I, III and IV store energy as a gradient.📖 LC17 S19
LC18 · Glycogen & gluconeogenesis

Q13Which enzyme is the regulatory step of glycogen synthesis?

✅ A · Professor: glycogen synthase is the one enzyme to know for building (regulatory step).📖 LC18 S5, S9; transcript

Q14Glucagon acts on the liver's PFK-2/FBPase-2. Which sequence follows?

✅ B · Glucagon → cAMP → PKA → phosphorylation inhibits the kinase half, activates the phosphatase half.📖 LC18 S25–26

Q15Fatty acids cannot be used for gluconeogenesis. Which part of a triglyceride can?

✅ A · Glycerol → DHAP → glucose. Fatty acid carbons end as acetyl-CoA (lost as CO₂).📖 LC18 S18
LC19 · Amino acid catabolism

Q16What is the normal BCAA:AAA ratio, and what can it fall to in severe liver disease?

✅ D · Liver failure → AAA pile up (bigger denominator) → the ratio falls.📖 LC19 S9

Q17Why do high circulating aromatic amino acids cause neurologic signs?

✅ C · AAA (phenylalanine, tyrosine) are neurotransmitter precursors and share transport into the brain.📖 LC19 S9, S20

Q18Why is urea a safer form of nitrogen waste than ammonia?

✅ B · Urea: not a base, not K⁺-sized, can't be made into glutamine → dumped in blood for the kidney.📖 LC19 S15–16; transcript
LC20 · Lipid metabolism & ketones

Q19Triglycerides made in the liver are carried to the rest of the body in:

✅ D · Adipose stores triglyceride; liver ships it out as VLDL.📖 LC20 S6

Q20Which enzyme is the committed, rate-limiting step of fatty acid synthesis?

✅ C · ACC: acetyl-CoA → malonyl-CoA. HSL is the main LIPOLYSIS enzyme.📖 LC20 S5; transcript

Q21Ketoacidosis is defined as serum ketone bodies above:

✅ A · 0.1 normal → > 0.5 ketosis → > 1.5 ketoacidosis (pH < 7.35).📖 LC20 S17
LC21 · Cell signaling

Q22Vasopressin binds V2 receptors in the kidney collecting duct. Which sequence follows?

✅ A · V2 = water (cAMP → aquaporins). V1 = vessels.📖 LC21 "Vasopressin/ADH Signaling"

Q23Which is an example of contact-dependent signaling?

✅ D · Contact-dependent: signal stays on the surface (synapse formation; leukocyte adhesion).📖 LC21 "Contact-dependent Signaling"

Q24Lipid-soluble hormones such as thyroid hormone usually:

✅ C · Lipid soluble → carrier proteins in plasma → cross membranes → cytosolic/nuclear receptors.📖 LC21 "Solubility of Signaling Molecules"

Q25A drug described as "selective" for one receptor still causes side effects through a related receptor. Why?

✅ B · Receptors prefer ligands but can bind relatives (e.g., oxytocin on vasopressin receptors).📖 LC21 pt2 transcript

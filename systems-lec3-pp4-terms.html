★ pink row = professor emphasized
Pulmonary arteries carry…Deoxygenated blood (only arteries that do) · pulmonary veins = only veins with oxygenated blood
Pulmonary artery pressure~25/8 mmHg, mean ~15 (systemic ~120/80, mean ~93–100)
Pulmonary capillary pressure (rest)~10 mmHg
★ Structural evidence of low pulmonary pressureThin arterial walls with little smooth muscle
★ Why pulmonary pressure must stay lowProtect the fragile ~0.3 µm blood-gas barrier (higher pressure → leak, edema, rupture)
Pulmonary vs systemic pressure dropSame blood flow, but lung pressure drop (~10) is ~1/10 of systemic (~100) → resistance very low
Mean pressure estimateDiastolic + 1/3 (systolic − diastolic)
PulsatilityPulmonary capillary flow is still pulsatile (systemic capillaries are not)
Pulmonary hypertensionOften silent/idiopathic · overloads right heart
Lung vs systemic blood flow controlLung takes ALL cardiac output; doesn't direct flow between organs
★ Pulmonary capillary pressure in exerciseHuman ~30–35 mmHg · horse >100 mmHg → capillaries rupture (EIPH)
Transmural pressureCapillary lumen pressure − alveolar pressure
Alveolar vessels (capillaries)Surrounded by alveolar pressure → squeezed when alveolar pressure is high
Extra-alveolar vesselsArteries/veins pulled open by radial traction as lung expands
★ Pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR)(Mean pulmonary artery pressure − left atrial pressure) ÷ cardiac output · very LOW
★ 2 ways PVR falls in exerciseRECRUITMENT of closed vessels (esp. apex/dorsal) + DISTENSION of open ones
Why PVR must fall in exerciseCardiac output ↑ ~5× · if pressure rose 5× the barrier would rupture
★ PVR vs lung volumeLowest at moderate volume (~functional residual capacity) · high at low volume (extra-alveolar vessels narrow) AND high volume (capillaries stretched)
★ Too much positive-pressure ventilation↑ alveolar pressure squeezes capillaries → no blood flow → no gas exchange → blood gases WORSEN · >40–50 cm H₂O can rupture lung
Where is the resistance? (lung volume)Low–moderate volume: extra-alveolar vessels · near total lung capacity: stretched capillaries
★ Why not breathe near total lung capacity?Capillaries stretched → PVR very high → overloads right ventricle (and lung is stiff)
Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstrictionHypoxic region's small arterioles constrict → shifts blood to better-ventilated areas (helpful regionally)
★ Brisket disease mechanismAltitude → whole lung hypoxic vasoconstriction → ↑ pulmonary pressure → right heart fails → pressure backs up → edema in brisket/chest
Altitude for brisket disease~5,000–6,000 ft is enough · ~60% of Colorado cattle respiratory losses
Treat high-altitude pulmonary hypertensionGo down (or be taken down) or give high O₂ · reversible if the heart recovers
Species most affected at altitudeCattle + pigs (most pulmonary smooth muscle) · horses low-intermediate · dogs + sheep low
Other active controls of PVREndothelins (constrict), low pH + hypoxia (constrict), alpha-adrenergic (weak), nitric oxide (dilates)
Nitric oxide in exercising horseLowers PVR · blocking it (L-NAME) raises PVR
Fick principleO₂ uptake = cardiac output × (arterial O₂ content − venous O₂ content)
Resting example5,000 mL/min × (20 − 15 mL/100 mL) = 250 mL O₂/min
Horse spleen in exerciseHematocrit 35% → up to ~70% · arterial O₂ content can exceed 26 mL/100 mL
★ Gravity and blood flowMost blood flow goes to the BASE (dependent lung)
Horse during exerciseGravity isn't the whole story → more flow to DORSAL lung (active regulation)
Ventilation vs perfusion with gravityBoth highest at base, but perfusion changes MORE
Ventilation-perfusion ratio up the lungIncreases toward apex/dorsal → apex has higher PO₂, lower PCO₂
Ventilation-perfusion ratio values~1 over most of lung · ~2–3 at apex (over-ventilated)
★ TuberculosisLikes high PO₂ → apex (sanatoria at altitude) · bats hang upside down → TB at the base
Lymphatics in the lungKeep lung dry · LOW capacity (few mL/hr) → easily overwhelmed by high pressure → edema, perivascular cuffing

⚠️ Precision traps

  • Pulmonary ARTERY = deoxygenated · pulmonary VEIN = oxygenated
  • PVR falls in exercise by recruitment AND distension (two mechanisms)
  • PVR is HIGH at both low and high lung volumes, lowest in between
  • Regional hypoxic vasoconstriction helps · global (altitude) → brisket disease
  • Blood flow → base · but horses in exercise send more to the DORSAL lung

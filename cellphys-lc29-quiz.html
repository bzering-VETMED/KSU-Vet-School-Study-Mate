Q1In a liver cell, very-low-concentration epinephrine signals through the stimulatory G protein (Gs) cascade to release glucose-1-phosphate. Per the slide, the total amplification is about:

✅ C · S6 labels the whole cascade ~100,000,000-fold amplification.📖 S6

Q2Compared with a direct-effect signal (1:1 ratio), second messengers allow which three things?

✅ D · S4–S5: second messengers allow multiplication (amplification), integration and modulation of the signal.📖 S4–S5

Q3In the liver stimulatory G protein (Gs) cascade, which enzyme directly activates glycogen phosphorylase?

✅ A · S6: protein kinase A (PKA) → phosphorylase kinase → glycogen phosphorylase → glucose-1-phosphate.📖 S6

Q4A receptor stays on the cell surface but is phosphorylated by a kinase and bound by arrestin, giving much less effect for the same signal. Which desensitization method is this?

✅ B · S8: disabled signaling = kinase phosphorylation + arrestin binding at the surface; membrane cycling internalizes the receptor.📖 S8

Q5An asthmatic cat on a long-acting beta-2 adrenergic agonist shows a falling bronchodilator response over several months. Per the slide's terms, this long-term loss is called:

✅ C · S7: short term (milliseconds–minutes) = desensitization; long term (days–years) = tolerance.📖 S7

Q6On a Michaelis-Menten dose-response curve, efficacy and potency are described by which parameters?

✅ B · S10, S12–S13: efficacy = maximal effect (Emax); potency = EC50.📖 S12–S13

Q7Drug A has Emax 100 and EC50 100. Drug B has Emax 100 and EC50 25. Which statement is correct?

✅ D · S13: same Emax, lower EC50 = more potent.📖 S13

Q8Drug A has Emax 100 and EC50 100. Drug C has Emax 150 and EC50 100. Which statement is correct?

✅ A · S12: same EC50, higher Emax = more efficacious.📖 S12

Q9A dog receiving high-dose morphine is given buprenorphine. Based on the slide, what happens to the mu-opioid receptor response?

✅ A · S19: buprenorphine is a partial agonist; partial agonists can act as antagonists against more efficacious agonists.📖 S19

Q10Goserelin is used in prostate and breast cancer. At the luteinizing hormone-releasing hormone receptor (LHRHR) it acts as a:

✅ C · S19: goserelin is an LHRHR super agonist used to downregulate LHRHR and sex hormone production.📖 S19

Q11For an inverse agonist to produce a measurable effect, the receptor must have:

✅ D · S18: inverse agonism is often caused by receptors that have basal signaling activity with no agonist.📖 S18

Q12With an antagonist present, the agonist curve shifts right, but enough agonist still reaches the full Emax. Which drug best fits this pattern?

✅ B · S21: competitive antagonist (naloxone) raises EC50 and is overcome by more agonist. Ketamine lowers Emax; buprenorphine (partial agonist) caps the max; Ro15-4513 is an inverse agonist. NMDA = N-methyl-D-aspartate; GABA = gamma-aminobutyric acid.📖 S21

Q13In the slide's lock-and-key model, a car boot that stops the car whether or not a key is in the ignition represents a:

✅ B · S17: car boot = non-competitive antagonist binding an allosteric site; key blank = competitive antagonist.📖 S17

Q14Per the slide, raising agonist concentration overcomes a competitive antagonist by:

✅ A · S21: more agonist does not kick out the antagonist but competes for binding sites; ability depends partly on antagonist k-1.📖 S21

Q15An antagonist binds the agonist binding site but has essentially no measurable off rate (koff). Its dose-response effect will look like:

✅ C · S22: an irreversible antagonist (actual or practical) looks non-competitive regardless of where it binds.📖 S22

Q16Using the slide's off rates (koff), which ligand has the shortest receptor residence time?

✅ D · S22: acetylcholine has low/sub-millisecond residence times (koff ~1000/s), the fastest off rate listed.📖 S22

Q17A dog with a pheochromocytoma is prepped for surgery. Why is phenoxybenzamine chosen to prevent norepinephrine-driven hemodynamic instability?

✅ A · S24: a non-competitive inhibitor should reduce maximum epinephrine response no matter the dose; phenoxybenzamine is an irreversible, non-competitive alpha-adrenergic inhibitor.📖 S23–S24

Q18Which of the following is NOT a method of receptor desensitization shown on the slide?

✅ C · S8 lists three methods: disabled signaling (kinase + arrestin), membrane cycling, reduced expression. Inverse agonism is a ligand type (S18).📖 S8

Apex of the fibrous pericardium → ventral diaphragm
📝 Slide note (professor)
Note:
The phrenicopericardial ligament is a continuation of the fibrous pericardium. It serves as a stability agent to help hold the heart in place in the thoracic cavity. It attaches to the diaphragm and the sternum.

You likely will have fat covering this ligament, however, try to find this attachment to the diaphragm like in the image.

❓ Name it (complete + specific)
Auricular surface
📝 Slide note (professor)
Note:
The heart has surfaces that you need to identify. One surface is the auricular surface. This surface is named because you see the auricles from this view. This surface faces the left thoracic wall.

You will have to pick through the fat covering the pericardium to find the right and left auricles, the left auricle can sometimes be difficult to find and you may only see a small portion of it.

Coronary groove
🧩 Where?
Between atria + ventricles; coronary vessels + fat
📝 Slide note (professor)
The coronary groove or sulcus, lies between the atria and the ventricles. This groove contains the coronary vessels and fat. It is hard to fully visualize the coronary groove as the auricles and atriums cover it, however, you can see a coronary artery branch off the aorta as it travels within this groove.

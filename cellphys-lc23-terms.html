⚠️ Precision traps

  • Early fasting = glycogen breakdown · late fasting = glycogen gone → gluconeogenesis + ketogenesis
  • Brain: glucose → glucose + ketones (late fasting) → ketones (starvation)
  • Exercise hormone = epinephrine, not glucagon · exercise liver makes glucose from lactate
  • Starvation liver AND kidney make glucose from glycerol
  • Every breakdown enzyme ON = phosphorylated (PKA) · every build enzyme ON = dephosphorylated (phosphatases)
  • Insulin builds protein by blocking breakdown · cortisol = catabolic · T3 does both

🔄 LC23 · Integration of metabolism (Mon 10/19)

Integration of metabolismWhich pathways run when, in which tissue, under which hormone.
5 metabolic statesFed (just ate) · early fasting (1–6 h) · late fasting (6–48 h) · starvation (>48 h) · exercise.
Fed state · hormonesHigh INSULIN, low glucagon.
Fed state · bloodFull of glucose, triglycerides and amino acids.
Fed state · liverMakes glycogen, fatty acids and triglycerides.
Fed state · adiposeMakes triglycerides.
Fed state · muscleMakes glycogen and proteins.
Fed state · brainBurns glucose.
Early fasting · hormonesInsulin falling, glucagon rising.
Early fasting · liverBreaks down glycogen to put glucose into the blood.
Early fasting · adiposeBreaks down triglycerides → fatty acids + glycerol into blood.
Early fasting · muscleBreaks down its glycogen; burns glucose and fatty acids.
Late fasting · hormonesLow insulin, high GLUCAGON.
Late fasting · liverGlycogen depleted → gluconeogenesis (glucogenic amino acids, glycerol) + ketogenesis (ketogenic amino acids). Same as a ketogenic diet.
Late fasting · muscleBreaks down protein → amino acids into the blood.
Late fasting · brainBurns glucose AND ketone bodies.
Starvation · hormonesVery low insulin, high (but moderating) glucagon.
Starvation · bloodFalling glucose, high fatty acids and glycerol.
Starvation · liverGluconeogenesis from glycerol; β-oxidation → ketone bodies.
Starvation · kidneyJoins in: gluconeogenesis from glycerol.
Starvation · muscleWasting; burns fatty acids.
Starvation · brainBurns ketone bodies.
Starvation time courseExogenous glucose → liver glycogen (first day) → gluconeogenesis → ketone bodies climb over days.
Exercise · hormoneEPINEPHRINE.
Exercise · bloodOxygen delivery falls behind muscle use.
Exercise · muscleBurns glucose + fatty acids; more fuel than oxygen → makes lactate.
Exercise · liverGluconeogenesis from lactate (Cori cycle), during and after exercise.
Exercise · adiposeBreaks down triglycerides → fatty acids + glycerol into blood.
Glycolysis controlPFK-1, activated by fructose-2,6-bisphosphate; PFK-2/FBPase-2 dephosphorylated; insulin → protein phosphatases.
Gluconeogenesis controlFBPase-1, freed from fructose-2,6-bisphosphate inhibition; PFK-2/FBPase-2 phosphorylated; glucagon/epinephrine → PKA.
Glycogenesis controlGlycogen synthase, ON when dephosphorylated; insulin → protein phosphatases.
Glycogenolysis controlGlycogen phosphorylase, ON when phosphorylated; glucagon/epinephrine → PKA.
Lipogenesis controlAcetyl-CoA carboxylase, ON when dephosphorylated; insulin → protein phosphatases.
Lipolysis controlHormone-sensitive lipase, ON when phosphorylated; glucagon/epinephrine → PKA.
Growth hormone → IGF-1Growth hormone raises insulin-like growth factor 1 → more protein synthesis.
Testosterone (anabolic steroid)Synergizes with growth hormone: ↑IGF-1, ↑protein synthesis.
Insulin and proteinAnabolic by INHIBITING protein breakdown; insulin insensitivity blocks much of the growth hormone effect.
Thyroid hormone (T3)Triiodothyronine: ↑protein synthesis + ↑growth hormone, but ↑breakdown at supraphysiological levels.
Glucocorticoids (cortisol)Catabolic: increase protein breakdown.

Intervenous tubercle
📝 Slide note (professor) Note:
The intervenous tubercle is a transverse ridge of tissue found between the two caval openings into the right atrium.

It diverts the inflowing blood from the caval veins towards the right atrioventricular orifice.

It is hard to visualize this on a 2D image.

Fossa ovalis
📝 Slide note (professor) Note:
The fossa ovalis is a slitlike depression found in the right atrium, it is just caudal to the intervenous tubercle.

This structure is a fetal remnant of the foramen ovale which allows for blood to bypass the fetal lungs and head straight from the right atrium to the left atrium.

Once the fetus is born, this foramen closes and it becomes the fossa olavis.

This may be a very small depression in some animals but see if you can find this structure near the intervenous tubercle.

📝 Slide note (professor) Note:

Right atrioventricular valve
📝 Slide note (professor) Note:
The right atrioventricular orifice contains a valve, this valve is the right atrioventricular valve or right AV valve or tricuspid valve.

The valve has parietal and septal cusps. The septal cusps are the cusps that attach to the septum. (most ventral cusp in the image) The parietal cusps attach to the parietal wall (most dorsal cusp in the image)

Trabecula septomarginalis
📝 Slide note (professor) Note:
Another common name for the trabecula septomarginalis is the moderator band.

This structure is found within the right ventricle and assists with conducting electrical impulses throughout the heart.

As you were cutting into the right ventricle, you may have inadvertently transected this band, forcefully opening the ventricle may have ripped this band as well, if that happened, try to find the stumps where this band connect on both the parietal and septal walls.

Pulmonary valve semilunar cusps
📝 Slide note (professor) Note:
At the junction of the right ventricle and the pulmonary trunk is the pulmonary valve.

This valve consists of 3 semilunar cusps which control blood flow.

Try to elevate up one of the cusps to view what it looks like. In the image, the probe is holding up a semilunar cusp.

You will need to clean the latex out of this area to find the valve, you may also need to extend your incision in the right ventricle further cranially to better access the area.

🫘 LC55 · Renal System 4

Basel · tubule microanatomy & proximal tubule · Wed 11/4 · LC55+56 S1–19 · 35 terms · say it, then tap the pink box

Why creatinine, not glucose, measures GFRBoth are freely filtered, but glucose is ~100% REABSORBED → none in normal urine; creatinine is not reabsorbed (S2)
★ Nephron order (Basel: "say it in your sleep")Glomerulus → proximal tubule → thin descending loop → thin ascending loop → thick ascending loop → distal convoluted tubule → collecting duct (S6)
Pars convoluta containsGlomeruli + proximal convoluted tubules (~80% of tubules) + distal convoluted tubules · rare bits of collecting duct (S8)
★ Proximal tubule on H&ETall, big, very PINK cells (packed with mitochondria) · MESSY lumen (microvilli) (S8, S12)
★ Distal convoluted tubule on H&ESmaller, paler cells · CLEANER lumen · minority of cortical tubules (S8)
★ Collecting duct on H&EBig but PALE cells · large clean lumen · cells BULGE into the lumen · perinuclear halos (S8–10)
Pars radiata (medullary ray) containsLate (straight) proximal tubule · thin loops · thick loops · collecting ducts · NO glomeruli (S9)
Thick loop of Henle on H&ECuboidal, shorter, less pink cells with a cleaner lumen · looks like distal tubule but sits in the medullary ray/medulla (S9–10)
Thin loop of Henle on H&ESQUAMOUS cells · often collapsed · nearly identical to capillaries (S9–10)
★ Thin loop vs vasa rectaLook the same (squamous) · tell them apart FUNCTIONALLY: part of the nephron = thin loop · part of the blood supply = vasa recta (S10)
Medulla containsThin loops · thick loops · collecting ducts · vasa recta (S10)
Proximal convoluted vs proximal tubuleSame segment · 3 parts: first two convoluted (pars convoluta), last one straight (medullary ray)
★ Why reabsorption costs energyFreely filtered = same concentration on both sides → moving it back means pushing it UP a gradient (S12)
★ The one pump the proximal tubule spends its ATP onNa⁺/K⁺-ATPase (basolateral) → keeps cell Na⁺ low → powers SECONDARY active transport (S12–13)
Proximal tubule tight junctionsPresent but LEAKY (to water, later Cl⁻ and Na⁺) → key for passive reabsorption (S12, S15)
★ Glucose reabsorptionSGLT1/2 (sodium-glucose cotransporters, apical) bring glucose in with Na⁺ → GLUT1/2 let it out basolaterally (passive) → glucose → 0 by end of proximal tubule (S13)
Amino acid reabsorptionFamilies of Na⁺-coupled amino acid cotransporters (apical) + passive amino acid transporters (basolateral) (S13)
★ Water in the proximal tubuleFollows solute ISOSMOTICALLY through leaky tight junctions → osmolarity stays ~300, volume shrinks (S13, S15)
Organic anion secretionOAT1/3 bring anions in (using a dicarboxylate gradient, refilled by Na⁺-dicarboxylate cotransporter NaDC) → MRP2/4, ABCG2 pump them into the lumen (S14)
Organic cation secretionBasolateral uptake → apical exchange for H⁺ (H⁺ gradient made by the Na⁺/H⁺ exchanger) (S14)
What gets secreted this wayProstaglandins, many drugs, hormones, oxalate → efficient blood clearance (S14)
Ethylene glycol (antifreeze)Metabolized to OXALATE → pumped into the tubule → kidney damage/crystals
★ Late proximal tubule chlorideCl⁻ is not reabsorbed early → its concentration RISES as water leaves → Cl⁻ leaks out passively (paracellular) (S15)
★ Lumen-POSITIVE charge #1 (Basel: "write this 17 times")Late proximal tubule: Cl⁻ leaves → lumen becomes positive → drives Na⁺ (and other cations) out passively (S15)
★ Proximal tubule reabsorbs (S15, S19)~100% glucose · ~100% amino acids · ~100% bicarbonate · ~65% of filtered Na⁺ and water
Protein endocytosis receptorsMEGALIN + CUBAM (cubilin/amnionless) bind filtered proteins → endocytosis → lysosome → amino acids back to blood (S16)
High urine protein suggestsFiltration barrier breached (too much delivered) or proximal tubule failure (S16)
★ Megalin and nephrotoxic antibioticsMegalin binds aminoglycosides and colistin → concentrates them in proximal tubule cells → damage (S16)
★ Myoglobin and the kidneyRhabdomyolysis → myoglobin (17 kDa) partially filtered → endocytosed → HEME/iron cause oxidative damage → proximal tubule death → AKI (S16)
Bicarbonate size + why it matters~62 Da → freely filtered · losing it = severe metabolic acidosis (S17)
★ Bicarbonate reabsorption (steps)1 Na⁺/H⁺ exchanger pumps H⁺ into lumen · 2 H⁺ + HCO₃⁻ → CO₂ + H₂O (luminal carbonic anhydrase) · 3 CO₂ diffuses into the cell · 4 cell carbonic anhydrase remakes HCO₃⁻ · 5 HCO₃⁻ exits to blood (S17)
Proximal tubule bicarbonate transportersNO apical bicarbonate transporter → uses carbonic anhydrase + CO₂ instead (S17)
Le Chatelier's principle (bicarbonate)Adding H⁺ in the lumen pushes the reaction toward CO₂; removing H⁺ in the cell pushes it back toward HCO₃⁻
Glycosuria / proteinuria / alkaline urine togetherProximal tubule problem: exceeded transport maximum (e.g., diabetes mellitus) OR proximal tubule failure (S19)
Fanconi syndromeProximal tubule cell failure (e.g., nephrotoxic antibiotics) → glycosuria + proteinuria + alkaline urine at once (S19)

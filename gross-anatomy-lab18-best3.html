🧩 Cusps? 3 semilunar: right, left, intermediate
🧩 Cusps? 3 semilunar: right, left, intermediate
📝 Slide note (professor) Note:
At the junction of the right ventricle and the pulmonary trunk is the pulmonary valve.

This valve consists of 3 semilunar cusps which control blood flow.

Try to elevate up one of the cusps to view what it looks like. In the image, the probe is holding up a semilunar cusp.

You will need to clean the latex out of this area to find the valve, you may also need to extend your incision in the right ventricle further cranially to better access the area.

❓ Identify the highlighted structure.
Dorsal branch of vagus nerve
🧩 What? CN X: parasympathetic (+ sensory) to thoracic + abdominal viscera
🔀 Joins? Opposite dorsal branch → dorsal vagal trunk on the esophagus
📝 Slide note (professor) Note:
Follow the vagus nerve as it courses over the heart, giving off cardiac branches. The vagus nerves are bilateral, each nerve gives off a dorsal and a ventral branch. These branches right and left dorsal branches unite and the right and left ventral branches unite caudally to form vagal trunks. These trunks are found on the dorsal and ventral surfaces of the esophagus. The dorsal trunk unites much further caudally than the ventral trunk, the dorsal trunk is often very hard to visualize as it unites deep within the thoracic cavity right before passing through the diaphragm.

Dissection:
You will need to dissect out the vagus nerves and their branches to find where they unite to form the trunks. This is a tedious process of picking through fascia to follow these nerves. These nerves may also have already been transected if when removing the lungs, it was cut too close to the heart. If that is the case, try to identify the stumps of each nerve and find the trunks. (Figure 5-57)

Auricular surface
📝 Slide note (professor) Note:
The heart has surfaces that you need to identify. One surface is the auricular surface. This surface is named because you see the auricles from this view. This surface faces the left thoracic wall.

You will have to pick through the fat covering the pericardium to find the right and left auricles, the left auricle can sometimes be difficult to find and you may only see a small portion of it.

★ pink row = professor emphasized
★ Flow equationFlow = (atmospheric pressure − alveolar pressure) ÷ airway resistance
Why the lung moves with the chest wallVisceral + parietal pleura held together by a few mL of pleural fluid (~100 µm space)
Intrapleural pressure at rest (functional residual capacity)~ −5 cm H₂O (lung recoils in, chest wall springs out)
★ Most important inspiratory muscleDiaphragm · costal part = prime mover · crural part not a prime mover
Diaphragm central tendonCollagen (not muscle) · lines of strain radiate from the muscle fibers
Diaphragm innervationPhrenic nerves · C3–5 (human), C5–7 (dog, horse)
Diaphragm in the galloping horseRib cage locked → diaphragm = piston · very oxidative · inertia of viscera adds only ~4–5%
Breathing-stride couplingHorse canter/gallop = 1 breath per stride
Exhalation at restPassive recoil · HORSE = exception (active exhalation even at rest)
External intercostalsRaise ribs → inspiration (recruited in exercise)
Internal intercostalsPull ribs down → expiration (exercise)
Main expiratory muscles in exerciseAbdominals (rectus, obliques, transversus) → push diaphragm up
Respiratory muscle "steal"↑ breathing work → diaphragm takes blood flow from limbs → exercise intolerance
Inspiratory pressure in exercising horse~ −40 to −50 cm H₂O → tends to collapse nares, pharynx, larynx
★ "Roaring" in horsesLeft laryngeal hemiplegia: left recurrent laryngeal nerve damaged → arytenoid sucked in on INSPIRATION
Treatment for roaringSurgery ties the arytenoid back
"Honking" in toy dog breedsWeak intrathoracic trachea collapses on EXPIRATION (dynamic compression)
ComplianceChange in volume ÷ change in pressure · human ~200 mL/cm H₂O
Compliance curve shapeSigmoid · steep near functional residual capacity · flat (stiff) near total lung capacity
★ Why not breathe near total lung capacity?Lung is stiff there (lots of pressure for little volume) + high vascular resistance
HysteresisInflation and deflation curves differ (surface tension)
Measure intrapleural pressureEsophageal balloon
Lung compliance with ageIncreases
★ EmphysemaHoles in the elastic weave → HIGH compliance, LOW recoil → airways collapse → obstructive, hard to get air OUT
Emphysema on auscultationCrackles = collapsed airways snapping open
Emphysema causesSmoking (humans) · dust/pollution (animals) · alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency
Where emphysema startsApex/dorsal lung (most stretch)
★ FibrosisStiff lung → LOW compliance, HIGH recoil → hard to get air IN ("wetsuit too tight")
Fibrosis causesDust, asbestos (macrophages won't eat it), scarring after EIPH
Fibrosis patient body conditionThin · ribs/intercostals visible (huge breathing work)
3 determinants of lung elasticity1 elastin + collagen · 2 surface tension · 3 interdependence
Collagen weaveLike nylon stockings: fibers don't stretch, but the weave is distensible with recoil
Surface tensionIntermolecular forces at a gas–fluid interface
Law of LaplacePressure = 4 × surface tension ÷ radius (alveolus 2T/r) → SMALLER bubble = HIGHER collapsing pressure
Without surfactantLung very STIFF + UNSTABLE (small alveoli empty into big ones and collapse)
SurfactantFrom Type II cells (lamellar bodies) · lowers surface tension · not a lubricant
Saline-inflated lungCompliance ↑ (no gas–fluid surface)
InterdependenceTo collapse, an alveolus must pull on all its neighbors → keeps lung stable
Why ventilation goes to the baseApex alveoli pulled onto stiff part of curve · base on compliant part
PneumothoraxLung recoils to below residual volume · chest wall springs OUT · diaphragm appears low
Spontaneous pneumothorax (humans)Fast-growing teenage boys · can reabsorb in 2–3 weeks
Intrapleural pressure: emphysema vs fibrosisEmphysema less negative · fibrosis more negative
Small animals/neonatesMore compliant chest wall → more atelectasis
★ Where is most airway resistance?LARGE airways (>70% in horse in trachea + upper airway)
★ Where to "hide" a quarter-sized tumorDeep in the lung (small airways) · in a large airway it raises resistance
Airway resistance vs lung volumeFalls at high volume (airways pulled open)
★ High expiratory flow at high lung volumeHigh recoil (alveolar pressure) + low resistance (airways held open)
★ Effort independenceMuch of the forced expiratory flow–volume curve is set by lung mechanics, not effort (dynamic airway compression)

⚠️ Precision traps

  • Emphysema = HIGH compliance, LOW recoil (more compliant ≠ more elastic)
  • Fibrosis = LOW compliance, HIGH recoil
  • Roaring = horse, INSPIRATION, larynx · honking = toy dog, EXPIRATION, intrathoracic trachea
  • Laplace: SMALL alveolus has the HIGHER collapsing pressure
  • Pneumothorax: lung goes IN, chest wall goes OUT

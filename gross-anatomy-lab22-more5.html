Falciform ligament
📝 Slide note (professor)
The falciform ligament is a fold of peritoneum that passes from the umbilicus to the diaphragm. It is also attached to the liver between the left medial and quadrate lobes. Observe the large fatty mass of the falciform ligament as you reflect the rectus abdominis cranially.

Identify the highlighted structure.📚 course slide · Lab 22 slides #14
❓ Name it (complete + specific)
Median ligament of the bladder
📝 Slide note (professor)
Caudal to the umbilicus, the fold of peritoneum is called the median ligament of the bladder. In the fetus, the urachus and the umbilical arteries are in the free border of the median ligament of the bladder. Notice how you can see the ligament stretching down to the bladder to help suspend it in place.

Caudal epigastric artery
📝 Slide note (professor)
The caudal epigastric artery and vein course cranially on the deep face of the caudal part of the rectus abdominis. Remember finding the cranial epigastric artery and vein, similar concept here. The origin of the artery is the pudendoepigastric trunk of the deep femoral artery. Dissection: Clean the fat and fascia away from the lateral border the of falciform ligament and the median ligament of the bladder to find these arteries and veins coursing cranially.

Identify the highlighted structure.📚 course slide · Lab 22 slides #23
❓ Name it (complete + specific)
Greater omentum
📝 Slide note (professor)
The greater omentum is he first structure that is seen after reflecting the abdominal wall. It has a lace like appearance and is very delicate. This omentum is very easy to tear and rip. The greater omentum largely covers the jejunum and ileum. It contains a superficial leaf and a deep leaf. Between the two leaves is a cavity, this cavity is called the omental bursa. Try to elevate the greater omentum to see the two leaves and the cavity they create.

Identify the highlighted structure.📚 course slide · Lab 22 slides #28
❓ Name it (complete + specific)
Urinary bladder
📝 Slide note (professor)
The urinary bladder when empty, is contracted and lies on the floor of the pelvic inlet. When full or distended, it lies on the floor of the abdomen and conforms in shape to the caudal part of the abdominal cavity because it displaces all freely movable viscera. In your cadaver, the bladder should be empty. Appearing as a sphere like shape, trace the median ligament of the bladder down to the bladder itself if you are struggling to find it.

❓ Name it (complete + specific)
Cervix
📝 Slide note (professor)
The uterus consists of a short cervix, a body and two long horns. Trace the uterus throughout the abdominal cavity to find these parts. You should be able to palpate the cervix which will palpate as a thickening caudal to the body.

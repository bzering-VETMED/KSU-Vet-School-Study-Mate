LC14 · Protein structure & diagnostics

Q1Mature hemoglobin needs two alpha and two beta chains to function. This describes which level of protein structure?

✅ A · Quaternary = several polypeptides forming one protein unit (only some proteins).📖 LC14 S13

Q2Alpha helices and beta sheets are held together mainly by:

✅ D · Secondary structure = local folds from backbone hydrogen bonds.📖 LC14 S9, S11

Q3Feral pigs have been seen near a Texas hobby herd that now has infertility and abortions. A tube agglutination test is run. What is it detecting?

✅ C · Agglutination uses antigen (Brucella suis) to probe serum for antibody; antibody cross-links → clumps.📖 LC14 S20–21
LC15 · Protein regulation & enzymes

Q4A newly translated protein has no signal peptide and no targeting sequence. Where does it end up?

✅ B · Cytoplasm is the default; signal or target peptides send proteins elsewhere.📖 LC15 S5

Q5Cortisol has just released the glucocorticoid receptor from its heat shock protein complex. What happens next?

✅ B · Cortisol → shape change → HSP released → dimer → nucleus → immunosuppressive transcription → later ubiquitinated.📖 LC15 S11–12

Q6A pathway's end product binds the pathway's first enzyme away from the active site and slows it. This is:

✅ D · Later product → inhibits earlier enzyme = feedback inhibition; binding away from the active site = allosteric.📖 LC15 S23–24
LC16 · Glucose transport & glycolysis

Q7Which three glycolytic steps are irreversible?

✅ C · Those three have large negative ΔG and are the regulated steps (and the ones gluconeogenesis must bypass).📖 LC16 S13–14; LC18 S22

Q8Fructose-1,6-bisphosphate builds up in a muscle cell. Which enzyme does it activate, and how?

✅ A · PFK-1's product feed-forward activates pyruvate kinase, keeping the pathway moving together.📖 LC16 transcript; LC15 S24

Q9Cattle moved from pasture to grain are depressed, with foul diarrhea and reduced rumen contractions. Which statement best explains it?

✅ C · Grain overload: acid rumen → Lactobacillus wins → lactic acid → kills other microbes, slows rumen, may cause acidosis.📖 LC16 S27
LC17 · TCA cycle & electron transport

Q10No step of the citric acid cycle uses O₂, yet the cycle stops without it. Why?

✅ B · The cycle uses up NAD⁺/FAD; only the ETS (with O₂ as final acceptor) empties them again.📖 LC17 S9

Q11A toxin makes the inner mitochondrial membrane leaky to protons. Electron transport speeds up, but ATP production collapses. Which explanation fits?

✅ D · Application: ATP synthase needs the proton motive force; leaked protons bypass it.📖 LC17 S21–22 (applied, like the DNP quiz question)

Q12About how much ATP does each NADH yield compared with each FADH₂?

✅ A · NADH moves ~10 H⁺, FADH₂ ~6 H⁺ (skips complex I). 10 and 6 are protons, not ATP.📖 LC17 S23
LC18 · Glycogen & gluconeogenesis

Q13A curly-coated retriever puppy has exercise intolerance, hepatomegaly, and fasting hypoglycemia. Which enzyme defect explains it?

✅ A · Glycogen storage disease IIIa (recessive): can only nibble the outer ends → glycogen piles up (big liver) but glucose isn't released.📖 LC18 S27

Q14Glycerol from triglycerides enters gluconeogenesis at which intermediate?

✅ D · Glycerol → glycerol-3-phosphate → DHAP, skipping the energy-costly steps (net energy positive).📖 LC18 S18, S24

Q15Why can muscle glycogen not be used to raise blood glucose?

✅ B · Only liver has glucose-6-phosphatase to make free glucose for export.📖 LC18 S13
LC19 · Amino acid catabolism

Q16Which amino acids does the liver preferentially catabolize in the fed state, while others pass through to muscle?

✅ C · Liver eats AAA; branched-chain amino acids pass through to muscle.📖 LC19 S8–9

Q17What is the first step in breaking down most amino acids?

✅ C · Transamination first (amine → α-ketoglutarate = glutamate), then deamination releases NH₄⁺.📖 LC19 S7

Q18Select all that apply. Which of the following contribute to ammonia's toxicity?

✅ A, C, E · Three harms from lecture: basic pH, K⁺ mimicry, ↑glutamine (glutamine is from the transcript, not a slide).📖 LC19 S15, S21; transcript
LC20 · Lipid metabolism & ketones

Q19Fatty acid synthesis uses which reducing carrier?

✅ D · Building (anabolism) uses NADPH, not NADH.📖 LC20 S5

Q20Fatty acyl-CoA is made in the cytosol. How does it reach the β-oxidation enzymes?

✅ B · Fatty acyl-CoA can't cross the mitochondrial membrane; carnitine shuttle does it. Malate/aspartate carries NADH electrons.📖 LC20 S7–8

Q21A dairy cow at peak lactation has serum ketones of 1.0 mM. How is this classified?

✅ A · Normal ~0.1 · ketosis > 0.5 (normal state) · ketoacidosis > 1.5 mM (pathological).📖 LC20 S17
LC21 · Cell signaling

Q22Histamine causes vasodilation during inflammation through which sequence?

✅ D · Paracrine pair: histamine (H1 on endothelial cells) → NO diffuses to smooth muscle → guanylyl cyclase → cGMP → relaxation.📖 LC21 "Paracrine Signaling"; "Histamine & Nitric Oxide"

Q23Glucagon stimulates one hepatocyte, and its coupled neighbor also starts breaking down glycogen. How?

✅ B · Cell coupling: small second messengers (cAMP) move through connexin channels and synchronize the response.📖 LC21 "Cell Coupling: Gap Junctions"

Q24Antidiuretic hormone in pigs differs from that of dogs because pigs make:

✅ C · Most mammals: arginine vasopressin. Pigs and marsupials: lysine vasopressin.📖 LC21 pt2 transcript

Q25A patient given Pitocin (oxytocin) to induce parturition develops fluid retention. Why?

✅ A · Receptors are selective, not specific: oxytocin differs from vasopressin by 2 amino acids and cross-reacts.📖 LC21 pt2 transcript

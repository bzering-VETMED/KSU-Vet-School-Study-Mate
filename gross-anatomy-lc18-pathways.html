The map · learn this first

Every part, labeled once

The pathway diagrams below hide labels you don't need. This one shows them all.

🧠 The highwayHis picture: the sympathetic trunk is a highway. The white ramus is the on-ramp, the gray ramus is the off-ramp back to the spinal nerve. Chain ganglia are the stops.
Translating his human slides (22–25)posterior root = dorsal root · anterior root = ventral root · posterior ramus = dorsal branch · anterior ramus = ventral branch · sympathetic trunk ganglion = chain (paravertebral) ganglion. Use the dog terms on the exam.
The one rule

Every sympathetic route starts the same way

  1. Lateral horn of the thoracolumbar cord
  2. Ventral root → spinal nerve
  3. White ramus → onto the sympathetic trunk

Only the ending changes. Ask one question: where does neuron 1 synapse?

SPOKEN ONLY · 10/8A student asked if what you need to know for the pathways is where the synapse happens. "Yes, that is correct… where is the synapse between neuron number one and neuron number two… that is the main table."
🧠 Count the stops before the target Spinal nerve: synapse in chain, back out the gray ramus · Postganglionic nerve: synapse in chain, out its own nerve · Splanchnic: synapse in prevertebral · Adrenal medulla: no synapse at all
Pathway 1 · slide 22

Spinal nerve pathway

Use it for: blood vessels, sweat glands and hair (arrector pili) muscles of the body wall and limbs.

  1. Starts in the lateral horn (thoracolumbar cord) → out the ventral root → into the spinal nerve.
  2. Takes the white ramus onto the sympathetic trunk.
  3. Synapses in a chain ganglion. It may ride the trunk up or down first.
  4. Neuron 2 takes the gray ramus back into the spinal nerve.
  5. Rides the dorsal or ventral branch to the target.
SPOKEN ONLY · 10/8"The most common is the spinal nerve pathway." Neuron 1 synapses in the closest available chain ganglion, or rides the trunk up or down first, then re-enters the spinal nerve.
Most likely testedThis is the only pathway that goes back into the spinal nerve. That is what the gray ramus is for.
Dog · Scenario 1 (slide 32)Body wall and limbs. Gray rami join every spinal nerve, so limb nerves carry sympathetic fibers too. → Connects to L19: every brachial plexus nerve on slide 21 "contains sympathetic fibers."
Pathway 2 · slide 23

Postganglionic sympathetic nerve pathway

Use it for: the heart (cardiac plexus), plus head, neck and thorax.

  1. Starts in the lateral horn (thoracolumbar cord) → out the ventral root → into the spinal nerve.
  2. Takes the white ramus onto the sympathetic trunk.
  3. Synapses in a chain ganglion.
  4. Neuron 2 leaves on its own nerve. It does not take the gray ramus back.
  5. Reaches the heart through the cardiac plexus.
SPOKEN ONLY"The main difference is it is not going to go back to the spinal nerve… it's going to create its own separate exit from that highway." If the heart needs to beat faster, this is the route.
Dog · Scenario 2 (slide 33): head, neck, thorax · 10/8 There are no chain ganglia in the head and neck. Neuron 1 leaves the thoracic cord, passes the thoracic chain ganglia without synapsing, and picks one of three cervical ganglia:

The loop of sympathetic trunk around the axillary artery is the ansa axillaris. He'll find all three ganglia and the vertebral nerve in lab.

Pathway 3 · slide 24

Splanchnic nerve pathway

Use it for: abdominal and pelvic organs, like the intestine. This is his exception (long pre, short post).

  1. Starts in the lateral horn (thoracolumbar cord) → out the ventral root → into the spinal nerve.
  2. Takes the white ramus onto the sympathetic trunk.
  3. Passes through the chain ganglion with no synapse.
  4. Travels in a splanchnic nerve.
  5. Synapses in a prevertebral ganglion, out in front of the vertebrae.
  6. Short neuron 2 → the organ.
The exceptionGeneral sympathetic rule: short pre, long post. Splanchnic flips it: long pre, short post. Paravertebral = beside the vertebrae (chain). Prevertebral = in front of them.
Dog scenarioNervesGanglion

4 · Pelvis (slide 35)lumbar splanchnic nerves → hypogastric nervecaudal mesenteric ganglion → pelvic plexus
SPOKEN ONLY · 10/8Celiacomesenteric = celiac + cranial mesenteric, for the stomach and small intestine. Lumbar splanchnic nerves serve the more caudal organs; the large and descending colon synapse in the caudal mesenteric ganglion. "When you're scared, you pee": sympathetics reach the bladder through lumbar splanchnic nerves → caudal mesenteric ganglion → hypogastric nerve. The pelvic plexus is a "truck stop": fibers pass through without synapsing.
Pathway 4 · slide 25

Adrenal medulla pathway

Use it for: the adrenal medulla. The whole-body fight-or-flight hormone surge.

  1. Starts in the lateral horn (thoracolumbar cord) → out the ventral root → into the spinal nerve.
  2. Takes the white ramus onto the sympathetic trunk.
  3. Passes through the chain ganglion, no synapse.
  4. Travels in a splanchnic nerve.
  5. Passes through the prevertebral ganglion, no synapse (his slide says so).
  6. Ends directly on the adrenal medulla. There is no neuron 2.
✅ Confirmed in the 10/8 lectureThe adrenal medulla's chromaffin cells are modified postganglionic sympathetic neurons (a modified neuron 2). Neuron 1 synapses directly on them, and they secrete epinephrine and norepinephrine. A student asked if this is the only sympathetic pathway with no synapse in a ganglion: "That is correct."
Side note he gaveAdrenal cortex = "salt, sugar, and sex" (mineralocorticoids, glucocorticoids, sex hormones). The medulla = epinephrine and norepinephrine.

Terminal vs. named ganglia · 10/8Only the vagus (and the sacral/pelvic outflow) uses true terminal ganglia in the organ wall. The four named head ganglia (III, VII, IX) sit near their target, not in its wall. He'll find the ciliary and pterygopalatine ganglia later, in the head lab.
Dog · slide 38 (vagus)left vagosympathetic nerve trunk → vagus nerve → cardiac nerves, recurrent laryngeal nerve → along the esophagus as the dorsal vagal nerve trunk (→ celiacomesenteric nerve plexus) and ventral vagal nerve trunk (→ liver & stomach). Left and right dorsal branches join into the dorsal vagal trunk (dorsal to the esophagus in the mediastinum); the ventral branches do the same. Both pass through the esophageal hiatus. The recurrent laryngeal nerve U-turns around the aortic arch back to the larynx.
Precision pair: both reach the pelvic plexusHypogastric nerve = sympathetic (from the caudal mesenteric ganglion). Pelvic nerve = parasympathetic (from S1–S3). Slide 40 shows both meeting at the pelvic plexus.
Lab trap he flaggedSympathetic ganglia are visible in lab. Parasympathetic terminal ganglia are not; they sit in the organ wall.
✅ Closed 10/8: nerve nameSlide 30 says "pelvic splanchnic nerves" (human term). He said to drop "splanchnic" to avoid confusing it with the sympathetic splanchnics: "As long as you call it pelvic nerve you are good."

Basel · loop of Henle, distal tubule, collecting duct · Wed 11/4 · LC55+56 S20–37 · 38 terms · say it, then tap the pink box

Osmolarity leaving the proximal tubule~300 (everything so far is isosmotic) (S21)
★ Thin descending limbPermeable to WATER, not Na⁺ → water leaves into the salty medulla → filtrate rises to ~1200 · no energy used (S21, S24)
★ Thin ascending limbPermeable to Na⁺, not water → Na⁺ leaves passively → filtrate falls back toward 300 (S21, S24)
Net effect of the thin loopISOSMOTIC loss of water + Na⁺ · osmolarity back to ~300, volume much smaller (S21)
Thin loop share of reabsorption+15% of filtered water (→ 80% total) (S24)
★ Rule for the second half of the nephron (Basel)"If water CAN move, it WILL move" · build a hypotonic filtrate, block water, then DECIDE with aquaporins (S33)
★ Thick ascending limbCuboidal, active, WATER-IMPERMEABLE · NKCC + Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pull NaCl out → HYPOtonic filtrate (S22)
★ NKCCNa⁺/K⁺/2Cl⁻ cotransporter · electroneutral (2 +, 2 −) · thick ascending limb (S22)
★ Lumen-POSITIVE charge #2Thick ascending limb: K⁺ leaks back into the lumen → positive lumen → extra paracellular Na⁺ (and Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺) reabsorption (S22, S25)
Loop of Henle share of Na⁺ reabsorption+25% of filtered Na⁺ (→ 90% total) (S22, S25)
★ Loop diureticsFurosemide · block NKCC → no hypotonic filtrate → Na⁺ and water lost in urine · used in racehorses (S22, S25)
Loop diuretic side effectsWaste Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺ → risk of hypoKALEMIA + hypoCALCEMIA (S25)
★ NCCNa⁺/Cl⁻ cotransporter · DISTAL convoluted tubule · does not need K⁺ (S27)
Distal tubule share of Na⁺ reabsorption+5% (→ 95% total) → filtrate even MORE hypotonic (S27, S30)
★ Thiazide diureticsBlock NCC → weaker diuretic than loop diuretics (S27, S30)
★ Why loop diuretics are stronger than thiazidesNaCl is easiest to pull out at the thick ascending limb (most transport there) + NKCC builds the medullary gradient
Thiazides: K⁺ and Ca²⁺POTASSIUM-wasting (weakly) · CALCIUM-sparing (S30)
Distal tubule calcium roleNot the main Ca²⁺ reabsorption site, but the DECISION point for the last ~8–10% (S28, S30)
★ ECaC (TRPV5)Epithelial calcium channel · apical Ca²⁺ entry in the distal tubule (S28)
Distal tubule basolateral Ca²⁺ exitNCX (Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger) + PMCA (plasma membrane Ca²⁺ pump) · calbindin buffers Ca²⁺ inside (S28)
★ Parathyroid hormone (PTH) in the distal tubule↑ apical ECaC → more Ca²⁺ reabsorbed → raises blood Ca²⁺ (S28, S30)
★ Collecting duct principal cellsENaC (Na⁺ in) + apical K⁺ channels (K⁺ out) + aquaporins (S32–33)
★ Aldosterone in principal cells↑ apical ENaC → more Na⁺ reabsorbed, K⁺ secreted → water stays EXTRACELLULAR → ↑ blood volume/pressure (S32)
Collecting duct share of Na⁺ reabsorption+3–5% (→ 98–100% total) (S36)
Water WITH Na⁺ vs water WITHOUT Na⁺With Na⁺ (aldosterone) → stays in ECF → ↑ blood pressure · without (ADH alone) → spreads into ICF + ECF → dilutes the body
AmilorideBlocks ENaC → less Na⁺ reabsorbed, less K⁺ lost → K⁺-SPARING (S32)
ANP in the collecting ductInhibits ENaC and Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase → opposite of aldosterone (S32)
★ K⁺-sparing vs K⁺-wasting diureticsBlock ENaC/aldosterone (amiloride, spironolactone) = SPARING · loop/thiazide send more Na⁺ to ENaC = WASTING (S32)
★ ADH (vasopressin) in principal cellsMoves aquaporins from inside the cell to the APICAL membrane → water rushes back into the body (S33)
Basolateral aquaporinsALWAYS present · only APICAL aquaporins are regulated (S33)
No ADH → ?No apical aquaporins → water stays in filtrate → large volume of dilute urine
"Hormone so nice we named it twice"Vasopressin = antidiuretic hormone (ADH)
Intercalated cellsCollecting duct cells "squeezed in between" principal cells · the ones that bulge into the lumen (S34)
★ Alpha-intercalated cellsSECRETE H⁺ into the lumen, return NEW HCO₃⁻ to blood → carnivores/omnivores, acidosis (S34, S36)
★ Beta-intercalated cellsSECRETE HCO₃⁻ into the lumen, return H⁺ to blood → herbivores, alkalosis (S34, S36)
How the kidney "reabsorbs >100%" of bicarbonateAlpha-intercalated cells make NEW bicarbonate from blood CO₂ (S34)
Alpha ↔ beta switchingProbably the same cell type · flip transporter sides with acid-base status (e.g., respiratory acidosis → more alpha cells)
Cumulative Na⁺ reabsorption (S24–36)Proximal 65% → loop 90% → distal 95% → collecting duct 98–100%

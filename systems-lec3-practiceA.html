Q1A dog with chronic bronchitis has: pH <7.4, HCO₃⁻ 34 mM, PaCO₂ 70 mmHg. Which description best fits?

✅ B · High CO₂ explains the acid = RESPIRATORY · bicarbonate above 24 = kidneys compensated = CHRONIC (his review: 'bicarb really high → kidneys retaining a while')

Q2An anesthetized dog that is being over-ventilated has: pH >7.4, HCO₃⁻ 24 mM, PaCO₂ 22 mmHg. Which description best fits?

✅ C · Low CO₂ = respiratory alkalemia · bicarbonate still 24 = kidneys haven't kicked in = ACUTE

Q3A diabetic cat in ketoacidosis breathes deeply and rapidly. Which receptors drive this response?

✅ D · Metabolic (blood) acid → PERIPHERAL only

Q4During anesthesia the CO₂ absorbent is exhausted and the dog rebreathes CO₂. Which chemoreceptors drive the increase in breathing?

✅ A · CO₂ is uncharged → crosses the blood–brain barrier (central) AND stimulates the carotid bodies

Q5Climbers who reach the summit of Mount Everest without supplemental O₂ tend to have:

✅ B · Review session: Pizzo's alveolar PCO₂ was 7 mmHg; better carotid-body sensitivity = higher on the mountain

Q6Pulmonary emphysema (increased compliance) decreases peak expiratory flow because:

✅ C · Flow = alveolar pressure ÷ resistance

Q7Why do the airways collapse during expiration in emphysema?

✅ D · Recoil normally tethers airways open; trapped air → high residual volume

Q8Use the Fick equation: a Thoroughbred at maximal exercise has cardiac output 350 L/min, CaO₂ 24 mL/100 mL, CvO₂ 3 mL/100 mL. VO₂?

✅ B · 350 × (24 − 3) ÷ 100 = 73.5 L/min

Q9A dog in heart failure: cardiac output falls from 4 to 2 L/min while VO₂ stays 200 mL/min. What is its a–vO₂ difference (mL/100 mL)?

✅ D · 200 mL/min ÷ 2,000 mL/min × 100 = 10 (half the output → double the extraction)

Q10A llama at 10,000 ft (barometric pressure 523 mmHg) has PaCO₂ 32 mmHg (R = 0.8). Calculate alveolar PO₂.

✅ C · (523 − 47) × 0.21 = 100 · 100 − (32 ÷ 0.8 = 40) = 60

Q11In the upright lung, compared with the apex, the base has:

✅ A · Review: base gets more of BOTH; V/Q ~1 at base, up to ~3 at apex

Q12A dog has overt hypoxemia and you suspect a right-to-left shunt. How might you confirm and quantify it?

✅ B · Review: 'have the animal breathe pure O₂, take a blood gas, look at the iso-shunt graph'

Q13Animals do not breathe close to total lung capacity during exercise because:

✅ C · Stiff lung (mechanics) + ↑ pulmonary vascular resistance → right heart (vascular)

Q14A horse makes a roaring noise while galloping. Which statement is correct?

✅ D · He confirmed a roaring question · left recurrent laryngeal nerve most often affected

Q15The two primary mechanisms causing exercise-induced pulmonary hemorrhage are:

✅ A · Positive inside + negative outside summate across the barrier

Q16How does the equine nasal strip reduce EIPH?

✅ B · Strip = outside (extravascular) pressure · Lasix = inside (blood volume)

Q17Cattle grazing at 9,000 ft develop brisket disease. The underlying mechanism is:

✅ C · Whole lung constricts → right heart must generate higher pressure → fails → fluid leaks (brisket)

Q18Why do Thoroughbred racehorses become hypoxemic during galloping, primarily?

Q19What are normal mixed venous PO₂ and PCO₂ at rest?

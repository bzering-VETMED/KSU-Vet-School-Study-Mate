⚠️ Precision traps

  • Basel: MEMORIZE Gs, Gi, Gq. Gs = adenylyl cyclase ON → cAMP ↑ · Gi = adenylyl cyclase OFF → cAMP ↓ · Gq = phospholipase C → DAG + IP₃ (no cAMP)
  • Same PGE₂ → EP2 = Gs (↑cAMP) but EP3 = Gi (↓cAMP). TxA₂ → TPβ = Gq. Don't swap EP2/EP3.
  • PTGS1 = CONSTITUTIVE (always on: gut, platelets) · PTGS2 = INDUCIBLE (PAMPs/DAMPs: soft tissue, endothelium). Don't flip them (transcript, S7).
  • Gs and Gi oppose each other at the SAME enzyme (adenylyl cyclase). Gq skips adenylyl cyclase entirely (transcript, S10–S11).
  • Glucocorticoids block PLA2 INDIRECTLY: transcription → ↑ annexin A1 → annexin A1 inhibits PLA2. Not a direct PLA2 inhibitor (transcript, S15).
  • Block COX-2 → TOWARD clotting (coxib; lose endothelial PGI2). Block COX-1 → AWAY from clotting (low-dose aspirin; lose platelet TxA2). Aspirin = COX-1 more than COX-2 (transcript, S26).
  • Gut: PGE2 = AUTOCRINE survival (same cell: don't die + mucus) · LTB4 = PARACRINE damage (neutrophils, chief cells, parietal cells). NSAID → shunt to 5-LOX → ↑ LTB4 (transcript, S21).
  • 5-LOX = 5-lipoxyGENASE (slides). Basel says "5-leukotriene oxidase" — write lipoxygenase (transcript vs S4/S6).
  • COX-1 vs COX-2 home tissues (S7 box colors): gastric mucosa and platelets (TxA2) = COX-1; joints/soft tissue, colon cancer, endothelium (PGI2), kidney, uterus = COX-2; airways = 5-LOX. S28 draws kidney as PTGS1/2. Do not flip platelets and endothelium.
  • TxA2 vs PGI2: TxA2 (platelets) = aggregation + vasoconstriction via Gq; PGI2 (endothelium) = platelet resting + vasodilation via Gs/cAMP. Opposites.
  • Where each drug hits: glucocorticoid → phospholipase A2 (via annexin A1, blocks ALL branches incl. leukotrienes); NSAID → COX-1 + COX-2; coxib → COX-2 only. None of the three block 5-LOX directly.
  • Gq goes to phospholipase C, not adenylyl cyclase: TPβ/EP1/FP → Gq → PLC → DAG + IP3 → Ca2+. Phospholipase C (signaling) vs phospholipase A2 (synthesis) — do not swap.
  • Adverse effect pairing: 1st-gen NSAID → gastric ulcers; coxib → cardiovascular events; BOTH → AKI risk in fragile patients; glucocorticoid → immune suppression + catabolism.
  • Kidney arterioles: PGE2 dilates the afferent; angiotensin II constricts the efferent. NSAIDs prevent afferent dilation AND (via ↓ renin) efferent contraction.
  • Cardinal sign causes: rubor = vasodilation; tumor = vascular permeability; calor = leukocyte activation (cytokines); dolor = nociception. Rubor and tumor are easy to swap.
  • Same word, different enzyme: "cytoprotection, anti-apoptotic" appears for gastric mucosa (COX-1, PGE2 + PGI2) AND colon cancer (COX-2, PGE2).
  • Do not memorize the receptor table — but know its Main Point: different G protein couplings explain why one prostaglandin causes different effects in different tissues.

🔥 LC28 · Eicosanoids (Mon 10/26)

EicosanoidS4, S5; transcriptSignaling molecule made by oxidizing arachidonic acid: prostaglandins, thromboxanes, leukotrienes. Lecture treats them as inflammatory signals; repro roles come next semester.
Arachidonic acid (AA)S5, S6; transcriptPolyunsaturated fatty acid in membrane phospholipids; the starting material for ALL eicosanoid classes. ★ "Only thing I want you to get off this slide."
Diacylglycerol/phospholipid (membrane source)S6Starting substrate at the top of the synthesis pathway; phospholipase A2 releases arachidonic acid from it.
Phospholipases A1 / A2 / C / DS6; transcriptNamed by where they cut a phospholipid: A1/A2 cut fatty acids off glycerol; C cuts the head group (→ IP3 + DAG).
Hydrophobic → autocrine/paracrineS5; transcriptVery hydrophobic with NO dedicated carrier proteins → mostly paracrine/autocrine; a few are endocrine (in reproduction, later).
Eicosanoid transportersS6; transcriptProducts are bulky and oxygenated, so they need transporters to leave the cell and act as paracrine signals.
Phospholipase A2 (PLA2)S6, S15; transcriptCuts a fatty acid off the glycerol backbone, releasing arachidonic acid. Blocking it (glucocorticoids via annexin A1) cuts off ALL branches.
Prostaglandin synthase (PTGS) = "cyclooxygenase"S6; transcriptPTGS is the current name; "cyclooxygenase (COX)" is historical because the enzyme oxidizes and cyclizes arachidonic acid.
Constitutive vs inducibleS7; transcriptConstitutive = always expressed by certain cells (PTGS1). Inducible = turned on by PAMPs/DAMPs (PTGS2).
PAMPs / DAMPsS7; transcriptPathogen-associated / damage-associated molecular patterns; trigger inflammation and induce PTGS2.
Prostaglandin-G/H synthase 1 (PTGS1) = cyclooxygenase-1 (COX-1)S6, S7, S26; transcript★ CONSTITUTIVE (always expressed). Digestive mucosa PGE2 (cytoprotective, anti-apoptotic); platelet thromboxane A2.
Prostaglandin-G/H synthase 2 (PTGS2) = cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2)S6, S7, S26; transcript★ INDUCIBLE (PAMPs/DAMPs trigger it). Soft-tissue inflammation/pain, endothelial PGI2, colon cancer prostaglandins; S7 also colors kidney and uterus.
5-lipoxygenase (5-LOX)S4, S6, S21; transcriptTakes arachidonic acid toward leukotrienes. Basel says "5-leukotriene oxidase"; slides say lipoxygenase (use that). 5-LOX drugs exist, used mostly in lungs.
Prostaglandin H2 (PGH2)S6COX-1/COX-2 product; the branch point that becomes prostaglandins, prostacyclins, and thromboxanes.
5-hydroperoxyeicosatetraenoic acid (5-HPETE)S6, S21; transcript5-LOX product; leukotriene precursor. Transcript: "You don't need to memorize this" name (slide typo: eicostetraeoic acid).
Prostaglandin / prostanoidS4, S5COX-derived eicosanoid family (prostanoids); e.g. PGE2, PGD2, PGF2α.
Prostaglandin E2 (PGE2)S5, S7, S21, S28Most versatile prostaglandin: gastric cytoprotection, inflammation/pain, colon cancer survival, afferent arteriole dilation in kidney.
Prostaglandin D2 (PGD2)S7, S9COX-2 prostaglandin in joints/soft tissue (inflammation, pain); receptors DP (Gs) and CRTH2 (Gi).
Prostaglandin F2α (PGF2α)S7, S9Prostaglandin in joints/soft tissue and in the uterus, where it causes luteolysis.
Prostacyclin = prostaglandin I2 (PGI2)S7, S9, S26; transcriptEndothelial (PTGS2) paracrine signal → Gs → cAMP → ↓ Ca2+: platelets rest, smooth muscle relaxes. Normally STRONGER than TxA2.
Thromboxane A2 (TxA2)S5, S7, S11, S26; transcriptPlatelet (PTGS1) product → Gq → ↑ Ca2+: autocrine platelet clotting + paracrine smooth muscle contraction.
LeukotrieneS4, S5, S7; transcript5-LOX-derived eicosanoid with NO ring (oxidized only); bronchoconstriction + mucus in lungs.
Leukotriene B4 (LTB4)S5, S7, S21; transcriptPARACRINE damage signal in the gut: activates neutrophils (oxidative damage), chief cells (digestive enzymes), parietal cells (HCl).
LTC4, LTD4, LTE4S7Airway leukotrienes (with LTB4): bronchoconstriction and mucus secretion.
CytoprotectionS4, S7Protective, anti-apoptotic eicosanoid effect: COX-1 PGE2/PGI2 in gastric mucosa; COX-2 PGE2 in colon cancer.
LuteolysisS7Breakdown of the corpus luteum; the uterine PGF2α effect in the functions table.
Eicosanoid receptorS8, S9G protein-coupled receptor; can couple Gs, Gi, Gq, or minor G proteins (G12, G13, Gh).
Heterotrimeric G protein (α, β, γ)S10; transcriptInactive with GDP; ligand-bound receptor swaps GDP → GTP, and α dissociates from βγ to act on an effector.
Plug-and-play pathwayS9; transcriptBasel's term: a few G protein pathways reused in many tissues; memorize Gs, Gi, Gq once (Dr. Schultz, Wednesday).
G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR)S8Seven-transmembrane receptor that activates a heterotrimeric G protein; the receptor type for all eicosanoids.
Main Point: different G protein couplingsS9Same prostaglandin, different receptor/G protein in different tissues = different effects (e.g. vasoconstriction vs vasodilation).
Gs (stimulatory G protein)S9, S10; transcriptGαs activates adenylyl cyclase → ↑ cAMP. EP2, EP4, DP, IP. Example: sympathetic heart (cAMP on HCN channels speeds depolarization).
Gi (inhibitory G protein)S9, S10Gαi inhibits adenylyl cyclase → ↓ cAMP. Used by EP3, CRTH2.
Gs vs Gi opposeS10; transcript"Gs and Gi almost always oppose each other" — both act on adenylyl cyclase in opposite directions.
GqS9, S11; transcriptGαq activates phospholipase C → cuts head group: IP3 released + DAG stays in membrane (↑ Ca2+). EP1, TPβ, FP.
Adenylyl cyclase (AC)S8, S10Enzyme that converts ATP to cAMP; activated by Gαs, inhibited by Gαi.
Cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP)S8, S10, S26Second messenger made by adenylyl cyclase; activates protein kinase A.
Protein kinase A (PKA)S26, S28cAMP-activated kinase; mediates platelet resting and smooth muscle relaxation.
Phospholipase C (PLC)S8, S11Gαq-activated enzyme that produces DAG and IP3.
Diacylglycerol (DAG)S8, S11PLC product and second messenger (slide typo: diacylglyerol).
Inositol trisphosphate (IP3)S8, S11, S26PLC product that raises cytosolic Ca2+ (slide writes triphosphate).
EP1S9, S21PGE2 receptor coupled to Gq (↑ IP3, DAG, Ca2+); shown in gastric mucosa cytoprotection.
EP2S9, S10PGE2 receptor coupled to Gs (↑ cAMP).
EP3S9, S10PGE2 receptor coupled to Gi/G12 (↓ cAMP).
EP4S9PGE2 receptor coupled to Gs (↑ cAMP).
IP receptor (PGI2R)S9, S26Prostacyclin receptor coupled to Gs (↑ cAMP) on platelets and smooth muscle.
TPα / TPβS9, S11Thromboxane A2 receptors; both Gq (+G13, Gh). TPα also Gs (↑ cAMP); TPβ also Gi (↓ cAMP).
DP / CRTH2S9PGD2 receptors: DP = Gs (↑ cAMP); CRTH2 = Gi (↓ cAMP).
FPA / FPBS9PGF2α receptors; both Gq (↑ IP3, DAG, Ca2+).
Cardinal signs of inflammationS13Rubor (vasodilation), tumor (vascular permeability), calor (leukocyte activation/cytokines), dolor (nociception); eicosanoids mediate all four.
HyperalgesiaS13; transcriptMORE sensitive to pain stimuli (algesia = sensitivity to pain); an eicosanoid-mediated inflammatory effect.
AnalgesicS13; transcript"Anti-algesia": drug that reduces sensitivity to pain.
NociceptionS13; transcriptSensing of pain; ↑ nociception = dolor.
FeverS13; transcriptCalor strong enough to become systemic.
Osteoarthritis (racehorse case)S2, S16, S19, S24; transcriptRacehorse case. Can't be fixed — treat the pain: glucocorticoid (short-term), NSAID (watch hindgut ulcers), or coxib/firocoxib (watch clotting).
GlucocorticoidS12, S15, S17Stress hormone; synthetic versions are steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs that block PLA2 via annexin A1.
Glucocorticoid examplesS15; transcriptCortisol (most common natural one), prednisone, prednisolone, dexamethasone.
Glucocorticoid one-sentenceS17; transcript★ "Glucocorticoids promote glucose to the brain at the expense of peripheral tissues and the immune system."
Steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (SAID)S12, S17Synthetic glucocorticoid; treats inflammation and autoimmune disease but is immune suppressive and catabolic.
Annexin A1S15; transcript★ Glucocorticoids activate transcription factors → ↑ annexin A1 → it binds and inhibits PLA2 → no arachidonic acid. INDIRECT block.
Catabolic side effectS17; transcriptPeripheral breakdown of muscle, bone, CARTILAGE → amino acids → liver gluconeogenesis (anabolic in liver). Joint injection: horse feels better, joint breaks down more.
Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID), 1st generationS12, S18, S22; transcriptBlocks COX-1 + COX-2. Reduces inflammation/pain; potentiates ulcers (horses: hindgut more than gastric, same mechanism).
NSAID examplesS18; transcriptHuman: ibuprofen, naproxen, aspirin. Vet: garbled ("Haloxicam" likely meloxicam) — see flag.
Hindgut ulcers (horses)S18, S21; transcriptNSAID ulceration in horses is more often hindgut than gastric; same PTGS1 mechanism.
Coxib (2nd-generation NSAID)S12, S23, S27; transcriptSelective PTGS2 (COX-2) blocker; spares gut, but pushes toward clotting → adverse cardiovascular events (human coxibs: black box warnings).
FirocoxibS23; transcript★ The only approved coxib in veterinary medicine; fewer cardiovascular effects than human coxibs.
Black box warningS27; transcriptStrongest drug warning (risk of death); carried by human coxibs for adverse cardiovascular events.
AspirinS26; transcript★ NSAID that blocks COX-1 MORE than COX-2 → low-dose aspirin deactivates platelets (given after heart attacks).
COX-1 vs COX-2 clotting ruleS26; transcriptBlock COX-2 → toward clotting (lose endothelial PGI2). Block COX-1 → away from clotting (lose platelet TxA2).
Chief cell / parietal cellS21Gastric diagram: LTB4 receptor on chief cell → ↑ digestive enzymes; on parietal cell → ↑ HCl.
Polymorphonuclear leukocyte (PMN)S21Neutrophil-type cell; LTB4 via leukotriene receptor (LTR) activates it → oxidative damage to gastric mucosa.
Leukotriene shuntingS21; transcript★ NSAID blocks PTGS1 → all arachidonic acid goes to 5-LOX → ↑ LTB4 damage + ↓ PGE2 survival → potentiates ulcers.
PGE2 autocrine survival signalS21; transcriptIn gastric epithelium PGE2 acts on its own cell: anti-apoptotic ("don't die") + ↑ mucus.
Afferent arterioleS7, S28, S30 · slides only, not yet lecturedArteriole into the glomerulus; PGE2 dilates it to maintain glomerular perfusion.
Efferent arterioleS30 · slides only, not yet lecturedArteriole leaving the glomerulus; angiotensin II raises its resistance to support glomerular pressure.
Macula densaS28, S30 · slides only, not yet lecturedTubule cells that sense low NaCl and make PGE2 (PTGS1/2), which stimulates renin exocytosis and dilates the afferent arteriole.
Juxtaglomerular granular cellsS28 · slides only, not yet lecturedRenin-containing cells; PGE2 stimulates renin exocytosis.
Renin → angiotensin II → aldosteroneS30 · slides only, not yet lecturedCascade triggered by macula densa PGE2; raises efferent resistance and Na+/H2O retention.
Glomerular filtration rate (GFR)S30 · slides only, not yet lecturedFiltration rate set by glomerular hydrostatic pressure; NSAIDs can lower it.
Acute kidney injury (AKI)S12, S29, S30 · slides only, not yet lecturedIschemic kidney damage NSAIDs/coxibs may cause in fragile patients.
At-risk patients for NSAID AKIS29, S30 · slides only, not yet lecturedHypovolemic, hypotensive, or on RAAS inhibitors (angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors or angiotensin receptor blockers).
